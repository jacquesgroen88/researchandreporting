Finding 1 · The one that costs the most

GrandWest sits at 4.2, and it can no longer be fixed by collecting reviews

It carries 2,013 reviews. To lift it from 4.2 to 4.5 you would need 1,208 new five-star reviews — and that assumes not one further review below five arrives in the meantime. At current volumes that is years of work.

This is the hard truth about review volume: it is an asset while a rating is good and a lock once it is not. GrandWest needs the underlying experience addressed, and then review flow to hold the improvement in place. What it does not need is another year of nobody noticing, which is what a 4.51 group average buys you.

Finding 2 · The cheapest win on the estate

Cotswold has 70 reviews against a group median of 1,452

It is 21 times less measured than the typical Hussar Grill. That is almost certainly not a worse restaurant — it is an invisible one. A diner comparing Hillcrest options sees a 4.4 backed by 70 opinions next to competitors backed by hundreds, and discounts it.

Because the base is small, the maths runs the other way: 14 new five-star reviews move it +0.1. Thirty-five move it to 4.6. That is a fortnight of asking properly. Five more locations sit under 500 reviews with the same profile.

The honest context

We are not going to tell you your ratings are bad

They are not. We publish a quarterly benchmark of South African businesses, and the clearest finding in it is that average rating falls as review count rises — 4.89 for businesses under ten reviews, 4.50 for those above a hundred. A five-star average is usually a small sample, not excellence.

The Hussar Grill sits at 4.51 against a 4.50 benchmark for businesses at your review volume. You are performing exactly normally for your scale.

Which is the actual opportunity. Normal is beatable, and at 40,000 reviews a tenth of a star is a gap competitors cannot close quickly. Nobody drifts to 4.7 across 28 sites by accident. It is bought with a system.

What is actually broken

Not the restaurants. The measurement.

  • Reviews are collected by accident. Some managers care, most are running a restaurant. The spread from 4.2 to 4.7 across one brand with one menu and one standard is not a food problem, it is an attention problem.
  • Nobody at head office sees per-location numbers. The group average is 4.51 and looks fine, and it conceals both GrandWest and Cotswold.
  • There is no review moment. The strongest point in a restaurant is when the bill is settled and the meal went well, and at that exact moment nothing happens.
  • Nothing captures Casa Bella at all. This audit covers Hussar Grill; Casa Bella sits outside it and should be added before rollout.

What we will not do

Worth stating plainly, because in this category it is not a given, and because for a brand your size the downside is asymmetric.

  • We do not sell or generate reviews.
  • We do not gate. Screening diners so only happy ones reach Google breaches Google's policies and is widely sold as a feature. For a 28-site group the exposure is not one listing, it is the entire estate at once.
  • We do not lock hardware to a subscription. Stop the monthly programme and the units keep working.

Everything we sell does one thing: it removes the distance between a diner who has just had a good meal and a review form they were always entitled to fill in. Our full position on what Google permits.
