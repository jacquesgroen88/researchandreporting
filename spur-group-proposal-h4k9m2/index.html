Finding 2 · Why it will not fix itself

At that review volume, a rating cannot be collected back

Hussar Grill GrandWest carries 2,013 reviews. To lift it from 4.2 to 4.5 you would need 1,208 consecutive five-star reviews, and that assumes not one further review below five arrives in the meantime. Casa Bella GrandWest needs 634. At current volumes both are years of work.

This is the hard truth about review volume: it is an asset while a rating is good and a lock once it is not.

So we would not sell you review hardware for GrandWest. The experience has to change first, and review flow then holds the improvement in place. Everything else in this document is worth doing today.

The honest context

We are not going to tell you your ratings are bad

They are not. We publish a quarterly benchmark of South African businesses, and the clearest finding in it is that average rating falls as review count rises. It sits at 4.89 for businesses under ten reviews and 4.50 for those above a hundred. A five-star average is usually a small sample, not excellence.

Your division sits at 4.49 against a 4.50 benchmark for businesses at your review volume. You are performing exactly normally for your scale.

Which is the actual opportunity. Normal is beatable, and at 46,822 reviews a tenth of a star is a gap competitors cannot close quickly. Nobody drifts to 4.7 across 35 sites by accident. It is bought with a system.

What is actually broken

Not the restaurants. The measurement.

  • Reviews are collected by accident. Some managers care, most are running a restaurant. The spread from 4.1 to 4.7 across two brands with one standard is not a food problem, it is an attention problem.
  • The two brands are never compared side by side. That is the only reason GrandWest has stayed invisible: each brand looks at its own list, and the pattern only appears when you put them on one page.
  • Nobody at head office sees per-location numbers. The division average is 4.49 and looks fine, and it conceals both GrandWest and Cotswold at the same time.
  • There is no review moment. The strongest point in a restaurant is when the bill is settled and the meal went well, and at that exact moment nothing happens.

What we will not do

Worth stating plainly, because in this category it is not a given, and because for a brand your size the downside is asymmetric.

  • We do not sell or generate reviews.
  • We do not gate. Screening diners so only happy ones reach Google breaches Google's policies and is widely sold as a feature. For a 35-site group the exposure is not one listing, it is the entire estate at once.
  • We do not lock hardware to a subscription. Stop the monthly programme and the units keep working.

Everything we sell does one thing: it removes the distance between a diner who has just had a good meal and a review form they were always entitled to fill in. Our full position on what Google permits.
