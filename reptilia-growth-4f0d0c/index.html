The strategy in one paragraph. Reptilia doesn't need more traffic yet. It needs to stop wasting what it has. About 100 real South Africans a day visit the site, mostly from Google, mostly on phones, and 0.7% buy. The biggest single group, animal searchers, can't buy online and has no way to ask, so they leave. Nobody who leaves is ever followed up, nobody who buys is ever brought back, and a basket is never more than what the customer came for. So we build four engines in this order:
  1. Catch every animal buyer (enquire, reserve, notify-me).
  2. Bring back everyone who left or bought (abandoned checkout, reorder reminders, win-back).
  3. Grow the basket (setup lists, ice-pack prompt, cross-sells, kits).
  4. Only then buy more visitors (Google Business Profile, SEO, then ads with a hard stop line).
Engines 1 and 2 are built in this pack. Black Friday weekend (27–29 Nov) is the first chance to use them.

2. The four engines

ENGINE 1 · CATCH

Never lose an animal buyer again

  • Every animal page gets a "How you get it home" panel, an enquiry form (stored, never lost), a WhatsApp button with the animal's name prefilled, and "Reserve and collect" (mockup A).
  • At checkout, live animals can only be collected, so no more courier offered for a snake to Cape Town.
  • Sold-out pages become waiting lists: "Notify me" by email or WhatsApp, plus similar animals in stock (mockup B). The iguana 404 and the empty frogs page get the same treatment.
  • Staff use the same 10 WhatsApp quick replies, aiming to reply within 1 hour.
  • Weekly: the enquiry and waitlist counts become Willem's buying list.
ENGINE 2 · BRING BACK

Follow up everyone, automatically

  • Abandoned checkout: 3 emails at 1 hour, 24 hours and 72 hours, plus a personal WhatsApp for baskets over R1,000. For the first time, abandoned baskets are visible in wp-admin.
  • Reorder reminders for frozen feed (day 25) and live feed (day 18), timed to the 30-day typical gap between orders.
  • Back-in-stock alerts, which Omnisend's data ranks as the highest-converting automation.
  • Welcome, thank-you, and a Google review request on day 7.
  • Win-back to 563 quiet customers in waves, repeat buyers first.
ENGINE 3 · BIGGER BASKETS

Upsells and cross-sells that come from real orders

  • Animal page: "Complete the setup" list (enclosure, heat, hide, bowl, food) with one-tap reserve of the whole setup.
  • Feed page: size chips, ice-pack prompt, "remind me to reorder", bought-together with prices (mockup C).
  • Side cart: ice-pack bump only when frozen feed is in the basket, plus 3 "often added" items (mockup D).
  • Cart cross-sells: import the 40-product map from 5 years of co-purchases (frozen rats and mice with ice packs 42 times; crickets with mealworms 46 times). Live animals are never cross-sell targets.
  • Kits: 4 first-pet kits for December (hamster, guinea pig, leopard gecko, bearded dragon). The homepage already promises kits that don't exist.
  • After the sale: a cross-sell in the reorder email. The checkout stays clean, with no checkout pop-ups.
ENGINE 4 · MORE OF THE RIGHT VISITORS

Then, and only then, buy traffic

  • Google Business Profile: weekly "in store now" photos, products, and review requests at the till and by email. "Near me" searches get under 1% click-through today.
  • In-store capture: a QR card at the till ("Get feed reminders and new-animal alerts"). The shop's walk-in customers are a bigger list than the website's.
  • SEO: Product markup (code ready), category intros, the four species pages (drafts exist), dead-end fixes.
  • Ads: Google brand, feeders and local first, then animal enquiries and Meta, with tracking proved first and an R300-per-order stop line (ads).
  • Trade: bulk frozen-feed buyers (one order was 126 frozen rats) get a "breeder and keeper bulk" price list. That's Willem's call.

5. How we keep the risk small

RiskHow it's handled
Breaking checkout on a store that takes real moneyEvery cart and checkout change goes to staging first, runs the 10-test plan, and has a one-click rollback (deactivate the snippet). Product and page snapshots are taken before any change, as before.
Email going to spam, or bounced by our own SPFBrevo is added to SPF/DKIM before the first send, with a mail-tester check. Volume starts at about 150 a day, with pause rules at 3% bounces or 0.3% complaints.
POPIAPast buyers only, similar products only, unsubscribe in every message, recent buyers first, and a checkout opt-in from now on. Enquiry and notify forms carry their own consent line.
Ad spend wastedNo spend until purchases are tracked and the pages convert. Phase caps, a negative keyword list, weekly search-term reviews, and a kill switch at 2× the stop line (ads).
Promising animals or claims we can't stand behind"Captive bred", "since 2006", hold periods, permits and the courier wording all wait for Willem. Mockups mark every one of them.
Staff can't keep up with the enquiriesSame-day replies are part of the conversion. Quick replies make answers fast and consistent. If replies are slow, we pause the paid animal campaigns first.
Data lossEnquiries are stored (Flamingo), abandoned baskets stored, email logs kept (FluentSMTP). Customer data stays on the VPS and inside Reptilia's own Brevo account.
