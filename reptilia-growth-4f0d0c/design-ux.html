Reptilia · Design and user experience · 19 November 2026

What the site looks like now, what it should look like, and how much it costs us

I scored the live site on a real phone-sized screen (390px, logged out), on the five journeys that make money, against what a good South African specialist pet shop should have. I didn't change anything on the site. Everything below the scores is a mockup built from Reptilia's real products, prices and photos.

4.1/10The live site today, averaged across 13 areas
6.5/10The homepage v3 preview we built in August, on the areas it covers
8.5/10The target once the templates on this page are live

1 to 10. Each "why" is something I saw on screen or measured, not a judgement call.

Out-of-stock experience1The long-haired guinea pig (368 Google clicks) shows a faded photo and "Out of stock". No alternatives, no "tell me when it's back", nothing to do but leave.9Back-in-stock sign-up by email or WhatsApp, plus similar animals in stock.
Trust signals2No reviews (switched off), no Google rating, and no WhatsApp anywhere. My earlier note said WhatsApp was on the homepage; that match was an image file name, so the correction is that there is none. The phone number only appears as plain text in the footer, not as a tap-to-call link. The email is a Gmail address.8Tap-to-call and WhatsApp in the header, Google rating, product reviews, a shop@ email address.
Side cart and cart3The side cart is mostly empty white space with "View cart" and "Checkout". The cart page has no product image and no cross-sells, and local pickup is preselected with no explanation.9Ice-pack bump, 3 "often added" items, a clear delivery choice.
Checkout5Styled and clear, but email is the last field (so nothing can be recovered if they leave), phone is optional, and a coupon box at the top sends people off hunting for codes. A red warning about a "30% handling fee" sits right next to the pay button.8Email and phone first, delivery chosen before the address, the warning moved into the terms, the coupon field small at the bottom.
Speed8Cached pages load in 0.03 to 0.2 seconds from the server. Cart and checkout take about 1 second. Real-world speed is fine now.9Keep the cache on, and trim the 30-product grids that bloat pages.
Search7The August Relevanssi fix works. People mostly search for animal names (ball python, gecko, hamster, bearded dragon, tortoise).8Searches for animals not in stock should land on "ask us / join the list" instead of "nothing found".
Retention hooks1No email sign-up, no back-in-stock, no reorder reminder, no account perks.8The flows in messages.html.

Average now 4.1. The homepage v3 preview (page 39774, built 25 Aug) already fixes the hero, the search and a "Restocking feed?" shortcut. It's the right direction, but it covers the homepage only, and the biggest losses are on product pages, the side cart and out-of-stock pages.

4. What it should look like

Six templates, phone first because 75% of orders are placed on a phone. One brand green (#14200E / #1E6B23), orange #ED6744 only on buy buttons, yellow #FFC400 only for deals, PT Sans throughout. These are the existing site's own colours and font, used with discipline. The photos and prices are real; anything that needs Willem to confirm it is marked.

How this gets built without breaking anything.
  • Green lane: the animal-page delivery panel, enquiry form, WhatsApp buttons, care accordions, homepage content and Product markup. These change no checkout code. Snapshot first, then go live.
  • Red lane: the reserve-and-collect rule, checkout field order, side-cart bump, related-products count and the back-in-stock plugin. These go to staging first and need a test order for each delivery method.
  • Diane builds in the 10Web/Elementor templates, so she keeps the ability to edit everything.
