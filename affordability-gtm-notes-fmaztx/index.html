01
The bet
Working notes · AI affordability platform · go-to-market

Your brief is really one bet: that people will trust an AI with their budget before they go shopping.

If that holds, positioning and channels are mostly craft. If it doesn't, nothing else in the brief matters much. So I'd spend the first two weeks trying to break it, as cheaply as possible.

1People want a "what can I carry" answer early, before the dealer or the letting agent gives them one.assumption
2They'll tell an AI about their income and commitments to get it.assumption
+And if you want to get paid: someone on the supply side values a buyer who already knows their number.assumption

What I found

A couple of hours of reading changed my starting point

Four things about the category, and one thing the brief leaves out.

Affordability is already free

Zillow shows buyers the price and monthly payment they qualify for and labels every listing in or out of range. It's there to sell Zillow's own mortgages.3 researched

So "see what you can afford" won't work as the pitch.

Lenders pay, users don't

Credit Karma charges users nothing and made $631M in one quarter from lenders paying per action, click or lead.1 NerdWallet's loans revenue grew 141% in a year.2 researched

Which makes "first 100 users" half a target. The other half is one partner who'll put a price on them.

People think monthly, then search by price

94% of in-market UK car buyers had a monthly budget in mind (98% of under-35s),4 but only 20% searched used cars by monthly cost.5 researched

I think your product lives in that gap.

Nobody opens a finance app every day

Finance retains better than any other app category and still keeps only about 12 to 18% of users at day 30.6 researched

Build for the week before a purchase. That's also when a user is worth most to a partner.

What the brief doesn't mention: regulation

Once the AI recommends a finance product, a lot of markets call that credit broking. In the UK a broker who recommends has to consider affordability and suitability by rule.7 researched I'd expect most markets to have something similar. assumption

Picking the launch country is partly a legal decision, then. "Budget and discovery" is a much lighter position than "we match you to finance", and it's worth an hour with an adviser before launch rather than after.

04
Positioning

A first draft of the positioning

Something to test, not to frame and hang on the wall. The opening I see is the difference between what a lender says you qualify for and what you can actually carry.

What everyone else tells you
What you qualify for

One lender's number, there to sell that lender's loan. It ignores the rest of your month.

What you'd tell them
What you can carry

Rent, car, debt and everything else, worked out by someone who isn't selling the finance.

Draft line, v0.1

For people about to take on their first big monthly payment: see what you can actually live with before a salesperson shows you what you qualify for.

Getting to the first 100, working backwards

I'd count a user as a completed affordability profile. Downloads and waitlist emails don't count, because a partner won't pay for them. Rough maths for the car segment:

125finish it (25%)

100see a matched car or finance option

Every rate here is a guess assumption. Real numbers replace them in week three.

Search, at the moment of intent

A small paid budget on affordability searches, plus one calculator page that's actually useful. It's the quickest way to learn what a completed profile costs.

One partner, embedded

A dealer group or broker puts the profile on their finance page. You get users, they get buyers who know their number, and it doubles as the pricing test for risk 2.

Communities, for interviews

First car and first flat groups are where I'd recruit the 12 to 15 interviews. It won't scale, but it's where the copy will come from.

07
Questions

What I'd ask you first

Your answers would change this more than anything I could read.

1

Which country are you launching in, and has anyone checked whether matching people to finance makes you a credit broker there?

That decides what the product is allowed to say, and who the partners are.
2

Do you have a supply partner yet, or is the plan to build users first?

Users with nobody to pay for them is where this kind of business usually stalls.
3

Does the agent read bank data, or does it ask questions?

That's most of the trust problem in risk 1.
