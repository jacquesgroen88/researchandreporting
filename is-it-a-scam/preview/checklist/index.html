The Universal Scam Checklist

Scams change their costume constantly. The machinery underneath almost never does. Nearly every scam needs at least three of the six things below, and the more of them you can tick, the closer you are to certainty. You do not need to recognise the specific scam. You only need to recognise the shape.

Tip: print this page, or save it as a PDF, and put it somewhere your family can reach it.

The 30-second test

When something is happening right now and you have no time to read, do this.

  1. 1

    Stop.

    Say out loud that you are not deciding anything in the next ten minutes. The pressure is the attack, and naming it defuses it.

  2. 2

    Do not use their channel.

    Close the message. Do not click the link, do not call the number in it, do not reply.

  3. 3

    Reach the organisation yourself.

    Use the number on your bank card, your statement, or the official website you navigate to yourself.

  4. 4

    Ask one hard question.

    Where exactly does the money come from, or, can you confirm this reference from your side without me telling you what it is?

  5. 5

    Tell one other person.

    Say it out loud to somebody. Scams rely on isolation, and most of them fall apart the moment they are described to a third party.

The six questions

Work through these in order. Three or more hits means stop and verify independently before any money or information moves.

1. Who started this?

They found you.

Almost every scam begins with contact you did not initiate: a message, a call, a DM, an ad, a wrong number, a stranger at an ATM. When you go looking for a service, you control where you land. When it comes to you, someone else chose the moment, the framing and the story.

2. Why must this happen right now?

Urgency is manufactured to stop you thinking.

Time pressure is the most reliable single marker of fraud. Reversing a fraudulent payment in ten minutes. Securing the flat today. Releasing the prize before it lapses. Genuine institutions have processes, records and patience. Fraud needs you to act before you check, because checking is all it takes to defeat it.

3. How am I being asked to pay?

Irreversible payment methods are chosen deliberately.

Criminals steer you to payment channels that cannot be reversed: EFT to a personal account, e-wallet, cash, gift cards, crypto. This is not a preference, it is a requirement of the business model. Card payments and regulated gateways create a dispute route, which is exactly what they are avoiding.

4. What are they asking me to reveal or install?

Nobody legitimate needs your OTP, PIN or your screen.

There is no situation, ever, in which a bank, retailer, courier, government department or employer needs your one-time PIN, your card PIN, your password, or remote access to your device. Not to verify you. Not to help you. Not to stop a fraud. This one rule, held absolutely, closes the majority of attacks.

5. Can I verify this on a channel they did not give me?

Verification only counts if you choose the channel.

Every detail presented to you can be forged: caller ID, sender IDs, letterheads, logos, websites, review counts, video of a person's face, the voice of your own child. None of it is evidence. The only meaningful test is independent contact, using a number or address you already had or found yourself, on a different channel from the one they reached you on.

6. Does the money make sense?

Ask where the profit actually comes from, and insist on an answer.

Every offer implies a flow of money. A legitimate one can explain it in a sentence that survives a follow-up question. Guaranteed high returns, prices far below every other seller, a job with no interview, a stranger's generosity: each requires someone to be losing money for your benefit. Ask who, and why they would.

Rules with no exceptions

Everything above requires judgement. These do not. Hold them absolutely and most attacks fail at the first step.

  • No bank, ever, asks for your OTP, PIN or password. There is no exception and no emergency that creates one.
  • There is no such thing as a safe account, a holding account or a suspense account you must move your money into.
  • You never pay a fee to receive money. Not a prize, not a refund, not a loan, not an inheritance, not your own investment profits.
  • You never pay to be given a job.
  • Never forward a verification code to anyone, including someone who appears to be a close friend.
  • Never install remote access software because an inbound caller asked you to.
  • Never send money to someone you have not met in person.
  • Any change of banking details, on any invoice, from anyone, is verified by voice call on a number you already had.
  • Caller ID, sender IDs, logos, https padlocks and five-star reviews are all trivially faked. None of them is verification.
  • Anyone who contacts you offering to recover money you have already lost is scamming you again.
