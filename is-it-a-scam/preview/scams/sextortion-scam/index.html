Sextortion & Blackmail Scams

Also called: Webcam blackmail · Nude photo extortion · Email blackmail scam

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • A new online contact who escalates to intimate video unusually fast.
  • They push to a platform where video can be recorded, and steer the conversation quickly.
  • An immediate threat with a short deadline and a demand in crypto, gift cards or e-wallet transfer.
  • An email quoting a password you recognise from years ago. That is from a public breach, not from your device.
  • Screenshots of your contact list presented as leverage.

How it works

  1. 1

    Contact and rapid escalation

    An attractive stranger makes contact on Instagram, Snapchat, a dating app or Facebook, and moves quickly to intimate conversation and video.

  2. 2

    Material is captured

    The video call is recorded, or images are requested and saved. In many cases the other side is pre-recorded footage, not a live person.

  3. 3

    The demand

    Within minutes, contact turns to threat, usually with a screenshot of your friends list to show they know exactly who would receive it. A deadline of an hour or two is typical.

  4. 4

    It never ends with one payment

    Paying confirms you can be pressured. Demands escalate. This is the single most important thing to understand about this scam.

  5. 5

    The bluff variant

    Mass emails claim a device was hacked and a webcam recording exists, often quoting an old breached password as 'proof'. In these cases there is no footage at all.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Never share intimate images or perform on video with someone you have not met in person, however genuine they seem.
  • Lock down your friends and followers lists. Leverage in this scam comes from visible contacts.
  • Understand in advance that paying makes it worse, not better. Decide this now, not in the panic.
  • Talk to young people about it directly and without judgement, so that if it happens they come to you instead of paying alone.
  • Ignore mass blackmail emails quoting an old password, and change that password wherever it is still used.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Do not pay. Paying does not end it and funds further attempts.
  2. Stop responding entirely and do not delete anything. Screenshot the profile, the threats and any payment details.
  3. Report and block the account on the platform. Major platforms have dedicated sextortion reporting routes and act quickly.
  4. Open a SAPS case. Extortion is a serious criminal offence in South Africa.
  5. Tell someone you trust. Isolation is the entire leverage, and removing the secret removes the threat's power. Free counselling is available through the SADAG helpline.

Questions people ask

Will they really send it to my contacts?

Sometimes, but far less often than threatened, and much less often once you stop responding. These are volume operations that move on to people who engage.

An email says my webcam was hacked and quotes my password.

That password came from a public data breach, and in essentially all such cases there is no recording. Delete the email and change the password anywhere it is still in use.

I am a minor and this happened to me.

Tell a trusted adult immediately. You are the victim of a crime and you are not in trouble. Report it to the SAPS and to the platform, and do not pay.
