Parcel & Courier Delivery Scams

Also called: Customs fee scam · Failed delivery SMS · Post office fee scam

A message about a parcel you may or may not be expecting asks for a small fee, and harvests your card details in the process.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • A delivery message for a parcel you cannot specifically identify.
  • Any fee requested by SMS link rather than at the point of purchase.
  • The link domain is not the courier's real website.
  • A full card number and CVV requested for a payment of a few rand.
  • An OTP arriving for an amount much larger than the fee you were asked to pay. Read your OTP messages, they state the amount.

How it works

  1. 1

    A plausible interruption

    An SMS, WhatsApp or email claims a delivery failed, is held at customs, or needs an address confirmation. It arrives at a moment when most people are expecting some parcel.

  2. 2

    A trivially small fee

    R7.50, R32, R59. The amount is deliberately too small to trigger caution or to feel worth verifying.

  3. 3

    A convincing payment page

    The page is a clone of a courier or postal service checkout. It captures the full card number, expiry, CVV and often your ID number and address.

  4. 4

    The real charge comes later

    The small fee may never be taken at all. The card details are sold or used days or weeks later, when you no longer connect the two events.

  5. 5

    OTP capture

    Sophisticated versions run the card through a real transaction in real time and present you with an OTP field, so you approve the fraudulent charge yourself.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Never pay a delivery fee from a link. Go to the courier's website yourself, or to the retailer you actually ordered from, and check the order there.
  • Read the content of every OTP message before entering it. The amount and merchant are stated, and they will not match.
  • Use a virtual or low-limit card for online shopping so exposure is capped.
  • Remember that customs charges in South Africa are handled through the courier's official account process, not by random SMS.
  • Do not assume a message is genuine because you are in fact expecting a parcel. That is the coincidence the scam relies on.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Freeze or cancel the card immediately through your banking app.
  2. Check for and dispute any transactions you did not make, in writing.
  3. Change your online shopping account passwords if you entered them.
  4. Report the number and the link to your bank and network so they can be blocked.
  5. Open a SAPS case if funds were taken.

Questions people ask

I was genuinely expecting a parcel. Was I targeted?

Almost certainly not. These are mass-sent to millions of numbers. At any moment a large share of recipients are expecting something, which is what makes the technique so effective.

The fee never came off my account, so was it harmless?

No, that is a bad sign. It suggests the goal was your card data rather than the fee, and the charge is still coming.

How did they get my number?

Bulk lists from data breaches and number generation. It does not imply anything specific about you.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
