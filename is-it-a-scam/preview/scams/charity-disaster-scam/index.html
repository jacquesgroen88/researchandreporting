Fake Charity & Disaster Appeals

Also called: Crowdfunding scam · Disaster relief fraud · Fake NPO

Appeals that follow floods, fires and tragedies collect donations for causes that do not exist or never receive the money.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Payment to a personal account rather than a registered NPO.
  • No NPO registration number, or one you cannot verify with the Department of Social Development.
  • Emotional pressure and urgency without any verifiable detail.
  • The organisation has no history before this appeal.
  • Requests for cash, e-wallet transfers, gift cards or crypto.

How it works

  1. 1

    Speed after tragedy

    Appeals appear within hours of a disaster, before legitimate organisations have mobilised and while public emotion is highest.

  2. 2

    Borrowed imagery

    Photographs are taken from news coverage or from unrelated events entirely, and a compelling individual story is attached.

  3. 3

    Direct payment

    Funds are requested by EFT to a personal account, e-wallet or crypto, rather than through a registered NPO account or an established crowdfunding platform.

  4. 4

    No accounting, ever

    There is no follow-up, no reporting, and no evidence anything was delivered. Questions are met with offence or silence.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Give to established organisations you sought out yourself, rather than to appeals that reached you.
  • Verify the NPO registration number before donating.
  • Use official donation pages rather than direct EFT to individuals.
  • Be more sceptical, not less, in the first 48 hours after a disaster.
  • Ask how funds will be accounted for. Legitimate organisations answer this readily.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Report to your bank, though recovery of a small EFT is unlikely.
  2. Report the page or campaign to the platform so it is removed.
  3. Open a SAPS case, particularly if the amount is significant or many people were affected.
  4. Report a fraudulent NPO claim to the Department of Social Development.

Questions people ask

Is crowdfunding for individuals safe?

It can be, but verify independently who is behind the campaign and how you can confirm the outcome. Established platforms with identity checks are safer than a bank account posted in a comment.

How do I check an NPO?

Ask for the registration number and verify it through the Department of Social Development's NPO register. Legitimate organisations provide it without hesitation.

The one rule
