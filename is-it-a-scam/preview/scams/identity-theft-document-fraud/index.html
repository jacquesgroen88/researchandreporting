Identity & Account Takeover

Identity Theft & Document Fraud

Also called: ID fraud · Account opening fraud · Impersonation fraud

Your ID number and personal documents are used to open accounts, take out credit and commit fraud in your name, often for years before you notice.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Credit agreement or account statements arriving for products you never took out.
  • Being declined for credit when you expected approval.
  • Calls from debt collectors about accounts you do not recognise.
  • SMSes confirming a cellphone contract or delivery you did not order.
  • Post stops arriving, or a change-of-address confirmation you never requested.

How it works

  1. 1

    Documents are collected

    From fake job adverts, discarded copies, breached databases, corrupt insiders, or a stolen wallet. A certified ID copy and a proof of address is enough to start.

  2. 2

    Credit is opened in your name

    Retail accounts, cellphone contracts, micro-loans and vehicle finance. Applications are spread across providers to avoid triggering a single alert.

  3. 3

    Your address is redirected

    Statements go elsewhere, so nothing arrives to warn you. This is why the fraud typically runs for a long time undetected.

  4. 4

    You find out at the worst moment

    Usually when applying for a bond or a car, when a credit check reveals defaults and judgments you never incurred.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Register a free protective listing with the Southern African Fraud Prevention Service (SAFPS), which forces extra verification on any credit application in your name.
  • Check your credit report at least annually. You are entitled to a free report each year from each bureau.
  • Watermark every ID copy you hand over with the date and specific purpose.
  • Never send a selfie holding your ID unless you have independently verified who is asking and why.
  • Shred documents containing your ID number rather than binning them.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Register a SAFPS protective listing immediately.
  2. Dispute every fraudulent account in writing with the credit provider and the bureau. They must investigate.
  3. Open a SAPS case, as providers will require the case number.
  4. Get your credit report from all bureaus so you find every account, not just the one you noticed.

Questions people ask

Am I liable for debt opened in my name?

Not if you can show it was fraudulent, but the burden of proving it falls largely on you. Documentation and a police case number are essential.

How long does it take to clear up?

Realistically months, and sometimes years across multiple providers. This is why prevention, particularly a SAFPS listing, is worth far more than remediation.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
