SASSA & Social Grant Scams

Also called: Grant approval scam · SRD status scam · Grant increase scam

Fraudsters exploit grant applicants and recipients with fake approvals, fake status checks and fees for a service that is free.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Anyone charging a fee for a SASSA application, appeal or status check.
  • Being asked for your SASSA card PIN or an OTP by anyone at all, including someone in a uniform or a bib.
  • A message about an increase or approval that requires a payment or a click.
  • Someone offering to 'help' at the machine and taking the card out of your hand.
  • A status-check link sent by WhatsApp rather than typed by you into the official site.

How it works

  1. 1

    A fee for something free

    Agents at pension points or on WhatsApp offer to 'fast-track' or 'guarantee' an application for R100 to R500. Every SASSA application and status check is free, always.

  2. 2

    Fake status-check sites

    Lookalike websites and WhatsApp numbers ask for your ID number and phone number to 'check your status'. These details are then used to hijack the grant application or the payment destination.

  3. 3

    Banking details are changed

    With enough of your information, the payment destination is switched to a fraudster's account. The recipient simply finds nothing at the pay date.

  4. 4

    Fake approval and increase messages

    SMSes announce an approved grant or a special increase, and require a small 'activation' or 'card replacement' payment, or an OTP.

  5. 5

    Card and PIN capture

    At pay points, 'helpers' offer to assist at the machine, observe or take the PIN, and swap or retain the card.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Apply and check status only through official SASSA channels, and only by typing the address yourself.
  • Never pay anyone for a grant service. There is no fast-track, and anyone claiming one is committing fraud.
  • Never share your PIN or hand your card to anyone, including people who appear to be officials.
  • Cover the keypad and, where possible, take a trusted family member to the pay point.
  • Keep your registered contact number up to date so notifications reach you and not someone else.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Report immediately to SASSA on their official toll-free line and to the pay point manager.
  2. If a card was taken or copied, ask for it to be blocked and replaced at once.
  3. Open a SAPS case and get a case number.
  4. Ask SASSA to check whether your registered banking details were changed and by whom.
  5. Tell others at your pay point. These crews work the same site repeatedly.

Questions people ask

Does SASSA ever charge for an application?

No. Application, appeal and status checks are free through official channels. Any fee is fraud.

Someone said they can get my rejected SRD appeal approved. Is that possible?

Not through them. Appeals go through the official process and no third party can influence the outcome. Paying them buys nothing.

My grant went into someone else's account. Can I get it back?

Report it to SASSA and the SAPS immediately. Diverted payments can sometimes be recovered and redirected, but only if reported quickly.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
