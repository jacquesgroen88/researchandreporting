ATM Card Swapping & Skimming

Also called: Helpful stranger scam · Card swap · ATM jamming

Your card is jammed, distracted away from you, or physically substituted at an ATM while your PIN is observed.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Anyone offering unsolicited help at an ATM, however respectable they appear.
  • The card slot looks loose, thicker than usual, or has visible glue or residue.
  • The keypad feels raised, spongy or slightly misaligned.
  • Someone standing closer than necessary, or two people working the queue together.
  • The machine 'fails' and someone immediately suggests what you should press.

How it works

  1. 1

    The machine is prepared

    A thin device or strip is placed in the card slot so the card is retained, or the keypad is overlaid with a skimming pad.

  2. 2

    Help arrives

    A friendly stranger appears immediately, having watched you struggle. They suggest re-entering the PIN, which they observe, or press cancel 'to release it'.

  3. 3

    The switch

    In the confusion your card is returned to you, except it is a different card of the same bank. You leave believing you have your card.

  4. 4

    Immediate use

    Withdrawals and purchases begin within minutes, usually at a different centre and before the loss is noticed.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Accept help from nobody at an ATM. Not a security guard, not a bank-branded shirt, not a kind stranger. Use the intercom or go inside the branch.
  • Cover the keypad completely with your other hand, every time.
  • Inspect the card slot and keypad before inserting. Give the slot a gentle tug.
  • If a card is retained, do not leave the machine. Phone the bank from where you stand and block the card immediately.
  • Prefer ATMs inside branches or busy malls, and avoid month-end late-night withdrawals where possible.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Block the card immediately through the banking app or the number on your statement.
  2. Check the card in your hand. If it is not yours, that is proof of a swap and useful evidence.
  3. Open a SAPS case at once.
  4. Dispute the transactions with the bank in writing.
  5. Report the ATM location and time to the bank so the device can be found and removed.

Questions people ask

The person was wearing a bank shirt.

Uniforms are trivially obtained. Bank staff do not stand at ATMs offering to handle your card or watch your PIN entry.

Should I use a tap payment instead?

Contactless and app-based cardless withdrawals remove the card from the equation and are generally safer against this specific attack.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
