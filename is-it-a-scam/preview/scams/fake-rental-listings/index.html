Fake Rental & Accommodation Listings

Also called: Deposit scam · Ghost landlord · Student accommodation scam

A property that is not available, or not theirs to let, is advertised cheaply to collect deposits from multiple hopeful tenants.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Rent noticeably below comparable properties in the same area.
  • You cannot view the property in person before paying anything.
  • The landlord or agent will not do a video call from inside the property.
  • Pressure to pay a deposit today to secure it.
  • The agent is not registered with the Property Practitioners Regulatory Authority, or cannot produce a Fidelity Fund Certificate.
  • The same photographs appear on other listings at different prices.

How it works

  1. 1

    Real photographs, wrong seller

    Images are lifted from a genuine listing on a property portal, or from a legitimate sale listing, and reposted at a below-market rent.

  2. 2

    A reason they cannot meet

    The landlord is working abroad, currently in another province, or the agent is handling a portfolio remotely. The viewing is always just out of reach.

  3. 3

    Competition manufactured

    You are told several people are interested and the deposit secures it. Nothing produces bad decisions faster than a scarce home and a deadline.

  4. 4

    Deposit before keys

    Payment is requested by EFT to secure the place, sometimes with a professional-looking lease sent first to build confidence.

  5. 5

    Multiple victims, one property

    The same listing takes deposits from several people. Occasionally people arrive on moving day to find each other, and the real occupants, at the gate.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Never pay a deposit for a property you or someone you trust has not physically stood inside.
  • Reverse image search the listing photographs. Duplicated images across listings is the clearest single tell.
  • Verify the agent's registration and Fidelity Fund Certificate with the PPRA before paying.
  • Check the title deed owner's name against the person you are paying. A conveyancer or a deeds office search can confirm it cheaply.
  • Pay into a registered agency trust account, never a personal account.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Report to your bank immediately and request a recall.
  2. Open a SAPS case and get a case number.
  3. Report the listing to the portal or platform so it comes down before others pay.
  4. If an 'agent' was involved, report them to the PPRA.
  5. Look for other victims of the same listing. A group case is taken more seriously than a single complaint.

Questions people ask

They sent me a signed lease. Doesn't that protect me?

No. A lease signed by someone with no right to the property is worthless, and producing one costs the scammer nothing.

Is it safe if I use a well-known property portal?

Safer, but fraudulent listings do get posted to major portals. The protections that matter are viewing in person and paying into a verified trust account.

Can I recover the deposit?

Only with a very fast bank report. After a day or two the money is typically gone, which is why prevention is the whole game here.
