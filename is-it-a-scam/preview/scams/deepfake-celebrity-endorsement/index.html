Deepfake Celebrity Endorsement Ads

Also called: AI celebrity scam · Fake news article ad · Elon Musk investment ad

AI-generated video of a well-known South African business figure or broadcaster appears to endorse a trading platform that does not exist.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • A celebrity or public figure endorsing a specific trading platform in a social media ad. Real endorsements do not run this way.
  • A 'news article' whose links all lead to the same signup form.
  • Claims of government, Reserve Bank or major bank backing for a private trading scheme.
  • You are phoned within minutes of registering, by someone very good at their job.
  • The platform is not on the FSCA register.

How it works

  1. 1

    A face you trust, saying words they never said

    Video of prominent South African business people, broadcasters and news anchors is cloned with AI. Voice and lip movement are convincing on a phone screen.

  2. 2

    A cloned news environment

    The ad leads to a page styled as a real news publication, complete with masthead, bylines and comment sections, all fabricated.

  3. 3

    A very low entry point

    The pitch is a small starting deposit and a state-backed or bank-backed 'wealth programme'. The low figure removes hesitation.

  4. 4

    The account manager calls

    Within minutes of registering, a professional caller takes over. From here it becomes a standard fake trading platform with a rising dashboard and a withdrawal wall.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Treat every celebrity investment endorsement in a social feed as fake by default. Prominent South Africans have publicly disclaimed these ads repeatedly.
  • Check the FSCA register before depositing anything, without exception.
  • Look at the video closely: hands, teeth, blinking and the edges of the jaw are still where AI generation breaks down.
  • Search the person's name plus 'deepfake' or 'scam warning'. The disclaimers are usually easy to find.
  • Never accept investment guidance from someone who phoned you.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop depositing and refuse to pay any withdrawal fee.
  2. Report to the FSCA, the SAPS, and your bank.
  3. Report the advert to the platform and to the impersonated person's organisation.
  4. Preserve screenshots, the platform URL and all transfer records.
  5. Reject all recovery offers that follow.

Questions people ask

Would Facebook allow a fraudulent ad using a real person's face?

It happens constantly. Ad review is largely automated, fraudulent advertisers rotate accounts rapidly, and removal usually comes after the campaign has already run.

The article was on a site that looked like a real newspaper.

It was a copy. Check the domain against the publication's real address, and try clicking a headline that is not part of the pitch. On a fake, nothing else works.
