Identity & Account Takeover

WhatsApp Account Hijacking

Also called: WhatsApp OTP scam · Six-digit code scam · Family emergency scam

Someone tricks you into forwarding your WhatsApp verification code, takes over your account, and then scams your contacts while pretending to be you.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Anyone asking you to forward a code you received, for any reason. WhatsApp codes are never meant for another person.
  • A contact suddenly messaging from a new number and asking for money.
  • Urgency plus a request to keep it quiet, or an unwillingness to take a phone call.
  • Writing style that is subtly off, or the absence of shared history in the conversation.
  • A request to pay a stranger's account 'because my banking is not working'.

How it works

  1. 1

    The code request

    A message from a contact, whose account is already stolen, says they accidentally sent their verification code to your number and asks you to forward it. The code is actually yours.

  2. 2

    The takeover

    Forwarding those six digits registers your WhatsApp on their device. You are logged out. They now have your name, photo and contact list.

  3. 3

    They become you

    They message your contacts in your voice. The most common scripts are a new number and an urgent request to pay something on their behalf, or a phone lost and money needed right now.

  4. 4

    Group amplification

    In family, church and school groups, one hijacked account can reach hundreds of people who have no reason to doubt the sender.

  5. 5

    The chain continues

    Each new victim's account is taken the same way, which is why the request always seems to come from someone you know.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Turn on WhatsApp two-step verification now. It adds a PIN that a hijacker cannot obtain from an SMS, and it is the single most effective defence.
  • Never forward a verification code to anyone, including someone who appears to be a close friend.
  • Agree a family rule: any request for money over WhatsApp is confirmed by voice call before anyone pays.
  • Be suspicious of 'this is my new number' messages, always.
  • Ask a question only the real person could answer, and do not accept a plausible dodge.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Re-register WhatsApp on your own phone immediately. Receiving a fresh code logs the attacker out.
  2. Turn on two-step verification the moment you regain access.
  3. Warn every group and contact you have, publicly and quickly. Speed limits the damage to others.
  4. Report the account to WhatsApp via support.
  5. If contacts lost money, help them report to their banks and the SAPS at once.

Questions people ask

Can they read my old messages?

Generally no. Message history stays on your device and is not transferred by a re-registration. What they get is your identity and your contact list, which is the point.

How do I get my account back?

Simply re-verify your number on your own phone. Whoever holds it will be signed out. If two-step verification was enabled by the attacker, there is a waiting period of up to seven days.

Does two-step verification really stop this?

Yes, in almost all cases. Without your PIN, the stolen SMS code alone is not enough.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
