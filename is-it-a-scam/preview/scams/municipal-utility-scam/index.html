Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Payment demanded in cash, or by EFT to a personal account rather than the municipality's official account.
  • A technician who arrives unannounced and has no verifiable work order or identification you can check by phone.
  • Prepaid electricity offered below the regulated price. There is no legitimate discount on prepaid tokens.
  • Threat of disconnection today, with no prior written notice on your account.
  • A phone number given to you at the gate rather than one you find on your municipal bill.

How it works

  1. 1

    The disconnection threat

    An SMS, call or a person at the gate claims disconnection is imminent unless immediate payment is made, often demanding cash or an EFT to a personal account.

  2. 2

    The fake technician

    Someone in overalls with a clipboard arrives to 'inspect' or 'upgrade' the meter, charges a fee, and in some cases uses the visit to case the property.

  3. 3

    Discounted tokens

    Prepaid electricity is offered at 40 to 60% off, sold through WhatsApp groups. The tokens are either generated fraudulently, stolen, or simply invalid.

  4. 4

    The consequence lands later

    Fraudulent tokens are traceable. Households that use them face tampering investigations, disconnection and fines, long after the seller has vanished.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Pay only through official municipal channels, using the account details on your statement.
  • Phone the municipality on their published number to verify any disconnection notice or scheduled visit.
  • Never let an unverified 'technician' near your meter or into your property.
  • Buy prepaid electricity only from licensed vendors, retailers or your banking app. A discount is a warning, not a saving.
  • Keep your municipal statements so you can see genuine arrears at a glance.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Report to your municipality's revenue or fraud line and get a reference number.
  2. Open a SAPS case, particularly if someone entered your property.
  3. If you bought discounted tokens, disclose this to the municipality proactively. Voluntary disclosure is treated far better than discovery.
  4. Warn your neighbourhood group. These crews work an area systematically.
  5. Report the seller's number to the platform and to the municipality.

Questions people ask

How do I verify a technician?

Phone the municipality on the number on your bill and ask them to confirm the work order. A genuine technician will wait.

Are cheap prepaid tokens ever legitimate?

No. Prepaid electricity is sold at a regulated tariff. Any meaningful discount means the tokens are fraudulent, and the liability lands on your meter.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
