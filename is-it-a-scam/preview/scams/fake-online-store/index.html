Fake Online Stores & Social Media Shops

Also called: Ghost store · Instagram shop scam · Facebook Marketplace scam

A convincing storefront with unbeatable prices takes your EFT and either sends nothing, sends a counterfeit, or vanishes the moment orders slow down.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Payment by EFT to a personal account, or a business account whose name does not match the shop.
  • No physical address, no landline, and contact only through a WhatsApp number or DMs.
  • The domain was registered very recently, or the store has no history before its first ad.
  • Reviews are all five stars, posted in a short burst, and generic.
  • Product photos are lifted from other retailers, sometimes with the original watermark visible.
  • Prices far below every other seller with an urgent countdown timer.

How it works

  1. 1

    Paid ads do the work

    Fake stores buy Facebook, Instagram and TikTok ads. Appearing in a paid ad slot is a purchase, not a credential, and confers no legitimacy whatsoever.

  2. 2

    The price is the hook

    40 to 70% below market on items that are never discounted, often with a 'closing down' or 'liquidation' story to explain it.

  3. 3

    EFT only, or EFT preferred

    Card payments and legitimate gateways can be reversed and require verified merchant accounts. That is why you are steered to a direct EFT, often to a personal account with a name that does not match the store.

  4. 4

    Delivery is always almost happening

    Tracking numbers that do not resolve, courier delays, customs holds. The messaging is designed to run out your chargeback and reporting windows.

  5. 5

    The store disappears

    The page goes dark or is rebranded and reopens under a new name with the same product images, often within a week.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Pay by card or a reputable payment gateway, never a direct EFT to a stranger. Card payments give you a dispute route; an EFT gives you nothing.
  • Search the store name plus the word 'scam' before ordering, and check how long its social page has existed.
  • Do a reverse image search on the product photos. Stolen imagery is the fastest tell.
  • Look for a physical address and a company registration number, then actually check them.
  • If the price only makes sense as a mistake, treat it as bait.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. If you paid by card, lodge a chargeback with your bank immediately. There are strict time limits.
  2. If you paid by EFT, report to your bank anyway. Very fast reports occasionally catch funds before withdrawal.
  3. Report the page to the platform and leave a public review naming the account number used.
  4. Open a SAPS case, especially if you can group with other victims. Volume gets attention.
  5. Report the seller to the National Consumer Commission if the store presents as a registered business.

Questions people ask

It was a paid ad on Facebook. Doesn't the platform vet advertisers?

Not meaningfully. Ad approval is largely automated and fraudulent stores budget for it. A paid ad is evidence of a budget, nothing more.

The site had a padlock and https. Isn't that a security check?

No. TLS certificates are free and automatic. The padlock means your connection is encrypted, not that the business is real.

Can I get an EFT back?

Rarely, unless you report within hours and the account has not been emptied. This asymmetry is the whole reason EFT is requested.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
