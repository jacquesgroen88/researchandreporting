Tender & Supplier Fraud

Also called: Fake tender scam · Purchase order scam · Government supplier scam

Small businesses are shown a fake government or corporate order and asked to fund stock or pay a registration fee for work that does not exist.

  • Typical lossR10 000 – R900 000
  • Mainly seen inSouth Africa

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • An unsolicited order from an entity you have never dealt with.
  • You are told which supplier to use, or given their contact details.
  • Any fee to register on a supplier database. Central Supplier Database registration is free.
  • Contact only by cellphone and free webmail addresses rather than official domains.
  • Urgency and confidentiality around a substantial public order.

How it works

  1. 1

    An unexpected order arrives

    A purchase order on convincing letterhead from a department, municipality, hospital or large corporate, for goods you can supply, at a good margin.

  2. 2

    A specified supplier

    You are directed to a particular supplier for the stock, who is part of the operation. Their pricing is high but the order's margin absorbs it.

  3. 3

    You fund it

    You pay the supplier, often borrowing to do so, because payment terms mean you are paid after delivery.

  4. 4

    Nothing is real

    The supplier vanishes with your money, or the goods arrive and the buyer does not exist. Variants also charge a database registration or vendor accreditation fee upfront.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Verify every order by phoning the institution's official switchboard, using a number from their own website, never from the order document.
  • Never use a supplier nominated by a buyer you have not independently verified.
  • Know that CSD registration is free and that no legitimate tender charges suppliers to bid.
  • Check the buyer's VAT and registration numbers against official records.
  • Never borrow against personal assets to fund an unverified first order from a new customer.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Report to your bank immediately and request a recall.
  2. Open a SAPS case and, given the values involved, ask about the Hawks and the National Anti-Corruption Hotline.
  3. Notify the institution being impersonated so they can issue a warning to other suppliers.
  4. Get legal advice about any debt taken on to fund the order.

Questions people ask

Does registration on a government supplier database cost anything?

No. Registration on the Central Supplier Database is free. Any fee is fraudulent.

The order had official stamps and reference numbers.

Letterheads, stamps and reference formats are trivially forged. Only a call to the institution's own published switchboard is verification.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
