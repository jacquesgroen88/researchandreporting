Puppy & Pet Sale Scams

Also called: Pet deposit scam · Breeder scam · Puppy transport fee scam

Adverts for pedigree puppies at attractive prices collect deposits and escalating transport fees for an animal that does not exist.

  • Typical lossR1 500 – R35 000
  • Mainly seen inSouth Africa, Global

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Price well below what the breed normally sells for.
  • You cannot visit the animal or see its mother, in person or on a live video call.
  • Payment by EFT, e-wallet or crypto to an individual.
  • Escalating transport, crate, insurance or vet fees after the deposit.
  • The same photographs appear elsewhere online, or on other adverts.

How it works

  1. 1

    Photographs that are not theirs

    Images are taken from legitimate breeders' websites or overseas social media. The same puppy appears in many adverts.

  2. 2

    Distance is built in

    The seller is in another province, so viewing is impractical and transport is required. This is not incidental, it is the structure of the scam.

  3. 3

    Deposit to reserve

    A deposit secures the puppy, usually by EFT, often with a plausible contract and vaccination records attached.

  4. 4

    Transport fees multiply

    A crate deposit, a temperature-controlled carrier, pet insurance, a vet clearance certificate. Each is refundable on delivery. Each is paid because a family is now emotionally committed.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Never buy an animal you or someone you trust has not physically seen, with its mother.
  • Insist on a live video call where you can ask the seller to do something specific with the puppy.
  • Reverse image search every photograph in the advert.
  • Buy through a KUSA-registered breeder, a recognised rescue organisation, or adopt from a shelter.
  • Treat any post-deposit fee as confirmation of fraud, not as an obstacle to overcome.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop paying immediately, no matter how far in you are.
  2. Report to your bank and request a recall.
  3. Open a SAPS case and report the advert to the platform.
  4. Warn breed groups and community pages, where these sellers recycle the same photographs.

Questions people ask

They sent a video of the puppy.

Videos are stolen just as easily as photographs. Only a live call where the seller responds to your specific request proves anything.

Is a deposit ever normal?

Yes, with a legitimate breeder you have visited and verified. A deposit to a stranger you have not met, for an animal in another province, is the scam's basic shape.
