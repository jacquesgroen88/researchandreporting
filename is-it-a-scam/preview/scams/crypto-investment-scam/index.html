Crypto & Forex Investment Scams

Also called: Pig butchering · Fake trading platform · Forex mentor scam

A polished platform or a persuasive 'mentor' shows you profits that do not exist, then blocks your withdrawal behind an endless queue of fees.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Guaranteed or unusually consistent returns. Real markets do not produce smooth upward lines.
  • You must pay a fee, tax or deposit before you can withdraw your own profits. This is definitional fraud.
  • The 'opportunity' reached you through a stranger's DM, a wrong number, or a dating app.
  • Celebrity endorsement in a video ad. Deepfaked local business figures are heavily used in South Africa.
  • Pressure to deposit before a window closes, or a mentor who becomes emotionally invested in your success.
  • The company is not licensed by the FSCA, or claims a licence number that belongs to a different entity.

How it works

  1. 1

    Warm contact, not cold

    It rarely starts with an investment pitch. It starts with a wrong-number WhatsApp, a friendly Instagram DM, a dating match, or a slick ad using a deepfaked local celebrity or business figure.

  2. 2

    Trust is built patiently

    Weeks of ordinary conversation. They are not in a hurry. The investment is mentioned casually, as something that has worked for them, never as a pitch.

  3. 3

    A small deposit that works

    You put in R2 000. The dashboard shows R2 600 within days. You withdraw R500 successfully. This single successful withdrawal is what unlocks the large deposit, and it is the most expensive R500 you will ever receive.

  4. 4

    The numbers climb

    You deposit more. The dashboard is a piece of software showing you whatever the operator types in. No trade has ever been placed.

  5. 5

    The withdrawal wall

    When you try to take the money out, a fee appears. A tax. A liquidity requirement. A compliance deposit. Each one is the last one. Each is paid out of desperation to release money that was never there.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Check the FSCA register before you send a cent. If the firm is not licensed to give financial advice in South Africa, stop there.
  • Apply the withdrawal test early: try to take out a meaningful amount before you ever deposit a meaningful amount.
  • Understand that any legitimate investment deducts fees from your balance. It never asks you to send new money to release old money.
  • Never take investment advice from someone who found you rather than the other way round.
  • Tell one sceptical person about any investment before funding it. Isolation is the scam's main tool.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop paying immediately. There is no final fee. The next payment will not release the funds.
  2. Screenshot everything: the platform, the chat history, wallet addresses, deposit confirmations.
  3. Report to the FSCA, to the SAPS, and to your bank if funds left via EFT.
  4. If crypto was involved, report the receiving wallet addresses to the exchange you used. Occasionally funds are frozen at an exchange.
  5. Refuse all 'recovery agents'. Being scammed once puts you on a list, and the recovery scam is the second harvest.

Questions people ask

I withdrew money successfully once. Doesn't that make it real?

No, and it is deliberate. Paying out a small early withdrawal is the cheapest possible way to buy your confidence before the large deposit.

Can I get crypto payments back?

Almost never. Crypto transactions are irreversible by design, and this is precisely why criminals request them. The only realistic route is a freeze at a regulated exchange, which requires reporting within hours.

Someone contacted me offering to recover my funds for a fee. Is that legitimate?

No. Fee-charging recovery services targeting scam victims are essentially always a second scam run against the same victim list.
