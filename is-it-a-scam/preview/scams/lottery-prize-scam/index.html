Lottery & Prize Scams

Also called: You have won scam · Competition scam · Lucky draw scam

Notification of a prize you never entered for, released only after you pay a tax, delivery or clearance fee.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • You won a competition you never entered.
  • Any payment required to release a prize. This is illegal in South Africa.
  • You are asked for banking details 'to deposit the winnings'.
  • Contact by WhatsApp or a personal cellphone number rather than an official channel.
  • You are told to keep it confidential until collection.

How it works

  1. 1

    The notification

    An SMS, call or WhatsApp says you have won a car, cash, or a retailer's promotional prize. Often the brand of a genuine, well-known South African competition is used.

  2. 2

    You are already excited

    The framing is congratulatory, not transactional, which lowers scrutiny considerably before money is ever mentioned.

  3. 3

    A fee to release it

    Tax, courier, insurance, clearance or a legal fee. Always modest against the prize value, which is exactly the calculation you are meant to make.

  4. 4

    Escalation and data harvest

    More fees follow, and along the way your ID number, address and banking details are collected for further fraud.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Remember the rule in South African law: a promotional competition may not require any payment to claim a prize.
  • If you did not enter, you did not win. There is no exception to this.
  • Verify with the brand directly through their official website or switchboard.
  • Never provide banking details or an ID number to claim a prize.
  • Talk to older relatives about this one specifically. It targets them disproportionately.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop paying and cut contact.
  2. Report to the SAPS and to the brand being impersonated.
  3. If you gave banking details or ID, alert your bank and register a SAFPS protective listing.
  4. Report the number to your network provider.

Questions people ask

Do real competitions ever charge a fee?

No. Under the Consumer Protection Act, requiring payment to claim a promotional prize is unlawful. A fee is definitive proof of fraud.

They used a real retailer's name and logo.

Impersonating a trusted brand is the point. Verify through the brand's own published contact details, never those given to you.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
