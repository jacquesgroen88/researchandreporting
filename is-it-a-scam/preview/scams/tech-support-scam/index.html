Tech Support & Remote Access Scams

Also called: Microsoft support scam · Refund scam · AnyDesk scam

A fake technician convinces you your device is infected or that you are owed a refund, then uses remote access to take money directly.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • An unsolicited call about your computer. Microsoft, Apple and your ISP do not phone you about infections.
  • A pop-up with a phone number. Real security software never asks you to call anyone.
  • Any request to install remote access software.
  • Being asked to log into online banking while someone is connected to your machine.
  • A refund that requires you to send money back, especially by gift card, crypto or cash.

How it works

  1. 1

    Alarm arrives

    A full-screen browser pop-up with sirens and a warning number, or a cold call claiming your machine is sending errors to Microsoft. Neither company monitors your computer this way.

  2. 2

    Remote access is granted

    You are guided to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer or similar. This is the moment control is lost, and everything that follows is theatre.

  3. 3

    Fake evidence

    Ordinary system logs are shown as proof of infection, or a command window is used to display alarming but meaningless text.

  4. 4

    Payment or the refund trick

    Either you pay for worthless 'protection', or, in the more expensive version, they claim to have refunded you too much. They edit what you see in your browser to fake the deposit, then ask you to return the difference in cash, crypto or gift cards.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Never install remote access software because an inbound caller asked you to.
  • Close alarming pop-ups by force-quitting the browser. Never phone the number displayed.
  • If someone claims to have refunded you, log out, close everything, and check the balance in your banking app independently.
  • Agree with older family members that no software gets installed during a phone call, ever.
  • Remember that money can be shown on a screen without existing. Only your bank's own app is evidence.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Disconnect from the internet immediately and uninstall any remote access software.
  2. Change banking and email passwords from a different, clean device.
  3. Phone your bank's fraud line and have accounts monitored or frozen.
  4. Run a reputable security scan, and consider a full reinstall if banking was accessed.
  5. Open a SAPS case.

Questions people ask

They knew my name and my ISP.

Both are widely available from breached data and directories. Knowing basic details about you is the opening move, not proof of identity.

Can they still access my computer after the call?

Yes, if the software remains installed and configured for unattended access. Uninstall it and check for other new programs.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
