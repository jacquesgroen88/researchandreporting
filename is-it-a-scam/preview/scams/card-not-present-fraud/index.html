Card-Not-Present & Online Card Fraud

Also called: CNP fraud · Stolen card details · Online card abuse

Card details harvested from phishing, skimming or breaches are used for online purchases where the physical card is never needed.

  • Mainly seen inSouth Africa, Global

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Small unexplained transactions, especially to foreign or unfamiliar merchants.
  • OTPs arriving for purchases you did not initiate. Always read the merchant and amount.
  • Declined transactions on a card that should have funds.
  • Notifications of a changed delivery address or a new device on a shopping account.

How it works

  1. 1

    Details are obtained elsewhere

    From a phishing page, a compromised online store, a skimmer, or a breached merchant database. You may never have done anything wrong.

  2. 2

    Small tests first

    A tiny transaction, often under R50 to a foreign merchant, confirms the card is live. This is the moment to catch it.

  3. 3

    Rapid spending

    Once validated, purchases run quickly across digital goods, gift cards and gaming credit, which are easy to resell and hard to trace.

  4. 4

    3D Secure is bypassed

    Either the merchant does not enforce it, or you were tricked into approving an OTP that you believed related to something else.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Enable transaction notifications for every amount, including small ones, and actually read them.
  • Use a separate low-limit or virtual card for online shopping.
  • Read the merchant name and amount in every OTP message before approving.
  • Set an online purchase limit in your banking app.
  • Never save card details on sites you use rarely.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Freeze the card in your app immediately, then call the bank.
  2. Dispute the transactions in writing and ask for a chargeback. There are time limits, usually within 30 to 120 days.
  3. Get a new card number rather than just a new PIN.
  4. Open a SAPS case if the bank requires one for the dispute.

Questions people ask

Will the bank refund unauthorised card transactions?

Often yes, where you did not authorise them and reported promptly. It is much harder if you approved an OTP, which is why reading OTP messages matters so much.

How did they get my card details if I never lost the card?

Most commonly from a breached merchant or a phishing page. The physical card is irrelevant to this type of fraud.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
