Funeral & Insurance Policy Scams

Also called: Fake funeral cover · Unregistered burial society · Policy premium scam

Premiums are collected for funeral or insurance cover that is unregistered, uninsured, or simply does not exist, and the failure only appears at a funeral.

  • Typical lossR2 000 – R120 000
  • Mainly seen inSouth Africa

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • The provider is not licensed by the FSCA, or the underwriter cannot be named.
  • Cash premiums, or debit orders to a personal or unnamed account.
  • No policy document, or a document with no insurer, no policy number and no terms.
  • Cover that is unusually cheap for the payout promised.
  • The agent cannot produce an FSP licence number that checks out.

How it works

  1. 1

    Door-to-door or community sales

    Cover is sold in person, at churches, taxi ranks and community meetings, by agents who are persuasive and often known locally.

  2. 2

    Premiums are collected reliably

    Debit orders run for years without issue. The absence of a problem is mistaken for the presence of cover.

  3. 3

    No underwriter behind it

    The scheme is not registered with the FSCA and has no licensed insurer underwriting the risk. There is no reserve, only incoming premiums.

  4. 4

    The claim fails

    At the death of a family member, the claim is delayed, then disputed on a technicality, then the operator becomes unreachable.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Verify the provider and the individual adviser on the FSCA register before signing anything.
  • Insist on knowing which licensed insurer underwrites the policy, in writing.
  • Keep the policy document, and check that it names the insurer, the benefit, the waiting period and a policy number.
  • Pay by debit order to a named registered company, never cash to an individual.
  • Review cover every few years and confirm the provider is still licensed.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Complain to the provider in writing and demand a formal claim decision.
  2. Escalate to the National Financial Ombud Scheme, which handles long-term insurance and funeral cover complaints at no cost.
  3. Report the operator to the FSCA.
  4. Open a SAPS case where premiums were taken with no cover in place.

Questions people ask

Are burial societies illegal?

No. Genuine burial societies are a long-standing and valuable community institution. The problem is schemes presenting as insurance without a licence or underwriter.

How do I check my current policy?

Find the policy document, identify the insurer, and check that insurer on the FSCA register. If no insurer is named, that is the answer.
