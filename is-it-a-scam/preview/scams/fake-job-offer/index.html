Fake Job Offers & Recruitment Fees

Also called: Advance fee job scam · Fake recruiter · Work-from-home scam

A job that does not exist is used to extract registration fees, training costs, uniform deposits or your identity documents.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Any payment required to get a job. Legitimate employers and registered agencies never charge candidates.
  • Hired without a proper interview, or interviewed only over WhatsApp text.
  • The email address is a free webmail account rather than a company domain.
  • The role involves receiving money into your account and forwarding it. That is money laundering and you are the one who gets prosecuted.
  • Requests for a selfie holding your ID, or for your banking login 'to set up salary payments'.
  • Pressure to accept immediately because 'the position closes today'.

How it works

  1. 1

    The advert looks institutional

    Posted on a real job board, or circulated on WhatsApp with a government department, retailer or mine's logo. Salary is generous, requirements are low.

  2. 2

    You are selected quickly

    Often without a real interview, or after a short WhatsApp 'interview'. Being chosen easily is flattering, and flattery lowers scrutiny.

  3. 3

    A small fee appears

    Registration, criminal check, medical, uniform, training material, courier of the contract. Always modest, always framed as standard, always refundable on your first payday.

  4. 4

    Documents are collected

    ID copy, proof of address, bank statement, sometimes a selfie holding your ID. This package is worth far more on the fraud market than the fee they charged you.

  5. 5

    The start date moves, then evaporates

    Onboarding delays, a second fee, then silence. Some victims are also recruited unknowingly as money mules, receiving and forwarding stolen funds through their own account.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Hold one rule absolutely: you never pay to be employed. Not for training, uniforms, checks or courier fees.
  • Verify the employer through their official website or switchboard, never through contact details in the advert.
  • Watermark any ID copy you send with the purpose and date so it cannot be reused elsewhere.
  • Never let anyone use your bank account to receive money on their behalf, whatever the explanation.
  • Be most careful when you are most desperate. That is precisely when these adverts are targeted at you.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop paying and cut contact.
  2. Register a protective listing with the Southern African Fraud Prevention Service (SAFPS) so your ID is flagged for extra verification.
  3. Open a SAPS case, and report the advert to the job board and to the real employer being impersonated.
  4. Check your credit report for accounts opened in your name.
  5. If you received and forwarded money, disclose it to your bank immediately and get legal advice. Doing so voluntarily is far better than being investigated for it.

Questions people ask

Is a fee ever legitimate?

No. In South Africa, employment agencies are paid by employers, not candidates. Any fee charged to a job seeker is either fraud or an illegal practice.

They only asked for my ID copy, not money. Is that harmless?

It is worse than losing a few hundred rand. A full document pack enables loans, SIM swaps and accounts opened in your name. Register a SAFPS protective listing.

Why would a scammer want to pay money into my account?

Because they need to launder stolen funds through an account that is not linked to them. You carry the criminal liability, they take the cash.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
