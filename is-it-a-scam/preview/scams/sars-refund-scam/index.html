SARS & Tax Refund Scams

Also called: eFiling phishing · Tax refund email scam · SARS audit scam

Emails and SMSes impersonating SARS offer a refund or threaten an audit, to capture eFiling credentials or banking details.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • An email or SMS with a link to log into eFiling. Always navigate to the site yourself instead.
  • A refund amount quoted before you have submitted or been assessed.
  • Threats of immediate arrest, penalty or account freezing. SARS follows formal, documented processes.
  • The sender domain is not the official SARS domain, or uses a lookalike with extra words.
  • An attachment claiming to be a statement of account or audit letter that you did not request.

How it works

  1. 1

    Timed to the season

    Volume spikes during filing season when a message from SARS is entirely expected and a refund is plausible.

  2. 2

    Reward or threat

    Two scripts dominate: a refund of a specific, believable amount awaiting your banking details, or a notice of audit and penalties requiring immediate login.

  3. 3

    A cloned eFiling login

    The link opens a copy of the eFiling portal. Your username, password and OTP are relayed to a criminal logging into the real portal at the same moment.

  4. 4

    Details are changed quietly

    Inside the profile, banking details and the registered contact address are changed, so future correspondence and refunds go to them.

  5. 5

    The fraudulent return

    In some cases a return is filed with inflated deductions to generate a large refund into the fraudster's account, leaving you facing the assessment and penalties.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Never log into eFiling from a link. Type the address yourself or use the official app, every time.
  • Set up and check your eFiling profile's registered banking and contact details periodically.
  • Treat urgency and threats as evidence of fraud rather than of officialdom.
  • Use a unique password for eFiling and enable every available security feature.
  • Verify anything unexpected by phoning SARS on their published contact number.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Log into eFiling directly and change your password immediately.
  2. Check and correct your banking details, contact email and cellphone number on the profile.
  3. Report the incident to SARS through their official fraud reporting channel.
  4. Open a SAPS case if a refund was diverted.
  5. Ask your tax practitioner to review recent submissions for returns you did not file.

Questions people ask

Does SARS ever email about refunds?

SARS does send notifications, but genuine ones direct you to log in yourself rather than through an embedded login link, and they never ask for banking details by email or SMS.

Someone filed a return in my name. Am I liable?

You must report it to SARS immediately and formally. Assessments arising from fraudulent submissions can be disputed, but only if you raise it rather than ignore the correspondence.

I clicked but did not enter anything. Am I fine?

Probably, but change your eFiling password anyway and check your registered details. It costs five minutes.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
