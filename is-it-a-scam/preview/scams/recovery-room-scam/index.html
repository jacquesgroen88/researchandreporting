Recovery Room Scams

Also called: Fund recovery scam · Second-chance scam · Reload scam

Someone contacts a scam victim promising to recover their lost money for a fee, which is simply a second theft from the same person.

  • Typical lossR3 000 – R250 000
  • Mainly seen inSouth Africa, Global

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • They contacted you, not the other way around.
  • Any upfront fee to recover funds.
  • Claims of a guaranteed recovery, or of funds already located and frozen.
  • Requests for banking details or ID documents 'to process the return'.
  • Pressure to act before a court deadline or a freezing order lapses.

How it works

  1. 1

    They already know you were scammed

    Victim lists are traded between criminal groups, so the contact arrives knowing the platform, the amount and sometimes the dates. This specificity is what makes it persuasive.

  2. 2

    Authority is claimed

    They present as a blockchain forensics firm, a law firm, an asset recovery agency, or occasionally as regulators or law enforcement.

  3. 3

    Proof of progress

    You are shown a tracing report or told your funds have been located and frozen in a named jurisdiction. The documents are fabricated.

  4. 4

    Fees before release

    A retainer, then a court filing fee, then a release tax. Structurally identical to the original scam, which is often run by the same people.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Accept as a rule that no legitimate organisation charges an upfront fee to recover scam losses.
  • Recognise that anyone who contacts you unprompted about your loss learned about it from the criminals.
  • Route everything through the SAPS, the FSCA and your bank, none of which charge you.
  • Be especially careful in the months after a loss, when the desire for a fix is strongest.
  • Verify any law firm with the Legal Practice Council before engaging.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop paying and cut contact.
  2. Report to the SAPS, adding it to your existing case if you have one.
  3. Warn others in any victim group you belong to, since these lists circulate widely.
  4. Treat any further approach about your losses as fraudulent by default.

Questions people ask

How did they know exactly how much I lost?

Because they either ran the original scam or bought the victim list from whoever did. It is evidence of criminality, not credibility.

Do legitimate recovery services exist?

Genuine asset recovery exists through courts and regulated insolvency practitioners, usually only for very large or institutional losses. It never begins with a cold call and an upfront fee.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
