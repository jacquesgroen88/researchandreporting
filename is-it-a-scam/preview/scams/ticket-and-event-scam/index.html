Ticket & Event Scams

Also called: Concert ticket scam · Fake ticket resale · Festival ticket fraud

Tickets to sold-out events are sold on social media and resale groups, either duplicated, already used, or entirely fictional.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Payment by EFT to a personal account for a ticket.
  • A ticket sent as a screenshot or PDF rather than an official transfer through the ticketing platform.
  • The seller refuses to meet or to use an escrow or platform transfer.
  • Pressure created by the event being imminent.
  • A profile created recently, or with no mutual connections and little history.

How it works

  1. 1

    Scarcity does the selling

    The event is sold out, and the buyer is emotionally committed before any verification happens.

  2. 2

    A convincing seller

    A profile with history, a plausible reason for selling, and a screenshot of the ticket that looks entirely real.

  3. 3

    EFT and a PDF

    Payment by EFT, then a PDF or a barcode screenshot. The same barcode may be sold to twenty buyers, only the first of whom gets in.

  4. 4

    Discovery at the gate

    The failure happens at the turnstile, hours after the seller has gone quiet and long past any chance of recovery.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Only buy through the official ticketing platform's own resale or transfer function, which reissues the barcode to you.
  • Never accept a screenshot or PDF as proof of a valid ticket. Barcodes copy perfectly.
  • Pay by card or through the platform, never EFT to an individual.
  • Be most careful when you want it most. Desire for the event is the entire mechanism.
  • Verify that the ticket has been transferred into your own account before paying.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Report to your bank immediately, and lodge a chargeback if you paid by card.
  2. Report the seller and listing to the platform and the group administrators.
  3. Open a SAPS case, ideally grouped with other victims of the same seller.
  4. Contact the event organiser, who sometimes flags duplicated barcodes and occasionally assists.

Questions people ask

Can a ticket barcode be checked before the event?

Usually not by a buyer, which is precisely the gap this scam exploits. The only real protection is an official transfer into your own account.

The seller had a real-looking profile with photos.

Profiles are bought, stolen or aged deliberately. Profile appearance is not verification.
