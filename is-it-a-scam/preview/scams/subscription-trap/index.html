Subscription Traps & Free Trial Scams

Also called: Negative option billing · Free trial trap · Recurring billing scam

A free trial or once-off purchase quietly enrols you in a recurring charge that is deliberately difficult to cancel.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Card details required for something described as free.
  • The cancellation process is not visible before you sign up.
  • Terms are behind a link rather than shown at checkout.
  • The merchant name on your statement does not match the product.
  • Reviews mention difficulty cancelling. This is the single most useful thing to search for.

How it works

  1. 1

    An attractive trial

    A free sample, a R10 trial, a heavily discounted first month. Card details are required 'for verification' or a small delivery charge.

  2. 2

    Terms buried by design

    The recurring charge, its amount and the cancellation window are disclosed, but in small print, behind a link, or below the fold.

  3. 3

    Billing begins quietly

    Charges start at an amount below the threshold most people notice, sometimes under a merchant name unrelated to what you bought.

  4. 4

    Cancellation is obstructed

    No cancellation in the account, support that does not respond, or a requirement to phone during limited hours. The friction is the business model.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Search the product name plus 'cancel' before signing up for any trial.
  • Use a virtual card or a low-limit card for trials so you can close it easily.
  • Screenshot the terms at the point of purchase. You will need them in a dispute.
  • Diarise the cancellation date the moment you sign up.
  • Review your bank statement line by line once a month. Most of these survive on inattention.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Cancel in writing and keep proof of the date.
  2. Instruct your bank to stop the recurring card payment or debit order.
  3. Dispute charges taken after your cancellation, with your screenshots as evidence.
  4. Complain to the National Consumer Commission, since aggressive negative-option billing may breach the Consumer Protection Act.

Questions people ask

Can my bank stop a recurring card payment?

Yes. You can instruct your bank to block future recurring payments to a merchant, and you should also cancel with the merchant directly.

Is this actually illegal?

It sits on a spectrum. Clearly disclosed subscriptions are legal. Hidden terms, obstructed cancellation and misleading merchant names may breach the Consumer Protection Act and are worth reporting.
