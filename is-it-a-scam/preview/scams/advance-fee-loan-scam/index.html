Advance Fee Loan Scams

Also called: Guaranteed loan scam · Blacklisted loan scam · Loan insurance fee scam

A guaranteed loan, no credit check, requires an upfront fee that you pay and a loan that never arrives.

Red flags

If you are looking at this right now, these are the things to check first.

  • Any fee payable before a loan is disbursed.
  • 'Blacklisted welcome' or 'no credit check' promises. Registered credit providers must run affordability checks by law.
  • The lender is not registered with the National Credit Regulator, or quotes an NCR number that does not check out.
  • Contact only through WhatsApp or a cellphone number, with no physical office.
  • You are asked for your online banking username and password 'to verify income'.

How it works

  1. 1

    Advertised where desperation lives

    Lamp poles, WhatsApp broadcasts, Facebook groups and SMS blasts. 'Blacklisted welcome. No credit check. Same day payout.'

  2. 2

    Approval without assessment

    You are approved within minutes for an amount you did not really justify. Legitimate credit providers are legally required to assess affordability.

  3. 3

    The fee, framed as standard

    An initiation fee, an insurance premium, a legal or administration charge, payable before the loan is released. Legitimate fees are deducted from the loan, never paid in advance.

  4. 4

    It escalates

    Once the first fee is paid, a second is required: a compliance charge, a clearance fee, a bank verification deposit. Sunk cost keeps people paying.

  5. 5

    Your documents are taken too

    The application collects your ID, payslip, bank statements and sometimes banking login details, a full identity package for later fraud.

What it looks like in practice

How to protect yourself

  • Verify every lender on the NCR register before any contact. It is free and takes a minute.
  • Hold the line: legitimate lenders deduct fees from the loan amount and never require a payment first.
  • Understand that 'blacklisted welcome' is not a service offering, it is a filter for finding vulnerable people.
  • Never share online banking credentials with a lender. No legitimate one asks.
  • If you are in genuine debt distress, speak to a registered debt counsellor rather than a same-day lender.

If this has already happened to you

Work down this list in order. Speed matters more than anything else here.

  1. Stop paying. There is no final fee, and the loan does not exist.
  2. Report to the National Credit Regulator and the SAPS.
  3. If you gave banking credentials, change them immediately and alert your bank.
  4. Register a SAFPS protective listing if you handed over ID and payslip documents.
  5. Check your credit report for accounts opened using your details.

Questions people ask

Are initiation fees not legal in South Africa?

They are, but the National Credit Act allows them to be deducted from or added to the loan, not collected in cash before disbursement.

They gave me an NCR number, so are they registered?

Check it yourself on the NCR register. Quoted numbers are frequently copied from real credit providers.

Can I get the fees back?

Rarely. Report anyway, because volume of complaints is what drives enforcement against these operations.

The one rule

No bank, retailer, courier or government department will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password. Not to verify you, not to help you, not to stop a fraud.
