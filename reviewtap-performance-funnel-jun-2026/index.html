Executive Snapshot

One month, R136.7k in sales on R37.9k of spend.

The account is profitable and scaling. June spend nearly doubled May, sales held pace, and the true return stayed well above break-even. The story is not "is it working" but "how much return is being left on the table by allocation and checkout leaks."

Profitable and scaling

Spend rose from ~R19.0k in May to R37.9k in June. Sales kept up, so the account absorbed a near-doubling of budget without collapsing efficiency. That is the single most important signal for a business being prepared for sale.

Meta under-reports you

Blended 3.60x versus Meta-reported 2.05x. On a South African, mobile-heavy, iOS-attribution-lossy account this gap is expected and it works in your favour. The ads are pulling more weight than the dashboard admits.

The leak is checkout, not clicks

Traffic and add-to-cart are healthy for the category. The bleed is between reaching checkout and completing it: 264 reached checkout, only 111 finished. Fixing that one step is the highest-leverage move in this report.

What's happening

Prospecting is taking 61% of the new-structure budget and returning 1.20x, which is below break-even after product cost is thin but real at volume. Creative Testing, on a third of the budget, is returning 2.74x. The retargeting bucket, which should be the highest-ROAS layer in any account, is being starved at R589.

What should happen next

Shift budget down the funnel. Trim Prospecting until its ROAS recovers or its winning ad is isolated, roughly double Creative Testing while it holds above 2.5x, and fund Retargeting properly so warm traffic gets a real second touch. The winners already exist in the account, they are just under-fed.

The R3,976 lesson

Do not judge a creative by one placement. "Reviews Used to Be Hard" is a proven 3.69x static that is being torched at 1.31x purely because it is fronting a cold prospecting set with a 0.89% CTR. Move the budget, keep the ad.

High CTR is not the goal

"Begging Video" pulls a 3.02% CTR, the best in the account, and returns 0.51x. It is manufacturing cheap clicks from people who will never buy an NFC review stand. Clicks are a vanity metric here, ROAS is the scoreboard.

What's happening

Feed and Facebook, the lower-return options, are absorbing 84% of spend. Instagram Reels and Stories, the two highest-return placements at 3.60x and 2.98x, share just 13%. Meanwhile a thin trickle of budget leaks into Marketplace, Audience Network and rewarded-video placements that produce garbage clicks and zero sales.

What should happen next

Manually exclude Audience Network, Marketplace, rewarded video and Threads. Then either carve Instagram Reels and Stories into their own placement-specific ad sets, or use manual placements to push more budget there. This is the single highest-return, lowest-effort change available: the money moves toward a proven 3x+ return with no new creative required.

Biggest single leak

The checkout step. Nearly 6 in 10 people who reach checkout leave without buying. This is the one number that, fixed, moves everything downstream.

Second leak

Session to add-to-cart at 7.2%. For a considered B2B hardware purchase this is not disastrous, but the product page and offer clarity can lift it.

Working well

Cart to checkout holds 84.6%. Once someone commits to a cart, they mostly reach checkout. The bridge that breaks is checkout itself.

Where Conversion Is Lost · Device & Source

88% of traffic is mobile, and mobile converts at half the desktop rate.

The funnel leak is not evenly spread. It concentrates on mobile, and it concentrates in paid social traffic. Both are the majority of volume, so both are the highest-value places to fix.

Search converts at nearly double paid social but is only 4% of traffic. That is a demand-capture gap: there is intent-driven volume the account is not yet catching.

What's happening

Paid social sends the most people at the lowest intent, onto mobile, into a checkout that loses 58% of them. The three weakest links stack on top of each other for the majority of traffic.

What should happen next

Prioritise a mobile checkout teardown first, since it compounds across 88% of sessions and the lowest-converting source. Then open a small branded and category search layer to capture the higher-intent traffic that already converts at 4.4%.

Why this matters for the exit

A 9% repeat rate is normal for durable hardware: a business buys a review stand once. But it means the revenue is almost entirely new-customer acquisition, which a buyer will discount versus recurring revenue. This is the numerical case for the ReviewTap Pro recurring layer: it converts a one-time hardware sale into transferable monthly revenue, which is what actually lifts the valuation multiple.

What Should Happen Next

Six moves, in order of return per hour.

Note the through-line: the account under-funds its winners at the campaign, platform, and placement level all at once, so the same fix (move money toward proven return) pays off three times over.

The one-line takeaway

The ads work. The account is profitable at a true 3.60x and scaling cleanly. The upside is not in spending more, it is in pointing the budget at the winners it already has and stopping the 58% checkout leak that is quietly discounting every ad in the account.
