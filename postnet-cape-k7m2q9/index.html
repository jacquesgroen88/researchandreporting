Finding 1.

The problem is the spread, not the average

PostNet Kidds Beach sits at 5.0. PostNet Amalinda sits at 1.6. Same brand above the door, same services on the wall, same management and marketing fee out the door every month.

Twenty-seven of the 134 are below 4.0. Forty-eight are at 4.5 or better. Somebody searching PostNet in Milnerton sees 4.7. Somebody searching in Canal Walk sees 3.2. They are choosing between two companies that happen to share a logo.

The estate average of 4.24 conceals every bit of that, which is presumably why it has never been dealt with.

84% of your reviews are either a 1 or a 5. The entire middle, every two and three star review across 2,664 of them, comes to 5.2%.

This is what a parcel counter looks like on Google. Nobody writes a considered three-star review about a courier desk. Either the parcel was there and the person was pleasant, or it was late, or lost, or the store was shut when the website said open. You get the shout or the silence.

Which is the good news, and it is the whole reason to bother

Across those 2,664 reviews there are five five-star reviews for every one-star. The happy customers are not hypothetical, they already outnumber the angry ones five to one. They just have to be standing at the counter when somebody asks. Every store below 4.0 in your estate is a store where the angry minority got asked and the happy majority did not.

Finding 6.

Google has you filed as a courier company

All but six of the 134 stores are categorised on Google as "Courier service." You sell copy and print, stationery, mailboxes and digital services. In local search, that side of the business barely exists.

The six exceptions look accidental rather than deliberate. Oudtshoorn and Cape Town Metro are filed as "Print shop", Knysna as "Shipping service", Oakdale as "Copy shop", Bluewater Bay as "Digital printing service", NorthCrest Mthatha as "Print shop".

What goes into every store

One counter stand and two cards per store. That is the whole install, and it takes a staff member about four minutes to set up.

The stand

Lives on the counter facing the customer, branded to PostNet, tap or scan. It points at that store's own Google review page, not a shared link, so every review lands on the right profile. We build and test all 134 destinations before anything ships.

Card one, carried by whoever is serving

The counter stand catches people who are already looking down. The card is for the moment the parcel is handed over. Staff tap it against the customer's phone and the review page opens. In practice this is what moves the number, because it is a person asking rather than a sign hoping.

Card two, programmed with the store itself

This is the one worth thinking about. The card opens a page we host and control for that store, carrying whatever you want a customer to walk away with: the store's email address, WhatsApp number, address, real trading hours, a track-and-trace link, a print quote request, mailbox enquiries.

Because the destination is ours to change, the same physical card can do a different job next quarter without reprinting anything. Review push in September, print promotion in November, back again in January. Head office decides centrally and it changes across all 134 at once.

Why card two matters more than it looks

Finding 6 says Google has your whole estate filed as a courier service, so almost nobody searches you for printing. That is the problem on the search side. Card two is the same problem solved on the counter side: the customer is already standing in your store collecting a parcel, and they leave with your print and stationery offer saved on their phone. It turns a collection into a lead for the higher-margin half of the business.

Why this cannot be left to each store

The obvious objection is that PostNet stores are owner-operated, so reviews are each franchisee's own business and each should buy their own kit. It is a fair objection and we want to answer it properly, because we think it is wrong for five reasons.

1. That has already been tried. Finding 1 is the result.

Nobody ever decided that PostNet stores should range from 1.6 to 5.0. That range is simply what "leave it to each store" produces over time. Three Cape stores have already bought review hardware from us independently, on their own cards, at full retail, and the estate is still at 4.24 with twenty-seven stores under 4.0. Decentralised is not the alternative plan. It is the thing that produced the current numbers.

2. Three of the six findings cannot be fixed at store level at all

The shadow listings, the category misclassification across 128 stores, and the duplicate listings are Google account problems that sit above any single franchisee. A store owner can fix their counter. They cannot fix the brand's footprint on Google, and it would be unreasonable to ask them to.

3. The name on the door is not the franchisee's asset to damage

Somebody who has a bad time at Amalinda does not stop using Amalinda. They stop using PostNet, and the next time they need a parcel sent they try the courier down the road. Amalinda's 1.6 is being paid for by every other store in East London. Brand standards are enforced centrally everywhere else in the franchise agreement, and a public 1.6 is a brand standards problem.

4. The money already exists and is already being collected

Every franchisee already pays a combined management and marketing fee, which published PostNet franchise material puts at 9% of turnover. You will know the exact figure better than we do. Either way, head office already takes money from every store every month specifically to spend on marketing on their behalf. This is not a request for new budget. It is a request to point a very small slice of an existing budget at the thing that decides whether a local search becomes a walk-in. Across the Cape estate it works out at R71 per store per month. You can compare that against what each store already contributes far faster than we can.

5. One payer instead of 134

Run store by store, this becomes 134 quotes, 134 approvals, 134 artwork sign-offs, 134 invoices and 134 support relationships, of which perhaps a third ever say yes, and the stores that most need it are the least likely to be among them. Centrally it is one artwork approval, one delivery schedule and one invoice. Head office already invoices every store every month, so recovering R71 as a line on an invoice that already goes out is close to zero new admin. Head office can be entirely cash-neutral on this and still own the network view.

And the practical trick: opt-out, not opt-in

Default every store into the programme and let a franchisee opt out if they genuinely want to. Opt-in gets you roughly a third of the network and selects for the stores that were already paying attention. Opt-out gets you nearly all of it, including the stores whose ratings are actually dragging the brand. Those two paths produce very different numbers, and it costs nothing to choose the better one.

  • Early warning on the next Amalinda, while it is still at 3.8 and cheap to fix
  • Hard evidence for performance conversations and renewals, instead of anecdote
  • A number for the "own a PostNet" page, once the estate average is worth publishing

If head office will not carry it: the fallback is that they approve us as a supplier and set the standard, and each store buys its own kit at group rates on its own account. It works, it needs no central budget, and it is materially worse than the above, because the stores that need it most will not opt in and nobody ends up with the network view.
