One-Page Growth Plan · Prompt Pack

20 prompts that fill your plan

Copy a prompt, replace the words in [square brackets] with your own, paste it into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. The free versions are fine.

Three rules that make AI useful

  1. Start with prompt 0. It tells the AI about your business once, so every answer after it is about you, not a generic business.
  2. Give it real material. Paste your actual reviews, your actual menu, your actual website text. AI is only as good as what you feed it.
  3. Argue with it. If an answer is generic, reply "That could be any business. Make it specific to mine." It gets better every round.

0Set the scene

Run this first, in a new chat. Keep the chat open for the rest.

I own a business in South Africa and I want your help writing a one-page marketing plan. Act as an experienced marketing strategist who is direct and practical, and who asks questions when something is unclear. My business: - Name: [business name] - What we sell: [products or services] - Where: [town or area, and whether we serve customers locally, nationally or online] - Typical customer: [who usually buys] - Average sale: [R amount] and customers buy [how often] - Roughly how many enquiries we get a month: [number] - What we spend on marketing a month, and on what: [R amount, channels or agency] - What is working: [anything you know works] - What is frustrating me: [your biggest marketing frustration] Keep answers short and specific to my business. Use South African context and rands. Before we start, ask me the three questions that would most improve your advice.

1Who I sell to

Find the customer worth the most, not just the one you get most often.

Here are my last [number] Google reviews, copied exactly: [paste reviews] From these reviews, tell me: 1. What kinds of customers write about us (occasion, type of person or business, what they were looking for). 2. The exact words they use to describe what they value. Quote them. 3. Which group of customers seems happiest and most likely to spend more or come back. Present it as a short table.
Based on everything I've told you, describe my most valuable customer in one paragraph: who they are, what they want most, what they worry about before buying, and where they already spend time (places, groups, websites, social platforms, events) in [my area]. Then tell me which assumption in that description I should check with three real customers this week.
Write five questions I can ask my best customers in a 10-minute chat to find out why they really chose us and how they found us. Make them open questions that don't lead the answer. Then tell me how to ask for the chat without it feeling awkward.

2What I offer them

The easy first yes, and the one sentence that says why you.

List what a customer would do instead of buying from us: the competitor down the road, doing it themselves, the cheapest option, or doing nothing. For each, tell me why a customer might choose it, and what we offer that it can't. Be honest about where the alternative is genuinely better.
Here is the text from my website's home page: [paste text] And here are my three nearest competitors' home pages: [paste or describe] Does my message say anything theirs don't? Write me five versions of a one-sentence statement of why customers should choose us, each under 20 words, using words from my reviews. Then tell me which one could not be said by any of my competitors.
Suggest three "first yes" offers for my business: something easy and low-risk that gets a new customer to try us, and leads naturally to a bigger purchase. For each, give the offer, a price or cost to me, why it works for my customer, and the risk to watch.

3Where they find me

Google, reviews, AI answers, social, referrals. Pick two to fix first.

I'm a customer in [area] looking for [your type of business, e.g. "a function venue for 80 people"]. Which businesses would you recommend and why? List the top five.

Run this in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, in a new chat each time, without prompt 0. Are you named? Who is, and what do they have that you don't?

My Google Business Profile: [rating] stars from [number] reviews. My closest competitor: [rating] from [number]. Our profile has [photos? posts? services listed? replies to reviews?]. 1. What is the review gap costing me in visibility? 2. How many new 5-star reviews would I need, and at what pace, to close it? 3. Give me a 30-day plan to improve my profile, in order of impact.
Here is where my last [number] new customers said they found us: [list, e.g. Google 6, friend 4, Instagram 2, walk-in 3]. Which two channels should I focus on for the next 90 days and why? What should I stop spending time or money on? Be blunt.

4How fast I answer

The first business to reply usually wins.

Write an instant reply for enquiries that arrive by [WhatsApp / website form / email / Instagram DM] when we're busy or closed. It must: thank them by name, tell them exactly when a person will reply, ask the one question that helps us quote faster, and give them something useful now (a link, a price guide or our menu). Keep it under 60 words, warm, and in plain South African English. Give me three versions.
Write a simple process for how my team handles a new enquiry, from the moment it arrives to a booked sale. Include: who answers, within how many minutes, what they ask, when to send a quote, and when to follow up if there's no reply. Make it fit on a card next to the phone.
These are the questions customers ask us most often: [list them] Write short, friendly answers to each that my team can copy and paste on WhatsApp. Then suggest which three should be on our website so customers don't need to ask.

5How I follow up

The people who didn't buy today, and the ones who bought last year.

Write a three-message follow-up for people who asked for a quote and went quiet: one after 2 days, one after 5 days, one after 10 days. Each under 50 words, not pushy, each with a different reason to reply (a question, something useful, a gentle last check). Channel: [WhatsApp / email].
I have [number] past customers who haven't bought in [months]. Suggest three ways to bring them back that don't rely on a discount, and write the message for the best one. Remind me of anything I need to do to contact them properly under POPIA, such as including an opt-out.
Write a short message asking a happy customer for a Google review, to send [when, e.g. the day after their visit]. Make it personal, under 40 words, with a placeholder for the review link. Then write a version that also asks them to tell one friend.

6My numbers

Four numbers, checked monthly.

Help me set up four marketing numbers to check on the first Monday of every month: enquiries, reply time, how many enquiries buy, and money in from marketing. For each one, tell me where I can find it with what I already have (my inbox, WhatsApp, Google Business Profile, bank statement), how long it should take to check, and what a good target is for a business like mine.
Here are my numbers: enquiries [x] a month, about [y]% buy, average sale R[z], marketing spend R[s] a month. Work out roughly what each customer costs me to win, what each new customer is worth over a year, and which single number, if improved by 20%, would make me the most money. Show the working.

+Put it together

Using everything in this chat, fill in my One-Page Growth Plan. Six short sections: 1 Who I sell to, 2 What I offer them, 3 Where they find me, 4 How fast I answer, 5 How I follow up, 6 My numbers (with targets). Each section no more than four bullet points. Then list the three things I should do this week, in order. Keep it specific to my business, plain language, no jargon.
Now act as a sceptical business advisor. Read my plan and tell me the three weakest points, the assumption most likely to be wrong, and what I'd regret not doing in 90 days. Be direct.
