Filming Sunday 11 October

Ten video scripts

For TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels, promoting the One-Page Growth Plan workshop on Thursday 15 October. Film all ten in one 90-minute session. Read them off the phone, one line at a time; nobody will notice.

Workshop strategy · Strategy Lives
Filming setup, once
  • Phone vertical, at eye height, about an arm's length away. Your eyes in the top third of the frame.
  • Face a window. No light behind you. A lapel mic if you have one, otherwise film close in a quiet room.
  • Two spots only: at your desk, and standing with some depth behind you. Change shirts between spots so the videos don't look like one session.
  • Say each hook three times in a row with different energy. We pick the best in the edit.
  • [CUT] marks where a jump cut is fine. CARD is on-screen text added in the edit.

1 · The five-minute rule

Pillar: one box, one lesson · 35 s · post Sunday

Hook: "The first business to reply to an enquiry usually gets the booking. Most reply hours later."

A big study of over a million sales leads found that replying within an hour made a business about seven times more likely to win the customer than replying later. [CUT] And I keep seeing South African businesses take four hours, or a day, or never. [CUT] The fix isn't a new app. It's a rule: someone answers every enquiry within five minutes, even if it's just "thanks, I'll have a price for you by 3pm." CARD: Reply in 5 minutes

End: "That's box four of the one-page plan. I'm teaching all six live on Thursday at seven. Link in my bio."

Caption: How fast does your business really reply to a new enquiry? Comment your honest answer.

2 · Your Google rating isn't your problem

Pillar: one box, one lesson · 40 s · post Sunday

Hook: "Your 4.8-star rating probably isn't helping you as much as you think."

Here's what I see all the time. A business has 4.8 stars from 60 reviews. The place down the road has 4.4 from 900. [CUT] Guess who Google shows first, and who customers trust more? Usually the 900. CARD: 4.8 from 60 vs 4.4 from 900 [CUT] Quality matters, but volume and how recent your reviews are matter too. So the job isn't to protect your rating. It's to ask every happy customer, every time.

End: "Where customers find you is box three of the one-page plan. Live workshop Thursday at seven, link in my bio."

Caption: Rating or review count: which matters more for your business? We built review tools for 1,200+ SA businesses. Here's what we saw.

4 · Best month ever, bank balance went down

Pillar: teardown of your own business · 45 s · post Monday

Hook: "We had our best month ever, and the bank balance went down by twenty-one thousand rand."

Revenue was up. Everyone was happy. [CUT] Then I looked at the bank account. It had dropped. CARD: Revenue ≠ profit ≠ cash [CUT] Revenue, profit and cash are three different numbers, and most marketing reports only show you the first one. So now I check four numbers every month, and money in the bank is one of them.

End: "Those four numbers are the last box of the one-page plan. Thursday, seven pm, link in bio."

Caption: Which number do you check first every month? Revenue, profit or cash?
Note: this is your own story and your own number. Don't name which business it was.

5 · "Best quality, best service" isn't a reason

Pillar: one box, one lesson · 35 s · post Tuesday

Hook: "If your website says 'best quality, best service', you've said nothing."

Every competitor says the same thing. Customers skip right past it. [CUT] Here's a better test: read your own reviews and find the words customers use, not the ones you use. CARD: Use your customers' words Say you run a guesthouse and guests keep mentioning the breakfast and your local tips. That's your message. Not "luxury accommodation".

End: "Box two of the one-page plan. Thursday at seven, link in bio."

Caption: What do your customers say about you that you never say about yourself?

6 · The customers you already paid for

Pillar: one box, one lesson · 35 s · post Tuesday

Hook: "Your cheapest new sale is someone who already bought from you."

Just make sure they agreed to hear from you, and always let them opt out.
