Why "more detail on strategy" is the right call

Free marketing content on TikTok is mostly tactics: hooks, hacks, which app to use. Owners are drowning in tactics and short on decisions. Teaching strategy (who exactly, why you, at what price, through which channel, measured how) is rarer, harder to copy, and leads naturally to the Blueprint, which is a strategy product. Every topic below is a decision, not a tactic.

The seven workshops in detail

Thursday 15 October 2026 · pilot · R297

1 · The One-Page Growth Plan

"Your whole marketing plan, on one page, in one evening."

The foundation, already designed in the workshop strategy. Six boxes: who I sell to, what I offer, where they find me, how fast I answer, how I follow up, my numbers. Every later workshop goes deep on one or two of these boxes, which is how the series sells itself: "Box 2 is the one most of you struggled with. In November we spend 90 minutes on it."

Thursday 12 November 2026 · R497

2 · Positioning: why anyone should choose you

"If you can't say why you, your customers can't either."

The decision: what you want to be known for, for whom, against what alternative.

  • Your real competition. It's rarely the business down the road. It's doing nothing, doing it themselves, or the cheapest option. Owners list what customers would do if you didn't exist.
  • What only you have. Capabilities, proof, location, speed, specialism. Pull it from your best reviews (prompt: mine your Google reviews for the words customers use).
  • Who cares most. The customer segment for whom that difference matters most and who pays best for it. Most owners find they have been marketing to everyone.
  • The positioning statement, one sentence, tested against three competitors' websites: if it fits them too, it isn't positioning.
  • Live hot seat: two businesses repositioned on the call.

Leave with: a one-page positioning sheet: competition, unique strengths, best-fit customer, one-sentence position, three proof points. Credit: the approach draws on April Dunford's positioning work; name her and recommend the book, never copy her canvas.

Thursday 3 December 2026 · R497

3 · Your 90-day marketing plan for 2027

"Plan January before you go on leave."

The decision: the three marketing priorities for January to March, and what you stop doing.

  • Look back: what actually brought customers in 2026. Most owners cannot answer, which becomes workshop 7's hook.
  • Your seasonality map: when customers decide, not when they buy. The Acreage lesson, anonymised: year-end functions are chosen in August and September.
  • Pick three priorities using impact against effort, and one thing to stop.
  • The 13-week calendar: one priority per month, a weekly owner check, who does what.
  • Budget sense-check against last year's turnover.

Leave with: a quarter plan on one page, ready for the first Monday back. Timed for the week owners think about next year, before the December shutdown.

Thursday 21 January 2027 · R497

4 · The offer and the price

"Most businesses don't have a marketing problem. They have an offer problem."

The decision: what you lead with, how it is packaged, and what you charge.

  • The entry offer: the first, easy yes that starts the relationship. Examples by industry: a tasting evening, a first-visit package, a free check.
  • Good, better, best: three tiers so customers choose how much, not whether. Why the middle tier sells most.
  • Pricing without guessing: anchor to the customer's alternative, not to your costs. When to raise prices, and how to tell existing customers.
  • Risk reversal: guarantees that are honest and capped, and the ones that will bankrupt you.
  • Naming and wording the offer so it is understood in five seconds.

Leave with: an offer page: entry offer, three tiers with prices, guarantee, the five-second description.

Thursday 18 February 2027 · R497

5 · The customer journey: stranger to repeat customer

"Find the step where you lose people."

The decision: which step of the journey to fix first.

  • Map the six steps: discover, check you out, enquire, decide, buy, come back and refer. Write what actually happens at each today, not what should happen.
  • Do the mystery-customer test on yourself: enquire through every channel and time it. Attendees do it live on their phones.
  • Find the leak: estimate how many people reach each step. The biggest drop is the priority.
  • Fix the hand-offs: instant replies, follow-up sequences, the post-purchase message, the review request.
  • The repeat and referral loop: the cheapest customers you will ever get.

Leave with: a journey map with the leak marked and three fixes. This is the Business X-Ray done by the owner, so the bridge to a free X-Ray is natural: "Want us to do this from the outside?"

Thursday 18 March 2027 · R497

6 · Channels and budget: where your next R10,000 goes

"Stop spreading thin. Pick the two channels that fit."

The decision: which two channels you commit to, and how much each gets.

  • Channel fit: search (Google, AI answers), social, referrals and partners, direct (email and WhatsApp to customers), local and offline. Which fits how your customers buy.
  • What a customer may cost: work out how much you can afford to pay to win one, from your average sale and how often customers come back.
  • The budget split: most on what works, some on improving it, a little on testing something new.
  • Working with an agency or freelancer: what to ask for, what to measure, and when to fire them.
  • Live: one volunteer's budget rebuilt on the call.

Leave with: a channel and budget page: two channels, monthly spend, the most a customer may cost, and the test for this quarter.

Thursday 15 April 2027 · R497

7 · Measuring marketing: your numbers on one page

"Our best month ever, and the bank balance went down. Here's why that happens."

The decision: the few numbers you will check every month, and what you do when one moves.

  • The R21,000 story: revenue, profit and cash are three different numbers. Most dashboards celebrate the wrong one.
  • Four numbers: enquiries, reply time, conversion to sale, money in, plus one channel number.
  • Where to find each without new software: Google Business Profile, your inbox, your bank statement, a simple tally.
  • The monthly 15-minute review: what moved, why, what changes next month.
  • Agency reports decoded: which figures in a typical report actually matter.

Leave with: a one-page marketing scorecard and a recurring calendar slot. This closes the season: seven pages, one complete strategy.
