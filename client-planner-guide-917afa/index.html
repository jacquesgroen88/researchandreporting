How the Client Planner works

One live list of every client, what we owe each of them, by when, and who is doing it. Jacques and Diane work from the same page, and Claude keeps it loaded.

Open the plannerOpens on claude.ai. You need to be signed in and to have been given access.

What it is

The planner is a single page on claude.ai with a small shared database behind it. When either of you ticks a task, hands one over or answers a question, the other person's screen updates within seconds. Nobody has to save, sync or refresh.

It holds two kinds of record:

  • Clients. One card per client, carrying the engagement in a line, the fee and term from the agreement, the main contact, the last invoice and the next billing event. Ended clients are kept but folded away at the bottom.
  • Tasks. Each task belongs to a client, or to "General" for things like a bank check. It has a due date, an owner, a status, a priority and a short hand-over note written so the owner can act without asking.

It was first filled on 10 November 2026 from the signed agreements, the invoice records and the last two months of email, then checked by three independent reviewers. That's covered further down.

Getting in

  1. Jacques shares it with Diane. In the planner, open the Share menu and add Diane with at least Contributor access. Viewer access lets her read it but not tick or hand anything over.
  2. Say who you are, once per browser. The first time the page opens it asks "Who's using this browser?" Pick your name. That sets your default view to your own tasks and signs every hand-over you make. You can switch it later with the link under the title.
  3. Bookmark it. The planner works on a phone too. The filter bar stops sticking to the top on small screens so the list gets the room.

Reading the page

Across the top sit six counters: clients, open tasks, overdue, due today, waiting on Jacques, and with Diane. Under them are two switches. By date is the day-to-day view. By client is for account reviews and calls.

The owner switch defaults to Mine. Everyone shows the whole business. On the Jacques filter, anything that needs his answer shows even when Diane owns the task.

By client

Each card opens with a one-line health check: how many tasks are open, how many are overdue, and the next one due. The fee, term, contact and billing details sit behind a fold so the cards stay short. Each card shows its three nearest tasks with a link to see the rest, and an Add task button that pre-fills the client.

Handing work over

Every task shows its owner as a coloured pill: Jacques Diane. Tap the pill to hand the task over.

  1. Pick who it goes to.
  2. Optionally write one line they need to know, for example "Draft it, I'll approve before it goes".
  3. Tap Hand over. The task moves to their list with a New for you flag and your note at the top.

The page records who handed it over and when, so later you can see where a task came from. Nothing is reassigned by accident. The pill opens a choice; it never cycles on a stray tap.

Who owns whatDiane decides on sending anything already approved, chasing any invoice or overdue commitment, client updates on delivery dates, and closing a task once she has tested it end to end. Jacques decides on pricing, scope, contracts and anything that moves money out of the business. This follows Diane's role charter, and the planner's first load was split along this line.

Approvals: Diane prepares, Jacques says yes

Most of what used to sit on Jacques's list was paperwork that only needed his yes. The planner splits those into two halves. Diane does the work. Jacques answers one line.

To ask for an approval, open the task and set Needs Jacques to Approve, Decide or Sign. Write the question so it can be answered in a few words, add the rand value if there is one, and save. The task stays in Diane's name.

Jacques sees the question in the Needs Jacques lane. He can type an answer or a condition ("R9,499 now, rest at go-live") and tap Approve, or Send answer for a decision. His answer is written into the task's notes with the date, and the task goes back to Diane as New for you.

Waiting and chasing

Lots of client work stalls on the client: an access invite, a logo, a yes. Mark those Waiting and set a Next chase date. Until that date the task stays folded away under "Waiting, next chase later". On the chase date it comes back into Today on its own.

After each nudge, push the next chase date forward three to five days. A Diane-owned waiting task shows as "Chase" and a Jacques-owned one as "Waiting", because for her it's an action and for him it's a blocker.

The weekly rhythm

  • Every morning, five minutes each. Open on Mine. Clear New for you. Jacques clears the Needs Jacques lane. Work Overdue, then Today.
  • Mondays and Thursdays, Diane. Open "Waiting, next chase later" on Everyone. Chase whatever is due, then move each chase date forward.
  • Fridays, Diane. In By client, check every card has nothing overdue and that anything finished was actually sent and tested.
  • Month start, Diane. Filter on Billing and run the invoicing sweep from the cards' "Next bill" lines.

Where the data comes from and how it stays fresh

Claude filled the planner by reading:

  • every client folder's agreements, invoices, proposals and handover notes;
  • the invoice registers;
  • the existing waiting-on and board files;
  • two months of Jacques's email.

Where those sources disagreed, the newer one won and the conflict was written into the task's notes.

Claude can read and write the planner's database directly. That means a session can be asked to "update the planner from my email", "add what came out of today's call with the client", or "close everything the bank shows as paid". The changes appear on both your screens straight away.

One list, not fourMission Control already tracks work in the action board, the waiting-on register and the scoreboard. If all of them keep tracking client work, they will drift apart. The reviewers recommended this split:
  • The planner becomes the one record for client tasks.
  • The waiting-on register keeps only non-client dependencies, such as the accountant, Payfast and suppliers.
  • The action board keeps internal and product work.
  • The morning brief, the chase routine and the session wrap-up read from and write to the planner.
That change is waiting on Jacques's go-ahead.

How it was tested

Before the planner went to Diane, three reviewers worked through it separately, each with the code and data but not each other's conclusions:

  • a usability reviewer, playing a Monday morning as each user;
  • a chief-of-staff reviewer, asking whether it actually takes work off Jacques;
  • an auditor, cross-checking every task against the source files.
5/10Day-to-day usefulness, first version
3 → 7Load taken off Jacques, before and after the fixes
5/10Tracking accuracy, first version

Changes made from their findings:

  • A Mine default view, plus a Today group and a group for high-priority work with no date.
  • Hand-overs now ask who it goes to and record a note, instead of cycling owners on a tap. Diane gets a New for you flag.
  • The Needs Jacques lane with an answer box, so roughly 20 clerical items moved to Diane with a one-tap approval left for Jacques.
  • Waiting tasks got chase dates and fold away until they're due.
  • Detail text is clamped to two lines, client facts sit behind a fold, and tap targets are bigger on phones.
  • Saving a task only writes the fields that changed, so two people editing at once don't overwrite each other.
  • Duplicates were merged, one wrongly described payment was corrected, an invoice date was aligned with the onboarding plan, and about ten missing items were added (small debts, unconfirmed bookings, a never-sent pro forma).

After the fixes, Jacques owns about 26 open tasks and Diane about 72, many of them chases. If her list proves too long, the first thing to change is the chase dates, not the owners.

Known limits

  • The planner doesn't send notifications. You see new work when you open the page.
  • Client cards (fee, term, contact, billing) can be changed by Claude but not yet from the page itself. Tell Claude when a fee or term changes.
  • "Who are you" is remembered per browser. On a new device, pick your name again.
  • The page works only inside claude.ai, for people the planner is shared with.
