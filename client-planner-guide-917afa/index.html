How the Project Planner works

One live list of every client, what we owe each of them, by when, and who is doing it. Jacques and Diane work from the same page, and both of your Claude assistants can read and change it too.

Open the plannerNeeds your personal planner key the first time on each device.

What it is

The planner has three parts:

  • The database. A Supabase project called jce-client-planner holds every client, task and label, plus a record of every change.
  • The planner page. A private web page that works on any browser or phone.
  • The planner connector (MCP). It lets Claude or Codex read and update the same list in conversation.

All three use the same data. A change from the page, from your Claude or from Diane's shows up for the other person within about 30 seconds, or straight away when they switch back to the tab.

It holds two kinds of record:

  • Clients. One card per client, carrying the engagement in a line, the fee and term from the agreement, the main contact, the last invoice and the next billing event. Ended clients are kept but folded away at the bottom.
  • Tasks. Each task belongs to a client, or to "General" for things like a bank check. It has a due date, an owner, a status, a priority and a short hand-over note written so the owner can act without asking.

It was first filled on 10 November 2026 from the signed agreements, the invoice records and the last two months of email, then checked by three independent reviewers. That's covered further down.

Getting in

  1. Get your planner key. Jacques and Diane each have a personal key starting jcep_. It tells the planner who you are, so every tick, hand-over and answer is signed with your name. Jacques holds both keys and passes Diane hers privately. Never paste a key into email or a group chat.
  2. Open the page and paste your key. That browser remembers it. "Sign out of this browser", under the title, forgets it.
  3. Bookmark it. The planner works on a phone too. The filter bar stops sticking to the top on small screens so the list gets the room.
If a key leaksTell Claude "revoke Diane's planner key and issue a new one". The old key stops working at once and nothing else changes.

  • Calendar shows what's coming up.
  • By client is for account reviews and calls.

A label dropdown narrows any view to one category.

Calendar

A month grid. Each task sits on its due date, coloured by owner: blue for Jacques, pink for Diane, grey for unassigned. A waiting task sits on its next chase date instead and is marked ⟳. Owner and label filters apply here too, so "Diane + Finance" shows her billing month at a glance.

Labels

Labels are categories, and a task can carry several. The planner started with eight:

  • Finance
  • Delivery
  • Client input
  • Admin
  • Renewal
  • Sales
  • Build / tech

Tick labels inside any task. To make a new one, type a name, pick a colour and press Add label in the same box. It's available to both of you straight away.

By client

Each card opens with a one-line health check: how many tasks are open, how many are overdue, and the next one due. The fee, term, contact and billing details sit behind a fold so the cards stay short. Each card shows its three nearest tasks with a link to see the rest, and an Add task button that pre-fills the client.

Working on tasks

The sidebar holds every view: My tasks, Everything, Approvals, Calendar, Clients and Reports. Your labels are listed underneath it; click one to filter, click it again to clear. Click your photo at the bottom of the sidebar to change it.

The task panel and comments

Click a task name and its panel opens on the right. Every field saves as soon as you change it: name, assignee, due date, status, priority, client, labels, the question for Jacques, the description and the source. There's no Save button. Underneath are two tabs:

  • Comments. Use these for client replies, decisions and questions for each other. Ctrl+Enter sends. Tasks with comments show a speech-bubble count in the list.
  • Activity. Every change to the task, with who made it and when.

Changing things in the list

  • Due date: click it and pick a new day.
  • Assignee: click the name to hand the task over.
  • Several tasks at once: press Select, tick the tasks, then use the bar at the bottom to change the date, owner, label or priority, or mark them all done.
  • Calendar: drag a task to another day to reschedule it.

Client pages

Click any client in the Clients table, or a client name in a task row, to open that client's own page. It shows:

  • the open work, with an "Add a task" line already linked to the client;
  • recently completed tasks;
  • a client activity feed;
  • an editable details card (status, account lead, fee, term, contact, billing and notes);
  • a Wiki card with the links and SOPs filed against that client, and a button to add one.

On a tablet or phone, the client list moves from the sidebar to a row of chips under the menu. Swipe it sideways to reach every client.

Each client page has its own address, so you can bookmark it.

Inbox, alerts and the morning digest

The Inbox, at the top of the sidebar, collects everything aimed at you: tasks handed to you, new tasks, approval questions and answers, comments on your tasks, files attached to them, and @mentions. The badge shows how many are unread. Opening one marks it read.

The same updates arrive by email from jacques@jcemedia.com:

  • Morning digest: weekdays at 07:00. It lists what's new for you, approvals, overdue, today and the next three days.
  • Alerts: anything new for you is gathered and sent as one email, checked every 10 minutes, so a burst of changes doesn't flood your inbox. Updates you already opened in the planner are left out.

Click your photo at the bottom of the sidebar to switch either email off, or to change your photo.

Links and files

Every task panel has a Links section for order links, email threads, Drive docs and invoices, and a Files section for attachments up to 8 MB each. Task rows show 🔗 and 📎 counts.

Group, sort and saved views

In My tasks, Everything and on client pages you can group tasks by due date, client, owner, label, priority or status, and sort them by due date, priority, client, name or most recently changed. ☆ Save view stores the current filters, grouping and sorting under a name. Saved views appear in the sidebar on every device you use.

Calendar week view and drag

Switch the calendar between Month and Week. The week view shows every task for each day in full. To reschedule, drag a task to another day; on a tablet, hold it for half a second first. Waiting tasks move by their next chase date.

Milestones

Milestones are the big moments for a client: a site going live, a contract signed, a renewal date. Add one with the ◆ Milestone button in the toolbar, or + Add milestone on a client page. Milestones show with a ◆:

  • in the task lists;
  • on the calendar;
  • at the top of each client page;
  • in the Milestones card on the dashboard.

Mark one complete like any task and it shows as "Reached". In Claude or ChatGPT, a milestone is a task with the label milestone.

Reports: Dashboard and History

Reports in the sidebar has two tabs. The Dashboard shows:

  • open, overdue and completed counts, plus open milestones;
  • a donut of open tasks by client (click a slice to open that client);
  • a line chart of tasks completed each week over the last 12 weeks, split by you and Diane;
  • bars of open tasks by owner and by label;
  • upcoming milestones;
  • Added vs completed: tasks added and finished each week. If more are added than finished, the pile is growing, and the card says so;
  • Open and overdue: how many tasks were open, and how many were past their due date, at the end of each week;
  • By person: for Jacques, Diane and unassigned work, open, overdue, no date, waiting on Jacques, completed, on-time rate, average days to finish and the age of the oldest open task;
  • By client: the same numbers per client, worst first (most overdue, then most open).

Every number and chart point can be clicked. It opens the list of tasks behind it, and clicking a task in that list opens it. The By person and By client tables have a period picker: last 7, 30 or 90 days, this month or last month. "On time" means completed on or before the due date; only tasks that had a due date count. Clicking the on-time figure lists the ones finished late.

Each client page has a Report card under its open work, with the same numbers for that client, a period picker and its own added-vs-completed chart. Print / PDF opens a clean page with what was completed in the period, what's coming up in the next 14 days and what's still open, ready to print or save as a PDF. It uses the task titles as written, so read it before sending it to a client.

Two limits: the open-and-overdue history uses each task's current due date (earlier date changes aren't replayed), and the trends only start from when work went into the planner (10 November 2026), so the earlier weeks read zero.

History lists every update with a photo, a time and a link to the task. Filter it by person, by type (work completed, comments, hand-overs, approvals answered, date changes, new tasks) or by client. A summary at the top shows how many tasks each of you completed in the last seven days.

Recurring tasks

Some work comes round again: the daily ReviewTap orders, a weekly report, month-end invoicing. Make that a recurring task once instead of retyping it.

  • Open the task and choose an option under Repeats: daily, every weekday, weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, quarterly or yearly.
  • For weekly, tick the days it should land on (for example Monday and Thursday).
  • When you mark it complete, the next one appears straight away with the same owner, client, labels and links. The panel tells you the next date before you tick it off.
  • Monthly tasks keep their day of the month. A task due on the 31st lands on the last day of shorter months.
  • If a recurring task is late, completing it schedules the next one from its own pattern and skips any dates that have already passed. Missed dates don't pile up.
  • Rows show a ↻ chip with the pattern. To stop, set Repeats back to "Doesn't repeat".

Only the open copy is ever on the list. The completed ones stay in History.

Personal projects

Jacques and Diane each have a project called 🔒 Personal, at the top of the client list. Only its owner can see it. Use it for your own errands and reminders.

  • What stays private: the tasks, their comments, files and history, and any wiki page filed under that project (with every page inside it). The other person doesn't see them in any list, search, calendar, report, Inbox or email, and opening a link to one shows "not found".
  • ChatGPT and Claude follow the same rule, because each works with the key of the person using it.
  • Tasks stay yours. A personal task can't be handed over, assigned to the other person or sent to Jacques for approval, and an @mention on it doesn't reach them. To share one, move it to another project first. It then becomes visible like any other task.
  • What it doesn't cover: the database owner. Jacques owns the Supabase project, so he (and Claude, working for him) could still read the database directly. Treat Personal like a private folder on a company drive: hidden from your colleague, not from the admin.
  • Keep truly sensitive things (finances, health, passwords) out of the planner altogether.

Handing work over

Every task shows its owner as a coloured pill: Jacques Diane. Tap the pill to hand the task over.

  1. Pick who it goes to.
  2. Optionally write one line they need to know, for example "Draft it, I'll approve before it goes".
  3. Tap Hand over. The task moves to their list with a New for you flag and your note at the top.

The page records who handed it over and when, so later you can see where a task came from. Nothing is reassigned by accident. The pill opens a choice; it never cycles on a stray tap.

Who owns whatDiane decides on sending anything already approved, chasing any invoice or overdue commitment, client updates on delivery dates, and closing a task once she has tested it end to end. Jacques decides on pricing, scope, contracts and anything that moves money out of the business. This follows Diane's role charter, and the planner's first load was split along this line.

Approvals: Diane prepares, Jacques says yes

Most of what used to sit on Jacques's list was paperwork that only needed his yes. The planner splits those into two halves. Diane does the work. Jacques answers one line.

To ask for an approval, open the task and set Needs Jacques to Approve, Decide or Sign. Write the question so it can be answered in a few words, add the rand value if there is one, and save. The task stays in Diane's name.

Jacques sees the question in the Needs Jacques lane. He can type an answer or a condition ("R9,499 now, rest at go-live") and tap Approve, or Send answer for a decision. His answer is written into the task's notes with the date, and the task goes back to Diane as New for you.

Waiting and chasing

Lots of client work stalls on the client: an access invite, a logo, a yes. Mark those Waiting and set a Next chase date. Until that date the task stays folded away under "Waiting, next chase later". On the chase date it comes back into Today on its own.

After each nudge, push the next chase date forward three to five days. A Diane-owned waiting task shows as "Chase" and a Jacques-owned one as "Waiting", because for her it's an action and for him it's a blocker.

The weekly rhythm

  • Every morning, five minutes each. Open on Mine. Clear New for you. Jacques clears the Needs Jacques lane. Work Overdue, then Today.
  • Mondays and Thursdays, Diane. Open "Waiting, next chase later" on Everyone. Chase whatever is due, then move each chase date forward.
  • Fridays, Diane. In By client, check every card has nothing overdue and that anything finished was actually sent and tested.
  • Month start, Diane. Filter on Billing and run the invoicing sweep from the cards' "Next bill" lines.

Where the data comes from and how it stays fresh

Claude filled the planner by reading:

  • every client folder's agreements, invoices, proposals and handover notes;
  • the invoice registers;
  • the existing waiting-on and board files;
  • two months of Jacques's email.

Where those sources disagreed, the newer one won and the conflict was written into the task's notes.

Through the connector, any Claude session can be asked to "update the planner from my email", "add what came out of today's call with the client" or "close everything the bank shows as paid". The changes appear on both your screens within seconds, signed with the name of whoever's key was used.

One list, not fourMission Control already tracks work in the action board, the waiting-on register and the scoreboard. If all of them keep tracking client work, they will drift apart. The reviewers recommended this split:
  • The planner becomes the one record for client tasks.
  • The waiting-on register keeps only non-client dependencies, such as the accountant, Payfast and suppliers.
  • The action board keeps internal and product work.
  • The morning brief, the chase routine and the session wrap-up read from and write to the planner.
That change is waiting on Jacques's go-ahead.

How it was tested

Before the planner went to Diane, three reviewers worked through it separately, each with the code and data but not each other's conclusions:

  • a usability reviewer, playing a Monday morning as each user;
  • a chief-of-staff reviewer, asking whether it actually takes work off Jacques;
  • an auditor, cross-checking every task against the source files.
5/10Day-to-day usefulness, first version
3 → 7Load taken off Jacques, before and after the fixes
5/10Tracking accuracy, first version

Changes made from their findings:

  • A Mine default view, plus a Today group and a group for high-priority work with no date.
  • Hand-overs now ask who it goes to and record a note, instead of cycling owners on a tap. Diane gets a New for you flag.
  • The Needs Jacques lane with an answer box, so roughly 20 clerical items moved to Diane with a one-tap approval left for Jacques.
  • Waiting tasks got chase dates and fold away until they're due.
  • Detail text is clamped to two lines, client facts sit behind a fold, and tap targets are bigger on phones.
  • Saving a task only writes the fields that changed, so two people editing at once don't overwrite each other.
  • Duplicates were merged, one wrongly described payment was corrected, an invoice date was aligned with the onboarding plan, and about ten missing items were added (small debts, unconfirmed bookings, a never-sent pro forma).

After the fixes, Jacques owns about 26 open tasks and Diane about 72, many of them chases. If her list proves too long, the first thing to change is the chase dates, not the owners.

Known limits

  • Planner emails (digest and alerts) are paused until Jacques switches them back on. The Inbox works meanwhile.
  • Comments have no editing or deleting yet, so check a comment before you send it.
  • Your key is remembered per browser. On a new device, paste it again.
  • Updates from the other person arrive within about 30 seconds, not instantly. Switching back to the tab refreshes at once.
  • The database lives in the Supabase organisation that also holds the Die Veiling Klub project. Only Jacques's Supabase login can see it.
  • The first version of the planner, a claude.ai page, is retired. Its link now points here.
