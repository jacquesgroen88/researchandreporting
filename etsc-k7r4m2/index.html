What we found on etsc.co.za

Your website was built in 2018. The business has moved on.

None of this is a criticism of the people who built it. These are the things a SHEQ manager, an auditor or Google notices today.

Your terms of service belong to a coffee shop

Every enquiry must accept terms that talk about coffee "roasted per order", courier returns and US Pacific support hours. There is no POPIA notice.

No accreditation numbers anywhere

For a compliance trainer this is the first thing a buyer looks for. Two Gauteng competitors already show theirs, with prices and certificate checks.

The site software is past end of life

Joomla 3.8.4 stopped receiving security updates in 2023. The admin login is public and an old image script with a known exploit history is exposed.

Enquiries arrive without what you need to quote

There are three separate enquiry paths, and none asks for company, head count, site, dates or new versus re-licensing. Every lead needs a call-back.

2019 prices and 2018 offers are still live

R1,000 forklift training, a "July only" 5% code and an "up to 50% discount" banner are still on the home page.

Courses with no detail

All of your published courses are listed, but none shows duration, class size, what the learner receives or how long the card is valid, so buyers cannot self-qualify.

Nothing has been measured since 2023

The site still runs Universal Analytics, which Google switched off in July 2023. There is no way to know where enquiries come from.

Google can barely read it

No H1 on the home page, headings and even the phone number are images, and there is no sitemap or business schema.

Catalogue errors

C37/C38 and C39/C40 have identical names, the MEWP shares a code with hoists, and a package page still carries test data ("toets").

Your new brand is not on your site

The Facebook cover says "Building safer workplaces". The website still looks like 2018. We built the concept on your new identity.

What is changing around you

The paperwork is about to get heavier, not lighter.

QCTO accreditation by 30 June 2027

After that date every lifting machine training provider must be QCTO accredited, and legacy SETA qualifications stop being certified. Records need to be clean and electronic before then.

The 2024 Code of Practice sets hard limits

Up to 12 learners per facilitator for theory, 4 per machine for practical, one-on-one assessment for re-licensing, and learner records submitted electronically every quarter.

About 15 required fields on every certificate

Machine code, capacity, restrictions, attachments, assessor registration and more. Retyping them by hand is where upload rejections and delays start.

Fake certificates are sold openly

Online classifieds advertise operator "renewals" without training. A QR code that anyone can check sets your certificates apart.
