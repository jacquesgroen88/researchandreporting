How it works

One booking, start to finish

This is the path every job takes. Today it runs on phone calls, email, WhatsApp and paper. In KeyOps each step hands over to the next one.

1

A client asks

The request comes in from the website with the course, number of people and site already filled in.

2

You quote

The price works itself out. The client accepts online and uploads their purchase order.

3

You train

The trainer is booked with a machine. Learners sign in on the trainer's phone and are assessed there.

4

You certify

Once the moderator signs off, certificates, carry cards and the invoice go out together.

5

They come back

Two years later, before the cards run out, the client gets a reminder and a ready quote.

Your current website

Three things to fix first

We went through every page of etsc.co.za. Most of what we found is normal for a site built in 2018, but these three can lose you work.

The terms of service are a coffee shop's

Anyone who sends an enquiry has to accept terms about coffee "roasted per order", courier returns and US support hours. There's no POPIA notice.

Fixed in the new site

No accreditation numbers

It's the first thing a safety manager checks. Two Gauteng competitors already show theirs, along with prices and a certificate check.

Fixed in the new site

The site software is out of date

It runs Joomla 3.8.4, which stopped getting security updates in 2023. The admin login page is public.

Fixed in the new site
Seven smaller things we found
  • Enquiries miss what you need to quote. None of the three enquiry forms asks for company, head count, site or dates, so every lead needs a call back.
  • Old prices are still showing. R1,000 forklift training from 2019, a 2018 "July only" code and an "up to 50% off" banner.
  • Courses have no detail. No duration, class size or card validity, so buyers can't tell which course they need.
  • Nothing is being measured. The site uses Google Universal Analytics, which stopped working in July 2023.
  • Google struggles to read it. Headings and even the phone number are images, and there is no sitemap.
  • A few catalogue mistakes. C37 and C38 share a name, as do C39 and C40, and one package page still has test text on it.
  • Your new brand isn't there. Facebook says "Building safer workplaces". The website still looks like 2018.
30 June2027

The QCTO deadline

After this date every lifting machine training provider has to be accredited by the QCTO, and certificates under the old SETA qualifications stop being issued. Providers with clean, electronic learner records will find the move much easier. KeyOps keeps those records as you work.
