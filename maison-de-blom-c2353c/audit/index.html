The short version

Your guests love the place, and say so wherever they are asked. The problem is that few of them are asked, so the review count stays small, and the searches travellers use don't put you on the first screen. Your website and listings look good but don't show the proof, the price or a reason to book with you directly.

Working well

  • Every review is five stars or close to it
  • Direct booking already works online through NightsBridge, and Google shows it as the "Official site"
  • Owner replies on most LekkeSlaap reviews
  • 46 photos on Google, and strong photography on the website
  • A clear, well-titled homepage: "Luxury Self-Catering Cottages in Aliwal North"

Costing you bookings

  • Only 16 Google reviews. Competitors have 152 and 170
  • Four extra unrated Maison de Blom entries on Google Maps
  • No rating, review or price anywhere on your website
  • On a phone, no Book button and no WhatsApp button in the first screen
  • Booking.com pays to sit above your own site, at the same price

Quickest wins

  • Ask every guest for a Google review after checkout
  • Merge or remove the duplicate Google entries
  • Put "4.9 on Google" and three guest quotes on the homepage
  • Add a direct-booking perk so your site beats Booking.com
  • A WhatsApp button that stays on screen on mobile

Google Maps, "accommodation in Aliwal North", 30 October 2026. Even The Adante, with a 2.5 from two reviews, appears above you.

Google ranks local places on relevance, distance and prominence, and the number and freshness of reviews is a large part of prominence. Your rating is the best in town. The count is what's holding you back.

What to do

  1. Ask every guest for a Google review by WhatsApp after checkout, with a direct link to the review form. Every guest gets the link; anyone who scores 3 or lower also gets a private message first.
  2. Reply to every Google review within a day, and go back to the older ones still without a reply.
  3. Clean up the four duplicate entries. They are most likely room listings from a booking site's feed. Report them as duplicates on Google Maps, and check how the property and its rooms are set up on Booking.com so they stop being pulled in as separate places.
  4. Complete the profile: a description that uses "stopover", "self-catering" and "pet-friendly", all amenities (fire pits, braai, Netflix, secure parking, pets by arrangement, breakfast packs if you offer them), and a regular Google post.

What to do

  1. Show the proof on the homepage: "4.9 on Google · 5.0 on LekkeSlaap", three short guest quotes, and "from R1,300 a night".
  2. On mobile, put a Book button and a WhatsApp button on screen at all times, and open with a cottage photo.
  3. Give a reason to book direct: a small saving or a perk (free firewood, a late checkout, a breakfast pack). Since Booking.com's 2024 settlement with the Competition Commission, South African properties are free to price lower on their own websites.
  4. Use the word "stopover" on the homepage and in titles, along with self-catering and pet-friendly.
  5. Fix the cottage pages: a proper title, heading and description each, with the bed type and price.
  6. Add lodging structured data (address, rating, price range, amenities), alt text on photos, and tidy the sitemap.
  7. Add Google Analytics so you can see where bookings come from.

What to do

  1. Use one set of names everywhere, ideally Cottage 1 to 4 with a short description, so guests know which cottage they booked and Google stops creating duplicates.
  2. List each unit as a self-catering cottage, not a room in a guesthouse, on LekkeSlaap and Booking.com.

  4. Consider a free Tripadvisor listing: it's one of the sources ChatGPT and Google's AI read when travellers ask where to stay.
