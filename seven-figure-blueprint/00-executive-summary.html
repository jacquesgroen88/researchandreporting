The business is built for bulk and sold one unit at a time

Everything in this Blueprint follows from a single number: the average ReviewTap order contains 1.9 units. The margin structure, the product catalogue, the customer list and the search demand all point at multi-unit buyers. The acquisition machine does not.

Prepared by JCE Media for ReviewTap · 8 August 2026 · Built on live Shopify, Google Ads, Meta and pipeline data

The diagnosis in one paragraph

ReviewTap sells a product with very high gross margins on both cards and stands, to a market where the natural buying unit is a fleet, not a single item. Yet 93% of its orders are under R2,500, its entire paid acquisition is built to sell one stand to one owner-operator, and the search term attached to its highest-value product is switched off as a negative keyword. Meanwhile 6.7% of orders have quietly produced 31% of all revenue, franchise groups are buying branch by branch with nobody calling head office, and the single largest inbound in the store's history arrived unprompted last week.

The core finding

55 orders produced R299,458

Out of 817 lifetime orders, the 55 worth R2,500 or more account for roughly 31% of all revenue from 6.7% of orders. The top ten orders alone produced R131,934, about 14% of lifetime revenue from 1.2% of transactions. This cohort was not marketed to, sold to, or followed up. It happened by accident.

The Custom Smart Business Card is the highest revenue-per-order product in the catalogue and the only one that is natively bulk-shaped, because business cards are bought per employee rather than per premises. It did twelve orders in a year. The term nfc business card converted at a 33% click-through rate in the Google Ads account and is currently excluded as a negative keyword.

The four moves

Rebuild the offer around the fleet, not the unit

Introduce a published volume ladder, a branded-fleet package for multi-site groups, and free delivery above R2,500 so the courier fee stops killing exactly the orders worth winning. Bulk discounting is nearly free here: a 30% discount on a 50-unit stand order still returns about 78% gross margin.

Sell to head office, not to the branch

a national beauty franchise has already bought four separate times through three different franchisee email addresses. a national QSR group bought 22 stands in a single transaction. a national estate agency franchise and a retail services franchise each bought one branch's worth. Nobody has ever called a franchisor. Named-account outbound to franchise groups, hospitality groups and dealer networks is the highest-value unbuilt motion in the business.

Turn the search channel back on and point it at bulk

Google returned 13x blended ROAS on R535 of spend in thirty days against a budget it never delivered against. Real bulk intent is already showing up in the search terms report and going unanswered, along with an entire informational cluster the site has no pages for. Reinstate the smart-card keyword, build the bulk and industry pages that do not exist, and fund the only channel that has ever paid.

Build the reseller rail

An agency put three custom stands in a cart. Marketing firms and print shops appear repeatedly in the order history. At R3 landed cost on a card, a white-label reseller tier costs nothing to serve and turns competitors into distribution. Every international peer that scaled did it this way.

Why the recurring line matters most

The business currently rests on R15,548 of transferable monthly recurring revenue. Contracted recurring revenue is worth a multiple of its annualised value, while one-off hardware revenue is worth roughly what it earned. So the base case above does not just add revenue, it roughly doubles the recurring line, and that is the part that changes what the business is worth rather than just what it takes home this year. It is also why the group motion is weighted far above its rand contribution throughout this plan.

What we are deliberately not doing

  • Not competing on card price. Seven South African sellers are already racing to the bottom on hardware. ReviewTap wins by selling a managed system across a group of locations, which none of them offer.
  • Not changing single-unit shipping. That decision stands. The threshold applies to bulk only.
  • Not scaling cold Meta prospecting. It has run at roughly 1.2x in-account. It stays as a warm and creative-testing layer, not the growth engine.
  • Not touching the review-integrity line. Search demand exists for review bots and paid reviews. Refusing that demand publicly is a positioning asset for exactly the corporate buyers this plan targets.
