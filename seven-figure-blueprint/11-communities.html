Communities: where these buyers actually gather

Every ICP in doc 04 congregates somewhere specific. Associations with published member bases, one trade show that puts eight thousand of the right people in one building, and a set of online groups where a product link gets you removed within the hour. This document names them and sets out how to enter each one without getting thrown out.

Associations and events below are verified. Online groups are given with a discovery method rather than invented names, because group names change and a wrong name wastes a week.
The rule that governs this entire document

Communities are not a distribution channel for product links. Every group listed here will remove a member who posts one, and the removal is permanent. The only entry that works is answering questions that are already being asked, for months, before mentioning what you sell. Budget three months of participation before expecting anything. If that sounds slow, note that it is still faster than the six to nine months SEO takes, and it produces relationships rather than clicks.

How to approach an association

Join as a supplier member first

Pay the fee, get the listing, appear in the directory. This alone delivers the backlink and the citable source.

Offer a member benefit, not a sales pitch

Preferential pricing for members costs almost nothing at ReviewTap's margins and gives the association something to announce. Associations exist to deliver member value and are actively looking for benefits to add.

Contribute content to their channels

Most associations run a newsletter and are permanently short of good material. "What your members' Google ratings actually look like, benchmarked" is a piece any hospitality or franchise association would run, and it is a genuinely useful analysis rather than an advert.

Then, and only then, ask about the member list

By this point you are a contributing supplier member with a benefit on offer, not a cold vendor asking for data.

Do not exhibit in year one. Attend, walk the floor, and have thirty prepared conversations with groups whose ratings you have already pulled. A stand costs a great deal and generates browsers. Second reason to attend: the exhibitor list is a ready-made ICP 6 prospect list. Every exhibiting company is a team that is about to need business cards.

Tier 3: Online communities

Group names on Facebook and LinkedIn change, merge and die, so a fixed list would be stale within months. What follows is the discovery method plus the community types that matter, which stays accurate.

Discovery method

Facebook: search the group directory for each of these, sorted by member count, and join the three largest per category:
South Africa small business owners · SA restaurant owners · Guest house owners South Africa · SA franchise owners · Practice managers South Africa · SA salon owners · Plumbers South Africa · Security industry South Africa · Estate agents South Africa · SA marketing agencies

LinkedIn: search groups for South Africa hospitality, SA franchising, South African marketing, practice management. LinkedIn groups are quieter than Facebook but the members are more senior, which suits ICP 1 and 3.

WhatsApp: the highest-value and hardest to enter. Access comes through existing customers, not through search. Ask satisfied customers which industry groups they are in and whether an introduction is appropriate. a boutique guesthouse, a plumbing contractor and a national security firm are the natural first asks.

Reddit: r/southafrica and r/PersonalFinanceZA are large but hostile to promotion. Useful for listening and for occasional genuinely helpful answers, not for acquisition.

The unexpected benefit

The listening phase is worth doing even if nothing is ever sold through these groups. The exact wording people use for this problem, in their own language, is what makes the industry pages in doc 07 rank and the ad copy in doc 09 land. Two weeks of reading produces better messaging research than any amount of persona work.

Owned community: the one worth building

Not a priority for the first quarter, but worth naming because it changes the business's position from supplier to authority.

A quarterly SA review benchmark report

ReviewTap sees review capture across hundreds of South African businesses. Nobody publishes what good looks like by sector. A quarterly report showing average rating and review volume by industry (restaurants, lodges, dealerships, practices, trades) with regional splits would be genuinely novel, and it does three jobs: associations and trade press will run it, it earns links and citations, and it makes ReviewTap the source rather than a vendor. Data is anonymised and aggregated, never per-customer.
