What separates the cases

Conservative assumes the offer work ships but outbound is executed only intermittently, at roughly one group account per month. Base assumes nine hours a week of outbound sustained for twelve months. Aggressive assumes the same plus a second person selling from month four, plus one franchise network landing at scale. The single variable that moves this model most is whether outbound is done consistently, not whether the pages are pretty.

The cash caveat that always applies

Contribution is not cash in the bank. Growth consumes stock ahead of demand, and this plan explicitly requires restocking three products in week one. Expect R20,000 to R35,000 a month of that contribution to be reinvested in inventory while scaling. Separately, advertising hits the card daily while the payment processor settles two to three days later, so the binding constraint on scaling spend is float, not margin. Never step a budget up from a return figure alone.

Why the recurring line is the one to watch

The structural point

Recurring revenue is worth a multiple of its annual value. One-off hardware revenue is worth roughly what it earned.

The base case adds R114,000 of monthly revenue and R14,200 of monthly recurring revenue. The first number pays the bills this year. The second one changes what the business is worth, because contracted, transferable recurring revenue is valued as a multiple of its annualised figure while transactional revenue largely is not.

That is why lever B (group outbound) is weighted far above its rand contribution in this plan, and why every group deal is structured as hardware plus a monthly programme rather than as a large one-off order. A R55,000 hardware sale is a good month. A R55,000 hardware sale attached to a R9,500 monthly contract is a different business.

The honest summary

What is well supported

  • Bulk demand exists and is measurable: 55 orders, R299,458, 31% of lifetime revenue
  • Franchise demand is proved inside four networks already
  • The margin structure genuinely supports the discount ladder
  • Google returns are real even when halved for the tracking correction
  • Fulfilment at volume has already been run end to end

What is assumption, not evidence

  • Two group accounts per month. Nobody has run this motion yet.
  • Six active partners. The channel does not exist today.
  • 4% monthly reactivation. Reasonable, but untested here.
  • Organic revenue by month 5. SEO timelines are notoriously optimistic.
  • Margins holding at double the revenue

The conservative case is what happens if most of the assumptions disappoint and only the offer work ships: R58,000 of incremental monthly revenue and R7,000 of monthly recurring revenue, against about R305,000 of twelve-month cost. That case is worth executing on its own terms, which is the real test of whether a plan is sound.
