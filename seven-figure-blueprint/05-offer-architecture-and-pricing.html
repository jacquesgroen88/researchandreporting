Why the ladder is nearly free

A 40% volume discount still returns above 80% gross margin

At R649 list and a low all-in cost, a custom stand discounted 40% sells at R389 and still returns a high gross margin. A custom card discounted 40% sells at R269 against a low unit cost and remains highly profitable. Most businesses cannot discount because their cost base will not allow it. ReviewTap can discount aggressively enough to win a hundred-unit group order and barely feel it. Not having a published ladder is not protecting margin. It is refusing volume it could easily afford.

The shipping decision

Scoped exactly as agreed

Free delivery above R2,500. Nothing changes below it. The standing decision not to alter single-unit shipping stands and is not reopened. On a R424 single-card order the R125 courier fee is 29% of the order and giving it away is real money. On a R2,500 order it is 5% against a a high gross margin, which is nothing.

The threshold does two jobs at once. It removes the friction from exactly the carts worth winning, and it becomes an upsell mechanic: a customer sitting at R1,900 gets told they are R600 from free delivery, which is one more stand. This turns the largest single checkout leak in the business into a reason to add units.

Display rule: show the progress explicitly in cart ("Add R600 for free delivery"), not as fine print. The threshold only works if the buyer sees the gap.

The Branded Fleet package

The ladder handles quantity. It does not handle the thing a franchise or hospitality group is actually buying, which is a programme rather than a box of hardware. This is the offer that does not exist today and it is the one that unlocks ICP 1 and ICP 2.

The commercial point of the fleet package

Hardware is the entry, not the product. A 60-outlet group generates roughly R55,000 of one-time hardware revenue and R114,000 a year of recurring revenue. Because contracted recurring revenue is valued as a multiple of its annualised figure, that single account's recurring line is worth far more than the hardware order that opened it. This is why doc 16 scores every move on recurring revenue and not just on cash.

The reseller tier

Built directly on the models that already work in this category: NFC Tagify (no minimum order, white-label packaging), wCard.io (partner sets their own end price), a white-label operator (published volume ladder aimed at print shops), TapTag (fast turnaround as the headline promise).

The rule that makes partners trust it: neutral or white-label shipping by default, so the end client does not discover ReviewTap and go direct next time. This is the single most common reason resellers refuse to promote a supplier, and it costs nothing to solve.

Where the partner's real money is: the recurring system tier, not the hardware. A partner reselling RT Pro at R399 per client per month across fifteen clients has built a R6,000 monthly annuity that they did not have to build a product for. Lead the partner pitch with that, not with the card discount.

How hardware feeds recurring revenue

Hardware is the trojan horse, not the business

Every unit shipped is a business that now has a working review capture point and a visible number going up. That is the only proof that matters, and it is generated by a product with 90% margin.

RT Pro at R399 is the natural next step

Once reviews are arriving, the customer discovers they now have replies to write, sentiment to watch and a rating to defend. R399 a month is a small yes for someone who has just watched their review count double.

Growth at R5,999 and Scale at R9,500 are for groups

Multi-location management, response handling and group reporting are worth an order of magnitude more than single-site tooling, because the buyer is protecting a brand rather than a shop.

The trigger to upsell is measurable, so automate it

Any account whose review volume has doubled since installation is ready for the system conversation. That is a query, not a hunch, and it should fire automatically at 60 days.

What we are not changing

  • Single-unit shipping. Unchanged, as agreed.
  • List prices. The ladder discounts from current list. Nothing gets cheaper for the buyer who is already converting happily at R649.
  • No blanket discount codes. Discounts are earned by volume, published as a structure, and never negotiated ad hoc. A ladder that can be argued down is not a ladder.
  • No competing on the price of a chip. Six local sellers are racing to the bottom. The ladder exists to win volume, not to undercut retail.
