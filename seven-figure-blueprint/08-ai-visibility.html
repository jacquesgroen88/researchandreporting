AI visibility and answer engine optimisation

Group buyers research vendors before they call. Increasingly that research happens inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google AI Overviews rather than a results page. ReviewTap wins on one of those and is invisible on another, and the reason is an entity problem rather than a content problem.

The root cause

The brand name collides with five same-name companies abroad

"ReviewTap" is not a unique entity on the open web. At least five unrelated companies share the name in other markets. When an assistant is asked about ReviewTap it has to disambiguate between them, and when it is asked about review cards in South Africa it has no strong signal tying this company to this country and this category. The fix is entity disambiguation, not publishing more content. Writing another twenty blog posts against an ambiguous entity mostly produces twenty more ambiguous signals.

Why this matters commercially, not theoretically

The buyers this Blueprint targets are exactly the buyers who research before contact. A franchise marketing manager evaluating a group rollout, a practice manager comparing suppliers, an operations director shortlisting three vendors. The question they ask is not "what is ReviewTap", it is "who supplies Google review cards for multiple locations in South Africa". If the answer names three competitors and not ReviewTap, the deal is lost before an enquiry exists, and it never shows up in any analytics report.

The fix, in order

Disambiguate the entity

Every signal must tie ReviewTap to South Africa and to this category. Organization schema with correct sameAs links pointing at the real socials (review_tap, not the incorrect reviewtapsa currently in circulation), the Google Business Profile, and the registered entity. Consistent name, address and phone everywhere. Explicit geo signals on the site. An "About ReviewTap" page that states plainly what the company is, where it operates and since when, because that is the paragraph an assistant will quote.

Become citable by third parties

Perplexity's invisibility is a sourcing problem. It cites what other credible sites say about you, and almost nothing exists. The fixes are association member directories (FEDHASA, TBCSA, RMI, FASA), local business directories, industry publication commentary on the franchise inquiry, and customer case studies published on the customers' own sites. This work overlaps almost entirely with the link acquisition in doc 07 and the association work in doc 11, so it costs very little extra.

Write pages that answer the question directly

Assistants extract answers, they do not read brochures. Every hub and guide page must open with a 40 to 60 word direct answer to the question in its title, followed by specifics: price, quantity, turnaround, coverage. FAQ blocks worded exactly as people ask. The /reviews-integrity/ page from doc 06 doubles as the canonical answer to the compliance questions, which are among the most asked in this category.

Publish the comparison content

Assistants asked to compare options need something to compare. The alternatives pages in doc 07 (Popl, V1CE, Mobilo, and the local commodity tier) give the model something to cite, and comparison pages convert well with human buyers too. Write them honestly, including where a competitor is genuinely the better fit, because that is what gets cited rather than dismissed.

Feed the structured signals

Product schema with real prices and the verified 4.9 rating, FAQPage on every hub, HowTo on the setup guides, BreadcrumbList across the new architecture. Structured data is disproportionately useful to answer engines because it removes ambiguity about what is being claimed.

What not to do

  • Do not rename the company. The entity collision is real but the brand has 817 orders of recognition behind it in this market. Disambiguation is cheaper and does not destroy existing equity.
  • Do not mass-produce thin content. Twenty shallow posts against an ambiguous entity make the ambiguity worse. Twelve strong industry pages and five real guides beat fifty posts.
  • Do not chase every assistant equally. ChatGPT is already won and should be monitored, not optimised. Perplexity is the gap and it responds to citations. Spend the effort there.
  • Do not treat this as separate from SEO. Roughly 80% of the work is the same work. Budget it once.
