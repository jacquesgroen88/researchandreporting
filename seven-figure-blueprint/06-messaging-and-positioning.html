06 · Market and ICP

Messaging and positioning

ReviewTap currently sounds like a product company selling a gadget. To win group buyers it has to sound like a company that fixes a business problem across many locations. This document sets the position, the narrative, the proof stack, the voice rules and the messaging for each ICP, and removes three claims that cannot survive scrutiny.

Every proof point listed here is verifiable. Nothing in this document may be used if it cannot be evidenced.

The positioning statement

The one line everything hangs off

South Africa's review system for businesses with more than one location

Anyone can sell a card that opens a Google review page. ReviewTap is the only South African company that runs review capture, review response and review reporting across a whole group of locations, so head office can see which branch is delivering the experience the brand is promising.

That sentence does three jobs. It concedes the commodity fight rather than pretending to win it. It names the buyer (a group, not a shop). And it names the thing the buyer actually wants, which is visibility, not reviews.

The core narrative

Used in full on the website, compressed in ads, and as the spine of every outbound conversation.

Every business already earns the reviews it never gets. A guest checks out happy. A patient leaves relieved. A customer drives off in a car they love. In that moment they would have said something good, and almost none of them do, because the moment passes before anyone asks.

Then the next customer goes looking. They compare three businesses on Google before they call any of them, and the one with 140 reviews gets the call ahead of the one with 11. Not because it is better. Because it looks safer.

So the gap between what you deserve and what you show is costing you customers every day, silently.

ReviewTap closes that gap at the moment it opens. A card in your team's hand or a stand where the customer already is. One tap, four seconds, straight to your real Google page. No app, no forms, no incentives, nothing that puts your listing at risk.

And when you have more than one location, we run it as one programme. Every branch capturing, every review answered, one dashboard showing which sites are pulling the brand up and which are pulling it down.

Three claims to remove today

"4.8 out of 5 stars" is unverified and currently appears in three live ad creatives. The real, verifiable figure is 4.9 across 29 reviews. Use that.
"1,000+ South African businesses" is not supported by 817 lifetime orders across fewer unique businesses. Replace with "over 800 orders since 2025", which is true and sounds no weaker.
"One refund ever across 729 orders" cannot be used as a financial claim. Shopify records R948 of returns in July 2026 and R499 in June. The exchange-versus-refund story is defensible in conversation but it must never appear as a headline statistic, least of all in front of a serious counterparty who will pull the returns column.

There is also an internal contradiction to fix: the "Then vs Now" static shows four reviews in one panel and twelve in the other.

The integrity position

The search terms report shows real demand for 5 star google review bot, paid google reviews and gmb review provider. That demand should be refused, publicly and in writing, because it is worth more as positioning than as revenue.

The line, verbatim

"We never gate, filter, incentivise or generate reviews. Every customer who taps a ReviewTap device lands on your real Google review page and writes whatever they want. We remove friction, not selection. If a supplier is offering you reviews rather than a way to ask for them, they are putting your listing at risk, and no rating is worth losing your Google presence over."

This matters commercially, not just ethically. A franchisor rolling anything out across 200 outlets will ask the compliance question, and being the vendor with an unambiguous written answer wins that deal. It is also the cleanest available differentiator against the commodity tier, none of whom address it at all.

One nuance to get right

Review Shield routes unhappy customers to a private channel before the review page. That is legitimate service recovery and it is how it must be described. It becomes review gating the moment happy customers are given a different path to Google than unhappy ones. Keep the description precise: everyone can reach Google, and unhappy customers are additionally offered a private route first. Never describe it as filtering.

Voice

Do

  • Lead with the customer's problem, in their language
  • Use real numbers, always sourced
  • Short sentences. One idea each.
  • Concede what is true ("any capture system raises volume on both sides")
  • One ask per message
  • Give the reader an easy exit ("if the timing is wrong, tell me and I will stop")

Do not

  • No em dashes, anywhere, ever
  • Never say "GHL" or "GoHighLevel" to a customer. It is the Reviewtap System.
  • No unverifiable statistics
  • No hype adjectives (revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge)
  • No fake scarcity
  • No talking about NFC chips before talking about the problem
The tone test

Read it back as though you are the operations director of a 60-outlet group who gets nine supplier emails a day. If it sounds like a supplier, rewrite it. If it sounds like someone who has already looked at your ratings, it is right.

The five objections that appear everywhere

ObjectionAnswer
"Will this get us in trouble with Google?"No, and here is why in writing. Nothing is gated, incentivised or filtered. Every customer reaches the real review page. We remove friction, not selection.
"What if it brings in bad reviews too?"It will bring in more of both, and that is the honest answer. Volume dilutes: forty new reviews at 4.8 moves an average far more than one bad one drags it. And we can route unhappy customers to a private channel first so you hear it before Google does.
"Our staff will not use it"Mostly they do not have to. The stand sits where the customer already is. Where staff are involved it takes four seconds and we supply the one-line script.
"We can buy cards cheaper elsewhere"You can, and if a card is all you want then buy the cheap one. What you cannot buy elsewhere is the setup, the per-location destinations, the response handling and the group dashboard. The card is the cheapest part of what we do.
"We tried QR codes and nobody scanned"Because a QR code on a poster asks the customer to do work. A card handed over by the person who just served them is a completely different moment, and every unit carries both NFC and a QR fallback.
