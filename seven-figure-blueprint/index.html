The Seven Figure Blueprint

This is what a full marketing strategy engagement produces. Nineteen documents, built on live revenue, advertising and search data rather than on assumptions, ending in a dated project plan somebody can actually execute on Monday morning.

About this version

This is a real engagement, published as a worked example with the client's permission. Customer names and email addresses have been replaced with sector descriptors, and certain commercial inputs (supplier costs, unit margins, valuation figures) are withheld. Everything else is exactly as delivered: the analysis, the reasoning, the frameworks and the level of detail.

What the engagement found

The brief was to sell more smart business cards and win more bulk orders. The data said the business already had both and was suppressing them.

55
Orders produced 31% of all lifetime revenue, and nobody had ever looked at the distribution
2.1x
The highest revenue-per-order product earned twice the flagship, and got 12 orders in a year
4
Franchise networks had already bought at branch level. No head office had ever been called.
1
Negative keyword was blocking the entry point to the best product

How this was built

Evidence, not assumptions

Every figure was pulled live: order-level revenue data, the advertising accounts, the real search terms the business was paying for, verified supplier costs and the open pipeline. Where third-party keyword data was unavailable, the document says so and uses the business's own auction data instead, which is stronger evidence anyway.

Attackable, not decorative

The money model separates what is evidenced from what is assumed, and names the week the central thesis gets tested. A strategy that cannot be proved wrong is not a strategy. One of the findings corrected a rule of thumb the business had been using, in a direction that made the number smaller.
