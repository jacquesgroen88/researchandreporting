Paid channels and budget reallocation

ReviewTap spends the overwhelming majority of its advertising budget on the channel with the worst return and almost nothing on the channel with the best. This document reallocates it, rebuilds search around bulk and smart cards, and sets the rules that stop the same mistake recurring.

Google Ads · Meta · All figures re-verified 9 August 2026 against a full 60-day window, 10 June to 8 August 2026. The original 8 August draft carried short-window errors, corrected below and marked revised.
Read this before the rest of the document

As of 9 August 2026 ReviewTap's advertising is switched off. All 26 Meta campaigns are paused. Google non-brand search is paused, with its last spend on 4 August. The only live campaign in either account is Google Brand Search at R60 a day. The reallocation plan below is therefore not a rebalancing of a running system, it is a restart sequence.

The allocation problem, restated on verified data

91% of the budget sits in the 1.76x channel and 9% in the 3.51x channel

Google Search as a whole returned 3.51x across the 60 days on R5,487 of spend. Meta returned 1.76x on R55,384. The gap is real and the direction of the reallocation is unchanged, but it is a two-fold gap, not the six-fold gap the first draft described.

Revised What the original 7.04x and 40.97x actually were

The first draft reported non-brand at "R440 spend, 7.04x" and brand at "R95, 40.97x" over thirty days. Both figures are real numbers from the account, but neither is a thirty-day window.

R439.80 is exactly what non-brand spent across six days, 4 to 9 July — the campaign's first week. It did not exist before 4 July. Of the R3,095 of value in that window, R3,094 came from a single conversion on 9 July. R95.10 is exactly what brand spent across five days, 5 to 9 July, and its return rests on one order worth R4,395.

The consequence: the claim that Google "delivered R534.90 against a budget of roughly R108 a day and under-delivered by 84%" is an artifact of dividing a six-day spend by a thirty-day budget. Six days at R108 is R648, and R534.90 of that is 83% delivery, not 16%. There was no under-delivery problem. The non-brand budget is in fact R300 a day, not R108, and the campaign has since spent to it.

The finding that replaces it

Google non-brand recorded zero conversion value across its final twelve days. From 4 to 23 July it spent R2,132 and recorded R14,393 of value, a genuine 6.75x. From 24 July to 4 August it spent R2,938.74 across 83 clicks and recorded R0, then was paused.

The store was converting throughout that period — Shopify shows orders on 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 and 31 July and 3 and 4 August. A live store and a blank conversion column on the same dates points to the conversion tag breaking again after the 9 July double-count fix, not to the campaign failing. This must be diagnosed and proven before a single rand is reallocated, because every ROAS gate in the staging table below reads from that tag.

The bulk campaign will look inefficient on cost per click and it should be funded anyway. These queries are low volume and high value. One conversion is worth R2,500 to R20,000 against a R424 average. Judge it on enquiry value, never on cost per acquisition.

Structural rules for the search account

  • One ad group per intent cluster. Never mix formats, because the ad copy has to match the format the person asked for.
  • Every ad group points at the page built for that cluster in doc 07, not at the shop.
  • Sitelinks to the volume ladder and the multi-location enquiry on every campaign.
  • Negative keywords: free, bot, fake, buy reviews, paid reviews. The integrity position in doc 06 has to be enforced in the account, not just on the website.
  • Location targeting South Africa, with home and recent presence explicitly set.

Meta: what it is for now

Meta's job changes from being the growth engine to being the demand-capture and warm layer. The structure that exists (one broad prospecting campaign with pooled creative) is correct and should not be rebuilt again. What changes is the budget and the creative brief.

Revised Meta was understated in the first draft

The original figures were "~R22,500 at R750/day, ~1.2x in-account". Verified: Meta spent R20,161 in the last 30 days at roughly R650 a day, returning 1.86x in-account, and R55,384 over 60 days at 1.76x. Meta is a better channel than the first draft credited, though still well below Google. The weekly trend is the real story: spend fell steadily from R9,744 in the week of 10 June to R1,988 in the week of 5 August, and in-account return swung between 0.79x and 2.54x week to week. It was being wound down, not run at R750 a day.

Creative angles that are missing

The proven winners are time-saving framing (Time Counter, 2.16 to 2.6x), real testimonials (Hoffs, 2.25x warm) and social-proof statics (Static Orange, 3.21x warm but only 0.17% CTR cold, so warm-only). What does not exist is any creative addressed to someone buying more than one. Five briefs:

  1. The fleet shot. Twenty branded stands laid out together. "One for every branch." Speaks directly to ICP 1 and 2 and looks like nothing else in the feed.
  2. The team card. A sales team each holding their own smart card. "Everyone on your team, one tap." ICP 6.
  3. The technician handover. A plumber handing a card to a customer at a front door. ICP 5, and it is the most relatable review moment there is.
  4. The group dashboard. Rating by location on one screen. Speaks to the head-office buyer, who is not currently addressed by any asset.
  5. The refusal. "We will not sell you fake reviews." Contrarian, on-brand, and it separates ReviewTap from the commodity tier in one line.

Copy fixes required before any new creative ships

Three live creatives carry the unverified 4.8 star claim and the unsupported "1,000+ SA businesses" line. The "Then vs Now" static contradicts itself. Fix these first (doc 06), because scaling spend behind an unverifiable claim scales the risk with it.

Abandoned checkout recovery

Verified 101 abandoned checkouts across the 60 days to 8 August, roughly R80,000 of value. That is almost exactly the fifty a month and R40,000 a month the first draft estimated, and it is the one figure in this document that needed no correction. Standard recovery of 10 to 15% returns R4,000 to R6,000 monthly.

The store funnel over the same 60 days shows where the money actually leaks: 6,962 sessions, 461 added to cart, 408 reached checkout, 155 completed. Cart to checkout holds at 88.5%. Checkout to purchase is 38%, so 62% of people who reach the payment step leave. That is the R125 courier fee, and it is a far larger leak than the ad accounts.

  • Verify Shopify's built-in abandoned checkout email is actually on and firing.
  • Standing decision respected: recovery link and a human nudge only. No discount, no free-shipping re-pitch on single units.
  • Carts above R2,500 do not go into an email flow. They get a personal email from the owner, because a R5,115 cart is a sales conversation.
  • Dedupe against completed orders before every send. Roughly one in four apparent abandonments actually completed. One buyer abandoned three times then completed thirteen minutes later.

Two hard rules that override this table

1. Profitable is not the same as affordable. Advertising hits the card daily and immediately. Shopify payouts lag two to three days, and bank-deposit orders inside that revenue may never land at all. On a thin balance the binding constraint is cash float, not margin. Never scale spend from a ROAS figure alone. Check the balance and the payout timing first. A declined card has previously cost roughly a week of store revenue, which is far worse than running light.

2. Do not tinker. Every budget edit re-triggers learning on the proven Meta creative. Batch changes to a fixed schedule and leave them alone in between.

Operational cautions carried forward

  • A budget-only edit via the API sometimes force-pauses the entity and sometimes does not. It has behaved both ways on different dates. Always follow any edit with an activate call and read back the effective status.
  • New ad sets must be created with location types explicitly set to home and recent, or every ad set blocks publishing with the removed-location-option error.
  • When updating targeting via the API, pass the complete targeting object. Omitted fields are wiped, not preserved.
  • Some ad sets on this account reject a second ad with a dynamic-creative error even when the flag is false. One creative per ad set always works.
