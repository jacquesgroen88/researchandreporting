04 · Market and ICP

The ideal customer profiles

Six primary profiles and two channel profiles. Every one is anchored to businesses that have already bought from ReviewTap, so none of these are invented personas. Each profile states the unit maths, who signs, what triggers a purchase, the pitch, the objections and exactly where to find more of them.

Evidence base: 817 lifetime Shopify orders, the R381k Reviewtap System pipeline, and the Google Ads search terms report.

The organising principle

Per premises versus per person

The two buying units behave completely differently

A review stand is bought per premises. A single restaurant needs one or two, and demand caps there. A review or smart card is bought per person. A security firm with 44 guards needs 44, a dealership with 12 salespeople needs 12, a practice with 8 practitioners needs 8. ReviewTap's revenue is 72% stands, which is why the average order holds 1.9 units. Every high-value ICP below is selected because it has either many premises or many people, and preferably both.

ICP 1 · Franchise groups

National and regional franchisors with 15 to 500+ outlets · Sell to head office, never to the branch

Why this is first

South Africa has more than 800 franchisor brands and over 30,000 outlets. ReviewTap has already sold into at least four franchise networks without ever contacting a single franchisor. The proof of concept has been run inside their own businesses, at their franchisees' expense, and then discarded.

Evidence from ReviewTap's own order book

A national beauty franchise bought eight separate times, for eight different branches, from six buyer addresses, R10,943 total, four of them on the group corporate domain and four inside the last four weeks. A global attractions franchise bought four times across three cities through one buyer. A national optical franchise bought for three stores through two buyers inside three weeks. An optical group spent R12,331 across two orders and was never contacted again. A national retail services franchise bought for three stores. A multi-brand QSR franchisee bought 12 stands in one order covering three restaurant brands. A national estate agency franchise bought 6 stands and 2 cards. Two more national QSR brands, a restaurant franchise, a laundromat group and a fuel forecourt operator make up the rest. Fourteen networks, fourteen proofs, zero group conversations.

The unit maths

At a low all-in landed cost per custom stand, a 280-unit order carries about R20,000 of cost against roughly R180,000 of revenue even after a substantial volume discount. Rollouts can also be phased by region, which is how franchisors prefer to buy anyway.

Who signs

  • Marketing Manager or Brand Manager at head office is the entry point and the champion. They own brand consistency and they are measured on it.
  • Operations Director is the blocker or the accelerator. They care whether it adds work to a store manager's day.
  • Franchise Support or Franchisee Relations Manager is the hidden ally. Their job is proving head office adds value to franchisees, and this does exactly that.
  • CFO only appears if the group is funding it centrally. If franchisees fund their own units, the CFO never enters the deal, which is a much faster path.

Trigger events to watch for

  • A new Marketing or Brand Manager appointment (first 90 days, wants a visible win)
  • Annual franchisee conference or roadshow scheduled
  • A public reputation incident at one outlet dragging the brand average
  • A rebrand or store refresh programme
  • New store openings announced (each one needs the kit from day one)
  • The live Competition Commission franchising inquiry, which is pushing franchisors to evidence franchisee value right now

What they actually care about

Not review counts. Franchisors care that one bad outlet does not define the brand, that head office can see performance by location without asking, and that whatever they roll out does not require franchisee training or compliance chasing. The review count is the mechanism. The dashboard is the product.

The pitch

"Four of your franchisees have already bought our review stands independently. They did it because they were losing walk-in customers to better-rated competitors down the road. Right now you have no visibility of that, and no consistency across the group: some outlets are on 4.7, some are on 3.9, and the ones on 3.9 are pulling the brand average down in exactly the searches new customers run. We roll one system across every outlet, branded to your standards, with a head-office dashboard showing rating and review volume by location. Franchisees do nothing except put a stand on the counter. You can fund it centrally or let franchisees buy their own kit at group pricing. Can I show you what your network's ratings actually look like right now?"

Objections and answers

ObjectionAnswer
"Franchisees decide their own marketing spend"Then we price it as an approved supplier programme with group pricing. Head office gets the dashboard and the consistency at no cost, franchisees get a better price than they could get alone. Four of them are already paying full retail.
"We would need to run this past legal / the franchise council"Expected. We supply the one-page programme summary written for that meeting, and we can start with one region as a pilot so there is real data before it goes to council.
"Is this compliant with Google's policies?"Yes, and this matters. We never gate, incentivise or filter reviews. Every customer reaches the real Google review page. What we remove is friction, not selection. We will not build anything that risks the group's listings.
"We already have a reputation management tool"Most of those monitor and respond after the fact. None of them generate the review at the counter. We are usually additive rather than a replacement, and we integrate with what you have.
"What happens when a franchisee leaves?"Their location detaches from the group dashboard and the hardware is theirs. No stranded contracts and no group liability.

Where to find them

  • FASA member directory (fasa.co.za/franchise-members) and the annual FASA conference
  • Franchise brands already in ReviewTap's own order book: a national beauty franchise, a national QSR group, a national estate agency franchise, a retail services franchise
  • Franchise categories by size: QSR (19% of outlets), retail (17%), then health and beauty, automotive, and business services
  • LinkedIn title searches: "Franchise Marketing Manager", "Brand Manager", "Franchise Support Manager" filtered to South Africa
  • Gauteng first (41% of all outlets), then Western Cape (16%) and KZN (12%)

ICP 2 · Multi-site hospitality and tourism groups

Hotel groups, lodge collections, restaurant groups, guesthouse portfolios, attractions

Why this ranks first alongside franchising

Hospitality is the only category where the customer's rating is the product's distribution. A lodge with 4.8 stars and 300 reviews outsells a lodge with 4.6 and 40, on the same platform, on the same day. These buyers already understand the economics without being educated, which shortens every sales cycle.

Evidence from ReviewTap's own order book

a national restaurant group (two national restaurant brands, roughly 23 sites) made contact on 4 August 2026, unprompted, the largest inbound in store history. a coastal tourism operator bought 14 cards and 5 stands (R8,101). a restaurant group 11 stands (R7,139). a hospitality operator 10 stands. a game lodge operator 9 stands. a boutique guesthouse bought 7 stands and then 7 more a month later, unassisted. a hotel, a mountain lodge, a guesthouse, a guesthouse, a game lodge and a leisure operator all appear.

The unit maths

Hospitality buys more units per site than any other category because there are multiple guest touchpoints: reception, restaurant tables, bar, spa, checkout, and often in-room. A single 40-room lodge legitimately absorbs 8 to 15 units. A restaurant group takes 2 to 4 per site plus cards for waitstaff, which is the highest-value configuration in the entire catalogue because it combines premises and people.

Property typeUnits per siteExample groupGroup value

Lodge or hotel (reception, restaurant, spa, rooms)8 to 154 propertiesR25k to R45k

Attraction or venue4 to 10Single siteR3k to R7k

Who signs

Group Marketing Manager or Group GM in larger groups. In owner-operated collections it is the owner directly, which makes the cycle short. The person who will actually make it work day to day is the front-of-house or duty manager, and getting them to want it is what determines whether the units get used or end up in a drawer.

Trigger events

  • Season turn (pre-December and pre-Easter, when the rating that sells the season is set months earlier)
  • A cluster of bad reviews after a staffing change
  • New property acquisition or opening
  • Star grading or accreditation renewal
  • Direct-booking push, since rating drives direct bookings as hard as it drives OTA rank

The pitch

"Your guests already leave happy. Almost none of them leave a review, because the moment they feel it, they are already walking to their car. Across [group] that is a few hundred reviews a year you have earned and never collected, and the rating gap between you and the property down the road is what a traveller sees before they see anything else you spent money on. We put capture at every point where the guest is already happy, and give you one view of rating and review volume across every property. Lodges we work with typically triple their review volume within 60 days and direct bookings follow the rating. Fifteen minutes and I will bring your current numbers against your three nearest competitors."

Objections and answers

ObjectionAnswer
"Our staff won't remember to use it"They do not have to. The stand sits at the point of payment or on the table. The guest does the work. Where staff involvement helps we supply the 30-second brief and the one-line script.
"We're worried about attracting bad reviews too"Real question, honest answer. Any capture system raises volume on both sides. What it also does is dilute existing bad reviews, because 40 new reviews at 4.8 moves an average far more than a bad one drags it. We can also route unhappy guests to a private channel first, so you hear it before Google does.
"We use Tripadvisor more than Google"We build for both, and we already ship Tripadvisor units. Most groups run Google as primary and Tripadvisor secondary, and we can split by property.
"Not the right time of year"The rating that sells your December is being built now. Reviews collected in August are what a traveller reads in November.

Where to find them

  • FEDHASA member base, particularly the Hotels, Small Accommodation and Restaurants segments. ReviewTap should join under Industry Suppliers and Service Providers.
  • TBCSA member directory, the umbrella tourism business body
  • National Accommodation Association of South Africa (NAA-SA)
  • Hostex (Sandton Convention Centre, March, 7,935 visitors in 2026, up 16.5%, exhibitors up 60%). The single highest-density room of this ICP in the country.
  • Restaurant groups via the SA Chefs Association and SCASA networks
  • Existing customers as referral routes: a coastal tourism operator, a boutique guesthouse, Blueberry Hill, a game lodge operator

ICP 3 · Motor retail and service networks

Dealer groups, tyre and service chains, body repairers, parts networks

Why it works

In vehicle sales the buyer checks the dealership's rating before they check its stock. Dealers know this and most of them have far more satisfied customers than their review count reflects, because the handover moment (the single happiest point in the transaction) has no capture attached to it. This is also a two-unit category: a stand at the service desk and a card per salesperson.

Evidence from ReviewTap's own order book

a motor dealer group 17 custom stands (R9,378). a used vehicle dealership 13 mixed units (R3,496) plus a monthly landing-page fee. a motor dealership placed three orders inside one hour. Lemon Garage, a tyre fitment centre, Northgate Tyres, Autolectronix, Justcarz, Auto Motorcycles, Triumph Centurion and a regional dealership all appear. a vehicle solutions company sits in the pipeline at R15,000.

The unit maths

A single dealership takes 2 to 4 stands (showroom, service reception, parts counter) plus 6 to 15 smart business cards for the sales team. A five-branch group is therefore 15 stands and 50 cards, roughly R32,000 to R40,000, with the card component being the part nobody currently sells them.

Who signs

Dealer Principal in an independent, Group Marketing Manager in a network. The Sales Manager is the champion because rating pressure lands on them. In franchised dealerships there is often an OEM brand-standards overlay, so anything branded must pass manufacturer guidelines. Ask about that early rather than late.

Trigger events

  • New Dealer Principal or Sales Manager appointment
  • Manufacturer customer-satisfaction score reviews
  • Rating drop below a nearby competitor
  • New branch opening or acquisition
  • Sales team expansion (each new salesperson is a card)

The pitch

"Your buyer checks your Google rating before they check your stock. You have sold hundreds of cars to happy people this year and collected a fraction of the reviews, because the happiest moment in the whole process is the handover and nothing is capturing it. We put a review card in every salesperson's hand and a stand at service reception, so the ask happens at the exact moment the customer is pleased rather than a week later in an email nobody opens. Across a group we show you rating and volume per branch, so you can see which branch is actually delivering the experience the brand is promising."

Objections and answers

ObjectionAnswer
"The manufacturer controls our branding"We build to brand standards and supply artwork for approval before anything prints. Several groups run a manufacturer-approved version and a neutral version for the service side.
"Our CSI survey already measures this"CSI measures satisfaction for the manufacturer. Google reviews sell the next car to a stranger. They are different jobs and only one of them is public.
"Salespeople will not bother"They will if it takes four seconds and it is in their hand. We also report by salesperson, which turns it into a leaderboard, and that is usually all it takes.

Where to find them

  • RMI (Retail Motor Industry Organisation) and its eight constituent associations, principally NADA (new vehicle franchise dealers) and SAMBRA (motor body repairers), plus TEPA for parts and VTA for testing stations
  • Regional NADA committee structures, which are how the association actually operates
  • Existing customers as referral routes: a motor dealer group, a used vehicle dealership, a tyre fitment centre
  • Dealer group corporate sites (multi-branch groups list every branch, which is a ready-made unit count)

ICP 4 · Multi-practitioner professional practices

Medical, dental, aesthetic, optometry, physiotherapy, veterinary, legal · This is the Smart Business Card wedge

Why this is the highest-margin ICP in the list

Practices buy per practitioner, which makes them a card category rather than a stand category. Cards land at roughly R3 of COGS against R299 to R649 retail. A six-practitioner practice is a six-card order at almost pure margin, and each practitioner is a personal brand who will want their own card, which is the exact dynamic that produced the highest revenue-per-order product in the catalogue.

Evidence from ReviewTap's own order book

an optometry group bought 20 custom stands for R11,033, the sixth largest order ever placed. a medical practice 6 mixed units. an aesthetic clinic group 5 stands. an aesthetic clinic, a radiology practice, an aesthetic practice, a physiotherapy practice, a physiotherapy practice, a medical practice, a veterinary practice, a law firm, a law firm and a professional practice all appear as single or low-quantity buyers. a dermatology practice and a pharmacy sit in the open pipeline.

Every one of those is a practice that bought one or two units when it has three to twelve practitioners.

Who signs

The Practice Manager is the buyer in almost every case, and this is the single most important targeting fact in this profile. Practitioners are the users and the budget holders but they do not shop. The Practice Manager is the person who researches, compares and orders, and they are reachable, responsive and vastly under-marketed to.

Trigger events

  • A new associate or practitioner joining (needs cards on day one)
  • Practice rebrand, new rooms or relocation
  • A negative review appearing, which in healthcare is disproportionately damaging
  • Practice management software change or digital-front-door project
  • Medical aid or referral network positioning work

The pitch

"Your patients pick a practice on Google before they ever call you, and a practice with 12 reviews looks new next to one with 90 even when yours is better. The problem is nobody asks, because asking feels awkward at the desk. We give every practitioner a card they hand over at the end of the consultation, and one stand at reception. The patient taps it and lands straight on your review page. It takes four seconds and it never feels like a sales pitch, because it comes from the person who just treated them. Six practitioners is six cards, and it works from the first week."

Objections and answers

ObjectionAnswer
"Asking patients for reviews feels unprofessional"Which is exactly why it should not be asked verbally. The card sits with the appointment slip. No conversation required, and the patient decides in their own time.
"Patient confidentiality and HPCSA rules"Nothing about patient data touches our system. We do not collect, store or transmit anything about the patient. The card carries a link to your public Google listing, the same link already on your website. There is no incentive and no gating.
"Only one of our doctors cares about this"Then start with one card. Practices routinely reorder within two months once one practitioner's review count visibly pulls ahead of the others.
"We are not accepting new patients"Then this is about defence, not acquisition. Your rating is what medical aids, referrers and the next generation of patients see when the practice does open up.

Where to find them

  • Practice Manager as a LinkedIn job title, filtered by South Africa and by discipline. This is the single most effective search in the whole ICP set.
  • Practice-management software user communities and Facebook groups (medical practice managers SA, dental practice managers SA)
  • Discipline associations: SADA (dental), SAMA (medical), OPTOM SA, SASP (physiotherapy), SAVA (veterinary)
  • Aesthetic and dermatology clinics specifically, which are the most marketing-literate sub-segment and the fastest to buy
  • Existing customers as referral routes: an optometry group, an aesthetic clinic group, an aesthetic clinic, a physiotherapy practice

ICP 5 · Field-service and security operations

Security firms, plumbing, electrical, pest control, freight, cleaning, moving · Cards per field employee

Why this ICP produced the largest card order in the store's history

Evidence

a national security firm bought 44 custom review cards, 3 stands and 3 landing pages for R18,183, the third-largest order ever placed and by far the largest card order. That is one card per guard. a plumbing contractor bought 5 cards plus branding and a monthly landing page. a security firm 5 units, a services firm two orders, a two-brand repair group on the same day, a moving company 3, a freight operator 5, an industrial supplier 6, a solar installer 9, Ecopoise Pest, Lombard Contractors and Coetzee Pressure Clean all appear.

Why the economics are unusually good

Field-service businesses have no premises the customer visits. There is no counter to put a stand on. The technician is the touchpoint, which means the only viable format is a card per person, and headcount for these businesses runs from 10 to 200. This is the highest unit-count-per-account category in the whole document and the one with the lowest cost to serve.

It is also the one where the ask lands most naturally: the technician has just fixed the customer's problem, they are standing in front of them, and the customer is relieved. That is the single best review moment that exists in any business.

Regional service firm15 to 4020 to 45R8,000 to R18,000

Who signs

Owner or Operations Manager. These are usually owner-run businesses where the decision is fast and the cheque is signed by the person you are talking to. Turnover of field staff is high, which makes cards a consumable rather than a one-off, and that is the most valuable structural fact in this profile.

Trigger events

  • Headcount growth or a new contract won (every new technician is a card)
  • Tender or contract renewal where public rating is evaluated
  • Insurance or accreditation processes
  • A competitor visibly out-rating them in local search
  • Staff turnover, which is continuous and drives natural reorder

The pitch

"Nobody comes to your premises, so your reviews depend entirely on what happens at the customer's house or site. Your technician is standing in front of a relieved customer at exactly the right moment and has nothing to hand them. One card per technician, tap, done, four seconds. For a team of 20 that is 20 chances a day instead of an email nobody opens. And when a customer is comparing three plumbers on Google, the one with 140 reviews gets the call before the one with 11."

Objections and answers

ObjectionAnswer
"My guys will lose them"They will, and at R3 of cost to us we would rather you reorder cheaply than not use them. We price replacement packs so losing a few does not matter.
"I only need one or two"Then the customer only gets asked when that one technician is on site. The point of this is coverage, and coverage is per person.
"My customers are not tech-savvy"Every card carries a QR code as well as NFC. If the phone is old, they scan. There is no app and nothing to install.
"How do I know they are actually using them?"Review volume by month is the measurement, and we can issue per-technician links so you can see who is generating reviews and who is not.

Where to find them

  • Industry bodies: PSIRA-registered security firms, IOPSA (plumbing), ECA (electrical contractors), SAPCA (pest control), professional cleaning and facilities associations
  • Local business Facebook groups and WhatsApp business communities, where these owners actually spend time
  • Existing customers as referral routes: a national security firm (the flagship case study for this ICP), a plumbing contractor, a security firm
  • Google Maps prospecting by category and area, filtered for businesses with high star ratings and low review counts, which is a precise signal of this exact problem

ICP 6 · Corporate sales and professional teams

Smart Business Cards per employee · The catalogue's highest revenue-per-order product

Why this profile exists

This is a different product and a different job. The Smart Business Card is not about reviews, it is about a salesperson handing over their details without paper. It produces R2,423 per order and 4.0 units per order, the highest in the catalogue on both measures, and it received twelve orders in twelve months while its own keyword sat blocked in Google Ads.

Evidence from ReviewTap's own order book

an engineering group bought 25 Smart Business Cards plus a stand for R15,187, the fourth-largest order ever placed. a professional services firm bought 6 cards for R3,894 and then 4 more for R2,336 a month later, entirely unprompted. a brand agency 3, a software company 4, a construction firm 4, a coastal hospitality client 4 plus stands, a building products manufacturer 15 units, a financial services firm 12.

The unit maths

Teams buy one card per person, and then they buy again when people join. A 25-person sales team is a R15,000 order with a natural annual replacement cycle. Every new hire is an order. Every rebrand reprints the whole fleet.

Who signs

Marketing Manager or Sales Director for the fleet purchase. HR or Office Manager for the ongoing new-starter reorders, which is where the recurring revenue actually lives. Land the fleet through marketing, then move the reorder relationship to whoever handles onboarding.

Trigger events

  • Rebrand or new visual identity (reprints the entire fleet at once)
  • Sales team expansion or a new regional office
  • Conference or trade show season (Hostex, Africa Health, Decorex, industry expos)
  • Sustainability or paperless initiatives, which is a genuine and underused angle
  • New CRM rollout, where contact capture becomes a live topic

The pitch

"Your team is still handing out paper cards that get thrown away in the car park, and the details on half of them are already out of date. One tap moves your name, number, email and company straight onto the other person's phone, and when someone changes role you update it centrally instead of reprinting 200 cards. For a 25-person team it is one order, it looks considerably better than paper in front of a client, and nobody ever runs out at a conference."

Objections and answers

ObjectionAnswer
"Paper cards are cheaper"Per unit, yes. Per year, no, because you reprint every time someone joins, leaves or changes title. These update centrally and never go out of date.
"Will it work on every phone?"NFC on everything recent, and a printed QR code as the fallback. No app either side.
"We already use a digital business card app"Most of those are a link you have to send. This is a physical object you hand to someone in a conversation, which is a different moment. They coexist.
"What happens when someone leaves?"You deactivate or reassign the card centrally. That is the part paper has never been able to do.

Where to find them

  • Reinstate nfc business card and its cluster in Google Ads immediately. It clicked at 33% before it was blocked. This is the fastest route to this ICP and it is currently switched off.
  • LinkedIn: Sales Director, Head of Sales, Marketing Manager, at companies with 20 to 200 employees
  • Conference and expo exhibitor lists, which are literally a list of teams about to need cards
  • Existing customers as referral routes: an engineering group (the flagship case study), a professional services firm, a software company

ICP 7 · Agencies and print shops (channel)

Resellers, not customers · The distribution rail

Evidence they are already here

An agency (an agency) abandoned a three-stand cart. a marketing agency bought 4 custom stands. a second marketing agency bought 5 stands. a media agency bought 7 units for R3,193. a niche agency bought 3. These are firms buying for clients at full retail, one small order at a time, with no partner terms and no reason to come back.

The proven model to copy

Every international peer that scaled did it this way. NFC Tagify runs dropship and white-label packaging with no minimum order. wCard.io gives partners their own domain and lets them set their own end price. a white-label operator targets UV print shops specifically with published breaks from 10% at ten units to 40% at a hundred. TapTag pays 15% affiliate commission on a 30-day cookie.

Why the economics are trivially good

At a very low landed cost per unit, ReviewTap can offer partner pricing that looks generous to the partner and still clears a high margin. Partners bring their own client base, do their own selling and absorb their own client management. There is no advertising cost attached to a single unit sold through this rail.

What partners actually want

  • Margin they can quote confidently, published rather than negotiated each time
  • White-label or neutral shipping so the client does not go direct next time
  • Artwork templates and a quick-turn setup so it is not a project
  • A recurring line they can bill, which is where RT Pro at R399 per month per client becomes the real prize
  • Fast turnaround, because they are usually promising a client a date

The pitch

"You are already buying these for clients at retail. Come onto partner pricing instead: you set your own client price, keep the spread, and we ship neutral so the client stays yours. Most partners make more on the monthly review system than on the hardware, because once a client sees their rating move they stay. Setup takes a day and there is no minimum order."

Where to find them

  • Existing order history: a marketing agency, a second marketing agency, a media agency, a niche agency, an agency
  • Signage, promotional-products and print businesses, which is the type a white-label operator proved converts best
  • Local SEO and Google Business Profile specialists, whose clients need exactly this
  • Web design studios serving hospitality and trades

ICP 8 · Export and white-label partners (channel)

Secondary priority · Opens after the SA motion is proven

The unit economics travel. A card costs roughly R3 to make and weighs almost nothing, so international fulfilment is viable in a way that stands are not. The realistic entry is white-label supply to partners in other markets rather than direct-to-consumer selling, because the ReviewTap brand name collides with at least five same-name companies abroad and would have to fight an entity-recognition problem before it fought a commercial one.

Sequencing

This ICP is deliberately third priority. It should not be opened until the SA franchise and hospitality motions are producing repeatable revenue, for three reasons: it competes for the same attention, it introduces currency and logistics complexity, and it dilutes the "South Africa's review system" positioning that makes the domestic pitch work. The right time to open it is once the reseller rail is running smoothly domestically, since it is the same machine pointed at a different map.

Where to find them: print and promotional-product suppliers in neighbouring SADC markets, and the partner-programme directories of the platforms listed in doc 03 (their rejected or underserved applicants are a real source).
