Open decisions

D1 · How much working capital goes into the week 1 restock?

Context. Three products at or near zero, including the Smart Business Card, which produces R2,423 per order. Stand landed cost is an order of magnitude higher than a card, and unit prices have already risen between two consecutive orders, so ordering larger is cheaper per unit but ties up cash on a thin balance.

Recommendation: order deep on cards, moderate on stands. Cards have a very low landed cost and a very high margin, and are the format every bulk ICP buys. Tying up cash in cards is cheap. Stands are ten times the unit cost, so order to a sensible reorder point rather than speculatively. If capital is genuinely tight, prioritise Smart Business Cards first because they have the highest revenue per unit of stock.

D2 · Does anyone else sell, or does this stay solo?

Context. The base case needs nine hours a week of outbound sustained for twelve months. The aggressive case assumes a second seller from month four. There is an existing arrangement with a sales partner covering ReviewTap fulfilment and sales, and a standing position that the cash-recovery and lead work is the owner and Claude only.

Recommendation: solo for the first ninety days, then reassess at the doc 15 gate. The group motion has never been run and the first two deals will teach more about what actually works than any brief could transfer. Once two group accounts have closed, the pitch, the objections and the proposal are known and the motion becomes teachable. Bringing someone in before that means training them on guesses.

D3 · Is the ReviewTap brand kept, given the entity collision?

Context. The name collides with at least five same-name companies abroad, which is the root cause of the Perplexity invisibility in doc 08. It also has 817 orders of local recognition behind it.

Recommendation: keep the name, fix the entity. Renaming destroys real local equity, breaks the domain, the socials, the Google profile and every existing case study, and would cost more than the disambiguation work. The collision only matters internationally, and ICP 8 (export) is already scoped as a white-label motion where the ReviewTap brand does not need to lead.

D4 · Does the free group audit get given away without qualification?

Context. The audit is the conversion offer for four separate channels. It takes about an hour per group to produce.

Recommendation: qualify lightly, on locations only. Ask for the group name and number of locations on the form, and produce the full audit for anyone with five or more. Below five, send an automated single-location version. This protects the hour without adding friction to exactly the buyers worth winning.

D5 · Is exhibiting at Hostex worth it in year one?

Context. Hostex drew 7,935 visitors in March 2026, up 16.5%, and is the densest concentration of ICP 2 in the country. A stand is a significant cost.

Recommendation: attend, do not exhibit. Walk the floor with thirty prepared conversations, having already pulled the ratings of thirty groups. A stand generates browsers, a prepared conversation with a group marketing manager whose weakest property you can name generates a pilot. Exhibit in year two with the case studies in hand. Secondary benefit: the exhibitor list is a ready-made ICP 6 prospect list.

D6 · How aggressive should the reseller discount be?

Context. The proposed ladder tops out at 40% for distributors, matching the published a white-label operator ceiling. At 40% off a custom stand the margin is still 81%.

Recommendation: hold the published ladder and do not negotiate below it. The margin absorbs it easily, which makes it tempting to go further deal by deal. Do not. A ladder that can be argued down stops being a structure and becomes a negotiation, and partners talk to each other. The neutral-shipping promise is worth more to a partner than another five points anyway.

D7 · What happens to the low-performing product lines?

Context. Autotrader and Instagram variants produced R3,668 combined across a full year and all four are out of stock.

Recommendation: keep the pages, do not restock. They cost nothing to leave indexed and they catch long-tail search. Do not spend working capital, photography or promotion on them. If one starts generating enquiries organically, revisit.

D8 · Does the plan change if a major opportunity arrives mid-execution?

Context. A single large group, a partnership approach or an investment conversation can arrive at any point and pull attention off the plan.

Recommendation: no change to the plan, and use it as evidence. Everything here makes the business more valuable and more transferable: recurring group contracts, a documented partner channel, published pricing, a working fulfilment pipeline. Arriving mid-execution and finding a plan being worked is a far stronger position than a business being tidied up for the occasion. The one thing to avoid is starting anything in month eleven that only pays back in month eighteen.

What would make this plan wrong

Stated plainly, because a strategy that cannot be falsified is not a strategy

This Blueprint rests on one central claim: that the bulk demand visible in the order history is representative and repeatable rather than accidental. The evidence is strong (55 orders, R299,458, four franchise networks, a 50-unit order, an unprompted 23-site inbound) but all of it is retrospective. Nobody has yet gone out and created a group deal deliberately.

The test comes in weeks 4 to 8. If five well-researched group approaches produce zero conversations, the thesis is wrong and the plan should be rebalanced back toward paid acquisition and single-unit volume. If two produce conversations, the model holds and the effort should increase, not stay flat.

That is the honest position. The offer, pricing and search work in this plan pay for themselves regardless. The group motion is the bet, and it is a bet worth making because the downside is nine hours a week and the upside changes the shape of the whole business.
