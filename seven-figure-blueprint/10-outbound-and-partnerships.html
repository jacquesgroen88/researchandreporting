10 · Channels

Outbound and partnerships

This is the highest-value unbuilt motion in the business. Advertising sells one stand to one owner. A conversation with a head office sells a hundred. This document sets out the named-account list, the sequences, the reseller programme and the three partnership types worth building, in the order they should be attacked.

Every named target below is either an existing ReviewTap customer, an open pipeline opportunity, or an association with a published member base.

Why A and B come first

The warmest leads in the business are its own invoices

Motion A targets groups that have already bought and proved the product internally. Motion B targets customers who bought two units when they have twenty employees. Neither requires a cold approach, a new list, or any advertising spend. Cold outbound (motion C) is real and is included, but starting there while the warm list sits untouched would be the wrong sequence.

Do this one first, this week

A national restaurant group is an inbound enquiry about roughly 23 sites that arrived on 4 August. It is the largest opportunity ever to contact this business and every day it sits it gets colder. It also must not be handled as a price enquiry: the answer to "how much are your cards" from a 23-site group is a group programme conversation, not a quote.

The escalation sequence

Prepare the evidence before making contact

Pull the group's current Google ratings and review counts by location. Identify the three worst-rated sites and the gap to their nearest competitor. This takes about twenty minutes per group and it is the entire difference between a supplier email and a consultant email.

Open with their own franchisees, not with the product

"Four of your locations have already bought review stands from us independently" is the strongest opening line available to this business, and it is true for at least four groups.

Lead with the audit, not the pitch

The offer on the first call is a free group review audit: every location's rating and volume, ranked, with the competitive gap. It is genuinely useful, it costs an hour, and it makes the second conversation about their problem rather than your product.

Present both funding models

Central purchase, or approved-supplier with franchisees buying at group pricing. Offering both removes the budget objection entirely, and the approved-supplier route is usually the faster close because it does not need a budget line.

Propose a regional pilot

Ten to fifteen locations in one region, measured over sixty days, with the group rollout as the agreed next step if the numbers hold. Groups almost never say yes to a national rollout on a first proposal, and they very often say yes to one region.

Motion B · Reorder and expansion

The cheapest revenue in this document. Reorders are already happening entirely unprompted: a professional services firm bought six Smart Business Cards and then four more, a boutique guesthouse bought seven stands and then seven more, a coastal tourism operator placed five orders across three months. Nobody triggered any of it.

The trigger rules to automate

  • At 60 days after delivery: if review volume has visibly increased, send the system upsell. The proof is in their own numbers.
  • At 90 days: the expansion prompt, sized to their business ("most practices your size run one per practitioner").
  • At 180 days: the replacement and top-up prompt, aimed hardest at field-service where cards get lost.
  • On any second order: flag for a human call rather than an email. A second order is a buying signal, not a transaction.
The rule that protects credibility

Dedupe every list against completed Shopify orders before sending. Roughly one in four apparent abandonments actually completed. Emailing a customer about the order they already placed is the fastest way to look like a business that does not know its own customers.

Partner approach message

Hi [Name]

You have bought [n] review stands from us over the last few months, and I am guessing they went to clients rather than to your own reception.

If that is right, you should be on partner pricing instead of retail. That means you set your own client price and keep the spread, we ship neutral so the client stays yours, and there is no minimum order.

The part most partners end up making the most from is not the hardware. It is the monthly review system at R399 per client. Fifteen clients on that is a R6,000 monthly line you did not have to build a product for.

Setup takes a day. Worth fifteen minutes?

Why the association route is underrated

An association supplier listing does four jobs at once: qualified reach into the exact ICP, a credible third-party link for SEO (doc 07), a citable source that fixes the Perplexity gap (doc 08), and access to the events in doc 11. It is one membership fee producing four outcomes, which is unusual value.
