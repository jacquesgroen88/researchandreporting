03 · Market and ICP

Market and competitor research

The South African market has split into two tiers that do not compete with each other, and ReviewTap is priced in the lower one while owning the capability to dominate the upper one. This document sizes the opportunity, maps who is actually in it, and identifies the position nobody is holding.

Sources listed at the foot of this page. Market sizing from the Competition Commission franchise market inquiry, FASA and Hostex 2026 attendance data.

The Competition Commission's draft terms of reference for its franchising market inquiry put the South African franchise sector at more than 800 franchisor brands, over 3,500 franchisees and upwards of 30,000 outlets. FASA's own 2023 data counted 68,463 franchisees across 727 franchise systems. Fast food and quick-service restaurants are the largest single category at 19%, retail follows at 17%. Geographically, Gauteng holds 41% of outlets, the Western Cape 16% and KwaZulu-Natal 12%.

The timing advantage nobody is using

The Competition Commission has an active market inquiry into franchising. That means franchisors across the country are currently under pressure to demonstrate the value they deliver to franchisees. A group-wide review programme that measurably lifts every franchisee's Google rating is exactly the kind of franchisee-value story a franchisor wants to be able to point at right now. This is a wedge with a shelf life, and it will not be open forever.

The two-tier market

Tier 1: the commodity floor

South African sellers listing NFC review cards and stands as retail hardware. GeeWiz, Netram, Card Monster SA, SB Card, TapTick, Terrific Trophies, Miccory.

What they sell: a chip in plastic. NTAG213 cards, tap to review, done.

What they do not sell: setup, link derivation, reporting, review response, sentiment gating, multi-location management, or any recurring relationship.

Their pricing behaviour: descending. GeeWiz markets a stand at "as low as R31 over 6 months interest-free", which is a financing frame rather than a price, and tells you the category is being sold on affordability.

Tier 2: the platform ceiling

International digital business card and contact-capture platforms. Popl, V1CE, Mobilo, Tapt, Tapni, Wave, HiHello.

What they sell: a system with hardware attached. CRM sync, team management, analytics, enterprise seats.

Their pricing: V1CE from $79 for PVC up to $450 for gold. Mobilo $7 to $29 with Salesforce and HubSpot integration. Popl from around $13.49 with a free tier, now pivoted hard into enterprise event capture.

Their weakness in this market: none of them are built around Google reviews, and none of them have a South African presence, ZAR pricing, local support or local fulfilment.

The position nobody holds

Nobody in South Africa sells a managed review system across a group of locations

Tier 1 sells hardware without a system. Tier 2 sells a system that is not about reviews and is not in this country. ReviewTap already owns the entire middle: the card builder, the link generator and destination model, Review Shield sentiment gating, the reporting layer, RT Pro at R399 a month and the Growth and Scale tiers above it. It has been pricing all of that as though it were Tier 1.

Where this leaves the strategy

Do not fight on the floor

Six local sellers are competing on the price of a chip in plastic. ReviewTap's COGS advantage (roughly R3 on a card) means it could win that fight and it would still be the wrong fight, because the prize is a R424 transaction with no recurring tail.

Occupy the empty ceiling

Managed review programmes across groups of locations. Nobody in the country sells this. ReviewTap already has every component built.

Reach the market through a few hundred people

800 franchisors, the FEDHASA and TBCSA member bases, the RMI's eight constituent associations, and a few hundred agencies. That is the entire addressable market condensed into a callable list.

Turn the commodity tier into distribution

Print shops and agencies currently buying at retail become partners on published volume terms, following the white-label operator and wCard.io model. Competitors for hardware become channels for the system.
