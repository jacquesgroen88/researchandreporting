What this list is

Every person on it went through the whole buying process on reviewtap.co.za. They picked a product, picked a colour, chose a quantity, typed their name, email, phone number and street address, and landed on the payment screen. Then they left without paying.

That makes them the warmest list in the business. They are not enquiries and they are not cold leads. They are people who had their card out.

Your call sheet is sent to you separately. It has every person's details, a one-tap WhatsApp, call and email button for each, and your recovery code. This page is the playbook; the sheet is what you work from.

The list covers abandoned checkouts from 21 June to 6 September 2026. It is sorted for you already: recency first, then cart size, then whether the person tried more than once, then whether we have a phone number. Work it top down. Row 1 is the best call on the sheet.

How it was cleaned

The list was checked against all 200 real orders placed between 12 June and 7 September, on four separate keys: email address, phone number, customer name, and Shopify's own customer records.

16 people were removed because they abandoned and then bought anyway. Two of those paid under a completely different email address and were only caught by matching their phone number. One of them bought thirteen minutes after abandoning a R1,347 cart.

If you phone someone who has already paid, you damage a real customer relationship. Never work a version of this list that has not been re-checked. Ask Jacques for a fresh scrub before each calling session, not once at the start.

Why they did not pay

This is the single most useful thing to understand before you pick up the phone, because it tells you that almost nobody on this list rejected the product.

The store charges R125 for Courier Guy delivery on any order under R1,000. Delivery is free above R1,000. Nearly every cart on this list sits between R299 and R774, so the sequence is: a business owner picks a R649 branded stand, agrees with the price, types in all their details, and then watches R125 appear on the final screen. On a R649 item that is a nineteen percent jump, revealed at the last possible moment.

More than half of the people who reach the ReviewTap checkout leave at that screen. Not at the product page. At the payment page, after committing their time.

The example that explains the whole list

One shop owner on your sheet put two standard stands in the cart. Two at R499 is R998. The free delivery threshold is R1,000. They were two rands short, got charged R125, and left. They are near the top of your call order.

So the call is never "why didn't you buy". You already know why. The call is closer to: you got all the way to the end, let me just take this over the phone and sort the delivery out for you.

The move that solves it without a discount

You do not need permission to use this, and it is not a concession. It is the store's existing published policy.

Orders over R1,000 ship free. So for most people on this list, adding a second unit removes the delivery charge entirely. The customer gets a second stand for their second location or their counter, and the R125 disappears. That converts the exact thing that stopped them into a reason to spend more.

How to work a call

  1. Check the row before you dial

    Name, product, quantity, cart value, how many days ago, and whether they abandoned more than once. Ten seconds. You should never ask a question the sheet already answers.

  2. Open, then stop talking

    The opener below is short on purpose. Their first reaction tells you which of the four conversations you are actually in, and you cannot know that in advance.

  3. Name the delivery charge before they do

    Most of them are hesitating about it and will not say so. Raising it yourself is disarming, and it lets you go straight to the free-delivery ladder.

  4. Take the order on the call, or send the link while you are still on the phone

    Do not end the call with "have a look and get back to me". The one-click link is on their row. Send it while they are listening.

  5. Log the outcome immediately

    In the Outcome column, before the next call. A sheet that is filled in at the end of the day is a sheet full of guesses.

The opener

Say this

"Hi, is that [NAME]? It's Giorgio from ReviewTap.

I'm calling because you were setting up an order with us for the [PRODUCT] a little while back, and it looks like it didn't go through on our side. I wanted to check whether you still need it, or whether something got in the way?"

Now stop. Let them answer. Their answer is the entire call.

"It didn't go through on our side" matters. It removes any suggestion that they failed at something or changed their mind, and it gives them a clean way back in without having to explain themselves.

What they will say, and what you say back

"The delivery cost put me off." expect this most

"That's fair, it catches a lot of people. Here's the thing though: delivery is free over a thousand rand. So one stand is R774 all in, but two is R1,298 with nothing added. If you've got a second counter or a second branch, that's the one that makes sense. Which would you like me to put through?"

Always try this first. True, on-policy, needs no approval, and turns the objection into a bigger order.

If they only want one: "Then let me take the delivery off this one for you. I'll give you a code that takes R125 off at checkout. It's yours for the next few weeks."

Only after the two-unit option has been offered and declined. See the section below.

"I was just looking. I'm not ready."

"No problem at all. Can I ask what you'd be using it for, is it one location or a few?"

Do not push. Find out the size of the opportunity instead. Anything multi-location goes to Jacques the same day.

"I forgot about it." / "I got distracted."

"Easiest thing is I send you your cart exactly as you left it, one tap and it's done. Are you still on [EMAIL]?"

This is the fastest close on the sheet. Send the link while they are still on the phone with you.

"I found something cheaper."

"Which one were you looking at? The difference with ours is it's printed with your own logo and colours, and it opens your Google review page without the customer downloading anything. Happy to send you a picture so you can compare properly."

Never name or criticise a competitor.

"Does it actually work? Do people use it?"

"It works on any phone from the last few years, either by tapping or by scanning the code, and it takes the customer straight to your review page. The reason it gets used is that there's nothing to type and nothing to search for."

Explain the mechanism. Do not quote a star rating, a review count, or a success percentage. See the rules below.

"Not interested."

"Understood, thanks for your time."

End it. Mark it. Move on. One "no" is a no.

Your discount code

You have a recovery code that takes R125 off any order, which is exactly the delivery charge that stopped these people. The code itself is on your private call sheet, not on this page, and that is deliberate: this page can be opened by anyone who has the link.

Use it second, never first

The two-unit free-delivery option is always the opening move, because it grows the order instead of shrinking it. A customer who takes two branded stands is worth R1,298 to the business. The same customer given the code on a single stand is worth R649.

Reach for the code only when they have heard the two-unit option and want one unit anyway. At that point R649 beats losing the sale, so use it without hesitation.

The one-click link

Every row on the sheet has a One-Click Recovery Link in the last column. It reopens that person's exact cart, with their products, quantities, name and delivery address already filled in. They tap it and pay. Nothing to re-enter.

It is the highest-converting thing you have. Use it on every call you do not close outright: WhatsApp it or email it while you are still speaking to them, and confirm out loud that it has arrived before you hang up.

Each link is unique to one person. Do not forward one to anybody else, and do not post one anywhere.

Rules

  • Never contact anyone marked EXISTING CUSTOMER with the recovery script. Six people on the sheet have already bought something and then abandoned a second cart. They are an upsell conversation, not a recovery one. Their row says so.
  • Do not promise free delivery as a general policy. It is free over R1,000 and R125 below that. Quote the threshold, never a favour.
  • Offer the two-unit free-delivery option before you offer the code. The code is approved and live, but it is the fallback, not the opener.
  • Never publish the code. Spoken on calls and typed into direct replies only. It is capped at 61 uses and shared with nobody outside your sheet.
  • Do not quote a star rating or a review count. The "4.8 stars" figure that appears in some old material is unverified and must not be repeated to a customer.
  • Do not promise a delivery date on an out-of-stock product. Take the interest and hand it to Jacques.
  • Business hours only, 08:00 to 17:00 on weekdays. Almost everyone on this list is a business.
  • Three no-answers and the number is done. Switch that person to the email above rather than calling a fourth time.
  • Log every outcome as it happens, in the Outcome column, using the codes below.
