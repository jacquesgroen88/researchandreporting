Die Veiling Klub · pricing rationale

Why the numbers hold up.

Your crib sheet for the two questions Harry will ask: what we charge him each month, and what the memberships should cost. Arguments first, honest flags where they matter, and a line you can say out loud in each case.

01 · The R1,500 per-bar monthly fee

One member pays for it. The rest is his.

1 membership = the whole fee, covered
One Silver member (R1,499) per bar per month, or about three Bronze, pays the entire platform fee. Every member after that is money the bar keeps.

This is the line that ends the monthly-price conversation. Harry is not spending R1,500 a month; he is converting one regular into a member and the platform is paid for. At a founding intake of even 40 to 50 members a bar, the fee is a rounding error against what those memberships bring in.

What the R1,500 actually buys

  • The member card, the bar terminal and Harry's dashboard, hosted and running every day, not software sitting on a shelf
  • Fraud protection built in: codes rotate every 30 seconds, so a card cannot be shared or screenshotted
  • The measurement engine that ties every membership to real till spend, the thing that proves the programme works
  • Ongoing updates, new features, security, backups and support

Build vs rent

  • Building this cost R64,000 once. That bought the system, not the running of it
  • Owning it himself means a developer on call, servers, security and maintenance, far more than R1,500 a bar
  • POS loyalty add-ons and third-party apps typically charge 2 to 5% of turnover, or R2,000 to R5,000+ per site a month
  • R1,500 flat, per bar, is below market for a finished, staffed platform
Per-drink framing, if he pushes. A busy bar rings up hundreds of member pours a month. Spread the R1,500 across them and the platform costs a few rand per transaction, less than the cost of the free drink it is pouring. And it is cancellable on 30 days' notice, so there is no lock-in to fear.
"You're not paying R1,500 for software. You're paying us to run the whole thing, prove it works, and keep it safe, and one membership a month covers it. Everything past that is yours."
02 · The membership prices

Priced off three anchors, not a guess.

Every tier sits between a floor (what it costs the bar to honour the free drinks), a ceiling (what a genuine regular already spends in a month), and a value stack (drinks plus savings plus perks plus status). The price lives in that band.

The point of the table. The free drinks cost the bar almost nothing next to the fee. A membership is high-margin recurring revenue that lands before anyone orders a round. That is the bar-side justification, and it is strong at every tier.

But the real prize is not the fee

The membership fee is the floor. The return is frequency and attach spend: a member with a free draught waiting has a reason to choose Die Veiling over the pub down the road, and they rarely stop at the free one. The food, the second round and the friends they bring are the business case, and the system measures exactly that against each membership. So the price does two jobs: it filters for genuine regulars, and it funds the platform, while the attach spend is where the growth actually comes from.

Honest flag on Silver at R1,499. On tangible value alone (about R365 of beer plus modest savings) it is priced as a premium club, not a discount card. Defend it on the bundle, the status and the spend a true regular is already making, never on "you save money", because a light user does not. If Harry wants volume sign-ups instead of a premium few, a lower Silver (R699 to R899 for the same two drinks) fills faster and leans harder on attach spend. That is a real lever, not a mistake: fewer high-value members, or many lower-value ones. Bring it to him as a choice with a recommendation, so the pricing feels deliberate.
"The free drink is the hook, not the deal. We price it so it's worth your while to actually be a regular, and the money the bar makes is in everything you buy around it, which we can now prove for the first time."
03 · The yearly option

Two months free, and why it's worth giving.

Annual pricing at pay for ten, get twelve is the standard move, and it earns its discount:

  • Cash upfront. A year of fees in the account on day one, the same reason we took the build in two payments
  • Lower churn. An annual member cannot drift away after a quiet month; they are in for the year
  • A cleaner sell. "Two months free" is easier to say yes to than a monthly debit that nags every month

The two months given away cost the bar almost nothing in drink terms (see the table), so the discount is nearly all margin traded for commitment and cash. It is a good trade. The annual figures on the sign-up are illustrative until Harry sets the basis; the logic holds whatever the final numbers.
