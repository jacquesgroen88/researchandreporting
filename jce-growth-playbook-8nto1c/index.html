Read with: the 90-day survival plan

The target this playbook serves: about R40,000 a month of new recurring income signed by 21 February, with the Acreage retainer renewed. At an average of about R5,000 a month per client, that is 8 new clients in 12 weeks. If Jacques closes about 1 in 4 walkthroughs (hospitality has run at 1 in 20 with Giorgio closing, which is why Jacques now closes), 8 clients needs about 30 walkthroughs, which is roughly 3 a week.

That is a volume problem, not a talent problem. Jacques is the closer and should not also be the researcher, the booker, the note-taker, the proposal writer and the follow-up machine. This playbook moves everything around the close onto Diane, Giorgio and Claude, so Jacques spends his hours in conversations that end in a yes or a no.

The sales process, step by step

5. CloseA yes, a no, or a decision date in writing. Jacques
6. KickoffWithin 48 hours of the first payment. Diane

Rules that apply to every deal

  1. The pipeline is the truth. Every deal lives in the Jacques Personal Pipeline (JCE Media Group) or the ReviewTap pipeline for Giorgio's deals. A deal's stage moves only when something real happened: an email sent, a call held, a payment landed. If the register says we are waiting on a client but the stage never moved, we are waiting on ourselves.
  2. Every open deal has a next step and a date. No date means it is not a deal.
  3. Proposal within 24 hours of the walkthrough. Speed reads as competence. A proposal sent a week later reads as an afterthought.
  4. One ask per message. Every follow-up asks for one thing: a call time, a decision, a payment.
  5. Follow-up cadence: day 1 (recap and proposal), day 3 (one useful thing, such as their own Google numbers), day 7 (ask for the decision), day 14 (call), then monthly until they say no.
  6. Payment on signing, in two halves. First half at kickoff, second half on day 14 regardless of how fast the client gives access. The Maison de Blom terms are the template.
  7. Never claim what we cannot show. Real numbers from real clients only. If a part of the offer is new, say so.
  8. Jacques's 08:00 to 10:00 block is for asks: calls, closes, follow-ups. No building in that window.

December and January timing

South Africa slows down from about 15 December to 5 January. Use it, do not fight it:

  • Until 12 Dec: close what is warm. Guesthouse owners have December cash, so lead with the prepay option.
  • 15 Dec to 5 Jan: book January walkthroughs, send the useful-thing follow-ups, prepare proposals. Nobody signs, but diaries fill.
  • From 6 Jan: the main push. Training providers plan their year in January; guesthouses feel the post-season drop and are open to a win-back message.

3. How Diane helps win new business

Diane is already inside every client relationship, she is strong on the phone, and she is the person who makes onboarding happen. Those three things are exactly what a buyer needs to believe before signing. Her role in new business is to run everything around Jacques's close: find, book, prepare, write up, follow up, and turn existing clients into upsells and referrals.

She does not price, discount or promise scope. She can send anything already approved, any time, without asking.

How much of Diane's week this takes

About 8 hours a week, in two protected blocks (suggested: Tuesday and Wednesday mornings), plus speed-to-lead as it happens. To make room, Claude takes more of the delivery drafting, and recurring admin moves to templates. If delivery runs over, new-business blocks are the last thing to be cut, not the first.

What Diane needs from Jacques to do this

  • A standing yes to send any approved template or asset, and to book his diary for walkthroughs (two fixed walkthrough slots per day).
  • Access to the Jacques Personal Pipeline and the KeyOps prospect file.
  • The menu and the price pages, so she never has to ask what something costs.
  • A same-day price approval on every proposal she drafts.
  • A decision on how Diane is rewarded for deals she sources (see section 8).
