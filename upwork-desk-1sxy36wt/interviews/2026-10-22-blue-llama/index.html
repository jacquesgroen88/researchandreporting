04bHow I'd get to 8 qualified calls a month

Unit check: it's 8 a month, about 2 a week. Never say "8 a week".
"I'd work backwards from eight. That's about two a week, so every Friday I'd show you calls booked against that pace, by source, and the leading numbers that tell us whether next month is on track."

The six moves

  1. Agree what "qualified" means in week one. For example: 20 to 100 staff, a decision maker on the call, a real process problem, likely grant-eligible. Otherwise the number gets padded.
  2. One offer to book against: the AI Opportunities Workshop, with the grant up front. "Up to half funded by the Jersey Business Productivity Grant. Book a call to see if you qualify." That gives a cautious buyer a reason to talk now.
  3. The monthly webinar as the main engine. Promoted to the named list, with a booking link on the thank-you page and in the replay email, tagged as webinar-sourced in HubSpot. Three follow-ups: attended, watched later, no-show.
  4. Personal outreach to named accounts every week. About 20 accounts a week from two or three lead segments: a cupcake drop or a relevant case study, then LinkedIn, then a call. A person writes the message and AI does the research.
  5. Referrals and partners, asked for on a schedule. Ask every current client monthly. Build relationships with Jersey Business (it runs the grant), Digital Jersey, the IoD, accountants and IT providers who see the same firms.
  6. Proof every month. One new local case study or testimonial, used in all of the above, matched to the segment being targeted.

05Task 3: "What does marketing look like on a 100k island?"

"With about 1,430 firms big enough to buy from you, marketing stops being about reach and becomes coverage and trust. You can name every account. Eight calls a month is 96 a year, so about 7% of the whole market books a call every year. In three years you've spoken to a big slice of the island, and everyone talks to everyone. Reputation compounds in both directions."

Then three moves

  1. Work from a named list, not an audience. All ~1,430 firms in HubSpot by sector and size, each with a "last touched" date. Add one number next to the 8 calls: the share of the list touched this quarter.
  2. Pick two or three lead segments and give each a case study. Health, education and charity (fastest-growing, and EYECAN is proof). Finance and legal (biggest wallets, so lead with governance). Real estate and professional services (D2 is proof). One new named local case study a month is worth more than any ad.
  3. Personal beats automated. On an island a robotic LinkedIn message gets talked about at the next event. Use AI for research and first drafts, keep a person on anything a named prospect receives, and be careful with Dripify. The cupcake outreach is exactly right for this market, so praise it.

Engines

  • The monthly webinar is the content engine. One session becomes a month of LinkedIn, email and clips. Separate follow-ups for attended, watched later and no-show, each ending in a booking link tagged as webinar-sourced in HubSpot.
  • In-person events by segment. A finance breakfast in town, a charity and health session, and so on. Small rooms, high trust.
  • Partners who already have the trust: Digital Jersey, Jersey Business (it runs the productivity grant), the IoD, Chamber, accountants and IT providers.
  • The grant is the offer. "Up to half funded" belongs in the headline of the workshop campaign.
  • Ads and SEO are the supporting cast. They catch the people who heard about you from a friend and then searched.
