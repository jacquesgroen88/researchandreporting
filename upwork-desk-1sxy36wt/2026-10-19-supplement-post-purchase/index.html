THE LOOM SCRIPT

Talk from the cue card. These lines are the gist, not a teleprompter.

[0:00-0:20] Page 1, face in the bubble

Hi, Jacques here. You asked for four things in this video and I'll get to all of them, but I've started on your flow first, because I think that's more useful than me listing tools. Four minutes.

[0:20-0:55] Page 1, the reframe

The way I'd look at this: AOV is in the brief, but for a subscription brand I'd manage to revenue per customer at day ninety. Here's why. A good one-click upsell takes about four to five percent. That's worth having, but a supplement subscriber stays something like a year. So turning even one in twenty one-time buyers into subscribers beats most order bumps. And a "buy three, save more" offer on the first order can actually delay that subscription decision. That's one I'd want to test, not assume.

[0:55-1:30] Pages 2 and 3, the audit and the offers (this covers their ask 2, tools, in passing)

First I'd walk the whole flow on a phone. I noticed in your hiring history you've had CheckoutChamp funnels built as well as Shopify ones, so step one is mapping which checkout each traffic source actually lands on. Then the offers, in the order I'd build them. The subscription upgrade comes first, then one complementary product, then a small bump, a sixty-day downsell, and quantity breaks last, kept away from anyone likely to subscribe.
On tools: Shopify, including theme code and the checkout, my own subscription billing, GA4, Clarity for session recordings, email and WhatsApp flows in GoHighLevel, and Meta and Google on the traffic side.

[1:30-2:20] Page 4, the idea I'd test first (their ask 4)

You asked for one idea to test first. It's this. Most subscription offers say "save fifteen percent". But someone who's just paid isn't thinking about saving, they're wondering whether it'll work, and that takes more than one bottle. So version B sells the refill date. "Your bottle lasts about thirty days. Want the next one on the fourteenth, before you run out?" A win means a higher take rate and no drop in second orders, because you can always buy take rate with a bigger discount. And ten percent of orders see no offers at all, so the revenue we report is actually extra.

[2:20-3:05] Back to face, case studies (their ask 1, honest numbers)

On results, I'll be straight about what I've got. I run two Shopify stores of my own. On one, a single complementary add-on carried our order value. When it went out of stock, AOV dropped twenty-one percent. That's why page three says one good pairing beats a menu. On the same store, mobile was adding to cart at about a quarter of the desktop rate on ninety-two percent of the traffic, so I rebuilt the theme. On the other store, more than half of checkouts were dropping, and I traced it to a shipping fee people only saw at the last step. I've also run my own monthly subscription product, so I know the billing and churn side from the inside.

[3:05-3:45] Page 7, compensation and close (their ask 3)

On compensation, here's what I'd suggest. The first two weeks fixed at eighteen hundred dollars: the full audit, the offer map, and the refill test live with a holdout. After that it's hourly, around twenty hours a week to start at seventy-five an hour, plus a share of the extra revenue the holdout proves. That way you're mostly paying for results you can see. And the first thing I'd ask you: which checkout are your orders actually going through today? Cheers.
