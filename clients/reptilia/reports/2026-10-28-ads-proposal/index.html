Proposal

A 30-day paid ads test to lift online sales

Prepared for Willem, 28 October 2026 · Google Search first, Meta second, with a clear stop rule

Where we are. Google search visits to the site are climbing again, but online orders have not followed. June to August averaged about R15,500 a month. September closed at R7,523 and October is on track for roughly R9,000.

What we propose. A tightly targeted 30-day ads test, capped at R7,000 of ad budget, aimed only at people already searching for what you sell. We fix the sales tracking first so every rand of spend can be measured, and we stop on day 30 if the numbers do not hold up.

Why a careful test, not a big budget

Ads only pay if they reach ready buyers

Today roughly 7 in every 1,000 search visitors place an order. Paid clicks usually convert a little lower than that. If a click costs around R4, broad advertising would cost about R570 for every order, which is more than the profit on an average order.

So the test does two things at once: it only targets searches with clear buying intent, and it counts the long-term value of customers who come back. Your feeder customers (crickets, frozen rodents, mealworms) are the ones who reorder every few weeks, so winning one of them is worth far more than the first order.

The plan

Four steps over five weeks

  1. Turn on proper sales trackingWeek 1 · no ad spend The site currently reports visits but not sales to Google or Meta. We connect purchase tracking to both, test it with a real order, and hide products that are out of stock so paid visitors never land on something you cannot ship.
  2. Google Search, R150 a dayWeeks 2 to 5 · about R4,500 Three small campaigns, each aimed at people already looking to buy:
    • Feeders and food: searches like "live crickets for sale", "frozen mice for snakes", "mealworms".
    • Local: "reptile shop" and "pet shop" searches in Vanderbijlpark and the Vaal, for store visits and collections.
    • Your name: so anyone searching "Reptilia" finds you first, not a competitor.
  3. Meta (Facebook and Instagram), R100 a dayWeeks 3 to 5 · about R2,000 Starts only once purchase tracking is confirmed working. First, reminders to people who visited the site or added something to their cart and did not finish. Second, short videos of the animals and setups in your store, shown to pet owners near you.
  4. Day 30 review and decisionWeek 5 We put three numbers in front of you: orders from ads, sales from ads, and ad cost per order. You decide whether to continue, adjust, or stop.

The stop rule

When we would stop

  • Our working line is R300 of ad spend per order. We will confirm it against your real margins before launch.
  • If by day 30 ads cost more than that per order and the trend is not improving, we stop or restructure. You do not need to argue for it, it is agreed now.
  • If a single campaign is clearly losing money before day 30, we pause it early and move the budget to what is working.
What we have not proven yet

The honest part

  • We have never run ads for Reptilia, so click costs are estimates (likely R3 to R6). We confirm them in Google's own planner before any money is spent.
  • Ads do not explain September. Visits went up that month while orders halved. Something else changed, and it is worth us understanding it together, because ads will not fix it on their own.
  • The bigger sales are missing. Animals and enclosures used to lift the average order past R1,000. Until the question of selling animals online is settled, the test leans on food and supplies.
