Starting point

What the account looked like when we took it over

The account was running a single Google "Smart" campaign. Smart campaigns are a black box: Google decides everything, you can't see the searches you're paying for, and the reporting counts things like map-direction clicks and website taps as "conversions." It looked busy, but none of those numbers represented an actual person booking a trial.

Before — the old Smart campaign
  • A black-box "Smart" campaign eating the majority of the budget
  • "Conversions" were map clicks & website taps, valued at R1 each. Not bookings.
  • Zero visibility into which searches we were paying for
  • A second paused campaign still carrying wrong real-estate ad copy
  • Trial bookings from Google were being silently dropped, never recorded
After — the rebuild
  • Two focused Search campaigns targeting real gym intent
  • A proper Trial Booking conversion, valued at R300, now firing correctly
  • Full visibility into every search term that triggers an ad
  • Old Smart campaign paused so budget stops leaking
  • Every trial booking is now measurable end-to-end

The work

What we actually changed

1   Paused the wasteful Smart campaign

It was absorbing most of the daily budget while reporting vanity conversions. We paused it so spend redirects to campaigns we can see and steer.

2   Built two Standard Search campaigns

Local Demand Capture targets people searching for a gym in the area ("gym near me", "gym Cape Town", "gym Sea Point"). Brand Defence makes sure that when someone searches "Threezero" directly, they land on us and not a competitor's ad.

3   Fixed the broken conversion tracking the big one

This is the most important fix. Your website was sending trial bookings to the wrong Google Ads account. Every real booking Google generated was being silently thrown away, which is why the account showed "awaiting conversions" for weeks. We rerouted it to the correct account, pushed the fix live, and verified it end-to-end with a live test booking on 13 July. From that date forward, every trial booking from Google is measurable.

4   Cleaned up the plumbing

Added negative keywords to stop wasted spend (competitor-brand and foreign-language searches), demoted the vanity "map click" actions, and turned off Google's auto-apply, which had been quietly making changes to the account on its own.

5   Content & site, feeding the ads a stronger destination

6 blog posts are now published on the site, which gives Google more relevant content to rank and gives ad visitors more reason to trust and stay. We're currently bringing the blog's look and feel in line with the new homepage design so the whole site reads as one polished brand, top to bottom.

Inside the conversations

We read every trial chat. Here's what's working, and where we're losing people

The automated WhatsApp assistant does a genuinely good job: it replies in seconds, asks the right questions, and books people cleanly. Julia (does yoga & Pilates), Conrad (Andrew jumped in personally), Skyla, Yonela, Anelisiwe and Alexander all booked smoothly. But reading the full threads surfaced a clear, fixable pattern of lost bookings.

What's working

  • Instant, friendly replies, prospects get a response within seconds of enquiring
  • The assistant asks goals and books a slot without a human lifting a finger
  • When Andrew steps in personally (Conrad), the prospect lights up, that human touch converts
  • Clean end-to-end bookings for the majority who reply

Where we're losing people

  • ~6 of 17 went silent mid-booking — the bot offered slots and they never replied, with no follow-up nudge to bring them back
  • Slot mismatches — people asked for Sunday, Monday/Tuesday or 6am and were told "not available", then dropped off
  • Wayfinding — one booked prospect couldn't find the studio on arrival ("where is it? I can't see it")
  • Small polish glitches — a "Africa/Harare" timezone label shown to Cape Town clients, and a couple of name hiccups

The single biggest opportunity: the people who went quiet

Roughly a third of enquiries filled in the form, got as far as choosing a time, and then simply didn't reply, and nothing followed up with them. These are the hottest leads we have: they raised their hand and asked to book. A short automated reopener (a gentle "still keen to lock in your trial?" a day later) would recover a meaningful share of them at zero extra ad spend. This is now a priority next step.

Our recommendation: step Local Demand up to R150/day now

At R950/month per membership, a single new member pays back nearly a full month of that increase, and members stay for many months. The impression-share data proves the demand is already there and searching, we just aren't showing up for all of it. We suggest moving now to R170/day total (~R5,100/mo), then, once the newly-fixed tracking has 3–4 weeks of real cost-per-member data, scaling Local Demand further toward R250/day if the economics hold. We won't scale blind, we'll scale on proof.

What's next

Where we take it from here

  • Increase the Local Demand budget to R150/day (details above), to capture the ~40% of local searches we're currently missing.
  • Build a reopener sequence. Automatically follow up with everyone who enquired but didn't attend or didn't reply, a gentle "still keen to book your trial?" a day or two later. Reading the chats, this is the biggest quick win: about a third of leads went quiet mid-booking with nothing chasing them.
  • Let the fixed tracking accumulate data. Now that bookings record correctly, the next 3–4 weeks give us true cost-per-trial and cost-per-member for the first time, so we can bid toward bookings instead of clicks.
  • Close the attendance loop. A lightweight step (a daily WhatsApp prompt you reply to, or a simple Enquiry → Booked → Attended → Joined pipeline) so we can measure true conversion and feed "who actually joined" back to Google to sharpen targeting.
  • Tidy the booking assistant. Fix the "Africa/Harare" timezone label, widen the trial slots on offer (people asked for days/times we weren't showing), and add a "how to find us" line to confirmations, small fixes that recover real bookings.
  • Finish the blog restyle so the whole site matches the new homepage, giving ad visitors a consistent, credible experience end to end.
