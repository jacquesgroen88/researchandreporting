The three options

Pick the level of help you actually want.

Monthly, cancellable on 30 days' notice, and there is no penalty for moving up or down between them. All figures exclude advertising spend, which you pay Google directly.

Option one

Run the process

You want the discipline installed and measured, and nothing else yet.
R7,500per month
  • Enquiry measurement across both businesses, reported monthly against the four numbers
  • Follow-up cadence designed, documented and monitored
  • Two reactivation campaigns a quarter, written and sent
  • Review engine run properly, including replies
  • A monthly working session with you
Does not include advertising, the website, the corporate positioning work or any systems build.
Option two

Process and demand

Good & Proper is the priority and The Acreage is carried alongside it.
R14,500per month
  • Everything in option one
  • Google Ads managed for Good & Proper, pointed at corporate work rather than lead volume
  • The corporate front door: landing pages and quoting language aimed at the six-figure segment
  • The cross-referral system, including the bundled venue and catering quote
  • Quarterly strategy session and a rolling plan
If you are already paying an agency to run the ads, part of this fee replaces that cost rather than adding to it. Bring us that number and we will show you the real difference.
Option three

The group

You want both businesses driven properly, and the systems built as we go.
R24,000per month
  • Everything in option two
  • The Acreage advertised as its own account, once there is a site to send it to
  • Website content and iteration ongoing, rather than a single build
  • Systems included rather than quoted separately: booking flow, automation, the AI enquiry assistant
  • Monthly on-site or extended strategy session
This becomes the obvious choice if the corporate positioning works and the volume of enquiries rises past what option two covers.
Our recommendation is option two, but not yet.

Start with the free week. If it moves, option two is where the year-end window actually gets defended, because it is the only one that touches both the advertising and the corporate packaging. Option one is the sensible answer if you would rather see the discipline hold for a month before spending anything.
