Finding one
1
The Acreage · Severity: critical · Cost to fix: low

Your website is not on the internet, and every channel you own points somewhere else.

Observed What we found

  • Typing acreagevenue.co.za into a browser on 13 August returns a hosting placeholder page reading "Reserved for acreagevenue.co.za". There is no website there.
  • Over https it fails earlier still, with a certificate warning, because the certificate on that server belongs to your hosting provider (*.jnb2.host-h.net) and not to your domain. A visitor sees a security warning before they see anything else.
  • The website button on your Google Business Profile does not point to your domain. It points to facebook.com/acreagevenue.
  • The website field on your Facebook page points to a share.google link, which is your Google profile.
  • Your two biggest channels therefore point at each other in a circle, and neither one points at a website.
Check it yourselfOpen a private or incognito browser window and type your own web address. Then open your Google listing and press the website button, and see where you land.

Why it costs money

There is currently no page anywhere on the internet that tells someone what a function at The Acreage includes, what it costs, what dates are open, or how to book one. A prospect who has decided they are interested has nowhere to go and nothing to send to the other people who have to approve the decision with them.

That last part matters more than it sounds. A venue is almost never chosen by one person alone. It is chosen by a bride and her mother, or an office manager and a finance approver. Whoever likes you has to be able to forward something. Right now they can forward a Facebook page.

Modelled What it is costing

We cannot size this one honestly without your enquiry volume, which is why it is not in the arithmetic table at the end. What we can say precisely: this finding sits underneath findings two, three and five. Every one of them is worth more once there is a page to send people to, and each is capped until there is.

The fix

Restore or replace the site and point both profiles at it. Two concepts already exist for you to react to, built before this diagnostic and using only publicly available images: the warm direction and the editorial direction. If the domain simply needs re-pointing at existing hosting, this is a phone call to your host, not a project.

Finding two
2
The Acreage · Severity: critical · Cost to fix: Free, 20 minutes

Google has you filed as a restaurant that closes at half past three.

Observed What we found

  • Your Google Business Profile's primary category is "Restaurant". "Event venue" is secondary.
  • Your published hours are closed Monday, and 07:00 to 15:30 every other day. Your own Facebook page says the kitchen closes at 17:00 and stays open later for function bookings. The two contradict each other, and Google is the one people read.
  • There is no booking or reservation link on the profile.
  • We searched Google Places for "wedding and function venue" in Midrand and pulled the first 25 results. You are not among them. Accolades, Gallagher, The Forest Walk, Stones, Ambrosia, Plantation Café, Thandekile, The Venue at Midrand, Letlapa, Louisville, ULP House, Kyalami Sport and Kyalami Glen all are.
  • Your 68 reviews reflect this. Google's own summary tags on your listing are cheesecake, steak sandwich, great coffee, pet friendly, horse racetrack view. Two mention corporate events. None mention a wedding.
Check it yourselfSearch "function venue Midrand" on your phone, on data rather than the venue wifi, and count how far you have to scroll to find yourself.

Why it costs money

Primary category is the single largest lever on which searches a Google listing is eligible to appear for. Filed as a restaurant, you compete with restaurants and are largely invisible in the searches where venue decisions actually start.

The hours do a second, quieter kind of damage. A person planning a Saturday evening wedding reads "closes 15:30" and concludes you do not do evenings. They do not phone to check. Nobody phones to check.

And the reviews compound it: 68 people have publicly described you as a lovely breakfast spot with a view. That is a genuinely good restaurant reputation, and it is the wrong reputation for the part of the business that carries the revenue.

Modelled What it is costing

Absence from the local pack for your own category in your own suburb means the enquiries you pay for are close to the only enquiries you get. Local search is the cheapest enquiry a venue ever receives and yours is currently switched off. This is the line in the arithmetic table with the highest ratio of value to effort, because the effort is twenty minutes and no money.

The fix

Primary category to Event venue, Restaurant kept as secondary so you lose nothing you already have. Hours corrected to reflect function trading. Add the booking link once finding one is closed. Google takes a few days to re-rank. This is free, it is reversible, and it is the first thing we would do.

Finding three
3
The Acreage · Severity: high · Cost to fix: Already paid for

The best rating in the market, on a twentieth of the evidence, and it stopped growing in January.

Observed What we found

  • 4.7 from 68 reviews: 55 five-star, 9 four, 2 three, 1 two, 1 one.
  • No venue in Midrand with more than 100 reviews is rated above 4.5. Accolades sits at 4.4 from 1,478. Gallagher at 4.4 from 11,251. Plantation Café at 3.8 from 713.
  • Your review flow, taken from the 40 most recent: 13 in December 2025, then 1 in January, 3 in February, 5 in March, 3 in April, 3 in May, 5 in June, 1 in July.
  • Your most recent review is dated 15 July. As at 13 August that is four weeks with none.
  • You replied to 7 of the last 40.
  • Your Facebook page, which is where your Google listing sends every visitor, displays "Not yet rated (1 review)". The rating you have earned is not visible on the channel you are routing people to.
Check it yourselfOpen your Google listing, sort reviews by newest, and look at the date on the top one.

Why it costs money

Rating and review count do different jobs. The rating tells someone how good you are. The count tells them whether to believe the rating. At 68 against a competitor's 1,478, a first-time buyer reads you as small and unproven, which is the opposite of true.

There is a second thing in the arithmetic that is worth more than it looks. 34 more five-star reviews would move you from 4.7 to 4.8. For Accolades to move from 4.4 to 4.5 would take 296. You can still move your number; the market leader effectively cannot move theirs. That asymmetry is a real asset, and it stops being one the day they start collecting properly.

One observation offered as timing and not as proof, because we cannot see inside your operation: the December spike of 13 follows your ReviewTap stand purchase in November. Whatever produced that month has not run since January.

Modelled What it is costing

Every function you host puts between 50 and 3,000 people on your property who have just had an experience. At your current rate you are converting roughly three of them a month into public evidence. The gap between that and what a systematic ask produces is the finding. The mechanism is in the arithmetic table.

The fix

You already bought the tool. Reinstate the ask at the moment of payment for restaurant covers, and add a second ask to the post-function follow-up, which is where the emotionally strongest reviews live and where you currently ask for nothing. Reply to all of them, not a fifth. Replies are public, they read as care, and they are free.

Finding four
4
Both businesses · Severity: high · Cost to fix: low

Two businesses, one owner, and they have never once mentioned each other.

Observed What we found

  • goodandproperfoodtrucks.co.za contains no mention of and no link to The Acreage. Not on the home page, not in the process section, not in the FAQ.
  • The Acreage's Facebook page, Google profile and domain contain no mention of Good & Proper.
  • Good & Proper's own FAQ states it does not cater weddings. The Acreage hosts weddings.
  • Good & Proper's minimum is 80 guests, rising to 150 in October, November and December. The Acreage takes functions from 50.
  • A Google Places sweep across Gauteng on 13 August returned no business listing at all for Good & Proper Food Trucks (151 result pages scanned, zero matches). For a business buying Google Ads, that is a missing foundation underneath the spend.
Check it yourselfOpen the food truck site and use your browser's find function for the word "Acreage". Then search Google Maps for "Good and Proper Food Trucks".

Why it costs money

These are not two businesses that happen to share an owner. They are two answers to the same question, asked at the same moment, by the same buyer. Someone planning a 200-person year-end function has to choose a venue and choose catering in the same fortnight. You own both answers and you make them go and find each one separately.

Every corporate client Good & Proper caters at somebody else's venue is a venue booking that went to a competitor with your food at it. Every function at The Acreage catered by an outside supplier is margin leaving the group. Both happen silently, because nothing counts them.

The 80-guest minimum is the sharpest version of this. A 60-person function is turned away by Good & Proper and is a perfectly good booking for The Acreage, and right now there is no path between the two.

Modelled What it is costing

This one needs a number only you have: how many functions ran at the venue last year without Good & Proper catering, and how many Good & Proper events ran at a venue that was not yours. Both are countable from your own records in an afternoon. Until then it sits in the arithmetic table as a per-event recovery rather than a total.

The fix

A referral path in both directions, and one bundled venue-plus-catering price that nobody else in Midrand can quote, because nobody else owns both. Then a rule for the sub-80 enquiries so they land at the venue rather than in a polite no.

Finding five
5
Both businesses · Severity: high · Cost to fix: medium

You sell inventory that expires, and you price it as though it doesn't.

Observed What we found

  • No pricing, package or minimum spend is published anywhere for the venue that we could find, on any channel.
  • Good & Proper publishes a guest minimum and describes a "single price per head" model, but attaches no figure to it.
  • Neither business publishes a lead time, a peak-date tier, or a midweek product.
  • Good & Proper's enquiry form requires Company as a mandatory field, and its submit button reads "We'll get right back to you" with no timeframe attached anywhere on the page.
  • Your Google hours describe a business trading 07:00 to 15:30. The highest-value inventory you own, Friday and Saturday evenings, does not appear anywhere as a product.
Check it yourselfTry to find out, from anything you have published, what a Saturday in November costs versus a Tuesday in February.

Why it costs money

A venue is structurally the same business as a hotel or an airline: a fixed number of dates that cannot be stored. An empty Tuesday is not a quiet day, it is stock that expired and can never be sold again. Airlines organise their entire commercial function around this. Most venues price one way and hope.

Pricing every date the same has two effects at once, and they pull in opposite directions. The scarce dates, a Saturday in the season, are underpriced, because they would sell at more. The empty dates have no product at all, because nothing was ever designed for them. You lose margin at the top and volume at the bottom, from the same decision.

The submit button is a small thing that belongs to the same family. "We'll get right back to you" is a promise with no number in it, so it cannot be kept or broken, and nothing in the business is measured against it. In a purchase decided over weeks by more than one person, the speed of the first reply is one of the few things fully in your control.

Modelled What it is costing

This is the largest line in the document and the least visible, because no system anywhere in either business currently measures an empty date. The recoverable number is a function of your calendar utilisation, which we have not seen. It is the first thing we will want on the kickoff call.

The fix

Date tiers with published minimum spends on peak dates. A midweek corporate product designed for the dark part of the calendar rather than discounted off the wedding price. A stated response time on both enquiry forms, and something that measures it.

The other side of the ledger

What is already right, and it is the hard part

The experience is not the problem. It is the asset.

Fifty-five of your 68 reviewers gave you five stars, and the language they use is not the language of a transaction. They name your staff: Queen, Prince, Andrew. They describe warm blankets, the racecourse view, a get-away from the city buzz, family time. Google's own tags on your listing include "welcoming staff" on five separate reviews and "joyful smile" on two.

That is the single hardest thing in hospitality to build and it is done. Everything in this document is about getting more people in front of it. None of it is about changing it.

The location cannot be copied.

A working racecourse with over a thousand horses stabled on it, twenty minutes from Sandton. No competitor in the Midrand set can build that, discount their way to it, or open one next door. It is currently mentioned in passing on a Facebook page.

The honesty box

What we could not check, and why

This list exists so you know exactly where the edges of this diagnostic are. Every item on it is in the onboarding questionnaire, and every one of them replaces an assumption above with a fact.

  • Your Google Ads account. Access has not been granted yet, so we cannot see spend, search terms, landing pages or what a lead currently costs. Given finding one, where that traffic lands is the first thing we will look at.
  • Enquiry volume and source. We do not know how many people contact you a month, or through which channel. Every leak in this document is sized against that number.
  • Your conversion rate from enquiry to booking, and the time it takes. This is the number that decides whether the priority is more enquiries or better follow-up.
  • Average function value and calendar utilisation. Without these, finding five stays a mechanism rather than a rand figure.
  • Your image library. We built the website concepts from publicly available images only, which is why several are placeholders.
  • Anything about your costs. Nothing in this document is a margin claim. Every figure above is revenue, not profit.
If you only do one thing

Findings two and three, this week, before anything is built.

Only one constraint binds a business at a time, and work spent anywhere else produces nothing. Right now yours is not demand and it is not the product. It is that qualified people cannot find you and cannot see how good you are.

Finding two costs nothing and takes twenty minutes. Finding three uses a tool you already own. Both are reversible. Neither needs us, a website, or a rand of ad spend, and doing them first makes everything built afterwards worth more. That order is the whole argument of the Blueprint: fix the foundation, then build on it, because pushing traffic at a broken path only makes the loss faster.
