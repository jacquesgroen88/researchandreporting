1
The shift

Why putting the whole month into the venue is the right call.

The original plan weighted the work toward Good & Proper because that is where the revenue is. Concentrating month one on The Acreage changes the order, not the destination, and there are three good reasons for it.

The venue starts from zero

Good & Proper has a website, an ad account with two years of history and a functioning enquiry flow. The Acreage has none of that. There is no advertising at all, no functions page, no published capacity and no package. Everything built here is new ground rather than an optimisation.

The season is the venue's, not the caterer's

Year-end functions are chosen now, and they are chosen by people looking for a venue. The work that has to land before the window closes is venue work: the functions page, the package, the ads and the showcase.

Good & Proper is not being paused. Its follow-up rule and enquiry sheet still go in during week one, because they cost nothing and they are the highest-value fix in either business. Everything else on that side moves to month two.

5
The advertising account

A new account, in your name, with credit applied for.

The Acreage has never been advertised. That means starting clean, which is an advantage: a brand new account qualifies for Google's new-advertiser promotional credit, which the existing Good & Proper account does not.

How it is set up

  • Created in The Acreage's own name, on your billing details, so you own it outright
  • I am added as a manager, and can be removed at any time without affecting the account
  • Media is billed by Google directly to you, never through my invoice and never marked up
  • Conversion tracking set up to record enquiry value, not just count, so a wedding enquiry and a 300-guest corporate enquiry are not treated as the same thing

The credit we are going for

Google runs a new-advertiser promotion where matched spend inside the first sixty days earns an equivalent credit. We have used this on two other accounts and are aiming for the same here, in the region of R6,000 of free advertising.

The exact threshold and amount are set by Google at signup and vary, so I will confirm the terms when the account is created rather than promise a figure. It is applied for at setup and it cannot be added retrospectively, which is one reason the account gets created in week one.

The sequencing point, and it is the important one.

The campaigns get built in week two but do not go live until week three, when the functions page exists. Corporate searches cost roughly R83 a click against R23 for general terms, and sending those clicks to a homepage with no package behind them is how the existing account spent money without producing corporate work. The page comes first, then the traffic.

Accountability

What gets reported, and when.

Four numbers, every Friday, from whoever owns the enquiry count. Simple enough that it actually gets done, which is where the last system failed.

NumberWhy it is the one that matters
Enquiries receivedThe top of everything. If this moves, the advertising and the profile work are landing.
Quotes sent, and how fastSpeed is the difference between winning and being third in line. Measured in hours, not days.
Bookings confirmedThe conversion rate against enquiries is the single number this whole engagement is trying to move.
Reason lost, in one wordSilence, price, date, or another venue. Four weeks of this tells us more than any amount of analysis.
Rhythm.

A short call each week against two or three specific items rather than a status meeting, the Friday numbers by email, and a proper review at the end of the month where we look at all four against where they started.
