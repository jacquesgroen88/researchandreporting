8
The plan, confirmed

Sequencing is settled. Follow-up first, then the season.

Phase 0

This week, costs nothing

A follow-up rule on every open quote in both businesses. One shared enquiry sheet: date, channel, guests, outcome, one word for why if lost. Quote-validity terms, which Letitia has already proposed herself. The reactivation email to every past year-end client, while the window is open.

Track A

Convert what already arrives

The two-step qualifying form. The bundled quote template so corporate buyers compare packages, not line items, which is how the R96,475 year-end function was lost. The big-proposal assembly line rebuilt so a multi-vendor document ships the same day.

Track B

Go where the money is

The 58 six-figure clients worked as a named list, not an audience. Direct corporate SLAs, because the record invoices currently arrive brokered through events companies. More venue partnerships on the Prison Break and Fourways model. A shower package for the empty daytime calendar.

Track C

Look like what you charge

Brand, photography and the website, now unblocked: the photo shoot, Wessels Media library and drone footage arrived on 2 September. Built to convert a R300,000 enquiry, not a birthday party.

The season play

The Showcase: fill the year-end book by throwing the party first.

You already ran this play once. The April 2025 launch day cost you some champagne and canapés, drew about 30 people, and you called it worth doing. This is the same move, aimed at the exact people deciding their year-end functions this month.

58
names on the six-figure ledger, plus every past year-end client and the events companies that broker your biggest work. That is the invitation list, and it already exists.
The invoice ledger · Finding seven
2-for-1
One afternoon sells both businesses at once: guests stand on the lawn Absa is paying R80,000 for, eating from the trucks they would book. The venue is the pitch and the tasting is the proof.
The cross-referral mechanism · Finding five

You own the venue, the food and the list. A showcase costs you an afternoon and some stock, and it puts the people who sign year-end budgets on your lawn while they are deciding.

How it runsA late-afternoon open day in September, golden hour on the lawns, three or four Good & Proper trucks, the bar open, and one person whose only job is collecting date-and-guest-count conversations. Vendors may well trade their time for the exposure. You already agreed on the kickoff call that venue events should run quarterly, so this is the first one, placed where it pays fastest. A photographer on site also closes the imagery gap in Track C.
