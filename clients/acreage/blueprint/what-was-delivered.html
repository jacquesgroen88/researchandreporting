The evidence layer

The eleven findings, in one list.

Each is set out in full in The Numbers, with its source named underneath.

#Finding
1The constraint is conversion, not reach. Enquiries arrive in volume at both businesses and most of them die. This overturned the first roadmap, which had assumed the problem was visibility.
2The year is not flat. Both businesses concentrate hard into October to mid-December, and year-end functions are chosen in August and September.
3Good & Proper's money is in the tail. 23% of invoices carry roughly 78% of invoiced value, and the six-figure invoices average R386,000.
4Three quarters of invoices are repeat clients. Retention is already doing most of the work and nothing in the business sets out to trigger it.
5The two businesses barely touch. 134 of 147 Good & Proper events ran at someone else's venue; 42 of 55 Acreage functions were catered by someone else.
6Your scarcest date and your emptiest date cost the same. Pricing is flat across a year that is anything but.
7The six-figure ledger, counted. R30.05m across 97 invoices and 58 clients in 31 months, with Q4 carrying 48% of a year's value and 2026 running behind 2025.
8The year-end book was still largely open when this was written, against what the same months delivered the year before.
9Quoting is fast where jobs are small and slow where the money is. Single-vendor quotes ship in hours; multi-vendor six-figure proposals take days.
10Why enquiries fail. Of the last ten lost Acreage enquiries, four died in silence after information was sent. One recorded follow-up converted a three-week-silent enquiry the same day.
11The advertising buys the opposite of the strategy. The large majority of spend went to generic volume terms, and 30% of conversions came from search terms that were never set up as keywords.
