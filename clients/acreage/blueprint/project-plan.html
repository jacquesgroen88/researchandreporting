The Project Plan

Rebuilt on your figures and on what the South African market pays. It points somewhere different from the first draft, and it starts sooner, because the year-end booking window is closing while we write.

For The Acreage and Good & Proper Food Trucks · prepared for Jade Mann

Why this says "preliminary"

It is built on two things: the 23 answers you gave on 25 August, and desk research into what the South African market actually charges. Both are solid. What is missing is the one thing no figure can supply — why your enquiries fail — and that answer could move Track B ahead of Track A.

So read this as a plan with a known hole in it, not a finished document. Everything in Track A is safe against that hole and can start regardless. Tracks B and C firm up after the kickoff call.

The reframe

The first draft was pointed at the wrong business.

Part 1 could only see The Acreage from outside, so the plan it produced was mostly about The Acreage. Your invoice figures put that in a very different light.

Even allowing generously for what that bar leaves out, the asymmetry is the point.

Good & Proper is the larger business by an order of magnitude, it is the one with a stated growth ambition, and it is the one with a live R10,000 a month advertising spend. The plan below is weighted accordingly. The Acreage is not neglected — it gets the pricing work and the cross-referral, both of which are among the highest-return items here — but it is no longer the centre.

The ceiling

The category you are named after is priced at a fifth of the work you do.

What South African event catering charges, per head

Published 2026 ranges. Food truck catering is explicitly marketed as 50 to 75 percent cheaper than traditional plated catering.

Food truck rates from South African market sources for 2026; canapé, buffet, plated and premium bands from a Midrand-specific 2026 price guide. A separate Gauteng guide puts a full year-end function at R650 to R2,200 per delegate all in, and a themed gala with awards at R1,000 to R2,500.

Your own invoices say you do not belong in the top bar.

A mean invoice of R115,500 at food-truck rates of R80 to R250 a head implies somewhere between 460 and 1,440 guests at every event. Your stated minimum is 80, rising to 150 in season. You are not selling food trucks at food truck prices — you are selling event catering management, and being found under the cheapest name in the market.

You have already diagnosed this yourself, about weddings: "couples considering food trucks are often looking for a lower-cost option and tend to approach our individual vendors directly, who can quote more cheaply than we can." That is the category doing the damage. The same mechanism applies to a corporate buyer with a R650 to R2,200 per delegate budget, who is not searching for food trucks.

To be clear about what this is and is not.

This is not yet a recommendation to rename a business you have spent fifteen years building. It is a finding that the category name is doing measurable work against the segment you have said you want, and a request to test it on the call: how did the 44 six-figure clients find you, and what did they think they were buying? If they arrived through referral and relationship rather than category search, the answer is a second front door rather than a new name.

The shape

Three tracks, running in parallel rather than in sequence.

The first draft was a sequence of phases. That was right when we thought the constraint was reach and the work had to be built in order. It is wrong now, because the single most valuable window in your year closes in about four weeks.

What runs when

Track A is dated by your season. Tracks B and C are dated by dependency.

Gauteng year-end guidance: the booking window opens in April, buyers are advised to secure venues before August, premium venues sell out by September, and peak trading runs mid-October to mid-December. Your own Acreage enquiries ran 27 in June, 26 in July and 22 in August.

September to October · the value work

Lift the ceiling

Track A gets more out of the demand you already have. Track B changes what that demand is worth. This is where the difference between a R20,700 function and a R26,000 one lives, and where the six-figure catering segment is either reachable or not.

B1
Answer the eligibility question before designing anything for corporatesWeek 3
South African corporate and public-sector buyers run weighted scorecards where B-BBEE carries defined weight, and a Level 1 supplier receives 135% procurement recognition. Government work additionally requires Central Supplier Database registration, and not being registered is an automatic disqualification. At roughly R21.7m turnover Good & Proper would be a QSE rather than an EME, which means a verification certificate rather than a free affidavit. If this is not in place, the segment you want is partly closed and no amount of marketing opens it.

What would make this wrong.

The Acreage pricing line assumes demand is not price-elastic at that level, and we will not know until the loss reasons are recorded. The cross-referral line assumes ten events can find dates that work, which your own date-collision objection may cap. The reactivation line assumes those clients are contactable and were happy. And the conversion lines assume the failures are addressable rather than structural — if enquiries fail because dates are genuinely full, this is a capacity plan and not a marketing one, and most of the table falls away.

Scope

What this plan deliberately does not do.

  • No rebrand of Good & Proper. The category finding is real, but a fifteen-year-old name is not something to change on desk research. It becomes a question, then perhaps a second route, and only then a brand decision.
  • No new CRM. You built one in Monday.com and never opened it. A shared sheet that gets filled in beats a system that does not.
  • No increase in advertising spend. The existing R10,000 a month is enough to work with, and pointing more money at the current destination would make the loss faster.
  • No content or SEO programme. Both are slow, and both are worth far more once the category and the pricing questions are settled.
  • No changes to anything already booked. Date tiers apply from the next window. Changing terms mid-season spends goodwill for a rounding error.
  • No Acreage website before October. The most uncomfortable call in this plan, and the reason is timing rather than value: it cannot influence a season already being booked.
