How this was sequenced

Three rules decided the order, and none of them is "what is most exciting".

The rules

1. Free and reversible goes first. Anything that costs nothing, can be undone, and does not depend on us is done in week one. Not because it is the biggest win, but because it costs nothing to be wrong about and it starts compounding immediately. Google takes days to re-rank and reviews take months to accumulate, so the cheap things are also the slow things. They have to start first.

2. Nothing gets traffic pointed at it until there is somewhere for traffic to go. Part 1 established that finding one sits underneath findings two, three and five. Pushing spend or effort at a broken path only makes the loss faster.

3. Anything that needs a number we do not have is scheduled after we have it. That is why pricing is last. It is the largest line in Part 1 and it is still last, because designing a pricing architecture without your calendar is guessing in a spreadsheet.

And one constraint that is not ours: the season.

Good & Proper's published guest minimum Observed rises from 80 to 150 for October, November and December. Your own Google review history shows 13 reviews in December 2025 against between one and five in every other month we can see. Two independent signals, both from your own published information, pointing at the same thing: your year end is your season.

Year-end functions are chosen in August and September. That is now. It means the work in Phase 0 and Phase 1 is not merely sensible sequencing, it is dated. A Google category fixed in October is fixed after the enquiries for your best three months have already been placed elsewhere.

Gate 1 · end of September

The question: what are the four numbers, for the first time?

Enquiries per month by channel. Median time to first reply. Enquiry to booking rate. Reviews added this month. Four weeks of counting gives a rough baseline, and rough is infinitely more useful than absent.

What it decides: whether the constraint is now demand or follow-up. If enquiries are low and the booking rate is healthy, Phase 2 pushes on reach. If enquiries arrive and die, no amount of extra traffic helps and the next phase is follow-up instead. This is a real fork and we do not know today which way it goes.

Gate 2 · end of October

The question: did the review rate move, and did a single cross-referral happen?

Reviews are the slowest lever in the plan and the one with the longest tail, so October is the first honest read on whether the ask is running. The cross-referral test is binary and blunt: at least one function that came from a Good & Proper enquiry, or one catering job that came from a venue booking. One is enough to prove the path exists. Zero means the rule was written and never used, which is a management problem rather than a marketing one.

27 October – 28 November · Finding 5

The calendar is stock, and it expires

A venue is structurally the same business as a hotel or an airline. An empty Tuesday is not a quiet day, it is stock that expired and can never be sold again. This phase is last because it is the one that needs your numbers most, and it runs during the season because that is when the demand is visible.

The tasks

1
Map the calendar into three tiers: peak, shoulder and dark
Peak is what sells itself, a Saturday in the season. Dark is what has never sold. Shoulder is everything between. You almost certainly know this by instinct already; the task is to write it down so it can be priced against instead of felt.

Pricing every date the same underprices the scarce ones, because they would sell at more. Applies from January, not to anything already booked. Publishing the minimum also does a second job: it filters out the enquiries that were never going to convert, which gives you back the hours currently spent on them.

Scope

What we are deliberately not doing, and why.

A three-month plan that includes everything is a wish list. Each of these is a reasonable idea being declined for a stated reason, not an oversight.

  • No rebrand. Your identity is not the problem. Fifty-five five-star reviewers describe the experience in warm, specific language, and your logo and colours are already right. Part 3 documents what exists rather than replacing it.
  • No paid social, and no increase in ad spend. Spending more to send people to a domain that returns a certificate warning would make the loss faster. Revisit after Gate 1, when there is a destination and a baseline.
  • No content or SEO programme. Both are slow, and both are worth far more once the Google category is right and a site exists. Starting them now means writing for a listing filed under the wrong category.
  • No email newsletter. There is no list, and building one is a project that competes with the five findings for the same hours.
  • No CRM implementation. A spreadsheet that gets filled in beats a CRM that does not. Revisit when the counting from Phase 1 has outgrown it, which is a good problem and not this quarter's.
  • No new pricing on anything already booked. Phase 3 applies to the next booking window. Changing terms mid-season is how goodwill gets spent for a rounding error.

The edges of this document

What this roadmap assumes.

Same principle as Part 1. Where a recommendation rests on something we cannot see, it is listed here rather than buried.

  • Assumption That your year end is your season. Supported by two observed signals, Good & Proper's published minimums and your December review spike, but not by your calendar. If your peak is actually wedding season in autumn, Phases 2 and 3 swap.
  • Assumption That enquiry volume is low enough that reach is the constraint. Part 1 found five throttles ahead of the sales conversation, which points that way, but if you are already turning enquiries away then the entire plan should be follow-up and pricing instead. Gate 1 exists to catch this.
  • Assumption That the team has capacity to absorb more functions. If the constraint is kitchen, staff or parking rather than demand, more enquiries make things worse rather than better. Nothing we have seen tells us either way.
  • Assumption That you own or can recover the Google Business Profile for both businesses. Phase 0 is free only if you have the login. If either profile is claimed by someone who has left, that is a recovery process with its own timeline.
  • Assumption That the R10,000 a month of Google Ads is yours to redirect. If it is contracted to an agency on a term, the Phase 1 review is a conversation rather than a change.
The honest summary.

Phase 0 is safe against every assumption above. It is free, reversible, needs nobody but you, and is right regardless of which way any of these questions falls. That is the argument for doing it this week rather than after the presentation call. Phases 1 to 3 are drafted in good faith and should be expected to move once your numbers arrive on Wednesday.
