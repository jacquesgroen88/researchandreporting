Where this sits

Part 1 was built entirely from outside, with no access to your accounts, and it said so on every claim. It found five leaks and could size none of them, because sizing them needed figures only you have.

You have now given us enquiry counts, conversion rates, invoice distributions, a twelve-month event calendar and the cross-referral split, with your method shown for each. This document is the arithmetic table from Part 1, completed. Everything here is your data. Where we have interpreted rather than counted, it says so.

1
Finding one

The constraint is conversion, not reach.

This is the assumption that moved, and it moved the whole plan with it. Part 1 concluded that qualified people could not find you. They can. They are finding you and then going away.

Enquiries that turn into work

Every enquiry that reaches either business, and what happens to it. The coloured portion is the part that converts.

The Acreage · booked Good & Proper · invoiced Did not convert

Acreage: 53 event enquiries across June and July 2026 produced 9 confirmed bookings. August excluded because those enquiries relate to later events and have not had time to convert. Good & Proper: 421 email enquiries in 2025 produced 45 invoices from new clients, with repeat clients manually excluded so existing business did not inflate the rate.

Gate 1 of the roadmap asked precisely this, and it is answered five weeks early.

The roadmap said: if enquiries are few but convert well, push on reach; if they arrive and die, more traffic makes things worse and the next phase is follow-up instead. They arrive and die. Roughly 300 event enquiries a year reach The Acreage and over 500 reach Good & Proper. Around 96% of Acreage's and 89% of Good & Proper's produce nothing.

What we still cannot see, and it is the most important thing in this document.

Nothing in either business records why an enquiry fails. Slow follow-up, price, an unavailable date and an unqualified enquiry all look identical in the data, and they need four different fixes. This is the first question on the kickoff call and it is the one that decides where the money goes. Update, 31 August: answered. See Finding ten.

Finding two

The year is not flat, and neither is the work.

Good & Proper events by month, 2025

147 events across ten months of tracker data. November alone carries almost as much as March, April and June together.

Your 2025 event tracker. December's 24 events happened in roughly half a month, because the business closes around the middle of the month, so December's true run rate is the highest of the year.

The same shape shows at Acreage, on the data you have.

Nine events in November 2025 and nine in December, the December nine inside half a month before closing on the 15th. You began recording events electronically only in late October 2025, so the earlier months are estimated from invoices rather than counted. Your stated peak is October to mid-December for both businesses; the quiet months are January, February and the winter period.

Why this matters more than it looks.

Year-end functions are chosen in August and September. Everything in the plan that affects how an enquiry is handled has to be running before the enquiries arrive, not during. It also means the season is the wrong moment to change what you charge people, which is why the pricing work is designed now and applied to the next booking window.

Good & Proper invoices at or above each value, 2025

All 188 Good & Proper invoices issued in 2025. 57% fell below R50,000, 20% between R50,000 and R100,000, and 23% at R100,000 or above.

Your words, and they change the target

"Our objective is not simply to generate a higher volume of smaller enquiries. We want the marketing and sales strategy to help us attract and convert more of the larger, higher-value corporate and event opportunities, while continuing to service profitable smaller work."

That is a different brief from the one the Blueprint was drafted against, and it is the right one. It means the ideal client is not "80 guests or more". It is whoever the 44 six-figure jobs were. We do not yet know what those 44 have in common, and that is now one of the most valuable questions in the engagement.

The advertising is pointed at the other end of this chart.

Roughly R10,000 a month buys leads at R183 each, landing on a general food-truck page. Over the last 30 days that produced 36 calls and 18 form fills. Nothing about that setup selects for a R300,000 event, and a page built to convert a large corporate enquiry looks nothing like one built for volume.

Finding four

Three quarters of Good & Proper's invoices come from people you already know.

Good & Proper invoices by client type, 2025

188 invoices in the year. 45 went to clients won that year; the rest went to clients who were already yours.

New clients, 45 Repeat clients, 143

Derived from your figures: 188 invoices issued in 2025, of which 45 were to new clients arriving through the quotes email process. The 143 balance is repeat business.

The implication is a cheap one, which is why it is worth saying plainly.

A business where three quarters of invoices are repeat has a reactivation asset, not just a lead-generation problem. Every corporate client who ran a year-end function in 2025 is a warm, qualified, already-sold contact for 2026 who costs nothing to reach. At R183 a lead, the ads are the expensive way to reach the other quarter.

Finding five

The two businesses still barely touch.

Part 1 called this a mechanism with no number attached. It now has two, and they are the largest untapped figures in the account.

How often each business used the other

The coloured portion is the overlap: events where the venue and the catering were both yours.

Both businesses involved One business only

Your counts. The two windows differ (Good & Proper is the 2025 calendar year, Acreage is April 2025 to April 2026), so the two overlap figures of 13 are not necessarily the same thirteen events, and should be read as each business's own view rather than a single reconciled number.

Your two objections are good ones and the plan has to be built around them, not through them.

Peak dates collide. Clients want the same high-demand dates, Acreage can only hold so many events in a day, and a very large event may take the venue entirely, so Good & Proper will always need other venues. And small events may be worth more to Acreage catered directly, because Good & Proper earns by marking up vendor costs.

So this is not a target to move 134 events. It is a rule with a guest-count band and a date test, and the two of you have to set those on the call.

Finding six

Your scarcest date and your emptiest date cost the same.

Asked what a Saturday in November costs against a Tuesday in February, you answered: "it's the same, but we may be inclined to offer a reduced rate for a week day in Feb."

November is the strongest month of your year in both businesses, with 27 Good & Proper events and 9 at Acreage. January and February are the quietest. The instinct to discount the weekday is half of the answer. The missing half is that the dates which sell themselves are not priced as though they do.

What exists today is a floor, not a structure.

R100 per head for corporate, R5,000 for private, 50% deposit. That sets a minimum for the events you take; it does nothing about the November Saturdays that would sell at more, and nothing about the January dates that currently sell at nothing. There is a second version of this too: "we have lots of private bookings where they come during the day, we give them a section and they don't pay venue hire."

Finding seven · from the 31 August documents

The six-figure ledger, now counted instead of described.

After the NDA you sent the full list of invoices over R100,000, from January 2024 to 18 August 2026. Three things in it move the plan.

97 invoices · 58 clients · R30,053,691 of six-figure work in 31 months. 2024 carried R9.14m of it, 2025 carried R16.99m, and 2026 to mid-August carries R3.92m. The ledger's 2025 count is 44 invoices, the same 44 that Finding three worked from, so the two sources agree with each other.

One: the year-end quarter is half the year.

October to December 2025 held 19 of the 44 six-figure invoices and R8.22m of the R16.99m, which is 48% of the year's six-figure value in three months. 2024 was even steeper. Whatever is running by September decides the year; whatever ships in November is redecorating.

Two: 2026 is running roughly 41% behind 2025.

January to 18 August 2026 produced R3.92m of six-figure invoices against R6.70m in the same window of 2025. February 2025 did contain a single R1.5m outlier; strip it out and 2026 is still about a quarter down. This is the first number in the engagement that says the leaks are not theoretical: the tail you want to live in is thinning while the enquiry volume holds.

Three: the biggest checks arrive through other people.

Your own colour key splits the list into events companies, catering companies and direct clients. The largest lines route through intermediaries: Huge Events for Entelect at R1.50m and R998k, Blue Platinum for Mercedes-Benz at R1.50m, Tourvest for MTN at R1.12m, Starlight for Wesbank at R948k. Direct corporates are present and repeat well, Leroy Merlin three times, MiX Telematics four, SA Bank Note Company four, Radisson in all three years, but the record-setting invoices are still brokered. That is the arithmetic behind the direct-SLA strategy you described on the call, and it also names the risk: the intermediaries own the relationship on your biggest work.

Letitia pulled the last ten Acreage enquiries that did not convert, with the reason for each. This was the single most valuable unknown in the engagement, because it decides whether the plan is a follow-up plan, a pricing plan, a lead-quality plan or a capacity plan.

The last ten lost Acreage enquiries, by primary reason

Four died in silence. Four went to another venue, with budget a co-factor in three of those. One was budget alone, one a date clash.

Letitia's list, 31 August: Acts House of Education (100 guests, R25,850, budget), Sohail (35, plans changed and moved venue), Alaris (170, R96,475, "more inclusive and feasible package from alternative venue"), Loraine Eksteen (60, no reason), Nelmari Cloete (9, alternative venue), Debbie van Zyl (180, venue plus budget), Maryke (25, wanted external catering), Callan Murray (30–40, no reply), Marcelle de Jager (40, no reply), Tara at Ford Credit (60, date unavailable, then no further replies).

The counter-example that proves the fix, from your own inbox

A 40th-birthday enquiry arrived by WhatsApp on 27 June. Info went out on 29 June. Then silence for over three weeks. On 23 July Letitia sent one follow-up, and the booking was confirmed and paid the same day, for 17 October. The largest loss bucket is silence, and the one time silence was answered with a nudge, it converted on the spot. Letitia herself suggested quote-validity terms in the same email; she is already half designing the system this plan builds.

Two patterns in the losses deserve their own line.

The corporate year-end losses are packaging losses. Alaris did not say "too expensive", they said another venue offered a "more inclusive and feasible package". A per-item quote against a bundled competitor loses on legibility, not price. That lands directly on the bundle work in the roadmap, in the exact season it matters.

Bridal and baby showers keep asking for a package that does not exist. Letitia flags recurring enquiries specifically for bridal-shower packages, and two of the ten losses were showers. That is a productisable daytime offer for the venue's emptiest hours, and it costs a menu card, not a build.

Finding eleven · from the Google Ads account, 3 September

The advertising buys the opposite of the strategy.

You granted access to the Google Ads account and we pulled every campaign since January 2024, plus the account's earlier history. The account is competently run at the execution level. It is pointed at the wrong end of your own revenue chart, and the numbers that prove it were never visible to anyone, because a birthday phone call and a 2,000-guest corporate enquiry both count as exactly one conversion.

Where the money went, January 2024 to September 2026

Three campaigns, R278,543 of spend, roughly 1,540 counted leads (calls and form actions) at a blended R180 each.

Generic food-truck volume terms Corporate and event-catering terms 2024 catering campaign, killed Aug 2024

Campaign exports pulled 3 September 2026: search terms, keywords, landing pages, devices, schedules and monthly time series for Food Truck Hire MXC (Jan 2024 onward), Food Truck Hire Corporate MCPC (Jul 2024 onward) and LD Gauteng Catering MAXC (killed August 2024). The account has bought ads since September 2019 with no conversion values recorded at any point.

The decision to starve the corporate campaign was made on the wrong number.

On the kickoff call you said the corporate budget "apparently didn't work" and was folded into the food-truck terms. On lead count, that is true: volume terms produce leads at R97 to R160, corporate terms at R187 to R353. But the leads are not the same product. [food vendors for events] produced 65 leads at R353 each; your average six-figure invoice is R115,500, and the year-end function you just lost to a competitor's package was R96,475. One conversion from that keyword pays for a decade of the campaign. The volume terms, at R19 a click, buy precisely the price-shopping birthday crowd you named as the audience you are trying to leave, and they land on a homepage that quotes no minimums.

To be fair to everyone involved

Nobody wasted this money, and the agency has kept the account clean. Negative keywords are well maintained: across all three campaigns only about R8,600 ever matched birthday, party, cheap or for-sale searches. What went wrong is a question nobody could ask, because an account that can't see deal value can't tell you which end of the revenue chart to aim at. So the fix costs nothing extra. Turn the corporate terms back on, give them a corporate landing page that carries the bundle, and weight conversions by qualification, so the next budget call gets made on rands instead of lead counts.
