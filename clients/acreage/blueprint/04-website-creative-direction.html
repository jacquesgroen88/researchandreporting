Part 4 · Website

The site has one job, and it is not to look nice.

Its job is to turn an interested person into an enquiry, and to give that person something they can forward to whoever else has to say yes. Everything below is judged against those two sentences.

Prepared for Jade Mann · The Acreage + Good & Proper · 15 August 2026

The insight this whole document is built on

A venue is almost never chosen by one person. It is chosen by a bride and her mother, by an office manager and a finance approver, by a committee that meets on Thursday. Whoever likes you has to be able to forward something to the person who signs.

That single fact reorganises the priorities. It means the most valuable page on the site is not the home page, it is the functions page. It means a downloadable pack matters more than an animation. It means published prices matter more than they would for a restaurant, because the second decision-maker is not going to email a stranger to ask what it costs. They are going to look, and if there is nothing to look at they will look at Accolades instead.

Right now the answer to "can they forward something" is no. There is no page anywhere on the internet that states what a function at The Acreage includes, what it costs, what dates are open, or how to book one.

Where we are

Two concepts already exist. React to them.

Both were built in early August, before the diagnostic, using only publicly available images. They exist to give you something concrete to disagree with, which is faster than describing directions in words.

The warm direction

Forest and cream, a serif display face, a desktop hero video cut from your own Facebook footage. Closest to the way your reviewers describe the place: blankets, quiet, family.

The editorial direction

Oversized type, generous white space, photography treated as the whole argument. Reads more premium and more design-led, and it is far more dependent on having excellent photographs.

Open the editorial concept →

An honest note about both of them.

Neither concept is finished, and the reason is the same in both cases: the photographs are of empty rooms. Several images are placeholders. The editorial direction in particular will either be the strongest thing here or the weakest, depending entirely on whether real photography with people in it becomes available. See Part 3. Judge the layout and the tone now; judge the atmosphere after a shoot.

Our recommendation, offered as a view and not a decision.

Start from the warm direction. It is more forgiving of imperfect photography, it matches the language your customers already use about you, and it does not require a shoot to look right. Take the oversized type and the confidence of the editorial concept into it. If the shoot happens and is excellent, the editorial direction becomes available later; the reverse is much harder.

The tension this structure has to hold.

Sixty-eight reviews have established you as an excellent breakfast spot with a view. That reputation is real, it is earned, and it is the wrong front door for the half of the business that carries the revenue. The site must lead with the venue without disowning the restaurant, because the restaurant is the proof that the venue will feed people well. The restaurant is the evidence. It is not the headline.

The page that matters

The Functions page, specified.

If only one page gets built properly, this is the one. In order, top to bottom.

1
A photograph of a full lawn, and one sentence
"Functions from 50 to 3,000 on a working racecourse, twenty minutes from Sandton." Not a slideshow, not a video that has to load. One image, one sentence, one thing to do next.
2
The capacity table, high up
Each space, seated and standing, with a photograph of that space. This is the first thing a planner looks for and it is currently unavailable anywhere. Put it above the story, not below it.
3
What it costs, as a range, with what is included
"From R____ per head, including ____." A range is enough. The point is not to quote accurately, it is to let a person work out in four seconds whether this conversation is worth starting. "Enquire for pricing" reads as expensive to the people who can afford you and as evasive to everyone else.
Needs your number
4
The download button, and it is a real one
The function pack as a PDF. One page. Capacity, inclusions, indicative price, photographs, contact, next step. This is the single most important element on the page, because it is the only one that travels to the person who signs.
5
The occasions, split by buyer
Weddings, year-end functions, corporate days, birthdays, markets. Different people, different fears, different words. A year-end buyer wants to know you can move 300 people through a lunch; a wedding buyer wants to know it will be beautiful and calm.
6
Catering, and the one thing nobody else can offer
Venue and catering quoted as one number by one group. Every competitor in Midrand has to broker a third party for half of that. This paragraph is the commercial argument of the entire group and it currently appears nowhere on the internet.
7
Proof, in your customers' words
Pull the strongest reviews verbatim, with the reviewer's first name. You are rated higher than every venue of scale in Midrand and that fact appears nowhere you control. Also put the live Google rating on the page.
8
The enquiry form, in the page, not on another one
A person who has read this far should not have to navigate to act. Same form, same stated response time, prefilled with the occasion they were reading about.
The forwardable asset

The function pack is not a brochure.

It is a one-page PDF whose entire purpose is to survive being forwarded, opened on somebody else's phone, and read by a person who has never heard of you and did not ask to.

That constrains it usefully. One page, because two will not be read. Legible at phone size, because that is where it will be opened. Every fact it needs on it, because the person reading it cannot ask you a question. A date on it, because prices move and an undated PDF circulates for years.

It contains: the racecourse in one line, the capacity table, what is included, an indicative per-head range, four photographs, the Google rating, the bundled catering option, and one clear next step with a name and a number attached to it.

It does not contain: a history of the venue, a mission statement, or a paragraph about passion.

Conversion

The enquiry form, and the two mistakes to avoid.

What it asks

  • Name
  • Email and phone
  • Date, or "not sure yet"
  • Guest count, roughly
  • Occasion
  • Anything else (optional, one box)

Six fields. Every additional field costs completions, and everything else can be asked in the reply once a human is already talking to them.

What it promises

A stated response time with a number in it, on the button and in the auto-reply. Not "we'll get right back to you", which cannot be kept or broken and so is never measured.

The auto-reply goes out immediately, confirms the date and guest count back to them, attaches the pack, and says when a person will follow. That reply is doing real work: it is the thing they forward while they wait.

Do not repeat the Company field mistake.

Good & Proper's form makes Company mandatory, which silently turns away every private and family enquiry at the final step. On a venue site that would be worse still, because weddings and birthdays are a large share of what you host. If you want to know whether an enquiry is corporate, ask for the occasion, which is a question a private buyer can also answer.

The second mistake: a date picker that demands a date.

A meaningful share of venue enquiries arrive before a date is fixed, and often the venue's availability is what fixes it. A required date field turns your most flexible and most persuadable buyers away. "Not sure yet" has to be a valid answer.

Build considerations

Four things that are not design decisions but will decide whether it works.

  • Phone first, genuinely. Local venue searches happen on phones, standing in a kitchen, at night. The capacity table and the price range have to be readable without pinching, and the hero video in the warm concept is already set to desktop only so phones never download it.
  • Speed over effect. A venue site loaded with high-resolution photography on a South African mobile connection is a site nobody sees the bottom of. Compress everything, load the hero image first, defer the rest.
  • A valid certificate, and one canonical address. The current domain serves a certificate belonging to the host, so visitors get a security warning before they see anything. Whatever else happens, that has to be fixed, and every channel then has to point at exactly one address.
  • Structured data for an event venue. Marking up the venue, its location, capacity and reviews is invisible to a human reader and is how Google, and increasingly AI assistants, decide what your business actually is. It reinforces the category change in Phase 0 rather than duplicating it.

Scope note.

This document is the creative direction and the first look, which is what the Blueprint covers. The build itself is quoted separately and is not included here. Nothing in this part commits you to it: if you take this direction to another developer, it is specified well enough for them to build from.

The edges of this document

What Part 4 assumes.

  • Assumption That you want enquiries rather than instant online booking. Functions are consultative and a booking engine would be wrong for them. If you want the restaurant to take online reservations, that is a separate and much simpler piece.
  • Assumption That publishing an indicative price is acceptable to you. Some venues refuse on principle. We think it is the single highest-leverage change on the page, but it is your call and the page works without it, just less well.
  • Assumption That one site serves both the restaurant and the venue. The alternative is two sites, which doubles the maintenance and splits the search authority. We would not recommend it.
  • Assumption That Good & Proper's existing site stays where it is. This part adds a cross-referral page to it rather than rebuilding it. Whether it needs its own rebuild is a separate question we have not assessed.
