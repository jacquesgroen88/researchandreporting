For approval · 17 September 2026
The thinking, in three lines

Busy days stay full price. Quiet hours get a reason.

Every offer below points at a window that is currently empty: Tuesday morning, midweek afternoons, Thursday before the weekend, and the hours after the kitchen closes. Saturday morning, which is already your best trade, is left alone.

Each one is a named plate at a set price, never a percentage off. A named offer builds a habit. A discount teaches people to wait for the next one.

Running all year

Six small things, separate from the daily offers.

The second-visit card

Spend R200 or more on a weekend and leave with a card worth R50 off breakfast, valid Tuesday to Thursday for three weeks. It moves people from the days you are full to the days you are not, and it only costs anything when it works.

Any function enquiry can hold their date free for fourteen days, with no deposit. Not a discount, and it gives people a reason to enquire today rather than think about it. This is also what we would offer at the Open Day.

Four things only you can decide

The decisions behind the prices.

  • Friday evenings. Can the kitchen and the team work a Friday until about nine, even once a month? This is the biggest opportunity on the list, and the only one that needs real commitment.
  • Mondays. Staying closed to the public, or opening during school holidays and public holidays?
  • Which offers go on the board at the door and get mentioned at the table. An offer nobody says out loud does not work.
  • How many we start with. Our recommendation is three, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, for four weeks, then add Friday.
Three things we need

So we can tell you what worked.

  • A daily and hourly sales report from the till for the last three months, and a button per offer so redemptions can be counted. Without it we are guessing about which hours are actually quiet.
  • The real kitchen closing time, and whether the bar genuinely trades until 22:00. Your templates say 3pm and Facebook says 15:30.
  • Whoever answers the WhatsApp to note the offer name against each booking. Thirty seconds a booking, and it is how we know which offer to keep.
After four weeks every offer gets a verdict: keep it, change the price, or drop it. We will bring the numbers, not opinions.
