The Logo

Six Marks, Each With a Place

The stacked logo is the main one. The A on its own does the small jobs: profile pictures, the top of a section, the corner of a post.

Stacked, goldThe primary logo, on warm white or a quiet photograph.
Stacked, greenDocuments, menus and anything printed on white.
Stacked, whiteThe website footer and dark photographs.
HorizontalEmail signatures, banners, anywhere wide and short.
The A, goldAbove section headings, in the corner of posts.
The A, whiteOn photographs and Reels, top corner only.
Profile pictureThe gold A on warm white, for Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and WhatsApp.
Good & ProperThe catering partner. Beside the catering copy only, never next to The Acreage logo.
  • Clear space. Keep a margin all round equal to the height of the capital A in THE ACREAGE.
  • Smallest sizes. Stacked logo 150px wide on screen, horizontal 200px, the A 30px.
  • One mark per layout. If the name is already in the headline, the A goes small in a corner rather than the full logo.
  • Never stretch it, recolour it, add a shadow, or retype the name in another font.
Colour

Light First, Green for the Words

Your brand foundation asks for light, sun-drenched and understated, and warns against looking like a golf club. So warm white does most of the work, green carries the headings and the buttons, and gold is used sparingly.

On Social

Four Post Designs

Drawn at Instagram's sizes: 1080 by 1350 for the feed and 1080 by 1920 for Stories and Reels. Every post in the social plan uses one of these.

  • One idea per post. A dish and a price, or a space and a headcount.
  • Photographs run to the edge or sit in a clean frame on warm white. No borders, stickers or filters.
  • On Stories and Reels, keep words out of the top 250 and bottom 340 pixels, where Instagram puts its buttons.
  • The A goes in a corner, gold on light posts and white on photographs. Never the full logo on a post.

See every post in place at the social posts page.

Photography

Natural Light, Real Moments

Yes

  • Daylight and the last hour of it: the lawns, the water, the drive in.
  • People mid-moment: eating, pouring, laughing, walking in.
  • Food on the table with a hand in the frame.
  • Space around the subject, so words have somewhere to sit.
  • Upright framing, so it works as a post and as a Story.

No

  • Stock photographs.
  • Heavy filters, cold edits or flash.
  • Bins, cables, stacked chairs, power lines.
  • Anything saved from someone else's account.
  • Horses or racing as the subject. The estate, not the track.

Three frames from your brand shoot that set the standard.

Words

How The Acreage Sounds

The Voice
  • Warm, refined and understated. Short sentences.
  • Say the thing plainly: the dish, the price, the day, the time.
  • No exclamation marks, and go easy on emoji.
  • Always a next step: a WhatsApp number or a link.
  • Avoid stunning, nestled and hidden gem.
