Smart bidding is chasing taps on a phone number, the ads buy "food truck hire" traffic the website turns away, and both landing pages take about 15 seconds to show on a phone. The findings, in order of cost

  1. High

    Most "conversions" are taps on a phone number

    Over the last 90 days the two primary conversion actions recorded 112 phone-link taps and 60 form events, both imported from GA4 and both counted every time they fire. A tap is not a call. Google's own call tracking logged 1 call from ads and 3 calls from the website number in the same period. Google's native form tag logged 17 forms where GA4 logged 60. Roughly two in three conversions the bidding learns from are taps, and none carry a booking value.

  2. High

    The ads sell truck hire. The website says it does not do truck hire.

    The biggest keyword ever is "food truck hire" (R67.7k). The website FAQ says Good & Proper only provides catering and does not hire out trucks on their own, the form says no weddings and no truck hire to vendors, and the minimum is 80 guests (150 from October to December). Every hire-intent click is paid for and then told no.

  3. High

    Landing pages are slow and generic

    71% of 90-day spend lands on the homepage, 27% on /event-catering/, which is a near copy. Mobile Lighthouse scores 38 and 39, with the main content taking about 15 seconds to paint on a throttled phone. Google rates landing page experience Below average on 8 of the 9 live keywords that have a score.

  4. Medium

    The corporate campaign is mostly buying generic searches

    It spent R6,703 of visible search-term spend in 90 days on 18 conversions (R372 each). Its biggest term is "food truck for hire" (R1,558), a close variant also served by the main campaign at half the price. Searches that actually say corporate cost R123. There is no corporate landing page; its ads point at the homepage.

  5. Medium

    Conversion tracking is controlled from outside the account

    The account shows "Conversion goals and actions are managed by manager account". The goals sit with the managing agency's account, so changing or replacing the agency would also move or break the signal the bidding relies on.

  6. Medium

    Management has slowed to light maintenance

    Launch Digital made 311 of the 316 changes in the two years Google keeps. That was about 25 a month in early 2025 and about 5 a month in 2026, mostly negative keywords. Budgets last changed in November 2024, ad copy last changed on 1 June 2026, and nothing has changed since 20 October.

  7. Fine

    Hygiene is good

    Search network only, no display or search partners, Gauteng targeting, tight exact and phrase match, a steady stream of negatives. Clicks are cheap (R20 to R24) and click-through is high (19%). The problems are strategy and measurement, not neglect.

Ads

  • 18 live responsive search ads: 4 rated Good, 10 Average, 4 Poor. Three of the Poor ones are in the corporate campaign, which spent R3,732 on them in 90 days.
  • Every corporate ad points at the homepage. Display paths like /food-truck/hire and /event/catering do not exist on the site. That is harmless for display only, but it promises pages that are not there.
  • Ad rotation is set to "rotate indefinitely", which works against Maximize Conversions choosing the better ad.
  • Manual bid adjustments for location, device, schedule, age and gender were added through 2025. Under Maximize Conversions most of these are ignored, so they add complexity without control.

How actively the account is run

  • Of 316 changes, 311 were made by four Launch Digital logins. The commonest work is negative keywords (about 100 changes) and bid adjustments (about 70).
  • The budgets (R230 and R100 a day) have not changed since November 2024, while the main campaign loses 20% of its possible impressions to budget.
  • Two JCE reporting seats were added on 3 September. Diane's was removed by the system on 1 November.

What to do, in order

  1. Fix what counts as a lead before touching anything else. Make the Google Ads form tag (counted once) and real calls of 60 seconds or more the primary conversions. Move the phone-link tap to secondary. Reconcile 90 days of form events against Jade's actual enquiries. Then feed booked values back from the quote tool, so a corporate booking is worth more to the bidding than a birthday enquiry.
  2. Bring the conversion actions into the account itself, so the measurement belongs to Good & Proper whichever agency runs it.
  3. Make the ads say what the business sells. Food truck catering for events of 80 guests and more, in Johannesburg and Gauteng. Add negatives for hire-only, rental, trailer, wedding, party, birthday and out-of-area wording, and put the minimum in the ad copy so small events filter themselves out before clicking.
  4. Build two fast landing pages: corporate events (year-end, conferences, family days) and food truck catering Johannesburg. Form near the top, WhatsApp, a thank-you page, photos of the actual trucks, under 3 seconds on a phone.
  5. Give the corporate campaign only corporate searches. Negative the generic hire terms out of it so it stops paying R36 a click for searches the main campaign buys at R20.
  6. Then decide on budget. There is room (20% lost to budget), but more money into the current setup buys more phone-number taps.

Not covered

  • Real lead and booking outcomes. There is no CRM or enquiry data in this audit, so no figure here is a true cost per booking.
  • Whether the form conversion actually fires on submit. Nothing was submitted.
  • Sitelinks, callouts and other assets in detail, plus device and location breakdowns.
  • Anything that needs more than read-only access. Nothing in the account was changed.
