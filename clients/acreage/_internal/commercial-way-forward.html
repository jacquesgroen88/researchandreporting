And the one thing that moved in our favour

Giorgio takes nothing from this deal. That was the single biggest margin variable on the account and it is now closed. Every rand of what follows stays with JCE and JSG.

The trap to avoid.

Do not retroactively reprice what has been communicated. She audited JCE before paying and will remember every number she was sent. The Blueprint stays at R10,000 and the website figure stays honoured for the scope it described. The correction happens through scope and through what comes next, never by moving a number she has already been given.

The unknown that matters

How to find out what she pays Launch Digital.

This is the redirectable budget, and it decides whether a retainer is new money or a reallocation. Four ways, in order of how well they work.

1 · Ask her, and give a reason

Far the best option and entirely in keeping with what she has already shared. She has given you revenue distributions, conversion rates and an invoice tail. A management fee is a smaller disclosure than any of those.

"You said the ads are about R183 a lead and Launch runs them. What do they charge you on top of the media? I ask because if we end up recommending anything about that spend, I need the full cost of it, not just what goes to Google."

2 · Ulrike will know precisely

She handles finance and admin, she supplied the invoicing details, and she paid both deposits. A Launch invoice is a line item she sees monthly. If Jade is vague, Ulrike is the accurate route.

3 · Google Ads access tells you the spend, not the fee

Worth requesting anyway, but be clear about what it does and does not show. Management fees are invoiced separately and never appear in the platform. What access does give you is the account structure, the search terms and the landing page performance, which tells you whether the fee is good value regardless of its size.

4 · The benchmark, so you can sanity-check her answer

South African agencies charge R1,500 to R7,500+ a month, or 10 to 20% of ad spend, with minimum fees commonly R2,500 to R6,000. Published tier examples run R7,500 starter, R10,500 growth, R15,500 scale. At her roughly R10,000 a month of media, a percentage model would be R1,000 to R2,000, so she is almost certainly on a flat fee or a minimum.

Expect an answer between R3,000 and R7,500 a month. Launch publish a pricing guide but do not disclose their own rates in it.

Do not over-rely on the redirect story.

If the fee turns out to be R3,500, redirecting it funds almost nothing and a retainer is mostly new money. Find the number before you build a pitch on it.

Affordability

Turnover is not the same as what they can pay.

The most important caveat in this document.

Good & Proper invoiced roughly R21.7m, but in her own words it "operates as an event management company within the catering space and earns by adding a mark-up to the costs charged by the various vendors." Most of that R21.7m is passed straight through to vendors. If the markup is 15%, their gross profit is nearer R3.3m than R21.7m, and a retainer has to be sized against that, not against turnover.

We do not know the markup. It is not on the form and it is the single most useful commercial number still missing. Ask it.

10%~R2.2m8.0% of GP
15%~R3.3m5.3% of GP
20%~R4.3m4.0% of GP
25%~R5.4m3.2% of GP
Even at the pessimistic end it works.

At a 10% markup a R14,500 monthly fee is about 8% of gross profit, against a modelled R1.75m of recoverable annual revenue. That is defensible in any conversation. But say it in gross-profit terms, never in turnover terms, or it sounds like you are pricing off a number that is not really theirs.

The proposal

One retainer, not a list of projects.

Why a retainer. The work is ongoing by nature — conversion discipline, reactivation, measurement, ads. Selling it project by project means low margin, repeated sales friction, and a client who has to say yes five more times. It also gives JCE recurring revenue, which is what the R105,000 contribution line actually needs.

Why tiers. Her budget is unknown, she is careful with money, and she ran diligence before paying R6,000. Three options let her choose her own level of commitment rather than accept or reject a single number. Put the recommended one in the middle.

Why it starts after Track A has produced numbers. Track A is nearly free and mostly her team. Letting it run first means the retainer conversation happens against visible results rather than a promise — which is exactly how this client buys.

The awkward one

The website was quoted at R7,000 and that is now a problem.

Not because it is too cheap in isolation, but because Part 4 has specified something considerably larger than what R7,000 described.

The wrong move

Going back and saying it is now R18,000. She was given a number in writing in July. Changing it after she paid a deposit is exactly the kind of thing she was checking for when she ran diligence on JCE, and it would cost more in trust than it gains in fee.

The right move

Honour R7,000 and scope the build to fit it. A tight, well-made site covering the functions page, the enquiry route and the forwardable pack. Then everything Part 4 additionally specifies — the capacity tables, structured data, ongoing content — happens as retainer work rather than as a bigger one-off.

This protects margin and trust at the same time.

A R7,000 fixed-price bespoke build that takes forty hours is R175 an hour and is where agency margin dies. The same R7,000 against a tight fifteen-hour templated scope is R466 an hour, and the iteration that would have blown the budget becomes recurring revenue instead. Sell the build small and the improvement ongoing.

What Tier 2 would do for the business

R14,500 a month is about 14% of the R105,000 monthly contribution line from a single account, and it would immediately become JCE's largest retainer — the current base is ThreeZero at R4,299 and TO Strand at R1,899. It also dilutes the Pixsan concentration risk, which currently sits at roughly a third of monthly recurring revenue.

What could go wrong.

  • The R10,000 becomes the anchor. She has just bought a substantial piece of strategy for R10,000. A R14,500 monthly fee is more than that every month, and the contrast will be felt. Pre-empt it: the Blueprint was a fixed piece of thinking, the retainer is continuous execution, and they are different things.
  • Their margin is thinner than we think. If the vendor markup is under 10%, the affordability table gets uncomfortable and Tier 1 becomes the honest recommendation.
  • Swapping Launch out is not free for her. Thirty days' notice, a handover, and the risk of disruption inside her busiest season. It may be better to leave the ads alone until January and start with Tier 1.
  • New concentration. Replacing Pixsan concentration with Acreage concentration is not a fix. It is better, because it is a second large client rather than a substitute, but it is worth watching.
  • We have not been paid the balance yet. R4,000 is still outstanding and the completion date is being moved. Land that cleanly before opening a pricing conversation.
