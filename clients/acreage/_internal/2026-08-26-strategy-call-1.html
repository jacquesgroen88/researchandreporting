The roadmap said: if enquiries are few but convert well, push on reach; if they arrive and die, more traffic makes things worse. They arrive and die. Roughly 300 event enquiries a year reach The Acreage and over 500 reach Good & Proper, and the overwhelming majority of both go nowhere. Say something like this "We told you every assumption would be replaced by your numbers. Here is the first one that moved, and it is the big one. A low conversion rate can mean weak follow-up, or it can mean the enquiries are poorly qualified in the first place — a real possibility for Good & Proper, whose ads run to a generic page at R183 a lead. Both roads lead to qualification and follow-up rather than more traffic, but they are different fixes. Ask her which she thinks it is rather than asserting it.

Finding 4 was a mechanism with no number attached. It now has two.

She has already thought about this and her two objections are good. Do not pitch past them.

One: peak dates collide. Clients want the same high-demand dates, Acreage can only host so many events a day, and a very large event may take the whole venue — so Good & Proper will always need other venues. Two: for smaller events it can be more profitable for Acreage to cater directly, because Good & Proper earns by marking up vendor costs.

So the play is not "move the 134". It is a rule with a size band and a date test attached. Bring that to her rather than a target.

The fourth finding

She confirmed the pricing leak in her own words.

Her answer, verbatim "Saturday Nov vs Tuesday Feb: Acreage — its the same but we may be inclined to offer a reduced rate for week day in Feb or a weekday in general. Good & Proper — the same."

A November Saturday and a February Tuesday cost the same. November is her strongest month of the year: 9 events at Acreage, 27 at Good & Proper. January and February are the quietest. The instinct to discount the weekday is half right; the missing half is charging more for the dates that sell themselves. The scarcest inventory in the business is priced identically to the emptiest.

Two related things worth probing.

She wrote: "We have lots of private bookings where they come during the day, we give them a section and they don't pay venue hire." That is free use of the asset — possibly deliberate to drive restaurant covers at R155 a head, possibly just habit. Ask which. Current minimums are R100 per head corporate, R5,000 private, 50% deposit, which is a floor rather than a peak-date premium.

Two risks in her answers

Read these before you recommend the review engine.

The review stands are running on one waitress, and Jade is uncomfortable about it.

Her words: two stands are on the bar, two are packed away, waiters were told to take them to tables where they felt the experience had gone well, and it is no longer happening consistently. Then: "we became concerned when a disproportionate number of reviews began mentioning one particular waitress. As she can be quite pushy, we were concerned that customers may have felt pressured to leave reviews, which is not the type of review-generation approach we want."

That is a client raising an integrity concern unprompted. Do not walk in recommending "restart the ask" as though it is a switch. What she needs is an ask that reaches everyone at a defined moment rather than one chosen by a waiter's judgement, which removes the inconsistency and the pressure problem together. This makes Part 5 stronger, but only if you lead with her concern rather than the tool.

They built a CRM in Monday.com and never used it.

Mentioned in passing under how enquiries are recorded. This is the best predictor in the whole form of what will fail here. It validates the Part 5 recommendation to start with a shared spreadsheet, and it is worth saying out loud that you know about it and are deliberately not proposing another system they will not open.

The three that decide the shape of the strategy

25 min

Without these you cannot write a strategy, only guess at one. None of the three is answerable from her form.

  • Why do enquiries not convert? The single most important question on the call. Five of six Acreage enquiries and eight of nine at Good & Proper die and nothing records why. Slow follow-up, losing on price, dates unavailable, or never serious? Each points at a completely different strategy. Note she told you who follows up a quiet quote and not whether or when.
  • Can they absorb more work at all? Section F is empty. November already runs 9 Acreage events and 27 at Good & Proper. If they are at the ceiling in peak season then the strategy is margin and price, not volume, and the plan inverts a second time.
  • What does winning look like in three months, and what is available to spend? Both unanswered. Without a target the strategy has no shape; without a number you risk designing something she will not fund.

Repeat business, the big jobs, and the brand question

15 min
  • 76% of Good & Proper invoices are repeat clients. What happens to a corporate client after their event? Does anyone contact them the following year?
  • What do the 44 jobs over R100,000 have in common? Referral, repeat, industry, season, who introduced them. She has told you she wants more of these without telling you where the existing ones came from. This is the ICP for the whole strategy.
  • Do the two brands stay separate? She answered "not sure". It drives Part 3 entirely, the cross-referral design, and whether this is one website or two.

The cross-referral rule

15 min

Put 134-of-147 and 42-of-55 up, acknowledge both her objections first, then design the rule with her rather than presenting one.
