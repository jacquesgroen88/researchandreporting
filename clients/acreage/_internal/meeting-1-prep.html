Read this first

Sixty seconds of orientation.

She answered 23 of 58 questions yesterday, in bursts from 13:23 to 19:20, and the quality is unusual — she showed her method on nearly every one. Everything in sections A to E is answered. Sections F to K are untouched: capacity, voice, website, photography, background, access.

Her figures overturned our central assumption. We told her the problem was that qualified people could not find her. They can. About 25 event enquiries a month reach The Acreage and roughly one in six converts. The plan has been rebuilt and re-weighted as a result.

And the biggest thing we learned is arithmetic, not opinion. Multiplying her own counts and means puts Good & Proper at roughly R21.7m invoiced last year against about R1.1m of Acreage function invoices. We had been leading with the smaller business.

The one thing to leave this call with: why enquiries fail. Nothing in either business records it, and it decides whether this is a follow-up plan, a pricing plan, a lead-quality plan or a capacity plan. Everything else is secondary.

One correction to carry in, so you do not overstate it.

Industry benchmark for venue enquiry-to-booking is high-teens to low-twenties on all enquiries. Her 17% is roughly at market, not failing. The honest framing is "you convert about as often as venues do, at a lower value than venues do" — not "you are losing five out of six".

Framing

Why this meeting exists.

What it is

Strategy call 1 of 2, the kickoff the Blueprint has always included. Its job is to replace the last of our assumptions with her answers, and to agree the shape of the plan before we finalise it.

It is not the presentation. That is call 2, and the whole point of today is to make call 2 worth having.

What she is expecting

She paid the full R6,000 deposit on 17 August, both entities, without being chased. She then spent six hours filling in a form. She is invested and she is engaged.

She audited us before paying, so she will notice whether we actually read her answers. Quoting her back to herself is the single highest-value thing you can do today.

The first two minutes

How to open.

Suggested opening "Thanks for the answers — genuinely, that is the most thorough onboarding anyone has sent us, and you showed your working on nearly every one, which is rare. Both deposits came through on the 17th, so we are square there.

I want to be straight with you about something before we start. Your numbers changed our mind about the main thing. We told you the problem was that people could not find you. Your figures say they can find you, and the question is what happens after that. So today is less about us presenting and more about me asking, because there is one thing your data cannot tell me and it decides the whole plan."

Why open by conceding.

Part 1 promised that every assumption would be replaced by her figures. Leading with an assumption that moved proves the promise was real. With a client who ran diligence on you before paying, that is worth more than a confident plan.

2 · Why enquiries fail

20 min
Goal: the single most important answer of the call. Do not leave without it.
What she told us

"Approximately 1 in 6 enquiries (around 17%). This is based on June and July 2026, with 53 event enquiries resulting in 9 confirmed/invoiced bookings."

Good & Proper: "421 new enquiries through the quotes email process, of which 45 resulted in invoices from new clients."

On follow-up, the entire answer was: "Acreage - Letitia, Good & Proper - Andrew/Steph".

On what is recorded: "Acreage - nothing formal. Good & Proper - is documented. We also had a whole CRM system built in Monday.com that we have never used."

Set it up like this "You get about twenty five event enquiries a month at Acreage and roughly one in six books. That is actually about normal for venues, so I am not here to tell you that number is bad. What I cannot see is why the other five go away — and price, a date you could not do, a slow follow-up and someone who was never serious all look identical in your data. They need four completely different fixes."
Ask
  1. Take the last ten enquiries that did not convert. What actually happened to each?Push for specifics. This is the answer that shapes the plan.
  2. Do you lose on price, on a date you could not do, or on silence?
  3. When a quote goes quiet, who chases it and after how long? Is that a rule or does it depend?She named the people but never the interval, which usually means there is no rule.
  4. Are the Google Ads leads as good as the ones that come to you directly?Tests lead quality vs follow-up as the cause.
  5. You built a CRM in Monday.com and never used it. What went wrong there?Best predictor in the whole form of what will fail. Ask it warmly.
What good looks likeThree or four concrete loss reasons, and an honest answer on whether anyone chases quotes.

3 · Where the money actually is

15 min
Goal: confirm the revenue split, and find out where the six-figure jobs come from.
What she told us

Good & Proper 2025: "Importantly, there is a significant higher-value segment within the business" — 44 invoices at R100,000+, 28 at R200,000+, 15 at R300,000+, 8 at R500,000+, 6 at R900,000+, and 4 over R1 million. "The largest individual invoice was approximately R1.5 million".

"The mathematical average across all 2025 invoices was approximately R115,500, but this is heavily influenced by the larger events and therefore doesn't accurately describe a “typical” booking."

"Our objective is not simply to generate a higher volume of smaller enquiries. We want the marketing and sales strategy to help us attract and convert more of the larger, higher-value corporate and event opportunities, while continuing to service profitable smaller work."

Acreage by contrast: "The mathematical average across the period was approximately R20,700 per invoice, while the median was approximately R8,000."

The reframe "I did some arithmetic on your own numbers. 188 invoices at an average of R115,500 puts Good & Proper somewhere around twenty one million rand last year. Acreage's function invoices come to about one point one. Even allowing for the venue-hire-only distortion you flagged, we have been building a plan that led with the smaller business. That has changed."
Ask
  1. Is that roughly right? What did Acreage turn over including restaurant and bar?
  2. The 44 invoices over R100,000 — how did those clients find you?Referral, repeat, ads, an introduction? This is the ICP for the entire strategy and it is nowhere on the form.
  3. Is there anything those big jobs have in common? Industry, season, type of event, who introduced them?
  4. Which of the two businesses makes more margin per rand?Volume and margin may point in opposite directions.
  5. 76% of your invoices last year were repeat clients. What happens to a corporate client after their event — does anyone contact them the next year?
What good looks likeA pattern behind the 44, and confirmation of which business the plan should lead with.

4 · The category ceiling

15 min
Goal: test whether the name caps the price. Handle carefully — it touches a fifteen-year-old brand.
She has already diagnosed this herself, about weddings

"Good & Proper does not do weddings because, historically, wedding enquiries have converted very poorly… We believe couples considering food trucks for weddings are often looking for a lower-cost option and tend to approach our individual vendors directly, who can quote more cheaply than we can so we waste our time quoting."

On the ads: "The Google Ads are run by Launch Digital… It sends people to www.goodandproperfoodtrucks/food-truck. Google Ads currently generates leads at approximately R183 per lead… 36 phone-call leads and 18 website lead-form submissions."

On what she actually sells: "Good & Proper operates as an event management company within the catering space and earns by adding a mark-up to the costs charged by the various vendors."

The finding, framed as a question rather than a verdict "Here is something from the market research that I want to test with you rather than assert. Food truck catering in South Africa is published at R80 to R250 a head and openly marketed as the cheap alternative to plated catering, which runs R450 to R750. Your average invoice is R115,500. At food truck rates that would be four hundred to fourteen hundred guests at every single event, and your minimum is eighty.

So you are not selling food trucks at food truck prices. You are selling event catering management under the cheapest name in the market. And you already told me exactly this about weddings — that people come looking for a cheap option and go direct to the vendors. I think the same thing is happening to you on corporate."

Ask
  1. Has a large corporate ever gone elsewhere because "food trucks" sounded too casual for their event?
  2. When you quote R300,000, do you present as a food truck company or as an event caterer?
  3. Is the Good & Proper name something you would ever revisit, or is it the brand and it stays?Ask openly. If it stays, the answer is a second front door, not a rename.
  4. What is your B-BBEE level, and do you hold a verification certificate?Corporates score it; government requires it. At your turnover you would be a QSE, which needs a certificate rather than a free affidavit. This may gate the segment she wants.
  5. Are you registered on the Central Supplier Database, or on any corporate preferred supplier lists?
What good looks likeHer honest read on whether the category hurts, plus a clear answer on B-BBEE.

5 · The cross-referral rule

12 min
Goal: design the rule with her. She has already pre-empted the obvious pitch.
What she told us — including two good objections

Acreage functions not catered by Good & Proper: "42 of the 55." Good & Proper events at another venue: "134 of the 147."

"Firstly, particularly during peak event season, clients often select the same high-demand dates. There is a limit to how many events Acreage can accommodate on any one day, and for a very large event, Acreage may only be able to host that one event."

"Secondly, for smaller events, it can potentially be more profitable for Acreage to provide the catering directly."

On the sub-minimum enquiries: "We get 10 or less a month. Sometimes we do them, if we are not too busy. Otherwise we decline and advise that its below our minimum."

Lead with her objections, not past them "Your two caveats are right and I am not going to argue with either. So rather than a target, what I want is a rule: a guest-count band where Acreage always gets offered, and a date test for when it obviously cannot work."
Ask
  1. What guest-count band should always be offered Acreage first?
  2. What does Acreage charge Good & Proper for venue hire?She gave one example — R55,000 on a big year-end — but never a rate. The bundle cannot be priced without it.
  3. The ten-a-month you turn away for being under minimum — could those simply go to Acreage instead of a decline?
  4. Who would own that rule day to day?
What good looks likeA band, a date test, and the venue-hire rate.

6 · The calendar as inventory

8 min
Goal: agreement in principle on date tiers. Nothing changes for anything already booked.
Her answer, verbatim

"Saturday Nov vs Tuesday Feb: Acreage - its the same but we may be inclined to offer a reduced rate for week day in Feb or a weekday in general. Good & Proper - the same".

"Acreage - 50% deposit. Corporate bookings = R 100 per head. Private bookings = R 5000… We have lots of private bookings where they come during the day, we give them a section and they don't pay venue hire."

Busiest: "October, November and the first half of December." Quietest: "January and April/winter period."

The point "Midrand venue hire averages about twenty six thousand. Your mean is twenty and your median is eight, and you hold the best rating of any venue in the market. A November Saturday and a February Tuesday cost the same. The instinct to discount the February weekday is right — what is missing is charging more for the dates that sell themselves."
Ask
  1. What is the most you have ever charged for a November Saturday?You have the floor. You have never had the ceiling.
  2. The daytime private bookings that use a section for free — deliberate, to drive restaurant covers, or just habit?
  3. Are you open in principle to peak, shoulder and dark pricing from the next booking window?

7 · Collect, do not debate

10 min
Goal: unblock delivery. 37 fields are still outstanding, all in sections F to K.
Work down in this order
  1. Can anyone log in to the Google listing?Both free fixes depend on it. Ask this one first.
  2. The real trading hours, restaurant and functions separately.Google says closed Monday and 07:00–15:30. Her Facebook page contradicts it. We are about to correct it and do not know which is true.
  3. Photography — links, photographer, rights, and whether we can use Facebook and Instagram.Biggest single blocker on the website. Her library has no people in it at all.
  4. Capacity of each space, seated and standing, including the small lapa.
  5. What breaks first if enquiries double?November already runs 9 Acreage events and 27 at Good & Proper.
  6. Do the two brands stay separate? She answered "Not sure".
How to hand off the rest "The rest of the form is website detail, photos and access. Rather than spend the call on it, could you finish it this week? Same link, it remembers where you were."

8 · Close and book

5 min
Goal: three things start this week, and call 2 is in the calendar before you hang up.
Agree these start now, not after the presentation
  1. The Google listing fixes — category, hours, website field.Free, reversible, twenty minutes, needs only the login.
  2. Reactivate the 2025 year-end clients at Good & Proper.The booking window closes around September. This is the cheapest revenue available and the list already exists.
  3. Start recording enquiry outcomes in a shared sheet.A sheet, not a system. She already abandoned one CRM.
The close "Three things I would start this week rather than wait for the document, because your year-end booking window closes in about four weeks and the plan should not hold them up. Then Wednesday the 2nd we present the whole thing back — the strategy, the plan, the numbers, and what it is worth. Does the 2nd at ten work?"
