01 — What we found

Five things, all checkable in under ten minutes.

We went through both businesses before writing anything. These are findings, not opinions, and each one has the evidence attached.

01

Your reputation is excellent and almost invisible

You are rated 4.7 on Google. That is not the problem. The problem is volume: a venue with a handful of reviews does not surface in local search, no matter how good the score is. You are winning on quality and losing on the number that decides whether anyone sees you.

Rating, verified on Google Maps 6 August 2026
The Acreage — rating4.7
Nearest large competitor — rating4.4
The Acreage — number of reviews68
Nearest large competitor — number of reviews1,472

You beat them on the score and carry roughly one review for every twenty-one of theirs. Review counts are from our July audit and worth re-checking; the 4.7 was confirmed live today.

02

You already own the tool that fixes it, and it is not switched on

You bought a ReviewTap stand in November 2025. That is our product, so we can say plainly what it does and does not do on its own: a stand on a counter collects reviews only if asking is built into the shift. Without that, it becomes an ornament. Nothing in your review count suggests it is being worked.

Why this matters

This is the cheapest win available to you. No ad spend, no new supplier, no build. It is a process change plus something you have already paid for.

03

Your website is not loading securely, and has not been touched since 2021

acreagevenue.co.za has no valid certificate of its own. It is serving your hosting provider's certificate, so every visitor typing your address gets a browser security warning before they see anything. Over plain http it serves a hosting placeholder, not your site.

Evidence, checked 6 August 2026

Certificate presented on acreagevenue.co.za:

CN = *.jnb2.host-h.net — issued to the hosting platform, not to your domain.

The page served over http is 2,357 bytes, last modified 30 December 2021. You are paying for a venue that 3,000 people can stand in, and the front door on the internet is a security warning.

04

There is not a single person in your photography

We went through your whole image library. Every shot is an empty room, an empty lawn or a plate of food. It is well shot, and it cannot do the one job a venue's photography has to do, which is let someone picture their own wedding or their own year-end function in the space.

Proof that this is the constraint, not a style choice

You sent us two photographs on 6 August: a function with guests raising a toast, and a full lunch service. We dropped both into the website concepts in this kit. Compare those two sections against the rest. That difference is the whole argument, and it cost nothing but the right photograph.

05

Good & Proper is spending without attribution

Roughly ten thousand rand a month goes into Google Ads with no clear line back to booked work. That is not a claim that the spend is wasted. It is a claim that nobody currently knows, which is a different and more fixable problem.

What we would do first

Audit that account before anything else, at no charge, whether or not you go further. If it is underperforming you will see exactly where. If it is fine, you will know that too, which is worth having before you scale it.

02 — What it is worth

The honest version of the numbers.

We are not going to project a revenue figure for your business from the outside. What we can show you is what the same diagnosis produced somewhere else, with real numbers, so you can judge the shape of it.

Izi Travel — the closest comparison we have

A South African travel business with the same underlying problem: enquiries were arriving and quotes were going out fast, but almost nothing was closing. The bottleneck was not lead quality or pricing. It was that nothing followed up after the quote.

BEFORE
54 → 1
Fifty-four leads quoted. One booking. No follow-up sequence existed.
AFTER
R322,000
Travel booked in a single 30-day period, from 230 enquiries and 219 quotes, at roughly R32 per lead.

Their bookings were worth more each than a function booking, and your volumes are different. The transferable part is the diagnosis: the money was never in getting more enquiries, it was in what happened to the ones already sitting there.

Applied to you, the same logic says this. You do not have a demand problem. You have a 4.7 rating, a 3,000 capacity venue, and a location five minutes off the N1. What you do not have is a working front door, a reason for reviews to accumulate, or a way of knowing which of your marketing rands did anything.

06 — Your website, built twice

You said you struggle to find designers whose work you like.

That is the one objection a proposal cannot answer, so we did not try. We built The Acreage, in two deliberately different directions, using your monogram, your setting, your 4.7 rating, your real capacity and your own photography. Neither is a template and neither is a variation of the other.

Direction one

The warm one

Deep forest greens and cream, full-bleed photography, a softer and more traditional hospitality feel. On desktop the hero plays a short film of your restaurant in service.

Open direction one →
Direction two

The editorial one

Oversized type, a great deal of white space, numbered sections, near-monochrome with a single gold accent. Colder and more confident, closer to a design magazine than a venue brochure.

Open direction two →

The typefaces were taken from your own logo, not chosen to taste. Your submark is a high-contrast didone "A" and your wordmark is wide-tracked geometric capitals. The display face answers the monogram and the text face answers the wordmark. That is an argument you can check rather than a preference you have to trust.

If you like neither, say so. That is genuinely useful and it costs you nothing. Design fit is not something to argue a client into, and we would rather do the strategy well and have you take the build elsewhere than sell you a site you quietly dislike.
