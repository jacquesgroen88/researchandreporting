THE REAL PROBLEM

Four gaps
quietly costing TPI enrolments.

None of these are TPI doing anything wrong. They're the natural friction of running a growing training business on manual tools. Each one is a specific, fixable gap. The right system closes all four at once.

01
Everything lives in separate places
A manual tracker for courses, Google Calendar for scheduling, and contacts scattered across inboxes and platforms. There's no single place to see a learner's full history: how they first enquired, which course they did, whether they've been followed up. When it all lives in separate tools, things fall between them, and the whole operation depends on one person remembering.
02
Enquiries are chased platform-by-platform
Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram, email, WhatsApp. Each one checked and answered on its own. A question that comes in on Instagram while the office is deep in Facebook waits, and a slow reply is a lost enrolment. Jumping between platforms all day is real work that produces nothing except keeping up, and it's easy for a message to simply be missed.
03
Outbound campaigns are typed one at a time
TPI already runs the right play: gathering business contacts and emailing them to introduce the company. But it's done by hand, one message at a time. That caps how many businesses can be reached and how often, and there's no easy way to fire a promotion or a "next course dates" blast to every past attendee at once. The motion is proven; it's just being done manually, so it can't scale.
04
Leads go quiet, and no one knows where they came from
An enquiry that isn't ready to book today sits still, and a month later there's no prompt to pick it back up. And when a slow month comes, there's no way to see where enrolments actually came from (Google, Facebook, referrals, the business emails), so it's hard to know where to push. Without tracking, TPI is flying on instinct instead of on what the numbers say is working.
💡 Close these four and the office runs from one screen, no enquiry goes unanswered, campaigns reach hundreds at once, and David can see exactly which channels fill classes, so the marketing effort goes where it actually pays.

The unit that matters is one more seat in a class that is already running. The trainer, the travel and the venue are paid whether that seat is filled or not. Only materials and certification move with it. So a recovered seat is close to pure contribution, and that is the fair way to measure this.

Contribution above is R950 a seat less roughly R200 a head for materials and certification. That R200 is our estimate, not your figure, so it is the first thing worth checking together. If your real per-head cost is higher, break-even moves up, and you should know that before you commit to anything.

On those numbers, break-even is 6 extra seats a month at the intro rate and 8 at the standard rate. TPI runs ten to fifteen classes a month, so that is under one recovered seat per class. Everything above it is upside, and none of it counts a whole extra class filled from a campaign, or the admin hours the office gets back.
