ReviewTap, run through the HubSpot RevOps process

The full HubSpot method applied to ReviewTap's own data: audit the tech stack and data, set shared lifecycle definitions, measure the flywheel, work out unit economics, check pipeline health and the forecast, write the SLAs, then remove friction in order of rand impact. Sources pulled today: Shopify (store), Meta Ads, Google Ads, the CRM (GHL: 361 opportunities, 1,537 conversations, 1,676 contacts). The main window is the six months 1 May to 31 Oct 2026.

The verdict
LTV:CAC 2.7 : 1

The store motion pays for itself on the first order (paid CAC of R382 against about R828 gross profit per order), but it sits just under HubSpot's 3:1 line because only 11.3% of buyers ever buy again. The sales-led motion has closed 4 of 160 corporate opportunities. The biggest problem is not demand. It is that the three teams' systems (store, CRM, billing) do not talk to each other, so nobody can see a customer's whole lifecycle. That is the exact problem RevOps exists to fix.

Internal working document. Gross margin, close-rate and lift figures marked "estimate" are labelled assumptions, not pulled numbers. Currency ZAR.

The method

What HubSpot means by RevOps

HubSpot defines Revenue Operations as one function that aligns marketing, sales and customer service around a single revenue goal, a single data set and a single process. It teaches it on three pillars and a flywheel. This report runs each step on ReviewTap in the order HubSpot teaches it.

Operations

Process across the whole customer journey: lifecycle stages, handoffs, SLAs, pipeline stages, removing friction between teams.

Enablement

The tools, playbooks and training each team needs to run the process: CRM set-up, templates, sequences, the tech stack.

Insights

Clean, unified data and reporting: funnel conversion, CAC, LTV, pipeline velocity, forecast, attribution.

The flywheel, not the funnel. HubSpot replaces the one-way funnel with a loop: Attract strangers, Engage them into customers, Delight customers into promoters who feed the next turn. Growth comes from adding force (what speeds it up) and removing friction (what slows it). For ReviewTap that is unusually literal: every stand sitting on a customer's counter prints the reviewtap.co.za URL.

The nine steps run below

  1. Tech stack audit: which system owns which data, and what is connected.
  2. Data audit: CRM hygiene, duplicates, missing fields.
  3. Lifecycle stages: one shared definition of Lead, MQL, SQL, Opportunity, Customer, Evangelist.
  4. Flywheel metrics: Attract, Engage, Delight, with conversion at each stage.
  5. Unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC, payback, MRR.
  6. Pipeline and forecast: win rate, cycle length, velocity, next 60 days.
  7. SLAs: marketing to sales, and sales to service.
  8. Friction removal plan: ranked by rand.
  9. Operating cadence: the weekly, monthly and quarterly RevOps rhythm.

What's happening
Each tool answers its own team's question. Marketing sees ROAS, the store sees orders, the CRM sees chats. The customer record is split across three systems keyed on different identifiers (Shopify uses 0..., the CRM stores +27...).

Make the CRM the system of record. Pipeline design problems
  • Hospitality pipeline has no Won or Lost stage. Its last stage is "Had our first meeting", so it can never report a win. 191 deals, 0 won by construction.
  • "Didn't Qualify" is a stage, not a status. 19 deals sit there marked open.
  • Jacques Pipeline is empty (0 deals). Maison de Blom, signed 2 Nov, is not in any pipeline.
  • Corporate pipeline mixes sales and service: Onboarding, Active Client and Churned are customer stages bolted onto a sales pipeline.
  • Deal values mix setup fees and monthly fees in one number, so pipeline value is not comparable.
Identity and segmentation
  • No lifecycle stage field. Stage lives in over 25 tags (seg-c-cold-unanswered, seg-d-warm-nosale, tier-a, tier-b, vip, whatsappvip...). Tags overlap and nobody owns their definitions.
  • Shopify knows 840 buyers; the buyer tag appears on 437 CRM conversations. Roughly half of all buyers are invisible to any Delight motion run from the CRM.
  • Shopify holds 1,339 customer profiles; the CRM holds 1,676 contacts. No shared key.
  • Two revenue buckets are blended: RT product (stands, cards, Pro: the exit asset) and JCE Systems builds (IsoBella Vita, Maison de Blom: agency work, retained). The CRM does not distinguish them.
The fix (one sitting, effort S). Add a single-select Lifecycle Stage contact field and a Revenue Bucket field (A: RT product, B: JCE Systems). Make value, owner, source and lost reason required on stage change. Add Won and Lost to Hospitality. Move Onboarding, Active and Churned into a separate Customer Success pipeline. Retire the empty Jacques Pipeline or use it for Jacques-closed deals like Maison de Blom. Freeze new segmentation tags: anything new becomes a field value.

What is strong

Payback is instant. Hardware margins are very high (custom stand roughly R72 landed and printed against R649; card roughly R30 against R299). Every new customer covers their own acquisition cost on day one. In HubSpot's terms this is a business that can afford to buy growth, provided it holds the 2.5x ROAS floor.

What holds it under 3:1

LTV is almost entirely the first order. Lifetime revenue per buyer (R1,319) is only 24% above AOV (R1,062). The fix is not cheaper acquisition; it is a second purchase. Lifting repeat rate from 11% to 20% at the same AOV adds roughly R95 of LTV per buyer and takes LTV:CAC past 3:1 (estimate).

HubSpot pipeline velocity = open qualified deals x win rate x average deal value / cycle length = 9 x 2.5% x R7,650 / 44 days = about R39 a day, or roughly R1,200 a month. The 100 Hitlist leads do not count yet because none are qualified. Even if all 100 qualify at today's win rate, they produce about 2.5 wins.
Where the deals died

Of 126 lost corporate deals, 46 were lost at New Lead and 55 at Contacted: 80% died before a meeting. That matches the July diagnosis: the bottleneck is booking, not closing. Of 21 that reached Meeting Booked, 20 were lost, and 4 of 4 proposals were lost. One lost deal in 33 carries a reason, so the CRM cannot say why.

What the 4 wins share

One came by referral and closed in 9 days (Charissa Ash, R12,000). Two were upsells or group expansions of existing relationships (TPI, Clarkys x3). Every win was a warm relationship; the cold list push of June and July produced none.

Step 9 · Operating cadence

The RevOps rhythm, fitted to the existing rituals

Weekly (Monday, inside /weekly-review)
  • Sessions, store CVR, checkout completion
  • Blended ROAS vs 2.5x floor
  • New customers and paid CAC
  • MQLs, SQLs, meetings, wins (sales-led)
  • Inbound-last conversations older than 24h (target 0)
  • Repeat orders this week
Monthly (with /finances close)
  • LTV:CAC and repeat rate
  • MRR by bucket, new, churned, failed
  • Pipeline velocity and win rate
  • Lost-reason breakdown
  • Forecast vs actual
Quarterly
  • Re-run this report (same windows, compare)
  • Full-funnel and device audit (Oct 2026 prompt)
  • Review lifecycle definitions and SLAs
  • Tech stack: retire or connect anything outside the CRM

The weekly scorecard is deliberately eight numbers, not a dashboard. HubSpot's guidance is that RevOps reporting should change a decision each week; anything that does not belongs in the monthly or quarterly view.

Caveats and data gaps

What this report could not measure

  • Gross margin (78%) is an estimate built from known landed costs (stand ~R44 plus print ~R26 to R30, card ~R3 plus print R27.50), payment fees and courier on orders above R1,000. It moves CAC payback and LTV, not the conclusions.
  • CAC is blended: all paid spend divided by all new customers, including those who came direct from installed stands. True paid-only CAC is higher; Meta's own CPA is R439 on 264 pixel purchases.
  • Google July conversions (32 non-brand) came from the over-counting Shopify app tag and are not used. Google spend is used; its conversions are not.
  • Speed to lead could not be measured to the minute: the conversation search API returns only the last message. The inbound-last count is the proxy.
  • MRR is read from Payfast confirmation emails and the CRM, not the Payfast dashboard. TPI and Clarkys billing need verification before quoting.
  • Meta has one staged draft (re-enabling a deleted ad set) that cannot publish. Harmless, but worth discarding in Ads Manager.
  • Lifetime buyers (840) and repeat rate come from Shopify's customer counts since the store began (first orders Q1 2025).
