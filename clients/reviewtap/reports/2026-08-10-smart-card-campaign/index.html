Why it is built this way

  • No discount. The warmest segment of the list sees this first. Discounting your best buyers is money left on the table.
  • Bridges from what they own. The first two lines describe their stand doing its job, so the new product arrives as an extension and not as a pitch.
  • Save Contact is the hero benefit. It is the one thing paper cannot do and it is instantly obvious.
  • The Google review row is called out. It ties the new product back to why they know us.
  • Reply invitation. A soft second conversion path for people who will not buy off a button.

Why it is built this way

  • Mechanism before benefit. People who did not buy off email 1 usually did not picture it working. Three numbered steps fix that.
  • Two real reviews, both naming digital business cards. Chris Botha and Bonny-Jean Fitzpatrick are real Google reviews, not invented copy.
  • Objections answered in their own words. App, changing details, durability. Those three kill most of the hesitation.
  • The MyForeverBaby mockup earns its place. A pink card next to a pink page proves brand matching in a way a sentence cannot.
  • Team block near the end. It is the AOV lever. One order of eight is worth eight orders of one and takes the same effort.
  • Two identical buttons, not one. This is the longest email in the set. The first button sits the moment the mechanism lands, the second after every objection is answered.
  • The Google rating is a badge, not fine print. It was a grey underlined line and read as a disclaimer. It is now a bordered block with stars, linked to the live profile so the claim is checkable.

Why it is built this way

  • The deadline is real, not invented. It is the code expiry, with the true 5 to 7 business day print time stated next to it, so the urgency survives being questioned.
  • The discount arrives last. Everyone who was going to pay full price already has.
  • Risk reversal right under the button. Nothing prints until they approve the design, which is what actually happens.
  • An explicit exit. Saying nothing else is coming makes the list trust the next campaign.
  • P.S. carries the team ask. Most read P.S. before the body.
  • Nothing sits below the ask. The product shot was originally under the button, where it did no work. It now sits above the offer block, so the email ends on the close.

Design notes

  • The real ReviewTap logo is in every header, served from the store's own CDN (reviewtap.co.za/cdn/shop/files/) so it loads in every client and never needs re-hosting.
  • The footer keeps a text wordmark on purpose. The logo's "Tap" is black and would vanish on the navy footer. The footer follows the real logo's colour logic instead: orange "Review", light "Tap".
  • Colours were corrected against the actual logo. The first pass had the wordmark inverted, navy "Review" and orange "Tap". The real mark is orange #FFA84D "Review" and black "Tap".
  • Buttons stay at #ff6a00, not the logo orange. White text on #FFA84D is close to unreadable. Small uppercase labels use #e35c00 on white and the lighter #ffa84d on navy, so both directions stay legible.
  • Every image is sized to its own aspect ratio so no hero runs away down the page. 440px for the two portrait shots, 290px for the tall card-only shot.
  • Checked at 375px. Single column, no horizontal scroll, buttons full width at 52px tall, and the email 2 headline break is suppressed on mobile so it does not orphan a word.

Before you fire

Four things need a decision or a check. Everything else is ready.
  • The offer in email 3 is my recommendation, not your decision. It is hardcoded as 15% off, code TAPCARD15, expiring Friday 21 August. Edit it inside the block marked EDIT ZONE in email-3-last-call.html. The code does not exist in Shopify yet, so create it before sending.
  • Confirm the Google rating. Email 2 says 4.9 from 29 reviews, verified on 7 August. It is quoted with a live link to the profile, so check it still reads 4.9 on the day you send.
  • Swap the merge field syntax. The emails use {{first_name}} and {{unsubscribe_url}}. In the Reviewtap System that becomes {{contact.first_name}}. In Shopify Email it is a different tag again.
  • Suppression list. Pull everyone who has already ordered the Custom Smart Business Card, and re-suppress buyers between each send.
  • Send a test to yourself first, on a phone, and tap the button. All three emails are single column and mobile first, but nothing replaces looking at it.
