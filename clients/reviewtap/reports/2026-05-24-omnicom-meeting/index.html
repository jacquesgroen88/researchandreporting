The Core Opportunity
Four angles — lead with the one most relevant to how they open the conversation

1. ReviewTap as a client value-add that Omnicom deploys at scale

Omnicom's clients have hundreds of physical touchpoints. ReviewTap turns every one of them into a review engine. This is the primary pitch.

Lead Angle
The Argument
  • Omnicom manages media for brands with large physical footprints — MTN stores, Diageo on-trade venues (bars, restaurants), Unilever retail, SA Tourism properties
  • Every one of those locations has a Google Business Profile. Most have too few reviews to rank prominently on Google Maps
  • ReviewTap solves this at the location level — one tap, one review, zero friction for the customer
  • For Omnicom, this becomes a productised offering they recommend alongside their digital media strategy — and it makes their paid campaigns perform better because the destination is review-optimised
  • The more reviews a location has, the higher it ranks on Google Maps, the more organic traffic it receives, and the lower the effective CPL from Omnicom's paid media
The Numbers That Land This
  • Google reviews are the #1 local SEO ranking factor — more reviews = better Maps visibility = more organic traffic without ad spend
  • Businesses with 100+ reviews convert at 3.5x the rate of businesses with under 10 reviews
  • ReviewTap delivers 3–5x more reviews in month one — a result Omnicom can show their clients immediately
  • For a brand like MTN with 200+ SA stores: a single ReviewTap card per location is R60,000 in product. A full branded rollout across all locations is R200K–R400K. That's a meaningful new revenue line for Omnicom to recommend
  • Pilot ask: propose a 10-location test with one client. Results in 30 days. Then scale.

2. Reputation as a paid media performance lever

Omnicom spends billions on media buying. ReviewTap makes that spend more efficient by improving what happens after the click.

Performance Angle
The Argument
  • When Omnicom runs a Google or Meta campaign for a hospitality or retail client, that ad drives traffic to a location or a Google Business Profile
  • If that profile has 14 reviews at 3.8 stars, the conversion from ad click to visit or purchase is significantly lower than a profile with 200 reviews at 4.7 stars
  • Omnicom is paying to drive traffic to a leaky funnel. ReviewTap fixes the funnel.
  • Framed correctly, ReviewTap is not a standalone product — it's a media efficiency tool that improves the ROI of campaigns Omnicom is already running
How to Frame It for the Digital Lead
  • "Your campaigns are driving traffic to locations. We can ensure those locations convert that traffic at the highest possible rate."
  • Position ReviewTap as the "last mile" performance layer — after the media buy, before the conversion
  • This connects directly to Omnicom's Omni platform narrative — they're building a connected performance stack, and ReviewTap slots into the local reputation layer
  • Ask: "What does your current local SEO and reputation offering look like for clients with physical locations?"

3. White-label for Omnicom's agency brands

Branded ReviewTap devices carrying the client's brand — deployed under an Omnicom agency recommendation.

Partnership Angle
The Proposition
  • ReviewTap already offers custom-branded stands and cards (NFC + QR) — the product is built for white-label
  • Omnicom recommends branded ReviewTap devices to clients as part of their digital performance stack — devices carry the client's branding, not ReviewTap's
  • OMD or PHD earns a margin on every deployment they recommend — creating a new recurring revenue stream tied to client campaigns
  • ReviewTap handles manufacturing, shipping, and fulfilment — Omnicom handles the client relationship and takes a distribution margin
What a Pilot Looks Like
  • Choose one Omnicom client with 10–20 physical locations (a hospitality group, retail chain, or quick-service restaurant brand)
  • Deploy branded ReviewTap cards at all locations — cost to client is modest, result is measurable within 30 days
  • Omnicom presents the review volume uplift and Maps ranking improvement as a case study — can be used in future client pitches
  • If results hold, scale to full client base — Omnicom becomes ReviewTap's enterprise distribution channel in SA
  • Pilot pricing: offer Omnicom a significant discount on the pilot batch in exchange for a case study and preferred agency status

4. First-mover advantage before competitors enter SA

The NFC review space in SA is early. Omnicom can lock in the category with ReviewTap before a competitor arrives.

Strategic Angle
The Argument
  • NFC-based review generation is well established in the UK, US, and Australia — it's entering SA now
  • ReviewTap is the only established local player — SA-built, SA-priced, with 1,000+ customers already
  • International competitors will arrive. The agency that partners first owns the category narrative for their clients
  • For Omnicom, being the agency that introduced this technology to their clients is a differentiation story worth more than the product margin
Why This Matters Strategically
  • Omnicom is investing in its Omni platform and "data-fuelled" positioning — partnering with an innovative local tech product fits that narrative
  • Agency pitches increasingly require tangible innovation stories — "we deployed NFC review technology across 200 client locations" is a strong one
  • Being locked in as ReviewTap's preferred SA agency partner means no competitor agency can offer the same product
  • Ask: "Is Omnicom currently offering any physical-digital reputation management products to clients?"

Talk Tracks — What to Say and When
Use these as guides, not scripts. Adapt to where the conversation goes.
01
The Opening — How to Frame ReviewTap in the First 60 Seconds
Use when they ask "tell me about ReviewTap"
Say This
"ReviewTap is a physical NFC and QR device that lets customers leave a Google review with a single tap of their phone. No app, no friction, no typing a URL. We're seeing 3–5x more reviews for businesses in the first month.

We've built it for the SA market specifically and we're at 1,000+ customers already. What I want to explore with you today is whether there's a fit to take this into the enterprise space through Omnicom's client base — because the brands you work with have the exact footprint this is built for."
Why This Opening Works

It's specific, not vague — "single tap, no app, no friction" is a real product claim, not marketing language.

The 1,000+ customers line establishes that this isn't a concept — it's a working product with real adoption.

It immediately frames the conversation as a partnership pitch, not a product demo. You're not asking them to become a customer — you're asking them to become a distribution partner. That's a much more interesting conversation for a digital lead at a media group.

The phrase "the brands you work with have the exact footprint this is built for" shows you've done your homework without having to list their clients — it invites them to self-identify the fit.

02
The Media ROI Bridge — Connecting ReviewTap to Their Core Business
Use when they ask "how does this connect to what we do?"
Say This
"When you run a local campaign for a retail or hospitality client — Google Ads, Meta, whatever the channel — that campaign drives traffic to a physical location or a Google Business Profile. If that profile has 12 reviews at 3.6 stars, a meaningful percentage of that traffic doesn't convert. You've paid to get them there and the destination let you down.

ReviewTap fixes the destination. More reviews, higher rating, better Maps ranking, higher conversion on the traffic you're already buying. It's the last-mile performance layer that most media plans are missing."
Why This Works

A digital lead at a media group thinks in performance metrics and ROI. Framing ReviewTap as a media efficiency tool — not a standalone product — speaks their language directly.

"The destination let you down" is a tactful way of identifying a problem they've probably felt but never named. It creates the "that's exactly what happens" moment.

The phrase "last-mile performance layer" gives them a way to describe ReviewTap internally to their leadership and to clients — it slots into their existing vocabulary around performance marketing.

03
The Enterprise Pitch — Reframing ReviewTap from SMB to Scale
Use when they say "this looks like a small business product"
Say This
"The current product is SMB-priced because that's where we started. But the technology is enterprise-ready. What I'm proposing is a version designed for your clients — custom branded devices carrying the client's logo and colours, bulk pricing, a management dashboard showing review volume and rating trends across all locations, and white-label reporting that Omnicom presents as part of your digital performance delivery.

You're not reselling a review stand. You're delivering a branded reputation management capability to your clients. That's a different conversation."
Why This Works

It proactively addresses the "that's for small businesses" objection before it becomes a dead end.

The white-label framing is critical. A media group doesn't want to recommend a third-party product — they want to present a service they own. Positioning it as "a branded reputation management capability" gives them ownership of the offering even if ReviewTap is the underlying product.

The mention of a "management dashboard" and "white-label reporting" signals that the enterprise version is real and buildable — even if you need to develop those features. It's worth discussing the roadmap honestly if they bite.

04
The Pilot Close — How to End the Meeting with a Commitment
Use in the last 10 minutes to drive a concrete next step
Say This
"I'm not asking for a full commitment today. What I'd like to propose is a small pilot — pick one client with 10 to 20 physical locations. We deploy branded ReviewTap devices, run it for 30 days, and I give you a clean results report showing review volume before and after, Maps ranking movement, and any measurable traffic change.

If the results hold — and they will — you have a case study, a new client offering, and a repeatable model. If they don't, you've spent very little to find that out. Which client comes to mind first?"
Why This Works

"Which client comes to mind first?" is the pivot that moves from hypothetical to real. It's a question that's easy to answer — and answering it means they've mentally accepted the pilot concept.

The "if the results hold — and they will" phrasing projects confidence without being arrogant. You're not asking for permission — you're describing what will happen.

Offering a clean results report (not just "it went well") shows you're thinking in the language of a media agency — they need data they can put in front of clients and leadership.

The low-risk framing ("very little to find out") removes the fear of commitment. Most agencies won't say no to a structured pilot with minimal downside.

Objections & How to Handle Them
Every likely pushback — answered before it stops the conversation
This feels like a small business product — our clients are enterprise.
The current retail pricing is SMB-positioned, but the technology is the same one used by enterprise brands globally. What we're proposing isn't the R299 card — it's a custom-branded, multi-location deployment with a management dashboard and white-label reporting. The product scales — the question is whether the commercial model works for your clients. That's what the pilot is designed to test.
Google's terms of service say you can't incentivise or direct customers to leave reviews.
ReviewTap doesn't incentivise reviews — there's no reward, no discount, no prompt to leave a positive review specifically. The device simply makes the act of leaving a review faster and frictionless. Reducing friction is not the same as incentivising. Google's guidelines prohibit fake reviews and review gating — ReviewTap does neither. It's the equivalent of putting a "review us on Google" sign at your counter, except it works.
We already have a reputation management or local SEO offering.
That's actually good to hear — it means the client need is already validated. The question is what that offering looks like at the location level. Most reputation tools are monitoring and response tools — they manage reviews but don't generate them. ReviewTap generates volume at the physical touchpoint, which most digital tools can't touch. It complements an existing reputation offering rather than replacing it.
We'd need exclusivity or we can't commit.
Exclusivity is on the table for the right structure. If Omnicom commits to a minimum deployment volume and a defined rollout timeline, we can agree to preferred agency status in SA — meaning ReviewTap won't actively pursue or partner with your direct competitors (PHD/OMD competitors). Full exclusivity depends on the commercial commitment level, but preferred status is achievable immediately.
Our clients manage their own vendors — we don't typically recommend products like this.
Understood — and this doesn't have to be a formal recommendation. The lightest version of this partnership is simply introducing us to two or three relevant client contacts where you can see the fit. We handle the client conversation, close the deal, and Omnicom earns a referral fee. No endorsement required, no formal partnership documentation needed to start.

Questions to Ask the Digital Lead
Ask these to qualify the opportunity and understand where the real fit is
  • 01
    "What does your current local SEO and reputation management offering look like for clients with physical locations?"
    This tells you whether there's a gap to fill or a competing service to position against. If they have nothing, ReviewTap is an easy add. If they have something, you need to differentiate at the generation layer.
  • 02
    "Which of your clients would feel the most immediate impact from an improvement in their Google Maps visibility?"
    Gets them thinking in specifics rather than abstractions. The client name they give you is your pilot target — and it signals they've mentally accepted the premise.
  • 03
    "When you present performance reports to your clients, is local search ranking or Google Business Profile performance part of that reporting?"
    If the answer is no, ReviewTap helps them add a new performance metric to their client reporting — which strengthens their retention. If yes, they already care about this data and ReviewTap improves it.
  • 04
    "Are there clients where you're running paid local campaigns but feel the conversion rate at the location level is lower than it should be?"
    This is the media ROI angle. A yes answer means ReviewTap directly solves a problem they've already identified — and the sale becomes about the solution, not the problem.
  • 05
    "How does Omnicom typically evaluate new technology partnerships — is there a formal procurement process, or can you move quickly at the digital division level?"
    Tells you who else needs to be in the room and how long the decision process will take. Large agency groups can have long procurement cycles — knowing this early prevents wasted time chasing the wrong approval path.
  • 06
    "Is there an innovation or new products budget within your digital division, or does this need to be funded through client campaigns?"
    Determines where the money comes from. If there's a discretionary innovation budget, a pilot is much faster to approve. If it needs to go through a client campaign, you need a specific client to champion it.

Ideal Outcomes from This Meeting
Leave with at least one of these — in order of preference
  • 1

    A named pilot client and a 2-week timeline to scope it

    The digital lead names a specific client, agrees to introduce you to their contact, and commits to a 30-day pilot. This is the best possible outcome — everything else flows from a successful pilot.

  • 2

    A follow-up meeting with the relevant client team or account director

    If they can't commit to a pilot today, getting an introduction to the account director managing the right client is the next best outcome. You're one conversation closer to the decision-maker.

  • 3

    Agreement to present a formal partnership proposal

    They're interested but need a written proposal to take internally. If this is the outcome, ask them to specify exactly what the proposal needs to include and who else needs to see it. Send it within 48 hours.

  • 4

    A clear "not now" with a specific reason

    Even a no is valuable if you understand why. A "we don't do vendor partnerships at the digital division level" tells you to approach a different door. A "timing isn't right" tells you when to come back. Clarity is better than ambiguity.
