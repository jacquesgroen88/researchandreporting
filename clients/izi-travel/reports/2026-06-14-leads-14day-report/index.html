The headline: two numbers, and why they differ

Google Ads and the ReviewTap system are counting different things. Google counts conversion events; the system holds actual people you can contact. Here's how they reconcile — and the surprise is the opposite of what we expected.

Google Ads says

76 conversions
38 Mauritius + 38 Zanzibar. This is an event count — the quote-form action can fire more than once per person and includes interactions that never became a saved contact.

The system actually holds

62 real leads
Unique contacts that landed in ReviewTap/GHL. 50 came straight off the two ad campaigns' quote forms; 12 arrived through other paths.
Read it this way: The two ad campaigns drove 50 quote-form leads into the system (24 Mauritius + 26 Zanzibar), yet Google reported 76 conversions for them — Google is over-counting by ~52%, which is normal for event-based tracking. Separately, 12 more leads came in via the quick-quote widget, package landing pages, and direct/untagged entries (your "they just WhatsApp'd us" leads) — these are invisible in the per-campaign Google report but are very much real. Net: trust the system (62) for real lead volume, and treat Google's 76 as a directional signal, not a headcount.

What this means & next moves

  • Real lead rate is strong: 62 leads in 14 days ≈ 4.4 leads/day at R60.76 each — and 4 more already landed today (14 Jun) before this report ran.
  • Don't bill or judge on Google's 76. It's inflated ~50% by multi-fire events. The system's 62 is the source of truth for contactable leads; use it for the rev-share/lead reconciliation.
  • The 12 "off-report" leads matter: quick-quote widget, package pages and direct/WhatsApp entries never show in the campaign report. Worth tagging these at capture so we can attribute them properly going forward.
  • Confirm the conversion action: the in-account "conversion" definition should be verified — if it's firing on a button click rather than a completed quote, that explains most of the gap and is an easy tracking fix.
  • Scale headroom remains: search impression share sat low (Mauritius ~11%, Zanzibar ~16%) across the window — budget/bid room to capture more of the same quality traffic.
