Training Notes

Getting Terri onto the Opportunities Pipeline

Internal facilitator notes for the GHL training session — the lead flow, where Terri fits, and the simplest possible way to start so it never feels overwhelming.

⚑ INTERNAL — for the person running the training, not the trainee

1. How leads actually reach Terri

Before training her on the pipeline, she needs the full picture of what happens before a conversation lands in her lap. Most of the early work is already automated:

1. Google Ads — prospect clicks an ad and submits the lead form with their trip details (destination, dates, budget, etc.)
↓
2. Automatic opener message — the system instantly messages them back, echoing the details they submitted. No human needed.
↓
3. AI conversation bot takes over — runs the qualifying questions automatically and keeps the lead warm.
↓
4. Terri steps in — once the lead is qualified / asks for something the bot can't do, she takes over the human conversation and owns the deal from here.
The mindset shift to land: Terri is not responsible for catching raw leads or doing the qualifying — the ads, the opener, and the AI bot handle the top of the funnel. Her job starts at the warm, qualified stage. The pipeline is simply where she tracks and closes the deals the system has already warmed up for her. That framing removes the "this is more work" fear.

2. Where the pipeline fits the existing flow

The opportunity can be created automatically by the automation the moment a lead is qualified — or Terri creates it when she takes over. Either way, by the time she's actively talking to someone, there should be a card on the board. Her job is to keep that one card honest: which stage is it really in, and what's the next step.

The 6 stages (keep this simple)

  1. New Enquiry — qualified lead handed over
  2. Quote Requested — gathering final trip details
  3. Quote Sent — proposal out, waiting on them
  4. Follow-Up / Negotiating — chasing & adjusting
  5. Booking Confirmed (Won) — deposit paid
  6. Lost / Cold — gone elsewhere or quiet

3. The simplest way to start (so it isn't overwhelming)

Do not teach her all six behaviours at once. Roll it out in three small phases over ~3 weeks. Each phase adds exactly one habit on top of what she already does in Conversations.

Phase 1 · Week 1Just look

Goal: get comfortable with the board existing — zero pressure.

  • One new habit only: open the Opportunities board each morning for 5 minutes.
  • Look at Quote Sent and Follow-Up — message anyone who's gone quiet for 2–3 days.
  • She doesn't create or build anything yet. She just looks and chases.
Phase 2 · Week 2Move the card

Goal: keep cards in the right stage.

  • Add one habit: when she sends a quote, drag the card to "Quote Sent."
  • When a deposit lands, drag to Won. When it dies, mark Lost.
  • That's it — just dragging. The board now reflects reality.
Phase 3 · Week 3Own the card fully

Goal: full ownership including value tracking.

  • Now she creates the opportunity herself if one wasn't auto-created when she takes over a chat.
  • Add the estimated rand value of the trip, and name it Surname + Trip + Month.
  • By now the morning routine and dragging are automatic — adding value is the last small step.

4. Points to note during the session

Lead with the morning chase, not the data entry. The fastest way to make the pipeline feel valuable (not like admin) is the 5-minute morning follow-up — she'll book trips she'd otherwise have forgotten. Win first, admin second.
  • Use her real inbox. Don't demo with fake data — pull up actual qualified conversations she recognises so it feels real and relevant.
  • Let her type the stage names when building the pipeline together — ownership makes it stick.
  • Reassure on the AI handoff. She may worry about "missing" leads while the bot is talking. Make clear: the bot keeps them warm, she only needs to act once it's handed over.
  • "Lost" is not failure. Marking lost deals is how we learn the close rate — encourage it, don't let her leave dead cards sitting in Follow-Up.
  • One pipeline, few stages. Resist the urge to add more stages "to be thorough." Simplicity is what gets it used daily.

5. Watch out for

The overwhelm trap: if she feels she has to manage every raw lead from the ad, she'll disengage. Keep repeating that the system handles the top of the funnel — she only owns qualified deals.
The "later" trap: if updating the board becomes a separate end-of-day task, it won't happen. The whole point is to do it inside the conversation flow she already lives in — drag the card the moment the quote goes out, not at 5pm.

The two rules she should leave with

  1. Every qualified chat has a card — created automatically or by her when she takes over.
  2. The moment a quote goes out, the card moves to "Quote Sent" — then chase it every morning.
