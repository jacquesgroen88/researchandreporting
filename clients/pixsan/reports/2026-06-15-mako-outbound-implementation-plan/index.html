The approach

Personal notes, handled by relationship — not a campaign, and not blanket cold email.

✍️

The strategy depends on each email reading as a personal one-to-one note from a real person at Mako. That holds whether the recipient is a stranger or someone you already know — but how we treat them differs. Existing customers and people the team has dealt with are approached as warm reconnects; only genuinely new prospects are true cold outreach. Our job is to make GHL do the plumbing around all of it (logging, follow-ups, hand-offs) so the system stays sustainable while Kat's voice stays human at every touchpoint.

🤝

Relationship-aware

The list is tiered by how well Mako already knows each contact. Warm relationships are never treated as cold strangers.

🎯

Low volume, high craft

Hand-written notes earn far higher reply rates than templated sends — warmer conversations, not more noise.

⚙️

GHL does the plumbing

Logging, stage moves, follow-up reminders and clean hand-offs to Matt & Johnny — the admin disappears so Kat can focus on writing.

⚑ One proposed change — see Decision 4
Kat's draft included an "Engaged / Opened" stage driven by open-tracking. We recommend removing it. Tracking opens means embedding a pixel in every email — the same thing that makes a personal note start looking like a campaign to spam filters. We track replies (the signal that actually matters) without it, which protects both deliverability and the personal feel.

How the GHL build actually works

The wraparound automation — and exactly what we've verified.

One important mechanic up front: for GHL to log sends and run the automation, Kat sends from inside GHL using her connected Microsoft 365 mailbox. The email still goes out as her, from her address, through Microsoft — but routing it through GHL is what establishes the two-way sync. (A reply only threads and triggers automatically once the first message in the thread originates from the CRM.) This is a small change to where Kat hits send, not what she writes.
🗂️

Auto-logs every send

Each email Kat sends through GHL logs itself to the contact record via the Microsoft 365 connection — no double entry.

↪️

Replies move the card

A genuine reply fires the "Customer Replied" trigger and a workflow advances the stage. Out-of-office replies are filtered so they don't false-trigger.

⏰

5-day follow-up reminder

If a contact sits in "Email Sent" for 5 business days, GHL surfaces it so the follow-up never slips.

📨

Bounces handled

Hard bounces move to "Bounced" automatically and drop out of outreach.

🚀

Auto-graduation + alert

A positive reply creates the opportunity in your Mako pipeline at New Lead and notifies Matt & Johnny by SMS or email.

🏷️

Context travels with it

Custom fields — source, tier, industry, why-they're-a-fit, template used — live on the contact, so they're already attached when the opportunity graduates.

Deliverability, domain & sender

Sending each segment from the address that lands best, while protecting your main domain.

🌐

Dedicated cold domain

makoteam.co, with two inboxes (kat@ primary, contact@ backup) on Microsoft 365 so Kat stays in Outlook. AIS sets up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, then we warm the inbox for ~a week before sending.

📤

Right sender per segment

The new domain is ideal for genuinely cold prospects (~1,453). For warm contacts who already know Mako — existing customers, old relationships — a note from a brand-new unknown domain lands worse than one from the address they recognise.

⚑ Sender strategy — see Decision 1
Kat's draft routes everything through kat@makoteam.co. We'd suggest splitting it: send the warm segments (existing PTMW customers, job-changer reconnects, and the director-led lists) from a recognised Mako / PTMW address, and reserve the new makoteam.co domain for the truly cold prospects. Warm sends are low spam-risk, so they're safe from a known domain — and they perform better there.
⚑ For the record — the tenant decision
Mako has decided to add makoteam.co to the existing Microsoft 365 tenant (shared with makobond.com), on the basis that this is low-volume, low-risk outreach — reasonable for launch. One thing on record: because Microsoft also weighs tenant-level reputation, heavy spam complaints on the cold domain could in theory touch deliverability on makobond.com too. Mitigation: keep cold volume low and genuinely personal, send warm segments from the known domain (above), monitor reputation and bounce signals, and keep an isolated tenant or dedicated platform as the fallback only if Mako ever scales into automated sequences.
