The one thing I would change about your list

You have Brand Brain creation written as item two on two separate lists. It is not item two. It is the foundation everything else runs on, and it should be built first.

Once a client's Brand Brain exists, the keyword research is sharper because it knows the positioning, the content sounds like that client instead of like AI, the metadata matches the actual offer, and the monthly reporting can explain why a number moved instead of just reporting that it did. Build it once per client and every other item on your list gets faster and better. Skip it and you get generic output, which is exactly the "average results" problem you are trying to get away from.

Your biggest single win: the website build is one pipeline, not six tasks

Items two through seven of New Website Builds are the same chain. Right now your team runs each step by hand and carries the output to the next one. It runs end to end instead, and you review at the finish.

Brand Brain› Keyword research› Nav + IA structure› Page content + URLs› Metadata› Live via RankMath API

Worth noting: the SEO spreadsheet is an artifact of doing this by hand. It exists so a human can carry metadata from research into the site. When the last step writes to RankMath directly, the spreadsheet becomes optional. You told me that spreadsheet is two to four hours per site, plus about an hour to implement it.

No sales spin

Straight answers on the two catches

These are the parts I would want to know if I were the one spending the money.

1. Wix and Squarespace will not update themselves the way WordPress will

WordPress with RankMath is a clean story, because RankMath exposes a proper API and the system can write titles, descriptions, schema and redirects directly. Wix is partial, its SEO interfaces are limited and vary by plan, so it needs checking site by site. Squarespace has no meaningful way to write page level SEO from the outside, so there the system gives you the finished recommendations and a person still pastes them in. Analysis and recommendations work everywhere. Automatic implementation is a WordPress strength. If most of your SEO book sits on WordPress, this barely costs you anything. If a lot of it sits on Squarespace, you should know that before you sign, not after.

2. I cannot promise it beats your fulfillment partner

That is a result, and results depend on the client's market, budget and site as much as on the work. What I can promise is the things that usually produce better results: analysis that takes minutes instead of hours, so you act on opportunities while they are still live, consistency across every account instead of whatever attention each one happened to get, and far more iterations per month than a partner delivering an average service will ever run. If your partner is genuinely mediocre, that combination normally wins. I am not going to put a number on it before I have seen the accounts.

Why I am flagging these: you are a systems person, so you would find both of these in week two anyway. Better you hear them from me now, while you can still decide, than discover them after you have paid.
