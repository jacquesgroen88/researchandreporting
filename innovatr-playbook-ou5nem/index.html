What gets picked up

  • A named ranking with a winner in the headline (KLA, Ask Afrika, Kantar BrandZ, Human8). Brands and their agencies repeat it.
  • A headline number with SA relevance ("90% of…", "R422bn", "1 in 3"). This is what turns a release into editorial coverage.
  • A recurring franchise: quarterly or annual, same name every time.
  • AI and synthetic-respondent pieces are the topical hook right now.
  • Appointments and awards fill a calendar but don't travel.

How each answer page is built

The engines quote plain, single-purpose pages: Kantar's 24-hour page, Sagaci's one page per country, Market Instinct's how-to guide. Every answer page follows the same pattern.

Structure

  1. H1 is the buyer's question in their words.
  2. The first two sentences answer it with a number: "Innovatr tests concepts with 100 South African consumers in 24 hours, from R5,000 per concept."
  3. A price table, then how it works, then what it can't do (the limits section builds trust and gets quoted).
  4. 4 to 6 FAQ questions, marked up as FAQPage.
  5. One action: "Book a 15-minute scoping call" or "Start a Test24", behind a work-email form.
  6. Author box (Richard, credentials), and a "Last updated" date.

Rules

  • 900 to 1,500 words. Plain HTML text, never inside an image or a carousel.
  • Every number matches the fact sheet.
  • Name competitors fairly where the buyer asks for a comparison; engines trust pages that do.
  • Link each page from the service page it supports, and from the other answer pages.
  • Re-date and refresh each page at least once a quarter.
