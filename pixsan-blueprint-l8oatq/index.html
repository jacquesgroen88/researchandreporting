What we cover

Eight parts, one plan

These are the areas we work through in the sessions. Each one ends up as a document in the finished Blueprint. You keep all of it, and it's written so your own team (or anyone else) could run it without me.

1. The diagnostic

Where Pixsan actually stands, with the numbers.

  • Revenue by client and by service
  • How concentrated the book is
  • Delivery cost and margin per service line
  • Where leads come from today, and what each source is worth

2. Competitor teardown

The San Diego agencies you lose to, or would lose to.

  • What they rank and advertise for
  • We request their quotes and join their lists
  • How they package and price
  • Where they're weak, so you know where to push

3. Offer and pricing

What Pixsan sells, and in what order.

  • Your four services reviewed as products
  • Project vs retainer, and what keeps a client past month six
  • Where the AI and automation work fits as its own offer
  • A price ladder from first project to full retainer

4. Getting found

How the right businesses hear about you.

  • Local search and the content you already publish
  • Whether ChatGPT and Google's AI answers name you
  • Paid channels, only if the numbers support it
  • Partners who already sell to your buyers

5. Referrals, on purpose

Turning your best channel into something you can run.

  • When to ask, who asks, and what they say
  • What a referral partner gets
  • Reactivating past clients and the 75+ sites you've launched

6. From enquiry to signed

What happens after someone clicks "Let's Talk".

  • Response time and follow-up
  • The discovery call and the proposal
  • The automations that stop leads going cold

7. The money model

What the plan should produce, on three cases.

  • Downside, base and stretch, month by month
  • Every assumption printed so you can argue with it
  • What would break the model

8. The 90-day plan

The part your team works from on Monday.

  • Workstreams with an owner each (you, Anri, Samantha)
  • Weekly tasks for the first 90 days
  • The four numbers that tell you if it's working
What it looks like

Examples from a finished one

The best example I can give you is the Blueprint I wrote for my own product business, ReviewTap. It sells NFC review cards and stands in South Africa, so the figures are in rand, but the method is the same. The full thing is public: jcereports.netlify.app/seven-figure-blueprint.

Here are four pieces of it, and what the same piece would look at for Pixsan.

From the executive summary

One number that explains the business

55 orders produced R299,458Out of 817 lifetime orders, the 55 worth R2,500 or more brought in about 31% of all revenue from 6.7% of orders. Nobody had marketed to that group. It happened by accident.

For Pixsan: the same question asked of your client list. Which clients, services and lead sources actually make the money, and is any of it being chased on purpose?
