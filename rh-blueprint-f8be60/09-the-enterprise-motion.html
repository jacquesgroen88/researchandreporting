The enterprise motion

This is the largest unbuilt thing in the company and the one the seven figure model rests on. Forty-five enterprise accounts at month 24 produce 63% of the revenue from 7% of the customers. Today there is no form, no pipeline, no owner and no record, and demand is arriving anyway.

Based on the live site, the enterprise mailbox, the CRM and the production plan resource · 15 August 2026

What is happening right now

Demand exists and is not being caught

A $6,990 quote, invoiced in June, status unknown

A prospect needing roughly 1,000 hostnames asked for a quote on 16 May, including invoiced billing and a 100% uptime SLA. They were quoted $699 a month or $6,990 a year, sent a brochure and follow-ups, and had invoice details sent between 2 and 9 June. Whether they paid is not known to anyone in the business.

Two further evaluations took calls in June and July, at a US financial holding company and a manufacturer. Neither is in the CRM. The CRM contains three opportunities, all marked Attempted, none Responded.

Some of it came through the Typeform. Some arrived at service@redirhub.com, alongside SEO pitches, job applications and AWS abuse notices. All of it ended in the same place, which is nowhere.

Build it in this order

Chase the invoice. One hour.

Establish whether the $6,990 was paid. If yes, that is a reference customer and a case study candidate. If no, it is a live deal that has gone cold for ten weeks and is recoverable with one email. Either answer is worth more than anything else on this page.

Open a pipeline and put the three live opportunities in it. Half a day.

Six stages: Enquiry, Qualified, Evaluating, Proposal, Procurement, Closed. Every opportunity gets an owner, a next step and a date. The three known live ones go in on day one. This is the actual gap: the Typeform already captures, and its submissions currently land nowhere in particular.

Wire the form to the pipeline, and add a calendar. Two hours.

Route Typeform submissions straight into the pipeline with a one-business-day response commitment. Add a calendar step after submission so a qualified enquiry can book time without waiting for a reply. Point the footer's "Request a demo" and "Talk to sales" links at the form anchor rather than the bare page, where they currently land above it with no sign it exists.

Assemble the security pack. One day.

DPA, subprocessor register, security and compliance documentation, one published uptime commitment, and the entity details. All but the SLA already exist. Package as a single PDF, link it from /enterprise, and send it before the call rather than after. Sending it unprompted is a strong differentiator against competitors who make you ask.

Publish the entity. Half a day.

Legal name, registration number, registered address, and a real /contact page. Procurement cannot raise a purchase order against a company it cannot identify, and today the site, LinkedIn and public records give three different answers.

Write one case study. One day.

From an existing large account, with a number in it. "Migrated 3,200 URLs in a weekend, zero ranking loss." One proven story outperforms five unexplained logos, and it is the single asset most likely to move a stalled evaluation.

Honest reading

Reaching 1.75 net adds a month means roughly tripling opportunity flow and building a close process from nothing. That is demanding. But the first increment is almost free: catching what already arrives. Between the mailbox inbound and a working form, roughly 1.2 a month is reachable without any new demand generation at all. The content and outbound work covers the rest, over time.

Outbound, honestly assessed

The current state: 478 leads marked outreach-started, of which 464 are Instantly-marked, but only 52 rows carry an email address. You cannot contact 412 people you have no address for. Three CRM opportunities, all Attempted, none Responded.

This is not a channel with unknown return. It is a channel that largely did not run. Before restarting it:

  • Enrich first. 52 contactable leads is not a pilot, it is a sample too small to prove anything either way.
  • Cap the pilot at 50 to 100 verified contacts in one narrow segment with a concrete problem, such as organisations visibly running large domain portfolios.
  • Instrument reply, positive reply, meeting, opportunity and outcome before sending, joined to the lead row and the CRM.
  • Stop rules agreed in advance: no positive replies after the declared sample, or any deliverability degradation, ends the test.
  • Protect the sending domain. See 10 Lifecycle and Retention. Until the Mailcoach send path and DMARC outcomes are confirmed, cold outbound from the primary domain is an unnecessary risk to transactional mail.

The one number to watch

Not opportunities created. Not emails sent. Enterprise accounts closed per month, and the value of each. At the observed tier, one account a month at $1,200 adds $14,400 of ARR. Two a month, held, is the difference between this plan working and not working, and it is a number small enough that everyone in the company can know it.
