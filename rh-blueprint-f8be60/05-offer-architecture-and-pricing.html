Add-ons, already priced

SSO $200/mo · Custom nameservers $100/mo · Dedicated cluster $300/mo. These are well designed and correctly priced: they attach to exactly the buyer who can pay, and they are the natural expansion path for profile 2. They are also almost invisible, buried below a hostname slider on a page a G2 reviewer has publicly said "could be a bit clearer".

The structural problem

Nine identifiers for what is really three products, with 36 of 128 rows priced at zero and several organisations holding more than one row. The consequences are not cosmetic. MRR cannot be computed. Cohort analysis is impossible. Any price change becomes a migration project. And nobody can answer "what is an average account worth" without doing it by hand, which is why the previous internal brief reported 69 active rows when the live figure is 128.

The proposed architecture

Three self-serve tiers, one enterprise tier, one partner tier. Named for the buyer, not for the internals.

Four pricing decisions

Publish an enterprise anchor: from $699 a month

The May prospect was quoted $699 a month or $6,990 a year. That number already exists, was already deemed sayable to a customer, and is currently hidden behind Talk to sales. Publishing it does three things: it qualifies out the tyre-kickers before they consume time, it anchors the negotiation upward rather than downward, and it lets a procurement team build a budget line without a call. The annual figure also embeds a two-months-free discount, which is a clean reason to prefer annual.

Collapse nine identifiers into five, and resolve the zero rows

Thirty-six rows at $0 need classifying: genuinely free, comped, internal, or lapsed and should be closed. Until that is done nobody knows how many customers RedirHub has. This is a prerequisite for the money model, not a tidy-up.

Build the partner tier

Marginal cost per hostname is close to zero. Agencies already appear across the paid base, already manage domains they do not own, and the team-management feature set they need already ships. A partner tier turns a customer into a channel, and every peer that scaled in this category did it this way. Consolidated billing and client sub-accounts are the whole product.

Charge for migration, or use it deliberately as the land

Free migrations are already offered and promoted in llms.txt, but there is no page where a buyer can hand over the job. Either make it a paid onboarding service at the Business and Enterprise tiers, or keep it free and treat it explicitly as the land motion. What it cannot be is an unadvertised favour, which is what it is now.

Fixing the pricing page

A public G2 reviewer has already said the pricing page "could be a bit clearer". The hostname slider is a good idea that has become the whole interface. Three changes:

  • A three-column plan summary above the comparison matrix. Name, price, who it is for, one anchor feature, one call to action. The slider stays underneath for people who want to model their own volume.
  • Surface the add-ons as an expansion path, not a footnote. SSO, nameservers and dedicated cluster are the bridge from Business to Enterprise.
  • Put the enterprise anchor price on the page with a working way to start a conversation, which currently does not exist anywhere on the site.
Do not A/B test this yet

The pricing page currently appears to convert at 29.3% because landing_page stores the alphabetically highest page in a session, so /pricing absorbs nearly every multi-page session. There is no valid baseline to test against until the attribution fix in 12 lands. Fix the legibility problems above because they are self-evidently right, not because a test told you to. Save the message testing for when the measurement is real.

What not to change

  • The free tier stays generous. 100,000 requests and 5 hostnames is a genuine differentiator against Bitly, it is repeatedly praised in the reviews, and it costs almost nothing to serve.
  • API and MCP stay on every plan including free. That is the distribution wedge. Gating it would close the cheapest route into the accounts that pay.
  • No price rise on the existing base until churn can be measured. Raising prices while blind to cancellations is how a concentrated book gets quietly destroyed.
