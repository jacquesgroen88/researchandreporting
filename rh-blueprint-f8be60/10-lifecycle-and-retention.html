Lifecycle and retention

RedirHub owns a 22,188-person email list and cannot use it. Not because of strategy, but because nobody knows where the subscribers came from, 2,864 of them appear not to have opted in, and open rates collapse to 4.1% on large sends against a DNS configuration that hard-fails unauthorised senders. Meanwhile churn on the paying base cannot be measured at all.

Based on the Mailcoach API, the production plan resource, and public DNS · 14 to 15 August 2026

Part one: retention, which nobody can see

The structural gap

There is no append-only subscription event log. The admin resource exposes current plan rows only, with no cancellation, downgrade or plan-change history. Monthly churn, net revenue retention and cohort curves cannot be reconstructed, now or retrospectively. Every week that passes is retention history permanently lost.

This matters more here than at most companies. Roughly half of subscription revenue sits in three accounts. Losing one is a larger event than a quarter of acquisition work, and nothing in the current stack would record it having happened, let alone alert anyone.

What to build, in order

  1. An append-only subscription event table. Every plan create, upgrade, downgrade, cancel, expire and reactivation, with a timestamp, the organisation, the plan and the price at the moment of the change. This is a small engineering task and it is the highest-priority item in the entire data stack.
  2. A canonical MRR definition. One organisation, one MRR figure, annual prices normalised to monthly, zero-priced rows classified as free, comped, internal or lapsed. Today no such number exists anywhere in the business.
  3. A concentration report. Top five accounts as a share of MRR, refreshed monthly. If the top three really are half the book, that is the single most important number nobody currently sees.
  4. An at-risk signal. For a redirect platform, usage decline is visible in the product before it shows up in billing: hostnames removed, request volume falling, a certificate allowed to lapse. Watch it on the accounts that matter.

The retention motion that follows

Once the data exists, the highest-value retention work is not automated email. It is a quarterly conversation with each of the top ten accounts, owned by a named person. At this account count that is a calendar entry, not a customer success function, and it is the cheapest possible insurance on half the revenue.

The unresolved question

This is the strictest possible posture: SPF hard-fail plus DMARC p=reject, authorising only ZeptoMail and Zoho. Mailcoach is verified on the domain but absent from the SPF record. That is fine if Mailcoach relays through ZeptoMail. If it sends over any other path, every bulk message hard-fails and is rejected outright, which would explain 4.1% precisely.

Two checks settle it, and neither needs the Zoho API (which exposes attachment metadata but cannot download the DMARC XML): read MAIL_MAILER and MAIL_HOST on the Mailcoach instance or the sending-domain configuration in the Mailcoach Cloud interface, and log in to GlockApps directly, where aggregate reports are already being delivered and appear never to have been read.

Why this is a product risk, not a marketing risk

RedirHub is an infrastructure vendor. Sender reputation damage from bulk sending degrades transactional mail on the same domain: password resets, certificate-expiry alerts, monitoring notifications. Those are reliability failures that customers experience as the product breaking.

The consent position

Zero of 24,593 subscriber records carry a source. There is no consent-origin event anywhere, so the roughly 11,650 subscribers above the registered-user count cannot be classified as opted-in, imported or acquired. Separately, 2,864 active subscribers carry newsletter = 0, meaning they sit on the active list while apparently not having opted into the newsletter. Five EU locales are served, and there is no consent management platform on the site.

The recommendation is not to delete the list. It is to establish provenance before using it:

  1. Reconstruct origin where possible from extra_attributes.landing (present on 15,595 active records) and referrer (17,604), plus registration records where the user exists in the product.
  2. Suppress the 2,864 newsletter = 0 records from marketing sends until an opt-in can be evidenced.
  3. Instrument source and a consent timestamp on every new subscriber from now on. This is a small change that prevents the same problem recurring.
  4. Re-permission the segment that cannot be evidenced, rather than mailing it. A re-permission campaign to a cold segment is also the cleanest way to restore list health.
