06 · Market and Buyer

Messaging and positioning

The current site says "the infrastructure behind every click", which is a good line attached to a page selling a free URL shortener. The position is right and the proof stack underneath it is either missing, unsupported, or contradicted on the same page.

Based on the live site copy, the enterprise page, and the reviews inventory · 15 August 2026

The position

Position statement

Redirect infrastructure for people who cannot afford to lose a click

RedirHub is the managed edge layer that replaces a self-hosted redirect stack. It handles certificates automatically at portfolio scale, answers in 90 milliseconds from ten-plus points of presence, and is operable by an API or an AI agent as easily as by a person. It is not a link shortener that also does redirects. It is redirect infrastructure that happens to include a shortener.

Why this position and not another

  • It is where the money is. Three accounts at $599 to $2,000 a month bought infrastructure. Fifty accounts at $10 bought a convenience.
  • It is defensible. Bitly and Rebrandly cannot credibly claim it, and Cloudflare will not go after a niche this small.
  • It is provable. Sub-200ms measured response times and a single-hop apex redirect served by the company's own edge are facts, not adjectives.
  • It has a deadline attached. Shortening certificate lifetimes make the self-hosted alternative worse every quarter without RedirHub doing anything.

The narrative, in the order it should be told

Redirects look trivial until you have four hundred of them

One redirect is a line in a config file. Four hundred, across domains you acquired at different times on registrars you have never logged into, each needing a certificate that now expires eight times a year, is an operational problem with no owner.

The thing that breaks is never the redirect. It is the certificate.

A redirect that works is invisible. A certificate that lapsed on a domain nobody remembers owning takes down traffic that nobody is monitoring, and the first sign is a customer asking why the link in a printed brochure shows a security warning.

Point the domain. That is the whole migration.

CNAME or nameservers, your choice. DNS stays where it is. Certificates are issued and renewed automatically, including wildcards. Changes propagate globally in seconds.

And now your agent can do it too

An AI agent can generate a landing page in thirty seconds and then cannot point a domain at it. RedirHub closes that gap through a live MCP server, on every plan including free.

Proof that is missing

  • One case study with a number in it. "Migrated 3,200 URLs in a weekend, zero ranking loss" outperforms five unexplained logos. There is not one on the site.
  • An incident history or status record presented as evidence rather than as a link to a status page.
  • Any named human. No team page, no founder, no author bylines. For an infrastructure vendor asking to sit in the critical path of production traffic, anonymity is a cost. The two legal documents naming different entities compounds it.

Three claims to fix before selling harder

Claim 1

The SLA. /enterprise displays a stat block reading "100% SLA". The FAQ on that same page says the page "does not quote... a guaranteed SLA percentage". The homepage says 99.99% uptime. Three commitments, two on one page. The May prospect asked for 100% specifically. Pick one number, publish it, back it with a linked document. A 100% claim that the same page retracts is worse than no claim: it reads as either careless or evasive to precisely the technical buyer being courted.

Claim 2

The logo bar. Whirlpool and Ally appear under "Trusted by teams managing redirects at scale" with no case study, quote, named contact or link. Enterprises of that size normally require written consent for logo use, and sophisticated buyers discount unsupported logo bars anyway. Either earn one into a case study or reduce the bar to logos that can be stood behind.

Claim 3

Scale numbers that do not reconcile. The homepage carries "1M+ domains redirected daily", "500K+ hostnames actively managed", "100M+ requests in the last 24 hours" and "900K+ SSL certificates issued in the past 60 days". Individually plausible, collectively unexplained, and a technical buyer will try to reconcile them. Define each one, or publish fewer and defend them properly. Likewise "up to 4x faster than Bitly" needs a stated methodology or it should go.

Voice rules

  • Numbers over adjectives. "90ms across ten points of presence" not "lightning fast". The audience checks.
  • Name the failure mode. Technical buyers trust vendors who describe what breaks. The reviews page already publishes negative reviews with responses, which is the right instinct and should extend to the marketing.
  • Never claim what the FAQ retracts. Consistency across a page is a competence signal in this category.
  • Say what it does not do. "DNS stays where it is" is more persuasive than another feature.
  • No em dashes and no filler. Short sentences, concrete nouns.
