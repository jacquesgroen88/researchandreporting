Ten thousand users, ninety-two customers, and three accounts that pay for everything

RedirHub does not have a traffic problem or a product problem. It has a monetisation problem that looks like a marketing problem. Seven figures is reached by moving up-market to the accounts that already pay, not by adding free signups to a base of ten thousand that mostly never will.

Prepared by JCE Media for RedirHub · 15 August 2026 · Built on live ClickHouse funnel data, the production admin resource, the Mailcoach API, Baserow, DNS records and the live site

The diagnosis in one paragraph

RedirHub runs genuinely good infrastructure. Time to first byte measures between 152 and 181 milliseconds from a South African edge, the apex domain redirects in a single clean hop served by its own network, and it ships a complete llms.txt plus a live MCP server that almost no competitor in the category has. On top of that sits an acquisition funnel that converts 79% of signups into activated users. And then it stops. Of 10,533 registered users, roughly 92 pay for anything. Three plan rows produce about half of all subscription revenue. Enterprise enquiries are captured, through a Typeform on the enterprise page and another on the scalable-solutions page, and then owned by nobody: a $6,990 annual quote was invoiced in June and no one in the business can currently say whether it was paid.

The core finding

Three accounts are roughly half the revenue

Three visible plan rows at $2,000, $1,000 and $599 a month total $3,599 and plausibly represent around half of all subscription revenue. Fifty accounts at $10 a month together produce $500, roughly a seventh of what those three do. Every system in the business, the content, the free tier, the onboarding, the newsletter, is pointed at the fifty. Nothing is pointed at the three.

The four moves

Put a pipeline behind the enterprise enquiries already arriving

Capture exists. The /enterprise page's Book a demo buttons scroll to a #book-demo section holding a live Typeform, and /scalable-enterprise-solutions carries a second one. What does not exist is anything behind them. A 1,000-hostname prospect was quoted $6,990 a year and invoiced in June with no recorded outcome, and two further evaluations took calls in June and July. None are in the CRM, which holds three opportunities all marked Attempted. No owner, no next step, no recorded result. This is the single largest unbuilt thing in the company.

Point the content engine at the buyer who pays

The 22 comparison pages split cleanly into an infrastructure cluster (nginx, htaccess, Cloudflare, AWS, Vercel, Netlify, WordPress, redirection.io) and a shortener cluster (Bitly, TinyURL, Rebrandly, Short.io, Dub). The first maps to the accounts paying hundreds of dollars a month. The second maps to the free tier. Both are currently treated identically, and four of them are competing against duplicate versions of themselves. The 45-day certificate change is a forced-migration event aimed squarely at the first group, and the content for it already exists unpromoted.

Make the numbers real before spending against them

The landing_page field stores the alphabetically highest page in a session, not the first or the last. The table is called funnel_first_touch. In practice /pricing absorbs nearly every session that also touched a page starting a to o, which is why it appears to convert at 29% while the free tool shows 5%. There is no subscription event history, so churn cannot be measured. The trial event fires four times against 329 signups. Three small engineering fixes turn a blind business into a measurable one.

Sell the moat nobody knows exists

A live MCP server, a complete llms.txt, and a robots.txt explicitly welcoming GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot. An AI agent can generate a landing page but cannot point a domain at it. RedirHub closes that gap today and no competitor in the category has an answer. Nothing on the website tells a single visitor this exists.

Read the table this way

The enterprise tier is 7% of the accounts and 63% of the revenue. Forty-five enterprise accounts is the plan. Four hundred entry accounts is a rounding error that happens on its own. Any version of this plan that spends its energy on the bottom row does not reach seven figures, and it is the row every current system is built to serve.

What has to be true

Forty-five enterprise accounts by month 24 means roughly 1.75 net adds a month. A single six-month mailbox search surfaced four enterprise-shaped opportunities arriving with no capture mechanism, no outbound and a broken demo path, which is about 0.67 a month landing by accident. Reaching 1.75 requires roughly tripling opportunity flow and building a close process that currently does not exist. That is demanding. It is not fantasy, and the first tripling is mostly a matter of catching what already arrives.

What we are deliberately not doing

  • Not running paid acquisition yet. There is no conversion tag, no tag manager, and no valid attribution. Spending now buys traffic and learns nothing.
  • Not emailing the 22,188 list. Not one subscriber record carries a source, 2,864 active subscribers are flagged as not having opted into the newsletter, and open rates fall to 4.1% on large sends. Consent and deliverability get answered first.
  • Not chasing free signups. The funnel already converts 79% of signups to activation and it has produced 92 paying accounts from 10,533 users. More of the same input produces more of the same output.
  • Not competing on link shortening. Bitly, Rebrandly, Dub and Short.io own that conversation and it monetises badly here. The infrastructure position is defensible and the shortener position is not.
