The infrastructure SEO play

RedirHub has roughly a hundred blog posts, twenty-two comparison pages and eight translated locales. It is out-publishing most of the category. It is also competing against itself on the four highest-intent queries it owns, and a redirect company is shipping a sitemap containing redirects.

The sitemap

The certificate campaign

The wedge

A forced migration with a deadline someone else set

Shortening TLS certificate lifetimes turn manual redirect management from a chore into an operational failure waiting to happen, for precisely the buyer who pays the most. RedirHub has already published four pieces on it and promoted none of them: lets-encrypt-45-day-certificates, why-shorter-certificate-lifetimes-manual-redirect-management-unsustainable, redirect-infrastructure-checklist-45-day-certificate-era, and acme-vs-manual-certificate-management.

Campaign structure

One pillar page

The definitive resource on managing redirect domains through the certificate-lifetime change. The arithmetic (how many renewals a year at portfolio scale), the failure modes, the options including doing nothing, and what automation actually removes. Written to be cited, not to convert on the first visit.

Eight infrastructure comparison pages rebuilt to feed it

Consistent structure: the honest comparison table, the certificate maths for that specific alternative, a migration path, one call to action. The nginx and htaccess pages carry the most weight because that is the real incumbent.

A calculator or checklist as the linkable asset

"How many certificate renewals will your portfolio need next year." Takes a domain count, returns a number and a list of what breaks. This is the asset that earns links from people who will never buy, which is what moves the pillar page.

Distribution into the communities that have the problem

Hacker News, r/devops, r/sysadmin, r/webdev. RedirHub currently has no presence in any of them, which is the largest distribution gap in the business. Covered in 11 Communities and Distribution.

Measurement

Search Console is the measurement surface for this entire workstream and it is independent of the broken ClickHouse attribution. Track impressions, clicks and average position per comparison page, split by cluster. That gives a clean read on whether the infrastructure cluster is gaining while the shortener cluster holds, which is the whole thesis of this document.

One caveat worth stating in advance: no HTML verification meta tag was found on the site, so it is not externally confirmable whether Search Console is verified (it may be held by DNS). Confirming access is a week-one item.
