Decisions already implied by the evidence

  • No paid acquisition before month 7. There is no conversion tag, no tag manager and no valid attribution. Spending now buys traffic and learns nothing.
  • Consolidate pages before repairing attribution. The reverse order produces a first clean dataset that is already split across duplicate URLs.
  • Subscription event log before anything else in the data stack. Attribution can be backfilled from raw logs. Retention history cannot.
  • MCP and API stay on the free plan. It is the distribution wedge into the accounts that pay.
  • No price rise on the existing base until churn is measurable. Raising prices while blind to cancellations is how a concentrated book gets quietly destroyed.

What would make this plan wrong

The honest list
  • If the three large accounts are not what they appear. The concentration estimate is assembled by hand from plan rows, and plan rows are not organisations. If those three are one customer, or are annual rows being read as monthly, the shape of the model changes and the concentration risk gets worse rather than better.
  • If enterprise demand is genuinely 0.67 a month and not suppressed. The plan assumes a broken capture path is hiding real demand. If demand is simply that thin, the ramp fails and this becomes a demand-generation problem rather than a capture problem, which is slower and more expensive.
  • If churn is already high. Everything here assumes the existing base holds. It is unmeasurable today. If the business is losing accounts at a material rate, acquisition work is filling a bucket with a hole in it and the sequencing should invert entirely.
  • If the product cannot serve enterprise at scale. The plan assumes the platform supports 45 accounts of that size. That is an engineering question this Blueprint has not tested.
  • If nobody is available to sell. Marketing can create qualified opportunities. It cannot close a procurement-led infrastructure deal in five hours a week.

The three things to verify first

Each of these could change the plan, and each is answerable in under a day:

  1. Was the $6,990 invoice paid? One email. It reveals whether the close process works at all.
  2. What do the DMARC aggregates show? Already collected at GlockApps and apparently never read. Gates the entire owned-audience workstream and may indicate a live product risk.
  3. What is the real churn rate? Not reconstructable from current data, but the founder and support will remember roughly how many accounts have left in the last year. That informal answer is enough to know whether the sequencing above is right.
