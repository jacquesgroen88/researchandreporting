Five profiles, ranked by revenue density rather than volume. Each is anchored to evidence already in the business: a paid plan row, a mailbox thread, or an enrichment pattern. The first two carry the seven figure model. The last two are distribution, not revenue.

The inversion

The two profiles that carry the model are served by nothing and by a broken page. The two profiles that carry almost no revenue are served by the entire content library, the free tier, the onboarding and the newsletter. Effort is currently the exact inverse of value.

1. The domain portfolio operator

Domain investors, parking businesses, brand-protection holders, expired-domain traders

The evidence

The paid base contains organisations running tens of thousands of hostnames and records, and in one verified case hundreds of thousands of hostnames against hundreds of millions of recorded requests. The visible $2,000, $1,000 and $599 rows sit here. A prospect quoted in May needed roughly 1,000 hostnames and asked about invoiced billing and a 100% uptime SLA.

Unit maths

They do not buy a feature, they buy capacity. Value scales with hostname count, and the pricing slider already runs to 50,000. One account of this shape is worth more than the entire fifty-account $10 tier combined.

What triggers a purchase

  • Certificate management collapsing under portfolio scale, which shortening lifetimes now guarantees
  • Migrating off a self-hosted redirect box that one person understands and that person is leaving
  • A parking provider change, or wanting traffic and buyer enquiries without ad-tech junk pages
  • An acquisition adding several hundred domains overnight

Who decides

Usually one technical owner with budget authority. Short buying committee, fast decision, high scrutiny of uptime and SSL behaviour. They will test with a handful of domains before moving the portfolio.

The pitch

"Point the whole portfolio at us. Certificates are issued and renewed automatically on every hostname, 30 days before expiry, including wildcards. Ninety millisecond average response across ten-plus points of presence. Bulk CSV in, bulk CSV out, and an API so you never touch a dashboard again."

Objections and answers

  • "What happens if you go down?" This is the objection. It requires one published, contractual SLA number and an incident history, which is currently three contradictory claims. Fix before selling.
  • "I already have this working on nginx." Ask how many certificate renewals a year that becomes at 45-day lifetimes, and who does them.
  • "Can I get out?" CSV export, and DNS stays wherever it is. No lock-in is a genuine advantage over Cloudflare here.

Where to find them

NamePros and domain investor communities, expired-domain tooling audiences, the parking-provider switching conversation, and the /domain-parking and /redirect-typo-domains pages that already exist.

2. The enterprise web, IT or platform team

Corporate, financial services, manufacturing, education, healthcare, government-adjacent

The evidence

The paid base includes corporate, education, healthcare and enterprise email domains. The mailbox contains two live evaluations in June and July, one at a US financial holding company and one at a manufacturer, both of which took calls and neither of which reached the CRM.

Unit maths

Fewer hostnames than the portfolio operator but higher governance requirements: SSO at $200 a month, custom nameservers at $100, dedicated cluster at $300, on top of plan. Land at $260 to $600 and expand through add-ons and seats.

What triggers a purchase

  • A site migration or rebrand with a deadline and a ranking risk
  • A redirect outage that reached someone senior
  • Web team blocked waiting on infrastructure for server config changes
  • A security or compliance review forcing documentation of an ad hoc setup

Who decides

Longest committee of any profile. A web or marketing operations lead champions it, IT or platform validates it, security reviews it, procurement signs it. This profile is why the missing DPA, subprocessor list, entity details and SLA cost real money. Every one of those documents exists on /legal and none appears where the decision is made.

The pitch

"Your web team ships redirects without filing a ticket, and your infrastructure team stops owning a redirect server. Role-based access, SAML single sign-on, audit logs, a signed DPA, and a published uptime commitment."

Objections and answers

  • "Who are you?" Currently unanswerable from the website. No entity, no address, no about page, no case study. This is the first fix.
  • "Send me your security pack." It exists. Surface it on /enterprise and offer it before the call, not after.
  • "We would need to move DNS." No. CNAME works and the zone stays put.

Where to find them

Inbound on migration and SSL queries, the infrastructure comparison cluster, LinkedIn, and the enterprise mailbox that is already receiving them.

3. The agency and multi-client operator

Digital, web, SEO and IT service businesses managing domains they do not own

The evidence

Organisation names and email domains across the paid base indicate digital marketing, web, IT and service businesses, several running many domains with dozens to hundreds of redirect records.

Why they matter twice

They are a good growth-tier customer at $90 to $260, and they are a distribution channel. One agency brings its whole client book. Team management, role-based permissions and domain-level access already exist and are the exact feature set this profile needs. Nothing markets them.

The pitch

"Manage every client's redirects from one dashboard, with per-client access control, and hand a client their domains back cleanly when the relationship ends."

The unbuilt motion

There is no partner or reseller tier. For a product with near-zero marginal cost per hostname, that is free distribution left on the table. Covered in 05 Offer Architecture.

4. The developer and technical growth team

Engineers automating deployment, migration and campaign workflows

The evidence

User onboarding profiles explicitly labelled Developer. REST API and the MCP server are live on every plan including free.

Role in the plan

Not primarily a revenue segment. A distribution wedge. Developers adopt tools personally, then bring them into companies that become profiles 1, 2 and 3. The MCP and AI-agent story is aimed here, and it is the cheapest route into the accounts that pay.

The pitch

"Your agent can build the landing page. It cannot point a domain at it. Wire up our MCP server and it can do both, in the same conversation."

Where to find them

Hacker News, r/devops, r/sysadmin, r/webdev, MCP and agent-tooling communities, and the developer-adjacent comparison pages (nginx, htaccess, Vercel, Netlify, AWS). RedirHub currently has no presence in any of them.

5. The marketer using links as operating assets

Campaign routing, branded short links, dynamic QR codes

The evidence

Explicit Marketer onboarding profiles, and arrivals through pricing, free-redirect and QR-oriented journeys. This is the broadest top-of-funnel segment in the business.

The honest assessment

This profile is most of the 10,533 registered users and almost none of the revenue. It is also the segment the entire content library currently serves. Keep the free tier because it costs almost nothing and occasionally surfaces a larger buyer, but stop spending marketing effort competing for it against Bitly and Rebrandly.

The one exception worth keeping

Dynamic QR codes have a genuine enterprise use case: print once, repoint later, across a large physical estate. That is a profile 2 conversation wearing profile 5 clothes, and it is worth keeping for exactly that reason.

What changes because of this ranking

  • Content moves up-market. The infrastructure comparison cluster and the certificate deadline serve profiles 1 and 2. The WordPress and shortening content serves profile 5.
  • The enterprise page becomes a real page. Profile 2 cannot buy without entity details, a security pack, one SLA number and a way to book a call.
  • A partner tier gets built. Profile 3 is free distribution that currently has no route in.
  • Developer distribution becomes the top of the enterprise funnel, not a separate audience.
