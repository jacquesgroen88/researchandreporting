02 · Diagnosis

The leak map

Nineteen identified leaks, each with what it costs, what it takes to close, and how confident the costing is. Ranked by return per hour of effort. This is the list the project plan is built from.

Costs are stated as bands, not point estimates. Where a figure cannot be defended it is marked unquantified rather than guessed.

How to read the ranking

Three things determine order: the size of the loss, the hours to close it, and whether closing it unblocks other work. A cheap fix that unblocks four other fixes outranks an expensive fix worth more in isolation. Two leaks in this list are worth more than the rest combined, and both are measured in hours rather than weeks.

The costing rule used throughout

One enterprise account at the observed tier averages roughly $1,200 a month, or $14,400 a year. Fifty accounts on the $10 plan together produce $500 a month. Anywhere a leak plausibly costs one enterprise account, it costs more than the entire volume tier produces. That is the arithmetic that drives this ordering.

The two that matter most

Leaks 1 to 3, taken together

Enquiries are captured and then owned by nobody

Demand arrives and is caught. A 1,000-hostname prospect was quoted $6,990 a year and invoiced in June. Two more evaluations took calls in June and July. Some of it came through the Typeform, some into service@redirhub.com alongside SEO pitches and abuse notices. None of it reached the CRM, which holds three opportunities all marked Attempted. Nobody owns the follow-up and nobody can say what happened to any of them.

Closing this is one hour to chase an invoice and half a day to set up a pipeline. It is the cheapest and highest-value work available anywhere in the business, and it requires no engineering at all.

Sequencing: two leaks must be closed in order

Leak 8 before leak 5

Fixing attribution while duplicate pages still exist produces a clean dataset that is already split across duplicate URLs. The affected comparison pages would under-report their true contribution by roughly half, on exactly the highest commercial-intent queries in the category, and the first month of trustworthy data would be quietly wrong. Consolidate the pages first, then repair the measurement. This is the single most common way a measurement project produces confident nonsense.

What this list is not

There is no leak on this list called "not enough traffic" and none called "the product needs work". Twelve thousand visitors a month convert to 329 signups and 261 activations, and the activation rate of 79.3% is genuinely strong. The infrastructure is fast, correct and self-hosted on its own product. Every leak here sits between an activated user and a paid account, or between a buyer and a human being. That is the whole diagnosis.
