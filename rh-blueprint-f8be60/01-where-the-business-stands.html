Activation is not the bottleneck and has never been the bottleneck. People who sign up succeed at using the product. The break is entirely between activation and payment, and it is a break the current instrumentation cannot see into.

The trial number is not a finding, it is a fault

Four tracked trials against 261 activations is not a low trial rate. The event is gated in SubscriptionObserver on a transition into trial state with a once-only guard, and it is evidently not firing for most trial journeys. Any conclusion drawn from the trial stage today is drawn from noise. It also produces the 175% trial-to-paid figure that both prior reports correctly refused to use.

The distribution is the strategy

Three rows produce more than a hundred others

$2,000 + $1,000 + $599 = $3,599 a month from three plan rows. The fifty redirect-basic accounts together produce about $500. One enterprise account is worth roughly the entire volume tier. Nothing in the current marketing, product onboarding or content strategy reflects that.

Nine plan names for what is really three products

There are at least nine distinct plan identifiers in production, several of them overlapping or legacy: redirect-basic, redirect-regular, redirect-pro, redirect-plus, redirect-business, redirect-free, redirect-as-1, redirect-as-3, plus custom enterprise rows. redirect-basic at $10 and redirect-regular at $25 are not obviously different products. Thirty-six of the 128 rows are priced at zero.

This matters beyond tidiness. Plan sprawl makes MRR uncomputable, makes cohort analysis impossible, and makes any pricing change a migration project. It is addressed in 05 Offer Architecture and Pricing.

Do not act on this table

The landing_page column is defined as SimpleAggregateFunction(max, String) over every pageview in a session, so it stores the alphabetically highest page the visitor touched. Not the first. Not the last. /pricing therefore absorbs almost every session that also visited anything sorting before it, which is most of the site, while /free-redirect-service retains mainly single-page bounces. The 29.3% against 5.5% gap is alphabet and session depth. It is not a property of either page. Full detail in 12 The Measurement Rebuild.

One thing in the table is reliable. The (empty) bucket of 3,198 visitors is not merely unattributed: because an empty string sorts lowest, a session lands there only if no pageview recorded a landing page at all. That is a complete instrumentation hole across a quarter of all traffic.

What the product does well

Performance

Time to first byte measured at 152 to 181 milliseconds across repeated runs from a South African edge, served pre-rendered from Vercel with a cache hit. DNS 8ms, TLS 83ms. 51 of 58 homepage images lazy-loaded. HSTS set to two years.

Redirect hygiene

The apex domain resolves to www in exactly one 301 hop with no chain, served by RedirHub's own edge (X-Powered-By: RedirHub). The company runs its own product in production. That is a proof point worth using in the marketing and it currently appears nowhere.

International

Eight fully translated locales (de, es, fr, it, pt, ja, zh, ko) with correct reciprocal hreflang and per-locale canonicals. Genuinely well built. Not one of them appears in the sitemap.

AI readiness

A complete 17KB llms.txt mapping every feature and content cluster, a live MCP server, and robots.txt explicitly allowing GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai and PerplexityBot. Very few companies of this size have shipped any of it.

A 4.1% open rate on a 21,534-person send is not a copy problem. Typical business software sits between 20% and 40%. The apex SPF record reads v=spf1 include:zeptomail.net include:spf.zoho.com -all with DMARC set to p=reject, authorising only ZeptoMail and Zoho and hard-failing everything else. Mailcoach is verified on the domain but is not in that record. Whether that is a problem depends entirely on whether Mailcoach relays through ZeptoMail, which is not currently known. Proof and reputation

18 public reviews at 4.7 out of 5, fifteen on Trustpilot and three on G2. The rating is good and the volume caps everything it could support. The reviews page displays the figure and carries no JSON-LD at all, so no star rating can appear in search results and no AI answer engine can cite it. The homepage logo bar shows Duende, Whirlpool, Ally, Serb and Expiredomain with no case study, quote or link behind any of them.

The commercial gap in one place

What the website offers an enterprise buyer

Capture exists on exactly two pages, /enterprise and /scalable-enterprise-solutions, both via Typeform. Everywhere else there is none: no lead form on the homepage or pricing page, and no /about, /contact, /company, /team or /careers page, all five returning 404. No registered address or company number is published. The entity is disclosed but inconsistently: the Terms of Service name "RedirHub LLC, a New Mexico limited liability company" while the Privacy Policy says "RedirHub is a trade name of RedirHub Inc." Two different legal persons in two binding documents, with LinkedIn listing New York and UK Companies House carrying a REDIRHUB LTD registration on top.
