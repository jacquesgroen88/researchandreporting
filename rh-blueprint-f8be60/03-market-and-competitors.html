03 · Market and Buyer

Two markets wearing one category name

Link shortening and redirect infrastructure look like the same business and are not. One is a crowded consumer-grade market where the price floor is zero. The other is a quiet operational market where buyers pay hundreds of dollars a month and rarely leave. RedirHub competes in both and is positioned for neither.

Competitor set drawn from RedirHub's own comparison inventory, plus category research · 15 August 2026

The two tiers

Tier 1: Link shortening

Bitly, TinyURL, Rebrandly, Short.io, Dub, Switchy, tlinky.

Bought by marketers. Judged on branded links, click analytics, QR codes and price. Enormous free usage, low willingness to pay, high churn, and a category leader with overwhelming brand recognition.

RedirHub's paying customers are not here. The 50 accounts on the $10 plan are, and together they produce about $500 a month.

Tier 2: Redirect infrastructure

redirect.pizza, urllo (EasyRedir), redirection.io, Cloudflare Bulk Redirects, and self-hosted nginx or htaccess.

Bought by web, IT and platform teams. Judged on SSL automation, edge latency, bulk operations, API, governance and uptime commitments. Low volume, high value, and genuinely sticky because redirects sit in the critical path of live traffic.

This is where the $599, $1,000 and $2,000 rows live.

The positioning decision

RedirHub's own comparison pages already made this choice, and nobody noticed

Of 22 comparison pages, eight target infrastructure alternatives (nginx, htaccess, Cloudflare, AWS, Vercel, Netlify, WordPress, redirection.io) and seven target shorteners. The infrastructure eight map directly to the customers producing most of the revenue. Both clusters currently receive identical treatment, identical templates and identical calls to action.

The market timing that matters

The wedge

The industry is moving to dramatically shorter TLS certificate lifetimes, with 45-day certificates the direction of travel. RedirHub has already published on this repeatedly: lets-encrypt-45-day-certificates, why-shorter-certificate-lifetimes-manual-redirect-management-unsustainable, redirect-infrastructure-checklist-45-day-certificate-era, and acme-vs-manual-certificate-management. None of it is being promoted.

This is the rarest thing in B2B marketing: a forced migration event with a deadline someone else set, landing on precisely the buyer who pays the most. A team managing 200 redirect domains by hand does not have a preference problem when renewals go from annual to eight times a year. It has an operational failure waiting to happen.

The position nobody holds

Every competitor occupies one of three spots. Bitly and Rebrandly own marketing links. Cloudflare and nginx own we already have this in our stack. urllo and redirect.pizza own redirect tooling for teams.

Nobody owns redirect infrastructure that an AI agent can operate. RedirHub has shipped a live MCP server, a complete llms.txt, and robots.txt access for GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot. No competitor in the comparison set has an answer to it. The category has not yet had this conversation, which means the position is available and cheap to take.

The practical version of the claim: an AI agent can generate a landing page in thirty seconds and then cannot point a domain at it. That gap is real, it is felt by exactly the developer audience that will be building these workflows this year, and RedirHub closes it today.

What this means for the plan

  • Concede tier one. Keep the free shortener because it feeds the funnel and costs nothing, but stop investing marketing effort in competing with Bitly on shortening. That market's price floor is zero and its buyers do not become $1,200 a month accounts.
  • Win tier two on the certificate deadline. It is the only trigger in this category that forces a decision on a timetable, and the content already exists.
  • Take the AI-agent position before it is contested. It costs a page and a launch, and no competitor can respond quickly because none of them have shipped the capability.
  • Treat self-hosted as the competitor, not Bitly. The paying customers switched from nginx and htaccess, not from a shortener.
