Document 08 · Closing

Delivery That Sells

The Blueprint's two weeks are the real sales process for the retainer. Every touchpoint is either building the client's confidence that you understand their business, or wasting the chance to. This is the delivery playbook written with that in mind.

The line this document does not cross

Selling through delivery means showing the client what you can do. It never means making the plan harder to run alone, holding back a finding, or padding the hours. If a Blueprint concludes "you do not need us", it says so. That honesty is mechanism 5 in doc 01 and it is why the expensive tier gets chosen.

Day 0 · The welcome email

Subject: Your Blueprint starts now

Hi [name] and [partner],

Deposit received, thank you. Here is how the next two weeks run.

Kickoff: [date and time], 75 minutes, both of you please. Calendar invite attached.

What I need before then (whatever you have, nothing needs tidying):

  1. Access to Google Business Profile, Google Ads, Meta Ads and Google Analytics, if you have them. I will send the invites, you click accept.
  2. Sales or invoice totals by month for the last 12 to 24 months.
  3. Your last 10 enquiries that did not turn into business, with whatever notes exist.
  4. What you spend on marketing each month, and with whom.
  5. Anything you have written about the business: a profile, a proposal, a menu, a price list.

If any of this is sensitive, I am happy to sign an NDA first. Just say.

On day 14 I will present the full Blueprint to you both. Between now and then you will hear from me when I find something worth knowing.

Jacques

Day 5 · The first finding

The single highest-leverage message in the whole process. Two weeks of silence followed by a big reveal feels like a black box. One sharp, specific finding on day 5 tells them the money is already working.

"Hi Jade, quick one before the full presentation. Going through your invoices, 48% of your six-figure functions book in October to December, and those clients choose their venue in August and September. Nothing in your current marketing is timed for that window. It's the first thing in the plan. More on the 17th."

Acreage's actual sequencing insight, used as the model. Pick the finding that is most surprising, most specific, and has a date or a rand figure in it.

Day 12 · The quality check

Run before every presentation. A Blueprint that fails any of these does not get presented until it passes.

  1. The guarantee test. Can you list three things in it the client did not know? Write them down. They open the presentation.
  2. Every claim is tagged Observed, Modelled or Assumption, as in the Acreage parts. Nothing is presented as fact that is a guess.
  3. Their words are in it. At least five phrases from the Diagnostic and kickoff calls, used where they belong.
  4. Every phase states its hours and who would do them. No padding, no hiding.
  5. Every partner and owner is addressed by name. Acreage's Andrew was addressed nowhere. Do not repeat it.
  6. The DIY path works. Someone with a capable marketer could run phase one from the document alone.
  7. No internal mechanics: no margin reasoning, no pricing rationale, no tool names the client should not see.

What is standard and what must be bespoke

Standard, built once

  • Document structure, styles and section order
  • Competitor scrape and the review benchmark maths
  • The funnel map template and traffic analysis method
  • Roadmap format with hours per phase
  • The Working Together page and its three tiers
  • Welcome email, document request, NDA

Bespoke, every time

  • The evidence layer, from their own documents
  • The three findings they did not know
  • The order of the 90-day plan
  • The rand figures, from their numbers
  • Their language, in the brand and messaging sections
  • Which tier you recommend, and why

Building the standard half once, as a Blueprint kit in this folder, is task 2.3 in doc 11. It is what takes delivery from 40-plus hours (Acreage) to 29.

Day 14 · The presentation call (90 minutes)

Minutes 0 to 10 · What you did not know

Open with the three findings from the guarantee test. Ask after each: "Did you know that?" This settles the guarantee in the first ten minutes, out loud, before the balance is invoiced.

Minutes 10 to 40 · The diagnosis

Where the business stands, where it leaks, how competitors compare. Use their numbers and their words. Stop for questions.

Minutes 40 to 60 · The 90-day plan

Phase by phase, in order of what makes money first, with the hours each phase takes. Then the honest question: "Looking at those hours, who in your business would do this?" Let them answer. This is mechanism 3 from doc 01, and it is their answer, not your pitch.

Minutes 60 to 75 · Questions

Theirs, about the plan. Do not mention working together yet.

Minutes 75 to 90 · Three ways forward

Open the Working Together page. The script follows.

The three-paths close

"The Blueprint is finished, and it's yours. There are three ways to run it, and I want to be straight with you about each.

"One: you run it yourselves. If [name] has the time, phase one is all in there, and I'll do a free check-in call with you in 30 days to see how it's going.

"Two: you run it, and we keep you honest. That's R7,500 a month: we set up the measurement, run the follow-up system and sit down with you monthly against the plan.

"Three: we run it with you. R14,500 a month for [business], or R24,000 if we're doing both businesses properly. That includes the ads, the pages they land on, and the systems as we build them. Ad spend is separate and goes straight to Google and Meta. We never mark it up.

"There's one more thing. If you decide on two or three within 14 days, R10,000 of the Blueprint comes off your first month. It's month to month after the first 90 days, and at 90 days we review against this exact plan.

"If I were in your position, with [their hours answer from minute 60], I'd look at [the tier you actually recommend], because [one sentence, specific to them]. But you know your business. What feels right?"

Then stop talking. Whatever they choose, the next sentence is the same: "Great. I'll send the balance invoice for the Blueprint today, and [the retainer agreement / the date for our 30-day check-in]."

Terms for the retainer agreement

Acreage's proposal said month to month on 30 days' notice; the signed agreement said 90 days. Standardise on one so it never conflicts again:

Recommended standard terms

An initial 90-day term (three invoices, month one in advance), then month to month on 30 days' notice, with a formal review against the Blueprint at day 90. Ad spend paid by the client directly to the platform. Invoiced by JCE Systems Group. The proposal and the agreement must say the same thing, word for word.
