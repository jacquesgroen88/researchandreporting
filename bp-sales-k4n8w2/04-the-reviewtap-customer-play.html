Most of these are small businesses that bought one stand. Some are not. The job is to find the 5 to 10% who fit doc 03 and talk to them as individuals, while giving everyone else something useful and an easy door.

Three rules before a single message goes out
  1. POPIA and the entity line. These customers gave consent to hear from ReviewTap, not from JCE Media. Every message to the base goes from ReviewTap, signed by you as founder, offering a free Business X-Ray "from the ReviewTap team". JCE Media appears as the team that delivers the Blueprint, introduced once the person has replied. Unsubscribed customers get nothing, ever. Get the wording checked against POPIA section 69 (direct marketing to existing customers) in week one (doc 12).
  2. The exit. The customer list is part of what a ReviewTap buyer purchases. Selling JCE services to it is fine while you own it, and it also makes the list look more valuable, but record every JCE client sourced from it so the sale terms can deal with it cleanly. Tag them jce-sourced-from-rt.
  3. Fulfilment and dedupe first. Run the standing checks: drop anyone with an unfulfilled order, match against live Shopify, never pitch strategy to someone waiting for a stand.

How to build the tiers (one afternoon, Claude does it)

  1. Export subscribed buyers from Shopify with order count, total spent, email domain, company field and products bought.
  2. Remove unfulfilled orders, staff accounts and blacklisted contacts (the Smart Card cut did exactly this: 567 to 511).
  3. Flag business domains (not gmail, yahoo, outlook, icloud, webmail).
  4. Run Apify Google Places on the business names for category, rating, review count and number of locations.
  5. Score each against doc 03 using what is knowable from outside: size proxy (reviews, locations), segment, spend proxy (order value).
  6. Write the tier as a GHL tag on the ReviewTap location: bp-tier-a, bp-tier-b, bp-tier-c.

The bridge story

The single idea that connects a R649 review stand to a R30,000 strategy engagement. Every message in this document is a version of it.

The bridge

"Reviews get people to your door. What happens after they arrive is where most businesses leak."

You bought ReviewTap to fix one thing. It is one of seven places a business loses money: reputation, being found, speed to answer, follow-up, conversion, repeat business, and knowing which of your spend actually works. We built ReviewTap because we saw the first one break everywhere. We built the Blueprint for the other six.

And the opening gift that makes the bridge credible: their own review progress since the stand arrived. Apify gives you their review count today; their order date gives you the start. "Since your stand arrived in March you have gained 41 Google reviews and moved from 4.3 to 4.6." For the ones where it worked, that line proves ReviewTap delivered and earns the right to talk about the next thing. For the ones where it did not, it is a support conversation first, and the Blueprint can wait.

Do not overclaim

Only use the before-and-after where the numbers are real and favourable, and never imply the stand caused the whole movement. Say "since your stand arrived", not "because of your stand".

Tier A · Personal, from you

Volume: five a day, Monday to Thursday, inside the 08:00 to 10:00 ask block. 30 to 40 contacts over two weeks. Preparation per contact: the scan is built before you reach out, so the first message already contains a rand figure. That is ~45 minutes of Claude-and-you time per contact, which is why this tier stays small.

Step 1 · WhatsApp voice note (45 to 60 seconds)

"Hi [name], it's Jacques, the founder of ReviewTap. You've had our [stand / cards] since [month], and I was looking at your Google profile this morning. You've gone from [x] to [y] reviews since then, which is great to see. While I was looking I noticed a couple of other things that I think are costing you money, nothing to do with reviews. I put them on one page for you. I'll email it now, no catch, it's yours. If any of it is useful I'd love fifteen minutes to talk it through. Speak soon."

Voice, not text. It is the personal signal that separates you from every agency in their inbox, and you sound like an owner, which is what you are.

The RT System clients: a different conversation

Clients already paying monthly (IsoBella Vita, DotCom, Die Veiling Klub and the rest of the RT System roster) are not prospects. They are expansion. Do not send them the sequence.

  1. Ask Diane first for each account's health: is the system live, is anything late, are they happy. Never open an upsell on an account with an open complaint. IsoBella's review-request flow had a client-facing date that had to hold. Check it did.
  2. Use the next scheduled check-in. Show what the system has produced (IsoBella: every review answered, median 26 minutes, record calls and booking clicks in July). Then: "This handles what happens after a guest arrives. The next question is how many more you could be getting and from where. That is what the Blueprint answers. Because you are already a client, you would get the founding price."
  3. Harry (Die Veiling) is mid-build on R64,000. Do not raise a Blueprint until go-live is paid and running. A post-launch "marketing the Klub" Blueprint is the natural follow-on in Q1 2027.

The multi-site groups: open with their own data

The 11 August method still applies and still converts. ~20 minutes of Apify per group: site count, group mean rating, total reviews, the frozen site (lowest rating at high volume), the cheap win (fewest reviews against the median) and one free finding (a closed listing, a duplicate, a wrong category) given away unconditionally. The Blueprint for a group is a group plan: one set of messaging, one measurement, every site. The Branded Fleet programme stays the ReviewTap offer; the Blueprint is the JCE one, and a group can buy both.
