Document 09 · Keeping

After the Blueprint

The money model depends far more on retainers lasting than on Blueprints selling. A retainer that runs twelve months is worth six Blueprints. This document covers all three paths after the presentation, including the one that pays nothing yet.

Path 1 · Do it yourself

Expect roughly half of buyers here. They are not lost. They own a plan with your name on it, and every month they try to run it alone they learn how many hours it takes.

The day-30 check-in script

"How's phase one going? ... What's been harder than you expected? ... Which of these [the phase one tasks] are done, and which are still waiting?"

If most are done: congratulate them properly, ask for a referral, and book the day-90 refresh.

If most are waiting: "That's normal. It's usually not knowledge, it's hours. If it would help, the R7,500 option is designed for exactly this point: you keep doing the work, and we make sure it happens and measure it. No pressure either way." The 14-day credit has expired, so do not reopen it. A second credit teaches buyers to wait.

Path 2 · Retainer onboarding (the first 30 days)

The first month decides whether month four happens. A retainer client should see something move inside 30 days.

  1. Day 1: retainer agreement signed, month one invoiced in advance (minus the credit), GHL sub-account set up as "the ReviewTap System" or JCE workspace (never "GHL" to the client).
  2. Week 1: baseline captured: the four numbers from the Blueprint (usually enquiries, response time, conversion to booking, revenue from marketing). Without a baseline the day-90 review has nothing to compare against.
  3. Week 2: the first quick win from the plan is live. Pick it on the presentation day: the thing that is visible, small and inside your control.
  4. Week 4: first monthly strategy session, run against the Blueprint document itself, phase by phase. Thirty minutes, decisions only, detail in writing (the format Jade asked for on 15 September).

The day-90 review

The review is promised in every Working Together page. Run it properly and it does three jobs at once.

1 · It proves the retainer

The four baseline numbers, then and now. What was planned, what was done, what moved and what did not. Name what did not work; it is the most persuasive part.

2 · It refreshes the plan

The quarterly refresh: what the next 90 days should be, given what was learned. The client leaves with an updated Blueprint, which is the reason to keep paying.

3 · It produces the case study

For founding cohort clients this is the recorded call they agreed to. For everyone else, ask. Then the referral ask (doc 06).

Moving between tiers

Every Working Together page says there is no penalty for moving up or down. Mean it. A client who drops from R14,500 to R7,500 for two months during a slow season, then comes back, is worth far more than one who leaves. Offer the step-down before they ask to cancel.

Expansion

The Blueprint lists the systems builds. Quote them from the roadmap at the right point (a website, a WhatsApp booking system, a membership platform like Die Veiling's), always as separate fixed-price projects with an in/out scope table, never folded into the retainer.

The concentration rule

One client is ~41% of MRR right now

Acreage at R24,000 a month is the same concentration risk Pixsan was. The plan fixes it by adding retainers, not by shrinking Acreage. The rule from here: no single client above 30% of JCE MRR by March 2027, and none above 20% by September 2027. In the base case, four new retainers bring Acreage under 30% around February 2027.
