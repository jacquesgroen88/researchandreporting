The Cold Call Script

You make these calls yourself. A cold call doesn't sell the Blueprint. It asks permission, gives away a free Business X-Ray and books 15 minutes to walk through it. Rewritten 10 October 2026 so it works inside South Africa's direct marketing rules and against real call benchmarks (doc 14).

Index · Previous: 12 · Name: Business X-Ray, described as "a revenue leak check". Not legal advice: have a POPIA attorney check this script once before the first block.
The rules this script is built around
  • The Information Regulator treats a live sales call as electronic direct marketing under POPIA section 69 (Guidance Note, December 2024; confirmed by the chair in November 2025). That means the first call to someone who is not your customer may only ask for consent, once. The industry disputes this and the Regulator is looking for a court test. Plan around the Regulator's view, not the industry's hope.
  • Companies are protected too. POPIA has no business-to-business carve-out. A number on a Google listing is public, not consent.
  • Register as a direct marketer with the National Consumer Commission by December 2026 (R2,574). The national opt-out registry launched on 7 October 2026. Free list cleansing runs December to April, enforcement from May 2027. From then, clean every call list against it first.
  • Calling hours (CPA regulations): weekdays 08:00 to 20:00, Saturdays 09:00 to 13:00, never Sundays or public holidays.
  • Identify yourself and the company on every call: your name, JCE Media, and that you trade as JCE Systems Group (Pty) Ltd if asked.
  • Record and log every consent: date, time, who, what they agreed to, which channels. A "no" means never again, logged as do-not-contact.
  • No WhatsApp to anyone who has not said yes. That breaks POPIA and WhatsApp's own business policy.

None of this weakens the call. A clear "may we send you this, and contact you about it?" is also the best-performing kind of opener in the data: an honest permission ask with a concrete reason (doc 14).

The one rule: never call empty-handed

Before you dial, spend two minutes finding one specific, true thing about their business. Their review count against the nearest competitor, a broken booking link, a ChatGPT answer that leaves them out. That observation is your reason for calling. It is what separates you from the 30% of unknown South African calls people now flag as spam.

What the X-Ray is, in words you can say out loud

"It's a free Business X-Ray, basically a revenue leak check. We look at your business the way a new customer finds and contacts it, compare you with your three nearest competitors, and send you one page with the three biggest places you're losing money, with a rand figure on each. It costs you nothing, and there's nothing to sign."

The script

Identify yourself, and say it is recorded (15 seconds)

"Hi, is that [name]? ... [Name], it's Jacques Groenewald from JCE Media, we're the team behind ReviewTap. Just so you know, I record my calls. You weren't expecting me, so can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I'm phoning, and then you decide if it's worth carrying on?"

Honest permission plus a stated reason was among the best-performing openers in Gong's 2024 analysis of 300 million calls. Never say "is this a bad time?" It was the worst opener in the same data.

The reason: one observation (20 seconds)

"I've been looking at [category] businesses in [area], and I noticed one thing about yours: [the observation]. Usually when I see that, there are two or three other places a business is losing money that are easy to miss from the inside."

Stop and let them react. Do not pitch anything here. This is the reason you are asking permission, nothing more.

The consent question (20 seconds)

"So my question is: would you like us to send you a free Business X-Ray? [The definition above.] If yes, we'd send it on WhatsApp and email, and we'd like to be able to contact you about it and about our marketing services. You can tell us to stop any time. Is that OK?"

Yes: go to step 4, and log it (who, what, channels, time). No: "No problem at all. I'll make sure we don't call you again. Thanks for your time." Log do-not-contact.

This is the one approach the law allows to ask for consent, and it names the three things the guidance note expects: who you are, what you would market, and which channels.

Three questions, so the X-Ray is accurate (60 seconds)

"Great. So the rand figures are yours and not my guesses, three quick things:"

  1. "Roughly how many enquiries do you get in a normal month?"
  2. "What's a typical customer worth to you, roughly?"
  3. "Who looks after your marketing at the moment?"

These give you the X-Ray's rand figures and two of the five qualifying criteria in doc 03. Clearly under 10 points: still send the X-Ray, the light version, and point them to the cheaper option later.

Book the walk-through now (30 seconds)

"I'll have it on your WhatsApp by [day]. It's much more useful if I take you through it, so can we put 15 minutes in the diary for [day after]? Morning or afternoon?"

"And is there anyone else who owns the business or makes marketing decisions with you? It'd be good to have them on too."

Book it 1 to 3 days out. Same-day and next-day bookings show up far more often than ones a week away.

Close and confirm (15 seconds)

"Lovely. You'll get a calendar invite from me in a minute, and a WhatsApp so you've got my number. Thanks [name], speak on [day]."

Send both before the next dial, with the date and time in words. Remind them 24 hours and 1 hour before. Expect 15 to 25% no-shows even so, and phone any no-show within five minutes.

Total: under four minutes. No Blueprint and no price on this call. If they ask what you sell: "We help businesses like yours plan and run their marketing. But the X-Ray is useful whether you ever work with us or not. Let's start there."

The gatekeeper

"Hi, it's Jacques Groenewald from JCE Media. I'm hoping to speak to [owner name / the owner]. It's about something I noticed on your Google listing. Are they in?"

If asked what it's about: "I was comparing a few [category] businesses in [area] and spotted something that's probably costing you bookings. It's a two-minute conversation. When's the best time to catch them?"

Get the owner's name and the best time. Do not ask for a WhatsApp number and do not pitch the gatekeeper. Log the callback time; that is your second attempt.

Voicemail

"Hi [name], it's Jacques Groenewald from JCE Media, the team behind ReviewTap. I'll try you again [day]. If you'd like to reach me first, my number is [number]. Thanks."

Name, company, number. No offer, no marketing, and no follow-up WhatsApp. A recorded voicemail is the clearest case of "electronic communication" under the law, so keep it to a callback request.

Your number

About 30% of unknown calls in South Africa are flagged as spam, so an unfamiliar mobile number loses answers before you say a word. Call from one consistent number, give it a verified business name on Truecaller, and use the same number for WhatsApp Business with the JCE Media name and logo, so the confirmation message after a yes comes from the number they just spoke to.

The warm version: past ReviewTap customers

Use this only for the Tier A ReviewTap customers in doc 04. They are ReviewTap's customers, not JCE's, so the call is still a consent request naming JCE Media. The difference is the opener, which does a lot of work.

"Hi [name], it's Jacques Groenewald, the founder of ReviewTap. I record my calls, just so you know. You've had our [stand / cards] at [business] since [month]. Is it still working well for you? ... Good to hear. You've gone from [x] to [y] Google reviews since then.

Then steps 4 to 6. If the stand isn't working or an order is open, stop and fix that first. Log a yes as consent for JCE Systems Group, not ReviewTap.

Logging

Every dial on the JCE GHL Blueprint pipeline or the day's call sheet: outcome (no answer, gatekeeper plus callback time, voicemail, conversation, consent yes, consent no, walk-through booked), the three answers, the observation used, and for a yes the consent record (date, time, channels, recording reference). Source field: Cold call. After two weeks, Claude reports owner-conversation rate, consent rate and which observations worked.

Your time, at realistic rates

Three blocks a week is about 6 hours of calling plus list preparation, producing 3 to 6 X-Rays a week (2 to 4.5 hours to build). At 35% of X-Rays booking a walk-through and a third of those becoming Diagnostic Calls that close at a third, cold calling alone yields roughly one Blueprint every four to eight weeks. It is a supporting channel. The ReviewTap base, referrals and the landing page carry the volume.
