Document 13 · Getting the lead

The Cold Call Script

You make these calls yourself. A cold call doesn't sell the Blueprint. It gives away a free Business X-Ray and books 15 minutes to walk through it. Get both on the first call and the rest of the funnel (doc 07) takes over.

Index · Previous: 12 · Name: Business X-Ray, described as "a revenue leak check". Both are working names.
The one rule

Never cold call empty-handed

Before you dial, spend two minutes finding one specific, true thing about their business. Their review count against the nearest competitor, a broken booking link, a ChatGPT answer that leaves them out. Saying "I noticed..." in the first 30 seconds is what separates you from every other agency that phones them. It's also the hardest part to fake, which is why it works.

What the X-Ray is, in words you can say out loud

"It's a free Business X-Ray, basically a revenue leak check. We look at your business the way a new customer finds and contacts it, compare you with your three nearest competitors, and send you one page with the three biggest places you're losing money, with a rand figure on each. It takes us about 45 minutes. It costs you nothing, and there's nothing to sign."

Learn that by heart. Everything else on this page can be said in your own words.

The script

The opener (15 seconds)

"Hi, is that [name]? ... [Name], it's Jacques Groenewald from JCE Media. You weren't expecting my call, so I'll be quick. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I'm phoning, and then you can tell me if it's worth carrying on?"

Asking for permission gets more people to stay on the line than launching straight in. Nearly everyone says "go on". If they say "I'm busy", go to the callback line in the objections table.

The reason (30 seconds)

"We're the team behind ReviewTap, the Google review stands. Over 1,200 South African businesses use them. I've been looking at [category] businesses in [area], and I noticed something about yours: [the one observation]."

Examples of the observation:
"you're rated higher than [competitor], 4.7 against 4.4, but they have 1,400 reviews and you have 68, so Google shows them first."
"the booking button on your website takes me to a page that doesn't load on my phone."
"when I ask ChatGPT for the best [category] in [area], it names three of your competitors and not you."

Then stop. Let them react. Most people ask a question here, and a question means they're engaged.

The give (20 seconds)

"That's one thing I could see from the outside, and there are usually two or three more. So here's why I'm calling: we do a free Business X-Ray. It's basically a revenue leak check. [The definition above.] Would that be useful for [business]?"

Three questions, framed as making it accurate (60 seconds)

"Great. So the rand figures are yours and not my guesses, can I ask three quick things?"

  1. "Roughly how many enquiries do you get in a normal month?"
  2. "And what's a typical customer worth to you, roughly? A booking, a job, a sale?"
  3. "Who looks after your marketing at the moment?"

Those three answers give you the X-Ray's rand figures and two of the five qualifying criteria in doc 03 (size and current spend). If the answers clearly put them under 10 points, still send the X-Ray, but the light version, and point them to the cheaper option later.

Book the walk-through now, not later (30 seconds)

"Perfect. I'll have it on your WhatsApp by [day, two working days out]. It's much more useful if I take you through it, so can we put 15 minutes in the diary for [day after]? Morning or afternoon?"

"And is there anyone else who owns the business or makes marketing decisions with you? It'd be good to have them on too."

Booking the walk-through while they're still on the call is the most important step on this page. An X-Ray sent with no meeting booked usually gets read once and forgotten. The question about other owners is the Acreage lesson: book every owner from the start.

Close the call (15 seconds)

"Lovely. You'll get a calendar invite from me in a minute, and I'll WhatsApp you so you've got my number. Thanks [name], speak on [day]."

Send the invite and the WhatsApp before you dial the next number. Use the exact date and time in words: "Friday 23 October, 10:00".

Total: under four minutes. The Blueprint and the price are never mentioned on this call. If they ask what you sell, say: "We help businesses like yours plan and run their marketing. But honestly, the X-Ray is useful whether you ever work with us or not. Let's start there."

The gatekeeper

"Hi, it's Jacques Groenewald from JCE Media. I'm hoping to speak to [owner name / the owner]. It's about something I noticed on your Google listing. Are they in?"

If asked "what is it regarding?": "I was comparing your business with a few others in [area] and spotted something that's probably costing you bookings. It's a two-minute conversation. When's the best time to catch them?"

If they offer to take a message: get the owner's name and the best time to call, and ask for their WhatsApp. Don't pitch the gatekeeper. Thank them by name. You'll be speaking to them again.

Voicemail, and the WhatsApp that follows it

Voicemail (under 20 seconds)

"Hi [name], Jacques Groenewald from JCE Media, the team behind ReviewTap. I noticed something on your Google listing I think is costing you bookings. I'll send you a WhatsApp with the details. Speak soon."

WhatsApp, straight after

"Hi [name], Jacques from JCE Media, I just tried to call. I noticed [the observation]. We do a free Business X-Ray, a one-page revenue leak check comparing you with your three nearest competitors. Want one for [business]? No cost, nothing to sign."

Only WhatsApp a number that is published as a business WhatsApp line, or one the gatekeeper gave you. If someone says not to contact them, log it in GHL as do-not-contact and never call that number again.

The warm version: past ReviewTap customers

This replaces the Tier B call in doc 04, and you make it yourself. It's the same structure, but the opener does the work, because they already know you.

"Hi [name], it's Jacques Groenewald, I'm the founder of ReviewTap. You've had our [stand / cards] at [business] since [month]. Is it still working well for you? ... Good to hear. I actually had a look at your profile: you've gone from [x] to [y] reviews since then, which is great.

"The reason I'm calling: we've started doing a free Business X-Ray for some of our customers. It's a revenue leak check, looking at everything after the review: how people find you, how fast you answer, whether they book. One page, three leaks, a rand figure on each. Would that be useful?"

Then steps 4 to 6 exactly as above. If the stand isn't working, or an order is still open, stop and fix that first. The X-Ray can wait a week. A ReviewTap customer who feels ignored can't be won back.

Under decision D6 (doc 12), ReviewTap customers hear from you as the founder of ReviewTap, and JCE Media is introduced once they have said yes to the X-Ray.

Logging every call

Every dial gets logged on the JCE GHL "Blueprint" pipeline, or on a cold-list sheet that Claude imports daily. Record the outcome (no answer, gatekeeper, voicemail, conversation, X-Ray agreed, walk-through booked, do not contact), the three answers from step 4 and the observation you used. Set the source field to Cold call. After two weeks, Claude reports which observations and which segments got the most X-Rays agreed, and you keep what works.

Your time

Three blocks a week is about 6 hours of calling, plus about 1 hour of list preparation (Claude) and up to 7.5 hours making the X-Rays it produces. That's roughly 14 hours a week before a single Blueprint is delivered. If the warm ReviewTap play (doc 04) is producing calls, run cold blocks at two a week, not three, until the first January seats are sold.
