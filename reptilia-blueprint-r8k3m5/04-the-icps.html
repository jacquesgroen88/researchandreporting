The ideal customer profiles

Four profiles, each built from what people actually bought rather than from a workshop. Every number below is measured, and every profile carries the one thing the site should do differently for it.

Derived by grouping all 579 buyers by first purchase, order rhythm, basket composition, geography and lifetime value

How these were built

Standard practice is to invent three personas with names and stock photos. Instead, every customer was grouped by what was in their first order, then measured on what they went on to do. The four clusters below fell out of the data with no interpretation needed, and together they account for 96% of revenue.

ICP 1 — The Feeder

The single most valuable customer type in the business, and the cheapest to acquire

Who they are. They already own the animal. A bearded dragon, a leopard gecko, a ball python, a tarantula. They are not shopping for a pet, they are restocking food, and they will keep doing it every few weeks for the next ten to fifteen years.

What they buy

Crickets (126 different buyers, 43% of them repeatedly — the highest repeat rate of any product in the store), mealworms, superworms, Dubia roaches, frozen mice and rats by size, coco peat, sphagnum moss. Baskets of 2 to 4 lines, 7 to 20 units, R400 to R800. They buy combinations: crickets with mealworms 47 times, crickets with superworms 24 times.

What the data says about them

  • They run on a clock. 50% of all second orders in the store happen within 30 days, and 40% within 14 days.
  • They are price-aware but not price-led. They pay R160 shipping on a R500 basket routinely.
  • Only 13.6% add an ice pack to a frozen order over the full history, 27% in the last twelve months. That is a service failure as much as a revenue one.
  • They are the group most damaged by the relaunch: Superworms 70, Frozen Mice Pinkies and Mealworms 300 — three of their staples, with 31, 36 and 37 distinct buyers — are all dead URLs.
What to do for them

Build the reorder reminder and set it to 25 days. Restore the three dead feed URLs first. Put an ice pack prompt in the cart on every frozen line. Offer a standing order or a "same again" one-click reorder from their account. This profile does not need persuading; it needs remembering.

Acquisition note: a Feeder costs the least to acquire and returns the most. If Reptilia ever spends money on advertising, this is what the ads should sell — not animals, not cages. Crickets.

ICP 2 — The New Keeper

Highest first order, worst retention. The biggest missed opportunity in the business

Who they are. Usually a parent buying a first reptile for a child, or an adult getting into the hobby. They arrive to buy an animal — a bearded dragon, a leopard gecko, a corn snake — and they are nervous, because it is alive and it costs R1,500 to R4,500 and they have never met the seller.

The problem, stated plainly

190 orders have contained a live animal. Only 50 of them included any feed at all, and only 31 included a cage. Somebody buys a bearded dragon from Reptilia and then buys its crickets, its cage, its heat lamp and its substrate from somebody else — or from the pet shop down the road. Then they never come back, which is exactly what the 16.2% repeat rate says.

This is the conversion that matters most in the whole business: a New Keeper who becomes a Feeder is worth R1,587 instead of R1,111, and buys for a decade.

Why the site loses them

  • 133 animals are listed. 72 have a completely blank description. No sex, no age, no morph, no feeding history, no temperament, no care requirements.
  • Not one livestock page mentions a permit. Several species sold here need one, and a first-time buyer does not know that.
  • 94% of products have exactly one photo. For a R2,950 animal that is not enough.
  • Reviews are switched off store-wide, so there is zero reassurance from other buyers.
  • No care sheet, no "what else do I need", no setup bundle.
What to do for them

Give the top 40 animals real pages: sex, age, morph, current feeding, temperament, three photos, the permit position and the collection-or-courier terms stated up front. Then put a "Everything this animal needs" block directly on the page — cage, heat, UVB, substrate, hide, first month of food — as one add-to-cart. Follow with a care email at day 3 and a first-reorder prompt at day 21.

ICP 3 — The Habitat Builder

Biggest single basket, almost never seen again

Who they are. Somebody upgrading or setting up an enclosure. They buy a cage, a terrarium, a rack or a breeder unit, sometimes with heating. Often they already keep animals and are expanding; sometimes they are a breeder scaling up.

142 orders have contained a cage or terrarium. 52 of them contained nothing else at all — no substrate, no heating, no hide, no decor. On a R1,297 average basket that is a customer buying a box and walking away.

Reptilia has the attach categories to fix this and they are strong on their own: Substrates R50,787 lifetime, Heating and lighting R39,622, and 50% of cage orders already include a hide or decor without anyone suggesting it.

What to do for them

A "complete the setup" block on every cage page — substrate, heat source, thermostat, hide, water bowl — priced as a kit with a small saving against buying separately. Then a follow-up at 60 days, because the person who built one enclosure builds another. The custom cage-building service Reptilia already offers in the physical shop is not mentioned anywhere on the site, and this is the profile that would pay for it.

ICP 4 — The Bulk Account

Roughly 25 people carrying a fifth of the revenue, managed by nobody

Who they are. Breeders, smallholdings, lodges, rescue operations and serious multi-animal keepers. They buy 20 and 40 kilogram bags, frozen rodents by the hundred, and they order on a rhythm. They look like retail customers in the database and they are not.

The concentration risk

Montego Monty & Me Essential Adult 40kg is the second-biggest product in the catalogue at R18,759 — and it has two buyers, one of whom (Bianca Jansen Van Vuuren) accounts for R18,199 of it across 26 orders and has now been gone for 351 days. Marmoset Porridge: R13,652 from 18 buyers. JUAVA Adult Dog 40kg: R4,564 from 3 buyers.

These are account relationships being run as if they were shelf sales. Nobody calls them. There is no trade price, no standing order, no priority stock hold, no named contact. When one of them quietly stops, as has now happened with the largest, the store loses 6% of its revenue and finds out from a spreadsheet a year later.

What to do for them

Name them — there are roughly 25, it is a morning's work. Give them a standing order, a trade price band or a volume discount, and a person who phones every month. This is not marketing, it is account management, and it is the highest revenue-per-hour activity available to Willem personally. Start with the two Montego buyers and Frans Pieterse at Harrismith Inn (R6,541 across 3 orders, AOV R2,180, last ordered 23 May).

Who is NOT the ideal customer

The dog and cat aisle

Customers whose first purchase was dry pet food have the lowest lifetime value in the business at R838 and only 19.4% come back. The catalogue carries Dog Toys (46 never sold), Cat Toys (29 never sold), Fish Accessories (27 never sold), Bird Accessories (22 never sold) and Dog and Cat Disinfectants (26 never sold).

Reptilia is competing against Absolute Pets, Petshop.co.za and every supermarket in the country on dog food, with no advantage and no story, and it is diluting a specialist brand that does have an advantage. The bulk dry food accounts in ICP 4 are worth keeping — they are relationships, not a category. The general dog, cat, bird and fish accessory range is not, and it should be de-prioritised in every navigation, every campaign and every content decision. Nothing needs deleting. It just should not get attention.
