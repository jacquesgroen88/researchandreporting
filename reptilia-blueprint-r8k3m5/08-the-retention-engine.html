The retention engine

Reptilia sells food to animals that have to eat every fortnight for fifteen years, and has never once reminded anybody. This is the machine that fixes that, and it is five emails.

Built on the measured 30-day median repurchase cycle, the 443-customer lapsed list and the 37.8% repeat rate of the Feeder profile

The whole argument, in one sentence

There is a 90-day window in which a Reptilia customer decides whether they are a customer, and nothing in the business currently happens inside it

The 90-day repeat rate has fallen from 38.7% to 12.3% since 2022. Every rand of growth since then has come from finding somebody new. Recovering half of that decline is worth R12,916 a year priced at one extra order — and far more than that priced properly, because a recovered Feeder buys every 30 days for years.

2. The five flows

In build order. Flows 1 and 2 are the money; 3, 4 and 5 compound it. All five run on the same tool and the same customer list.

The win-back — one campaign, R31,688

Audience: 443 customers silent 92+ days who have spent R300 or more, worth R644,466 historically. Build this first because it needs nothing built.

Send in three waves, warmest first, so the copy can be adjusted between them:

WaveSegmentPeopleTheir AOVAngle
1Silent 6 to 12 months75R1,009Highest value in the list. "Your usual is back in stock" plus what has changed.
2Silent 3 to 6 months28R732Warmest. A short nudge, no discount needed.
3Silent 1 to 2 years133R890Needs a reason to return. New range, new site, an offer.
4Silent 2 years+207R877Cold. One send, expect little, costs nothing.

Before wave 1, phone the top 10 personally. Bianca Jansen Van Vuuren (R46,403, 42 orders, 321 days past her own 30-day cycle) should not receive a bulk email. She should receive a call from Willem.

The reorder reminder — the permanent machine

Trigger: 25 days after an order containing any consumable — frozen feed, live feed, substrate, dry food. Twenty-five, not thirty, so it lands before the customer runs out and goes looking elsewhere.

Content: exactly what they bought last time, as a one-click reorder. Not a newsletter, not a promotion. "Time to restock? Here is your last order." Plus the ice pack, prompted.

Why it works here: 32 customers are overdue against their own established rhythm right now, worth R19,595 if each placed one order. That list regenerates every month, forever, and today nobody is looking at it.

Refinement once it is running: use each customer's own median gap rather than 25 days for everyone. Bronwyn Roodt's cycle is 58 days; Brysen Naidoo's is 8.

The new-keeper welcome — turning ICP 2 into ICP 1

Trigger: an order containing a live animal. 105 customers a year enter here at an R845 first order, and only 16.2% ever come back — the conversion that matters most in the business.

  • Day 0 — order confirmation with the care sheet for the species bought. Not a generic receipt.
  • Day 3 — "how is it settling in?" with the three things new keepers get wrong for that animal, and a reply address that reaches a human.
  • Day 21 — first restock prompt. This is where a New Keeper becomes a Feeder, and a Feeder is worth R1,471 against R1,111.
  • Day 60 — the upgrade nudge: bigger enclosure, better lighting, a second animal.

The care sheets do not exist yet. They are the same content as the livestock product pages in document 07, item 3, so write them once and use them twice.

The abandoned checkout — R1,483 and a diagnostic

Trigger: an order created and not paid within an hour. 21 a year currently, down from a peak of 60 in 2024. 107 email addresses have reached the order stage across five years and never bought anything, ever.

The revenue is small because the failure rate has already improved from 23.8% to 5.4%. The reason to build it anyway is that it tells you why people fail — and right now nobody knows, including why the rate improved so sharply in the first place.

The review request — trust, compounding

Trigger: three days after delivery. Feeds leak 6, worth R5,763 a year on its own, and it is the only one of the five that gets more valuable the longer it runs.

Competitors show 920+ reviews. Reptilia shows none, on a 0.4% refund rate over five years and 998 orders. That refund rate is the single strongest trust signal in the entire business and it is currently invisible to every visitor.

3. What has to exist before any of it runs

Three prerequisites, in order
  1. A list, with consent. 249 customers — 43% of the base — have only ever checked out as guests. Reptilia has their email on an order and nothing else. Under POPIA, an order email supports transactional and closely-related follow-up; a broad marketing send to people who never opted in is a different question and needs a decision. Reorder reminders for something they bought are the safest ground; a general newsletter is not. Get the marketing consent checkbox onto the checkout now, so the list from here forward is clean.
  2. A tool. Reptilia has none. Options in section 4.
  3. Accurate stock. 1,481 website-flagged products show zero stock on hand and the site marks nothing sold out. Do not send a reorder reminder for something that is not there. One "sorry, out of stock" after a prompt costs more trust than the email earns.

Why the real number is bigger than R51,850

Flow 2 is priced at one extra order per recovered customer, which is deliberately conservative. A recovered Feeder orders every 30 days at an average of R589 to R700. One of them, held for a year, is worth R7,000 to R8,000. The model prices that at R700 so the total does not rest on a compounding assumption — but the business case does not.

Eight days of work against R51,850 in year one, on a business that turned over R192,099 in the last twelve months.

6. What to measure

Four numbers, checked monthly. If these move, the engine is working; if they do not, stop and diagnose rather than sending more email.

  1. 90-day repeat rate of each new cohort. The headline number. 12.3% today, 25% is the target, 38.7% was the 2022 reality.
  2. Customers overdue against their own cycle. 32 today. It should trend toward zero as the reminder catches them.
  3. Share of revenue from returning customers. 30.4% today, 48.2% in 2022.
  4. Size of the lapsed list. 443 today. It should shrink, and if it grows the reminder is firing too late.

All four come from the same order table this blueprint was built from, so they can be regenerated by re-running site/tools/audit-commercial-deep.mjs at any time. No analytics access required — which matters, because that is still blocked.
