The number that should be uncomfortable

Someone buys a live animal from Reptilia and 84% of the time they do not buy it a home

190 orders have contained a live animal. Only 31 included a cage. Only 50 included any food. A bearded dragon at R2,500 cannot live in the box it arrived in, and it eats within 48 hours. Those customers are buying the cage, the heat lamp, the UVB tube, the substrate and the crickets — they are just buying them somewhere else. And then, 84% of the time, they never come back: the livestock repeat rate is 16.2%.

3. The three bundles the data has already written

Bundle 1 — "Everything this animal needs"

Where: every livestock product page, directly under the add-to-cart.
Contents: enclosure sized for the species, heat source, thermostat, UVB where required, substrate, hide, water bowl, and the first month of the right food.
Price: as a kit, with a modest saving against buying the parts separately — 10% is enough; this does not need to be a discount play.

Why it works: livestock orders already average R1,213 against R731 store-wide, so the willingness to spend is proven. The species determines the kit, so it can be built once per animal type — bearded dragon, leopard gecko, ball python, corn snake, tarantula — not once per listing. Five kits cover most of the catalogue.

The second reason to do it: a New Keeper who leaves with the food becomes a Feeder, and a Feeder is worth R1,471 with a 37.8% repeat rate against R1,111 and 16.2%.

Bundle 2 — "Complete the setup"

Where: every cage and terrarium page.
Contents: substrate, heat mat or lamp, thermostat, hide, water bowl.
Target: the 52 orders a year that buy a cage and nothing else.

Substrates (R50,787 lifetime) and Heating & lighting (R39,622) are strong categories on their own, and 50% of cage buyers already add a hide or decor with no prompting at all. The demand is demonstrated; the prompt is missing. Cage orders average R1,297, so an extra R250 of attach is a 19% lift on the highest-value basket in the store.

Bundle 3 — the ice pack prompt

Where: the cart, whenever any frozen or live item is in it. Not a related-product tile — an interstitial the customer has to decline.
Copy: "Shipping frozen? Add an ice pack so it arrives the way it left."

Ice Pack appears in six of the store's twelve strongest co-purchase pairs, at lifts up to 0.84 — meaning 84% of Frozen Rats Hopper orders that could have included one, did. It is already the store's most widely bought item by headcount after crickets (53 distinct buyers, 157 units, 77 orders) and nobody has ever merchandised it.

This is a service fix as much as a revenue one. 446 frozen orders a year ship without a cold pack. The 0.4% refund rate is remarkable and it will not survive one warm summer.

5. The shipping question, answered honestly

Correcting the 13 August audit

That audit's fourth recommendation was a free-shipping threshold at around R750. It was a reasonable instinct and it does not survive being modelled.

ThresholdOrders already above itShipping given awayNear-miss ordersTheir shortfallGoods gainedNet
R500135R27,60085R119R5,052−R22,548

R1,00051R13,68073R247R9,001−R4,679

Modelled on the trailing twelve months at a generous 50% conversion of near-miss orders. Shipping earned R40,800 in that period across 272 orders.

The arithmetic is simple and unforgiving. The near-miss orders sit only R119 to R247 below the threshold, so each converted one adds about R200 of goods — but Reptilia forgoes R160 of shipping on that order and on all 89 orders that already cleared the bar without any help. The giveaway is bigger than the gain at every level tested.

The one question that flips it

This is modelled on revenue, not gross profit. If R160 does not cover what a courier consignment actually costs Reptilia, forgone shipping revenue is matched by forgone shipping cost and the answer changes completely.

Ask Willem: what does a national courier consignment actually cost, and does R160 cover it? If R160 is roughly break-even, revisit the R1,000 threshold — it is the least damaging of the three and it matches what Silkworm Shop already offers in Gauteng. If R160 carries margin, leave it alone and lift AOV through bundles instead.

What to do about shipping in the meantime

  • State it on the product page. "R160 nationwide, 2 to 3 working days. Free collection in Vanderbijlpark." Right now it is a surprise at checkout on a median R532 order.
  • Promote collection properly. It is free, only 9.6% of orders use it, and the 74 customers who have used it order 2.89 times against 1.55 for everyone else.
  • Consider a Vaal Triangle local rate. A R60 local delivery band would be cheaper than a national courier and would compete directly with the drive-in option.
  • Do not run a blanket free-shipping promotion. On these numbers it is a straight transfer from margin to nothing.

7. Sequencing

Two of these are worth doing before the summer season rather than during it. January to March carries 30% of annual revenue off 25% of orders, and it is the window when a child gets a first reptile and a parent buys the setup with it.

Now — cross-sells and the ice pack prompt

One and a half days. Immediate, permanent, and it protects the refund rate through summer.

September — the five livestock kits

Built alongside the livestock product pages, since both need the same species knowledge.

October — the win-back campaign

The weakest month of the year at R44,226 across five years. Fill the trough and warm the list before the season.

1 December — everything live for the season

Bundles, kits, cross-sells, reviews and the reorder reminder all running before January arrives. The season is decided before it starts.
