The site plan

How to improve reptilia.co.za, ordered by revenue rather than by how broken things look. Twelve items. None of them is a redesign.

Builds on the 13 August technical audit rather than repeating it · Live-site state as at 15 August 2026, after the search fixes shipped on 14 August

Where the site stands today

The 13 August audit scored the store 4.6 out of 10 across twelve dimensions. Its one-line summary is still the right one: a structurally sound store with a serious findability problem and an unrepaired migration. Payments work, the checkout is clean, the variable product structure is correct, images are nearly complete and the refund rate is near zero. The problem is that customers cannot find things.

The twelve items, in revenue order

1. Publish the redirect map P0

Worth R18,005 a year. Half a day, after one hour of checking.

1,591 hard 404s from the 5 August rebuild, carrying 29.8% of the last twelve months of line revenue. A 1,705-row map already exists at findings/redirect-map.csv: 589 rows high confidence, 800 needing a human eye, 313 with no match that fall back to the shop page.

Do not apply it blind. The automatic matcher sent /product/frozen-rats/ to frozen mice and /product/green-iguana-hatchling/ to a red iguana. The top 150 rows carry R387,202 of lifetime value and need about an hour of eyeballing first. Load the reviewed map into a redirect plugin rather than raw .htaccess, so it is manageable and reversible. There is currently no redirect plugin installed.

2. Turn product reviews on P0

Worth R5,763 a year, and more than that in trust. One setting plus an email.

woocommerce_enable_reviews is set to no. Zero reviews exist on a store that sells live animals at up to R14,495 to strangers, against competitors showing 920+ reviews and per-product star ratings.

Turn it on, then send a review request three days after delivery. Seed it first: Reptilia has a 4.6 Google rating and five years of customers, so ask the top 50 lapsed customers for a review in the same email that asks them to come back. Two jobs, one send.

3. Write the livestock pages P0

Worth R27,390 a year. Content, not code. Start tomorrow.

133 animals listed, 98 have never sold a unit, R178,945 of shelf value on those 98, 72 with a completely blank description and not one mentioning a permit.

Every livestock page needs, as a fixed template:

  • Sex, age and morph — the three things a keeper asks first
  • Current feeding — what it eats now, how often, when it last ate
  • Temperament and handling — beginner-suitable or not, stated plainly
  • Adult size and lifespan — the two things buyers regret not knowing
  • Permit position — required or not, and who arranges it
  • Delivery or collection terms — stated on the page, not discovered at checkout
  • Three photographs of the actual animal, not a stock image
  • "Everything this animal needs" — the attach block from document 09

Do the top 40 by price first. At roughly a day per ten animals including photography, that is four working days spread across a month.

4. Fix search properly P0

Unsizable without analytics, and almost certainly the biggest of all. Half a day.

Three entry points now exist after the 14 August work and all route to the product template. The underlying engine is still WordPress core search, which does not read categories:

A customer who owns a bearded dragon and types "lizard" is told the shop has nothing. Install Relevanssi (free), which indexes categories and tags, handles plurals and partial words, and turns "lizard" from 0 into 7 and "snake" from 10 into 75. It rewrites how every query resolves, so it is red lane — staging clone first, and not on the same day as any other search change.

5. Put categories in the navigation P1

The main menu has five links and not one of them is a product category. "Shop" is a 7,698-pixel wall of category tiles rather than products. There is no sort by price, no price filter and no attribute filter anywhere. 53 of 149 categories are empty, so drilling down can end in nothing, and two competing food category trees run in parallel.

Minimum viable fix: a dropdown with the eight categories that actually earn — Live Feed, Frozen Feed, Reptile Cages, Substrates, Heating & Lighting, Livestock, Small Animals, Accessories — then price sort and a price filter on category pages. Delete or hide the 53 empty categories and merge the duplicate food trees.

6. Page caching P1

Homepage median time to first byte is 3.21 seconds, ranging 2.24 to 4.37 on identical requests. A healthy store is under 800ms. 93% of that is server wait — 10Web Booster is doing good work on the front end and the server underneath is undoing it. No page caching, no CDN, a single 1,875 KB aggregated CSS file (13 times the size of the HTML it styles), four Google font families, and PHP 8.1 which is past active support.

Page caching alone does most of it. Half a day.

7. Add Product schema P1

Product pages carry no Product structured data, so Google sees no price and no availability. That means no rich results in search, and no route to Google Shopping without building a separate feed. Yoast free does not add it for WooCommerce; the WooCommerce SEO add-on or a schema plugin does. While in there: robots.txt is a default placeholder with no sitemap line, the homepage has no meta description and two empty H1 tags, and /returns-policy/ 404s because the real page is /refund-and-return-policy/.

8. Show shipping and stock on the product page P1

Shipping cost appears nowhere until checkout, so R160 is a surprise on a store whose median order is R532. Add a single line — "R160 delivery nationwide, 2 to 3 working days. Free collection in Vanderbijlpark." — to every product page.

More seriously: 1,481 website-flagged products have zero stock on hand and the site marks nothing as sold out. Customers are ordering things that are not there. That is a refund and a lost customer, and it is a plausible contributor to the retention decay in document 01.

9. Build the Product-Code import P1

929 products live against roughly 2,912 the shop actually sells. Fixing this by hand is the wrong move because a future wholesale re-import would wipe the work. Build it once, keyed on Product Code, writing Category, stock on hand and price only, never touching descriptions, images or SEO fields, reading codes as text (Excel has already corrupted five barcodes into scientific notation), and excluding rows flagged Non Website Related or Not Linked.

Two things to check in the same pass: 11 products flagged not-for-website are live including a R14,495 Fiji Banded Iguana, and 39 products are priced below the POS price, in every case with the site cheaper — a Small Animal Cage at R530 against R2,850.

10. Add EFT as a payment method P1

PayFast is the only method; bank transfer, EFT and cash on delivery are all disabled. A meaningful share of South African shoppers prefer direct EFT and currently have no option but to abandon. 107 email addresses reached the order stage and never bought anything, ever. This is a WooCommerce setting, but it touches the payment path, so it is red lane.

11. Put the shop on the website P2

Reptilia has traded from Vanderbijlpark since December 2006, holds a 4.6 Google rating, runs a reptile exhibition and a free public petting zoo, and builds custom cages to order. None of this appears anywhere on reptilia.co.za.

Customers who have collected in store order 2.89 times against 1.55 for everyone else. Whatever happens in that building converts people, and the website is the one place it is invisible. An About page with real photographs, the year, the rating and the staff. Plus the custom cage service, which is currently mentioned only in third-party directory listings.

12. Fix the product photography standard P2

The grid is correct — five columns of uniform 241x241 tiles. The source images are not: sampled natural dimensions from one row ran 300x225, 300x300, 272x300, 300x287, 253x300, 300x224 and 108x298. The tall narrow one renders as a sliver with white either side, which is why the results look uneven when the grid is fine. This is photographic, not CSS: consistent square aspect ratio on a consistent background. 94.4% of products also have exactly one photo, which is thin for a R2,950 animal.

What NOT to do

Three tempting things that would waste the quarter
  • A redesign. Nothing in the money model requires one. The store's problem is that products cannot be found and customers are not brought back, and a new theme fixes neither. The site was rebuilt ten days ago; rebuilding it again would break the same links twice.
  • Advertising. Spending money to send traffic to 1,591 dead URLs, a search that returns nothing for "lizard", and a homepage that takes 3.2 seconds is paying to fill a bucket with holes in it. Fix items 1 to 6, then revisit.
  • The free-shipping threshold. It was the fourth recommendation in the August audit and, modelled properly, it loses money at every level tested. See document 09.

Working rules for anyone touching this site

  • Snapshot before every change. The pipeline at site/tools/wp.mjs pulls the full site state to JSON and commits it, because an Elementor site lives in the database, not in files. The snapshot you did not take is the one you needed.
  • Green lane goes to live; red lane goes to staging first. Copy, images, meta and analytics are green. Cart, checkout, shipping, plugins, layout and categories are red. Reptilia takes real money through PayFast, and a broken checkout is lost revenue, not a cosmetic bug.
  • Simulate a user interaction before trusting any layout measurement. 10Web Booster delays JavaScript until first interaction, so an automated browser measures the pre-JS layout. This already caused one wrong "the mobile header is broken" diagnosis and one unnecessary revert.
  • One change at a time on live. The search plugin and the header search change must not ship on the same day.
