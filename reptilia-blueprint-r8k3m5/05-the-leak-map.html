Leak 1 — 443 lapsed customers nobody has ever mailed

R15,844 low · R31,688 working · R59,415 high

The evidence

443 past customers have been silent for more than 92 days and have spent R300 or more. They have already paid Reptilia R644,466 between them, and their combined average order value is R396,102. There has never been a win-back email, SMS or WhatsApp campaign — not one, in five years.

The assumption

A first-ever win-back to a warm list recovers one order from 8% of it, at each customer's own historic average order value. 8% is at the low end of published reactivation benchmarks precisely because this list has never been mailed and consent is thin.

Why it is first

It needs no development work, no site change and no approval from anybody but Willem. It is a spreadsheet and two days of writing. Nothing else in this document has that ratio.

The honest caveat

This is largely a one-time recovery, not an annual annuity. It becomes recurring at a lower rate once a standing lapsed-customer flow exists, which is document 08.

Leak 2 — the highest-value category has the worst product pages

R14,608 low · R27,390 working · R45,649 high

The evidence

133 live animals are listed. 98 have never sold a single unit. R178,945 of shelf value sits on those 98 alone, out of R200,560 across the whole livestock range. 72 have a completely blank description. Not one of the 133 mentions a permit, and several species sold here legally require one. Meanwhile livestock produced R121,351 of lifetime revenue at an order AOV of R1,213 against the R731 store average, so the demand is real and proven.

The assumption

Proper pages on the top 40 animals — sex, age, morph, feeding history, temperament, care requirements, permit position, collection or courier terms, three photographs — move 15 of the 98 never-sold listings in a year at their listed price (average R1,826).

Why it is second

It is pure content, no code, and it can start tomorrow. It is also the fix that makes ICP 2, the New Keeper, convert — and that is the profile with the highest first order and the worst retention.

Leak 3 — 1,591 old product URLs return "page not found"

R11,253 low · R18,005 working · R24,756 high

The evidence

The 5 August rebuild left 1,705 old product URLs without a home; 93.3% of a 300-URL sample return a hard 404, extrapolating to roughly 1,591 dead pages. 29.8% of the last twelve months of line revenue (R45,012) came through products that are no longer reachable at a working address. 467 dead URLs have a sales history worth R572,432 lifetime; 158 sold in the last twelve months; 58 sold in the last 90 days. /product/frozen-rats/ earned R41,283 across 1,315 units and sold on 2 August. It is a 404 today.

The assumption

Restoring a dead link recovers 40% of the demand that used to flow through it. The rest is people who would have found the product anyway through search or navigation, plus products that were genuinely discontinued on purpose.

The catch

A 1,705-row redirect map already exists, generated by title matching. Do not publish it blind. The matcher sent /product/frozen-rats/ to frozen mice and /product/green-iguana-hatchling/ to a red iguana. The top 150 rows carry R387,202 of the value and need about an hour of human checking. That hour is the best-paid hour in this document.

Leak 4 — new customers stopped coming back

R6,458 low · R12,916 working · R19,375 high

The evidence

Measured on an identical 90-day window so cohort age cannot explain it: the chance a new customer orders again within 90 days has fallen from 38.7% (2022) to 12.3% (2026). Ninety-day revenue per new customer fell from R1,249 to R784. Nothing has ever been built to prevent this: no reorder reminder, no post-purchase sequence, no loyalty mechanic, no newsletter capture outside the checkout page.

The assumption

Recovering half the lost repeat rate — from 12.3% back to about 25%, not to the 2022 peak — on 140 new customers a year, each returning for one additional order at R700.

Why the number looks small and is not

R12,916 understates this badly, because it prices one extra order per recovered customer. A recovered Feeder orders every 30 days for years. Priced at true lifetime value rather than a single order, this is the largest number in the document by a distance. It is stated conservatively on purpose, so the total does not depend on a compounding assumption.

Leak 5 — the website carries a third of what the shop sells

R5,724 low · R11,449 working · R22,897 high

The evidence

The POS export flags 2,912 items as website products. 929 are live. The gap is worst in Reptile Livestock (346 missing), Dog Accessories (182) and Reptile Accessories (115). Separately, 1,481 website-flagged products show zero stock on hand and the site marks nothing as sold out, and 11 products flagged not-for-website are live anyway, including a R14,495 Fiji Banded Iguana. 39 products are priced below the POS price, in every case with the site cheaper — a Small Animal Cage at R530 against R2,850.

The assumption

Counted at quarter weight against the dead-URL line to avoid double counting, since the two overlap heavily. Only products with a proven sales history are included.

The real point

The 39 underpriced products are a live margin leak of unknown size that is not in this model at all, because we cannot see cost. That one is worth checking this week regardless of everything else in this document.

Leak 6 — reviews are switched off store-wide

R1,921 low · R5,763 working · R11,526 high

The evidence

woocommerce_enable_reviews is set to no. There are zero product reviews on a store that sells live animals at up to R14,495 to people who have never met the seller. Reptilia holds a 4.6 Google rating, has traded from a physical shop in Vanderbijlpark since December 2006, runs a reptile exhibition and a free public petting zoo — and none of that appears anywhere on the website. Competitors do the opposite: Jozi Bugs displays 920+ customer reviews, Silkworm Shop shows per-product star ratings on its best sellers.

The assumption

A 3% relative lift in conversion on trailing-twelve-month revenue. This is the softest line in the model and it is flagged as such: without GA4 access there is no conversion rate to measure against, so this is an industry-shaped estimate rather than a measurement.

Leak 7 — nothing is ever sold alongside anything else

R2,025 low · R4,433 working · R6,650 high

The evidence

Zero upsells and zero cross-sells are configured across all 929 products. Zero coupons have ever been created. Zero products have ever been on sale. Across five years, 70 of 516 frozen or live feed orders included an ice pack (13.6%, rising to 27% in the last twelve months), despite Ice Pack appearing in six of the store's twelve strongest co-purchase pairs at lifts up to 0.84. 54 of 142 cage orders included substrate, heating or decor. Only 26% of livestock orders included any feed at all.

The assumption

Ice pack attach rises from 13.6% to 40% at R25 each. Cage accessory attach rises 15 points at R250 average. Livestock kit attach rises 15 points at R400 average.

Why the number is small and worth doing anyway

R4,433 is the smallest working figure on this list. It is still one day of configuration work, it is permanent, and it makes the ice pack problem — 446 frozen parcels a year shipping without a cold pack — go away. That is a refund-rate insurance policy as much as a revenue line.

Leak 8 — abandoned and failed orders are never followed up

R742 low · R1,483 working · R2,966 high

The evidence

181 orders have been cancelled or failed, worth R124,632. 107 of the email addresses behind them never went on to buy anything, ever, representing R83,767 of orders that were started and never completed. Nobody has ever emailed a single abandoned basket. PayFast is also the only payment method — EFT, bank transfer and cash on delivery are all disabled.

The assumption

A standing abandoned-checkout email recovers 10% of the 21 failures a year at the store average order value. The annual number is small because the failure rate has already been fixed — it fell from 23.8% in 2024 to 5.4% in 2026.

The finding underneath the number

Something improved and nobody knows what. A 23.8% to 5.4% drop in failed checkouts is a large, valuable, unexplained change. It should be identified and written down so a future plugin update or setting change does not silently undo it.

Leak 9 — the one that is not a leak

R0 · modelled and rejected

Correcting the 13 August audit

That audit recommended a free-shipping threshold at around R750. Modelled properly, it loses money at every level tested.

R50013585R119−R22,548
R7508978R204−R11,898
R1,0005173R247−R4,679

Shipping earned R40,800 in the last twelve months. Giving it away on the orders that already clear the threshold costs more than the basket uplift from the near-miss orders, whose average shortfall is only R204. Even at a generous 50% conversion the R750 threshold is R11,898 down.

The one question that changes the answer

This is modelled on revenue, not gross profit. If R160 does not actually cover what a national courier consignment costs Reptilia, then the forgone shipping revenue is matched by forgone shipping cost, and the arithmetic flips. That number is not in the data and only Willem has it. The question to ask him is: what does a courier consignment actually cost you? Until that is answered, the threshold stays off the plan and the AOV work happens through bundles instead — see document 09.

What is deliberately NOT in this model

Naming these matters as much as the nine above, because their absence is why the total is a floor rather than a forecast.

  • Traffic and conversion rate. GA4 and GTM are firing on the site but under an account JCE Media cannot access. We can measure what buyers did; we cannot measure how many people arrived and left. This is almost certainly the biggest number missing from the document.
  • Site speed. The homepage takes 3.21 seconds to first byte against a healthy sub-800ms. That has a real conversion cost which cannot be sized without analytics.
  • Search and navigation. 929 products, no category in the main menu, no filters, no sorting. Scored 2/10 in the August audit and known to be the single biggest conversion blocker, but unquantifiable without traffic data.
  • Margin. Everything here is revenue. Cost of goods is not in the WooCommerce data, so no line has been converted to profit. The 39 products priced below their POS price are a live margin leak of unknown size.
  • Paid advertising. No channel is running. Building demand before fixing the shop would be spending money to send people to 404s.
  • The physical shop. Twenty years of trading, a 4.6 Google rating, an exhibition and a petting zoo. Genuine assets, no way to size them from order data.
The summary

R113,126 a year, on a business that turned over R192,099 in the last twelve months

None of it requires a redesign, a rebuild, a new platform or a rand of advertising spend. Six of the nine leaks are closed with configuration and content. The two largest — the lapsed list and the livestock pages — need no developer at all.
