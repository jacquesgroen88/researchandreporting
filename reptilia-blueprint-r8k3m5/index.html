If you read one thing

The store is growing on new customers because it stopped keeping the old ones

A customer who first bought in 2022 had a 38.7% chance of ordering again within 90 days. One who first bought in 2026 has a 12.3% chance — measured on an identical window, so age is not the explanation. Revenue kept rising because acquisition covered it. There are now 443 lapsed customers who have paid Reptilia R644,466 between them and have never once been contacted.

The five moves

Publish the redirect map

1,591 dead URLs carrying 29.8% of last year's line revenue. Half a day, after an hour of human checking. R18,005

Mail the 443 lapsed customers

No development work at all. Phone the top ten first. R31,688

Turn reviews on, build the day-25 reorder reminder

Against a measured 30-day repurchase cycle. R18,679

Write the top 40 livestock pages

98 of 133 animals have never sold. 72 have a blank description. None mentions a permit. R27,390

Configure cross-sells on the top 60 products

All 929 are currently empty. One day. R4,433

How to review this

Open WIKI.html to read the documents side by side, mark each one approved or needs changes, and leave notes anywhere you disagree. Export the feedback as markdown and paste it back, and every note gets worked through: what was applied, what needs your decision, and what I would push back on.

The caveat that applies to everything here

Every number comes from the order database, which is complete and reliable. What is missing is traffic. GA4 and Google Tag Manager are installed and firing on reptilia.co.za under an account JCE Media cannot access, so conversion rate, traffic sources and landing pages are all unknown. We can say what buyers did. We cannot say how many people came and left without buying — and that is almost certainly the largest figure missing from this document. Getting that access is decision 1 in document 10 and a five-minute job for whoever set up the site.
