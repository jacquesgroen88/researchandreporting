Trailing twelve months: 272 orders, R192,099, average order R706. Revenue has grown every year since 2021. This is not a business in trouble. It is a business running on one cylinder.

The three things that matter

Finding 1 — the biggest one

New customers stopped coming back, and it happened gradually enough that nobody noticed

Measured on an identical 90-day window so cohort age cannot explain it: a customer who first bought in 2022 had a 38.7% chance of ordering again within 90 days. A customer who first bought in 2026 has a 12.3% chance. Ninety-day revenue per new customer fell from R1,249 to R784. Reptilia is acquiring more customers than ever — 161 new buyers in 2025 against 75 in 2022 — and each one is worth a third less.

The store papered over it with acquisition. Returning customers were 48% of revenue in 2022 and are 30% now. Nothing was ever built to hold them: there is no reorder reminder, no post-purchase email, no abandoned-basket follow-up, no loyalty mechanic and no newsletter capture outside the checkout page. For a shop whose top seller is crickets, bought by 109 different people at a median of 30 days between orders, that is the whole ballgame.

Finding 2

There is a list of 443 past customers that has never been contacted, once

443 people have gone quiet for more than 92 days — three full purchase cycles — and have already paid Reptilia R644,466 between them. Top of that list is one customer who spent R46,403 across 42 orders — 6.4% of everything the store has ever taken — and who last ordered in August 2025. There has never been a win-back email, SMS or WhatsApp to any of them.

Finding 3

The relaunch broke 1,591 links and the catalogue is a third of the real shop

Rebuilding the site on 5 August left 1,591 old product URLs returning "page not found", and 29.8% of the last twelve months of line revenue came through products that are no longer reachable. Separately, the POS export flags 2,912 items as website products and 929 are live. Everything else in this blueprint is worth less until those two are fixed, because they are the difference between a customer finding a product and a customer leaving.

One correction to the 13 August audit

That audit recommended a free-shipping threshold at around R750. Modelled properly, it loses money. Shipping earned R40,800 in the last twelve months. Giving it away on the 89 orders that already clear R750 costs more than the basket uplift from the 78 near-miss orders, whose average shortfall is only R204. Net at a generous 50% conversion: minus R11,898. The answer only flips if R160 does not cover the actual courier cost, and that number is not in the data. It is one question for Willem, in document 09.

What to do, in order

Publish the redirect map — half a day

1,591 dead URLs, with a reviewed map already built. One hour of human checking on the top 150 rows first, because the automatic matcher sent /product/frozen-rats/ to frozen mice. Nothing else on this list is worth full value until this is done.

Mail the 443 lapsed customers — two days

The single highest-return action in the business, and it needs no development work at all. Start with the top 50 by lifetime spend. One of them is worth R46,403.

Turn on reviews and build the reorder reminder — three days

Reviews are disabled store-wide on a shop selling live animals at up to R14,495 to strangers. The reorder reminder fires at day 25 against a 30-day median repurchase cycle.

Write the top 40 livestock pages — two weeks, part time

133 animals listed, 98 have never sold one unit, R200,560 of shelf value, 72 with a completely blank description and not one mentioning a permit. Highest AOV category in the store, worst pages in the store.

Configure cross-sells on the top 60 products — one day

Zero upsells and zero cross-sells exist across all 929 products. Ice Pack already appears alongside frozen feed in 70 orders with nobody prompting it. The data has written the bundles already.

Honest caveat, stated once and it applies throughout

Every number here comes from the order database, which is complete and reliable. What is not here is traffic. GA4 and Google Tag Manager are installed and firing on reptilia.co.za, but under an account JCE Media cannot access. That means conversion rate, traffic sources, landing pages and bounce are all unknown. We can say what buyers did. We cannot yet say how many people came and left without buying — and that number is almost certainly the largest single figure missing from this document. Getting that access is a five-minute job for whoever set up the site, and it is request number one in document 10.
