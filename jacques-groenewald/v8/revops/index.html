CASE 02

Lead-to-quote engine for a travel franchise

Travel agency franchise, South Africa | 2026, ongoing

Revenue operationsRevOpsSystems
2 minto prepare a quote, down from 1 hour
< 1 minto respond to every new lead
95%quote rate: 230 enquiries became 219 quotes in 30 days
$19.5kin 6 bookings in the same 30 days, at ~$2 a lead

The problem

Enquiries arrived through five web forms and WhatsApp. Each quote took about an hour to retype by hand from supplier PDFs, prices sometimes drifted in the retyping, and pipeline stages were moved by hand, so the CRM showed most quoted deals at $0.

What I built

  • Every form and the WhatsApp bot route into one CRM pipeline, with qualification fields and an urgency flag on departure date.
  • An AI quote tool that reads the supplier document, checks every price against the source, and moves the deal and its value when the quote is sent.
  • Booking confirmation and invoice tools on the same record, so each later stage updates itself.
  • When the data showed follow-up, not quoting, was the bottleneck, a follow-up engine triggered on stage entry, with daily tasks and five templates.

What changed

Quoting stopped being the constraint. A quote now takes about 2 minutes instead of an hour, every new lead gets a response in under a minute, and almost every enquiry was quoted within the month, at about $2 a lead. The diagnosis then moved the work to the right place. 54 warm quoted leads had no follow-up cadence behind them, so the next system was built around them instead of around faster quoting.

CASE 06

Five years of orders, one hidden leak

Specialist eCommerce retailer, South Africa | 2026

Data and insightInsightRetention
1,184orders analysed across five years
$6.8ka year in revenue leaks, sized and ranked
446lapsed customers identified for win-back

The problem

Sales looked steady, so nobody had looked at who was buying. The store was being rebuilt, and the owner wanted to know where to focus once it relaunched.

What I did

  • Pulled the full order history and grouped every customer by the year of their first order.
  • Measured each group's 90-day repeat rate and lifetime value.
  • Built a leak model with each line priced, from lapsed customers to missing follow-up.

What changed

New customers were coming back far less often than they used to. The recommendation moved the relaunch priority from acquisition to retention: start with the 446 lapsed customers, then follow up every new order.

CASE 07

A paid membership platform for a pub group

Four-venue hospitality group, South Africa | 2026, live

Systems builtSystemsProduct
  • 4 venues on one platform, launched together
  • Rotating redemption codes, verified by staff at the bar
  • Audited every redemption on an append-only ledger

The problem

The group wanted a paid club membership with weekly drink allowances across all four venues, priced so one membership per venue covers the platform fee, and a way to prove it lifts what members spend.

What I built

  • A production web platform for members, staff and owners, each with its own access.
  • An append-only ledger and versioned package rules, so every redemption can be audited and nothing is quietly edited.
  • Rotating redemption codes that staff verify at the bar, so a screenshot cannot be reused.
  • Owner, manager and staff roles: the owner sees all four venues, a manager only their own.

What changed

The group now has a membership product it can sell and measure: every redemption is recorded per venue, and member spend is tracked from till-slip numbers without a POS integration. For my consultancy it became a monthly fee, not a one-off project.

Stack: Supabase (Postgres, auth, row-level security), Netlify.

CASE 08

A full RevOps review on a live business

ReviewTap, my own review-collection hardware and software brand | November 2026

Revenue operationsRevOpsInsight
2.7:1LTV:CAC, with CAC recovered on the first order
$4.5kyearly cost of poor quality, 9.2% of revenue
8recurring failures costed and ranked by cost

The problem

Revenue lived in the store and the billing system, conversations lived in the CRM, and attribution lived in the ad platforms. None of them wrote back to each other, so nobody could see a customer's whole lifecycle or what it was worth.

What I did

  • Audited the tech stack and the CRM data, then worked through lifecycle stages, the flywheel, unit economics, pipeline health and SLAs on live data.
  • Wrote shared entry and exit criteria for every lifecycle stage, for both the self-serve and sales-led motions.
  • Costed eight recurring failures (cost of poor quality) and solved the biggest on an A3.
  • Set a weekly, monthly and quarterly operating cadence of eight numbers.

What changed

The review showed the problem was not demand: only 11.3% of buyers ever bought again, and almost none had ever been asked to. 42% of the poor-quality cost traced to orders arriving without setup details, so the top fix is one setup page in the order email that replaces two manual chases.

Method

How I work

The same four steps sit under every case above.

Diagnose from data

Pull the real numbers from the store, CRM and ad accounts before forming a view. Most leaks hide in data nobody has grouped.

Cost every leak

Put a money value on each problem and rank by value recovered per hour of effort.

Build the system

CRM stages, automation, SLAs and tracking, built hands-on so the fix does not wait on a vendor.

Run the cadence

A short weekly scorecard, a monthly review and a quarterly re-run, so the gains hold.
