CASE 01

B2B pipeline for UK and SaaS clients

Ensinger UK (engineering plastics, 3 brands), a UK B2B services firm, and AeroCRS (travel-tech SaaS) | 2022 to 2025

Demand genB2B
+53%contact actions for Ensinger UK
−75%LinkedIn cost per conversion, 43 conversions in 30 days
60%reply rate on SaaS outbound, 54% of replies booked a demo

The problem

Long-cycle B2B buyers, small target lists and expensive clicks. Each client needed more qualified conversations without simply spending more.

What I did

  • As fractional Head of Digital Marketing at Ensinger UK, ran digital for three brands, reporting to the Managing Director, and wrote the annual plan.
  • Split-tested LinkedIn video ads and ran Google Search for enquiries.
  • Built ABM outbound in Lemlist and LinkedIn, with sector-specific sequences.
  • Set up GA4 and Tag Manager so every enquiry was counted, then handed the function in-house.

What changed

More enquiries at a lower cost, measured on the client's own site actions rather than platform clicks. The Ensinger work ran for three years before the company took it in-house in November 2025.

CASE 02

Two brands grown from zero

ReviewTap (review-collection hardware and software) and MyForeverBaby (consumer eCommerce), my own brands | 2024 to present

GrowtheCommerce
R930,000+ReviewTap revenue in its first 18 months
3.6xblended ROAS in ReviewTap's record month (R136,000)
R919,000MyForeverBaby gross sales in 14 months, peak month R172,000

The problem

Two new brands with no audience, no reviews and no sales history. Every rand of ad spend had to pay back quickly, because the businesses funded their own growth.

What I did

  • Built each go-to-market from zero: positioning, pricing and a volume ladder, the store, paid acquisition, CRM follow-up and onboarding.
  • Rebuilt the Meta account into prospecting, retargeting and creative testing, judged on store revenue rather than platform-reported ROAS.
  • Mined order history and found 14+ franchise groups buying single units at retail, then built a multi-site account programme and outbound that opened with each group's own order data.
  • Ran a controlled ad audit against first-touch store data before resetting the account.

What changed

Both brands reached real revenue on owned P&L, with contribution margin and stock planning managed monthly. The ad audit showed simple ads beating automated variants by a wide margin, so the account was reset on that finding. The franchise outreach got 2 meetings and 1 yes from the first 9 contacts.

CASE 03

The Seven Figure Blueprint method

SaaS, travel tech, manufacturing, proptech and services companies in the UK, US and South Africa | 2021 to present

StrategyGTM
25+companies, including AeroCRS, WorldTicket, Ensinger UK and Club 720
3 weeksfrom first workshop to a dated 90-day plan
2engagements that became three-year fractional leadership roles

The problem

Founder-led companies usually know they want growth but not where it is leaking. Strategy decks built on assumptions get shelved because nobody can act on them on Monday morning.

What I built

  • A productised engagement in three workshops: diagnostic, ICPs, revenue leak map, offer and pricing, channel plan, money model and a 90-day plan.
  • Every leak costed in rand or dollars and ranked by value recovered per hour of effort.
  • In 2026, a RevOps layer: maturity score, lifecycle SLAs, LTV:CAC, cost of poor quality, an A3 summary and a change plan.

What changed

Clients leave with one ranked list and a plan with dates and owners. Ensinger UK and Club 720 both started as blueprints and ran for about three years as fractional leadership roles.

CASE 04

Lead-to-quote engine for a travel franchise

Travel agency franchise, South Africa | 2026, ongoing

RevOpsSystems
1 hr → 2 minto prepare a quote
< 1 minto respond to every new lead
95%quote rate: 230 enquiries became 219 quotes in 30 days
R322,000in 6 bookings in the same 30 days, at ~R32 a lead

The problem

Enquiries arrived through five web forms and WhatsApp. Each quote took about an hour to retype by hand from supplier PDFs, prices sometimes drifted in the retyping, and pipeline stages were moved by hand, so the CRM showed most quoted deals at R0.

What I built

  • Every form and the WhatsApp bot route into one CRM pipeline, with qualification fields and an urgency flag on departure date.
  • An AI quote tool that reads the supplier document, checks every price against the source, and moves the deal and its value when the quote is sent.
  • Booking confirmation and invoice tools on the same record, so each later stage updates itself.
  • When the data showed follow-up, not quoting, was the bottleneck, a follow-up engine triggered on stage entry, with daily tasks and five templates.

What changed

Quoting stopped being the constraint. A quote now takes about 2 minutes instead of an hour, every new lead gets a response in under a minute, and almost every enquiry was quoted within the month, at about R32 a lead. The diagnosis then moved the work to the right place. 54 warm quoted leads had no follow-up cadence behind them, so the next system was built around them instead of around faster quoting.

CASE 05

Five years of orders, one hidden leak

Specialist eCommerce retailer, South Africa | 2026

InsightRetention
1,184orders analysed across five years
R113,000a year in revenue leaks, sized and ranked
446lapsed customers identified for win-back

The problem

Sales looked steady, so nobody had looked at who was buying. The store was being rebuilt, and the owner wanted to know where to focus once it relaunched.

What I did

  • Pulled the full order history and grouped every customer by the year of their first order.
  • Measured each group's 90-day repeat rate and lifetime value.
  • Built a leak model with each line priced, from lapsed customers to missing follow-up.

What changed

New customers were coming back far less often than they used to. The recommendation moved the relaunch priority from acquisition to retention: start with the 446 lapsed customers, then follow up every new order.

CASE 06

A full RevOps review on a live business

ReviewTap, my own review-collection hardware and software brand | November 2026

RevOpsInsight
2.7 : 1LTV:CAC, with CAC recovered on the first order
R73,900yearly cost of poor quality, 9.2% of revenue
11 weeksof silently failing CRM email, found and fixed the same day

The problem

Revenue lived in the store and the billing system, conversations lived in the CRM, and attribution lived in the ad platforms. None of them wrote back to each other, so nobody could see a customer's whole lifecycle or what it was worth.

What I did

  • Ran the nine-step HubSpot RevOps process on live data: stack and data audit, lifecycle stages, flywheel, unit economics, pipeline, SLAs.
  • Wrote shared entry and exit criteria for every lifecycle stage, for both the self-serve and sales-led motions.
  • Costed eight recurring failures (cost of poor quality) and solved the biggest on an A3.
  • Set a weekly, monthly and quarterly operating cadence of eight numbers.

What changed

The review showed the problem was not demand: only 11.3% of buyers ever bought again, and almost none had ever been asked to. It also found that every CRM email since mid-August had failed, which was fixed the same day. 42% of the poor-quality cost traced to orders arriving without setup details, so the top fix is one setup page in the order email that replaces two manual chases.

How I work

The same four steps sit under every case above.

Diagnose from data

Pull the real numbers from the store, CRM and ad accounts before forming a view. Most leaks hide in data nobody has grouped.

Cost every leak

Put a rand or dollar value on each problem and rank by value recovered per hour of effort.

Build the system

CRM stages, automation, SLAs and tracking, built hands-on so the fix does not wait on a vendor.

Run the cadence

A short weekly scorecard, a monthly review and a quarterly re-run, so the gains hold.
