04
Comparing doors

How I'd compare the four directions

I'm not scoring them here, because I'd only be guessing. This is the frame I'd fill in week 3. For each door it asks what would have to be true, and the cheapest way to check before anyone builds.

DoorWhat would have to be trueCheapest check, kept manual
Compatibility linksPeople send a link to someone they already like, the other person finishes the test, and the result changes what they do next.Track sent to completed by recipient. Call five pairs and ask what happened after.
EventsCompatibility-informed introductions feel better than random pairing, enough that people come back and pay.Run the next event half compatibility-paired and half random. Compare "would meet again" and rebooking.
AI matchmakingA real pool, consent to be suggested, and a match quality people trust over their own swiping.Do it by hand first: you or I pick introductions for 20 users using the test data. If nobody says yes by hand, AI won't fix it.
Matchmaker partnersMatchmakers lose time or clients on a step that the test makes faster, and they'll pay for that.Three matchmaker interviews in week 2, then a concierge pilot with one of them.

Scored on your criteria: customer value, business potential, confidence, effort, dependencies and operational load. Confidence comes from evidence gathered in weeks 1 and 2, not from how good the idea sounds.

Track record

A time I decided not to build

You asked for one example. This is my own product, so every call in it was mine.

The productReviewTap, my NFC and QR card business that sends customers to a review page. We pre-program every device before it ships.
The ideaShip devices "unclaimed" with a self-serve setup screen, so customers connect their own Google profile on first tap. It sounded like a scale win.
The evidenceProgramming took about a minute per device, we'd had 1 refund in 729 orders, and links were already editable after shipping. Self-serve fixed a problem we didn't have and added new ones: devices that do nothing out of the box, people picking the wrong business, and support load moving onto customers.
What I choseNot to build it for fulfilment. I parked it as the onboarding step for the subscription product, the one place it would earn its keep.
What happenedNo build time spent and fulfilment quality held. The spec is ready if the subscription product needs it.
