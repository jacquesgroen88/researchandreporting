Why this works for a shop like yours

  • No live animals in a courier box. Nothing needs a permit check at an online checkout, and nothing arrives stressed.
  • Every animal leaves with a conversation. That is what your staff are good at, and it is how a new keeper becomes a regular.
  • Every visit is a full basket. The page lists the setup the animal needs, so the customer arrives expecting to buy it.
  • Sold-out animals still earn. "Ask us when the next ones arrive" collects a WhatsApp number instead of a dead page.
5The plan

Two jobs for year two

Job 1 · Store visits

Bring animal searchers to the counter

  1. Reservation pages for every animal in stock, kept in line with the POS stock so nobody reserves something already sold.
  2. Rewrite the 10 animal pages Google already sends people to, starting with teddy bear hamster: shown first on Google 960 times a month, clicked 8 times. Publish the three species pages that are ready.
  3. Google Business Profile tuned for "near me": weekly new-arrival photo posts, animals in stock listed, correct hours, and review requests to counter customers too.
  4. Local Google Ads on the searches that bring people in (see chapter 6).
Job 2 · Online sales

Sell more of what people come back for

  1. Sales tracking fixed first. Ready to install now, so every rand of ad spend can be measured from day one.
  2. Feeder reorder reminders. Frozen mice, rats, crickets and mealworms were October's top sellers by volume. A nudge when a customer is due to run out.
  3. Starter setups as one product. Hamster, leopard gecko and bearded dragon setups at one price each. This is how the average order gets back above R550.
  4. Win back past customers: about 440 people with roughly R640,000 of past spending, once you confirm we may contact them.
The December push, from 16 November. December 2025 took R14,200 online. We sell what makes a good gift and a good first week: gift vouchers (bought online, spent in store), starter setups with the animal reserved for collection after the family has spoken to your team, and a holiday feeder stock-up for existing keepers. No surprise pets; those are the ones that come back in January.

One fix outside the fee: the hosting itself. The site sits on a shared plan that switches off server power when it is busy. Every large stock import from the POS (more than 900 products) pushes it over that line, and for the next while shoppers get slow pages or "service unavailable" errors. We hit the same wall twice in August. More traffic from Google and ads makes it worse, not better. Until the move, we run imports after hours and in smaller batches. The lasting fix is a stronger plan with its own dedicated resources, and we handle the move.
9For today

Six answers that start it

  1. Animals: reserve and collect in store, no courier?Everything in job 1 depends on this.
  2. Who answers reservation WhatsApps in the shop, and how fast?A reservation that waits a day is a sale lost to the next shop on Google.
  3. May we email past customers?If they were offered a way to opt out when they bought, a short note about similar products is generally allowed. If you are unsure, we start only with people who opt in from now on.
  4. What does one courier parcel cost you?This settles whether a free-delivery level makes or loses money.
  5. Google Ads: yes or no on about R4,500 a month,and manager access to your Google Business Profile so we can measure calls and directions.
  6. Hosting: may we move the site to a stronger plan?The current shared plan has a hard limit on server power. A big stock import from the POS uses up that allowance, and while it runs, shoppers get slow pages or "service unavailable" errors. We bring you two or three priced options this month; the hosting bill stays in your name.

And one number, if you are willing to share it: roughly what the counter takes in a normal month. From now on the website's job is the counter as much as the checkout.
