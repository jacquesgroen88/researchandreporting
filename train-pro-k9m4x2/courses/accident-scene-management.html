Accident Scene Management

About this course

Accident Scene Management (Bystander Assistance Programs) is a non-profit organisation established in 1996. Thousands of motorcyclists have been trained since the programme began, and ASM is now looked to as a model for programmes worldwide. The goals are to reduce injuries and fatalities at the scene of motor vehicle crashes, reduce rescuer injury at the scene, and enhance the effectiveness of EMS by having trained bystanders tend to the injured until professional help arrives.

Who it is for

  • Motorcyclists and riding clubs
  • Lay rescuers
  • EMS workers
  • Anyone who rides in a group

What you will learn

  • Basic — 100 Series: A Crash Course for the Motorcyclist. One day. Motorcycle trauma basics using P.A.C.T., helmet removal, moving the injured and jaw-thrust rescue breathing.
  • Refresher — 200 Series: Basic Refresher. Three hours, hands-on, to maintain or refresh the Basic skills. Complete every two years to stay current.
  • Advanced — 300 Series: Advanced Bystander Assistance. Roadside medical concerns and how to assist EMS on arrival, with hands-on emphasis on P.A.C.T. and the A.B.C.S.S. of trauma.

Worth knowing

Do not let the name fool you

The Basic 100 Series is the most popular course and contains everything you need to respond to a trauma scene. It is aimed at the lay rescuer but works just as well for an EMS worker.

Take Advanced close to Basic

The 300 Series is only open to people who completed a 100 or 200 Series within the past two years, and is best taken as close as possible to the Basic training.
