Cold Call Playbook

Pick the tab for the business you are about to call. Each tab is one full call, in order. The How to dial tab covers when to call, how often, and the answers that work for everyone.

The job of the call is a booked 15-minute video walkthrough. You are not selling on this call and you are not explaining the system. A date and a time in the calendar is a win.

How oftenSix attempts before you give up

  • Try each number six times over two weeks, at different times and on different days. Most people are reached on a later attempt, not the first.
  • If a row has a landline and a mobile, try the landline first. The owner's mobile is for when the landline fails or the receptionist says they're out.
  • Log every attempt in Notes (for example "Att 3, Wed 15:10, no answer"), so you never call at the same bad time twice.
  • After six attempts with no conversation, mark Status: No answer and move on.

OpenThe first 20 seconds

  1. Greet and ask how they are. "Hi [name], it's Giorgio from ReviewTap." (or JCE Media, see each tab) "How's your week going?" Then let them answer. Always say the company in the first sentence.
  2. Give the reason for the call straight away. Every tab has its own reason line. Say it before anything else.
  3. Ask for 30 seconds, not for permission to exist. "Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why, and you tell me if it's relevant?"
Never open with "Did I catch you at a bad time?" or "Have you got two minutes?" Both invite a no before you've said anything.

Why: Gong's analysis of 90,380 cold calls found that stating the reason for the call more than doubled the booking rate, while "Did I catch you at a bad time?" made a booking less likely than saying nothing at all.

MiddleTalk, then trade questions

  • Know your 30-second story by heart. Every tab has one. Successful cold calls have a longer stretch where the caller explains clearly, not a string of nervous one-liners.
  • Then go back and forth. Short questions, short answers, many turns. Write their answers down in their own words.
  • Talk about their problem, not our system. Never name software, and never say "automation" or "workflow".
  • Say "we" and "us". "We'd look at that together" lands better than "I can sell you".

Why: In the same Gong research, calls that booked a meeting were about twice as long, had about 77% more back-and-forth, and spent roughly half the call on next steps.

CloseAsk for the time, specifically

"It's much easier to show you than to explain it. Do you have your calendar handy? I've got Thursday at ten or Friday at two, which is better?"

  • Always two named times. Never "when suits you?"
  • If neither works, take their time and book it before you hang up.
  • Straight after: send a calendar invite and a WhatsApp confirming the day, date and time.
  • Use the row's Opening line evidence on the walkthrough too. That's where the website findings belong, as proof, not as the reason for the call.

GatekeeperWhen someone else answers

"Hi, it's Giorgio from ReviewTap. I'm hoping to speak to the owner about guest reviews. What's their name, and when are they usually in?"

Write the name in Decision-maker and the time in Next step date. Don't pitch the receptionist.

01Reason for the call

"Hi [name], it's Giorgio from ReviewTap. How's your week going?"

"The reason I'm calling: I work with guesthouses on one thing, getting more of next year's bookings to come direct instead of through Booking.com. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you how, and you tell me if it's relevant?"

02Your 30-second story

"December is the month you meet the most guests all year. Most of them have a great stay, mean to leave a Google review, and forget once they're home. And unless they booked direct, you don't really have a way to reach them again."

"We fix both. Every guest gets a card in the room and a message after they check out, so the review gets written while they still remember the stay. And you end the season with a list of happy guests you can invite back in the quiet months, direct, without paying commission."

03Questions (pick two or three)

  • "Roughly how much of your business comes through Booking.com or the other booking sites?"
  • "Which months are the hardest to fill?"
  • "When a guest checks out, does anyone ask them for a Google review?"
  • "If you wanted to message everyone who stayed with you last year, could you?"

The last question matters most. If the answer is no, that's the problem you're solving.

04Use the row's observation as proof

After they've answered, connect what we found to what they just told you:

No own site or no booking on it: "That's partly why so much goes through Booking.com. Right now the easiest way to book you is through them."

Site marked not secure or out of date: "When a guest does look you up directly, the browser warns them off before they can book."

Good site already: "Your site is good. What's missing is the reviews and the guest list behind it."

"We're full for December.""That's exactly why now is the time. A full December is your biggest batch of reviews and your biggest guest list of the year. The question is whether you keep them."
