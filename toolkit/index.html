Part 1 · The rules

The 5 rules that fix any prompt

Most people type a command and get something generic back. These five lines separate a useless answer from one you can actually use. They work on top of every prompt below.

1.Make it ask you first

The single biggest fix. AI guesses when it lacks context, and guesses are generic. Make it interview you instead.

Before you answer, ask me everything you need to give me the best possible result. Then wait.

2.Give it a role and real limits

"Write me a plan" gets a textbook. A role plus your actual constraints gets a plan you can run on Monday.

Act as my [marketing strategist]. Budget [R2,000], [7] days, goal is [10 new customers]. Give me the plan.

3.Make it grade its own work

The first draft is rarely the best it can do. Asking for a score forces a rewrite, and the second version is almost always better.

Now score that out of 10 like a tough editor, and rewrite anything under 8.

4.Show it what good looks like

Describing the tone you want is slow. Showing two examples is instant, and it copies the pattern properly.

Here are 2 examples of what good looks like: [paste]. Match this exactly.

5.Make it read back as your customer

You are too close to your own offer. This catches the buried price, the vague ask and the line that loses people.

Now read that back as my customer. Would you act on it? If not, fix the exact line that lost me.

The Brand Brain

Write this once, use it forever

The reason AI sounds generic is that it does not know your business. Fill this in once, keep it in your notes, and paste it at the top of any chat before you ask for anything. Everything after it comes back sounding like you.

Paste this before any prompt

Fill in the brackets once. Save it. Reuse it forever.

Here is my business, use this for everything that follows. Business: [what you do, in one line] Customer: [who buys, and what they are worried about] What makes us different: [the honest reason people choose you] Offers and prices: [list them] Tone: [how you talk, e.g. warm and direct, no jargon] Never say: [words or claims you will not use] Ask me if anything is unclear before you answer.

What the rules actually change

Typical prompt
"Write a post about my coffee shop."
Comes back with generic filler about "great coffee and cosy vibes" that could belong to any shop in the world. You rewrite it yourself, so you saved nothing.
Same job, rules applied
"[Brand Brain]. Write a post to fill Tuesday mornings. But first, ask me 5 questions. Then score it out of 10 and rewrite anything under 8."
Comes back asking who your Tuesday customer is and what your slow hours cost you, then writes a post you can publish, and improves it before you have to.
