How it works in practice

What the guest sees when they tap

The card opens your restaurant's Google review box directly. The guest sees the star rating and the comment field straight away, on the correct listing, and submits from there. There is no app to install, no search, and no scrolling through a Maps listing hunting for where to leave a review.

On a phone signed into Google, which is almost every guest, that is one tap to a review form. A QR code by comparison asks them to unlock the phone, open the camera, aim it, wait, and tap a notification first. Same guest and the same goodwill, but a materially different number of people who finish.

If a listing needs to change

Each card points at a ReviewTap address that we manage, which forwards to that restaurant's Google review page. If a site moves, rebrands, or its Google listing is replaced, we change where that address forwards to and every card at that restaurant is correct again the same day. Nothing is reprinted, nothing is reissued, and nothing has to be collected off the floor.

That same layer is what makes the tracking possible, and it is why a code printed directly onto a card can offer neither. It is fixed at the moment it is printed.

Getting the team to actually use them

A three part video series is included, the same for every restaurant. It covers what the card is for, when in the service to present it, and how to handle the moment without it feeling like a request for a favour.

This matters more here than it would elsewhere, because these are franchised restaurants. Head office can set a standard but cannot stand in 34 kitchens. The videos mean a new waiter in Kimberley is told exactly what a waiter in Camps Bay is told, and a manager can point new staff at them without waiting for anybody. Cards that arrive in a box with no explanation end up in a drawer, and that is the most common way a rollout like this quietly fails.

The quickest way to see the difference

One of the Willowbridge franchisees has been running the 3@1 card for a while now. Three questions will settle this faster than anything written here.

  1. How many people tapped or scanned your card last month?
  2. How many of those went on to leave a review?
  3. What was your review rate before the cards went in, and what is it now?

None of the three can be answered. Not through any lack of attention. A printed code has no way of knowing. It opens a page and forgets it ever happened.

Ask us the same three questions about any of the 3,000 locations we run and the numbers come back the same day. For this estate the third is already covered: we captured the baseline rating and review count for all 34 restaurants on 10 August, before anything has been spent, so the before and after is measurable rather than arguable.

We only do this one thing

Group rollouts are most of what we do. We have run them for Mugg & Bean, Steers, Sorbet and Ford, and we are live in over 3,000 locations.

That is the difference underneath the table. A print shop prints for a dentist in the morning and a restaurant group in the afternoon, and that is a perfectly good business. It is simply a different one. We are specialists rather than generalists. Unique codes per site, reporting per restaurant, training for franchisees and redirecting a card when a listing changes are not a sideline for us. They are the entire product.
