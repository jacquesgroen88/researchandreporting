Measurement and the dashboard

Four numbers matter more than everything else combined. This document names them, sets out what gets tracked where, fixes the tracking that is currently wrong, and defines the weekly and monthly review that keeps the plan honest.

Tracking fixes come first. Measuring the wrong thing accurately is worse than not measuring at all.

The four numbers

If only four things are ever checked

Units per order · Group conversations opened · MRR added · Cash against daily spend

Units per order is the metric this entire Blueprint exists to move, from 1.86 toward 3.0. Group conversations opened is the leading indicator for 28% of the money model and it is measurable in week one. MRR added is what re-prices the exit. Cash against daily spend is the constraint that has already cost this business a week of revenue once.

A deliberate simplicity rule

Do not build a data warehouse for this. A single spreadsheet with a weekly tab and a monthly tab, filled in by hand in fifteen minutes, will outperform an automated dashboard that nobody looks at. Automate only the extractions that are genuinely tedious: the Shopify value-band report and the Google search terms export. Everything else is a habit, not a system.

The review rhythm

Monday, 15 minutes: the weekly check

Tier 2 metrics. Three questions: is units per order moving, were three group conversations opened last week, is anything in the pipeline older than fourteen days. Then set the week's outbound targets.

First Monday of the month, one hour: the monthly review

Tier 3 metrics. Compare against the doc 16 ramp. The key question is not "did we hit the number" but "which lever is behind and why", because each lever has a different failure mode and a different fix.

Quarterly, half a day: the strategic review

Tier 4 plus the decision gates from doc 15. Kill what is not working, using the pre-set thresholds rather than fresh judgment. Reallocate outbound effort toward whichever ICPs actually produced.

Reporting for the exit process

The business is under a live sale process, which changes what gets recorded and how.

  • Keep a clean monthly series of revenue, orders, MRR and contract count from August onward. A buyer will want twelve consecutive months of consistent figures, not a reconstruction.
  • Document each new MRR contract with its start date, term and value. Contracted, transferable recurring revenue is what the SaaS multiple is applied to, and undocumented revenue gets discounted.
  • Track owner-dependency honestly. Which revenue came from Jacques personally selling, and which came from channels that run without him. A buyer will ask, and the answer materially affects the multiple.
  • Never restate a metric. If the conversion tracking changed in July, say so in the pack rather than quietly rebaselining. Diligence finds restatements and they cost more trust than the number was worth.
  • Update the exit one-pager quarterly with verified figures only, using the real 4.9 across 29 rating and the actual returns line.
