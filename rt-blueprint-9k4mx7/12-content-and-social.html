12 · Content and assets

Content strategy and social media

ReviewTap's content currently sells a product to an individual. To reach group buyers it has to demonstrate three things repeatedly: that the problem is expensive, that ReviewTap can handle volume, and that it refuses to do the thing that gets listings banned. This document sets the pillars, the platform roles and the path from a post to a bulk enquiry.

Dated execution sits in doc 13. Existing assets available to reuse sit in doc 14.

What content is for here

The strategic job, stated plainly

Content is not the acquisition channel for this business. Paid search and outbound are. Content exists to do three jobs that neither of those can do: make the group buyer trust that ReviewTap can handle fifty units, feed the organic and AI-citation layer in docs 07 and 08, and give outbound something credible to reference. Judge it on those, not on follower growth.

The pillar that is entirely missing today

Nothing ReviewTap has ever published shows volume

Every asset shows one card or one stand. A marketing manager evaluating a 60-outlet rollout has no way of knowing whether this business can handle it. Pillar 2 exists to answer that question before it is asked, and it is nearly free to produce because the orders are already being fulfilled. Photograph every bulk order before it ships. That single habit produces the most valuable content this business can make.

Personal profile outperforms company page by a wide margin for this kind of B2B reach, so post from Jacques and reshare from the ReviewTap page.

The path from post to bulk enquiry

A group buyer sees a benchmark or proof post

They are not looking to buy. They are recognising a problem they already suspected.

They check the profile and the website

This is where the Branded Fleet page from doc 05 has to exist. If they land on a shop selling one stand for R649, the group thought dies there.

They take the free group audit

Not a demo, not a call. Their own locations' ratings, ranked, with the competitive gap. This is the single highest-converting offer in the whole plan because it is useful whether or not they buy.

The audit becomes the sales conversation

The proposal writes itself, because it is a response to their own numbers rather than a generic pitch.

One offer to build before any content ships

The free group review audit. It is the conversion point for LinkedIn, for outbound (doc 10), for LinkedIn ads (doc 09) and for association work (doc 11). Four channels converge on one asset. Build it first: a simple landing page, a form asking for the group name and number of locations, and a templated report that takes under an hour to produce per group.

The honest constraint

Six hours a week of content plus nine hours a week of outbound (doc 10) is fifteen hours. That is a real constraint on a business with one operator and other commitments. If something has to give, cut content before cutting outbound. Outbound produces revenue in weeks, content produces it in months, and this business has a live exit process on a shorter clock than that. The calendar in doc 13 is built so the first four weeks are the highest-value pieces, so partial execution still lands the pieces that matter.
