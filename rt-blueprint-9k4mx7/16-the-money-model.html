What separates the cases

Conservative assumes the offer work ships but outbound is executed only intermittently, at roughly one group account per month. Base assumes nine hours a week of outbound sustained for twelve months. Aggressive assumes the same plus a second person selling from month four, plus one franchise network landing at scale. The single variable that moves this model most is whether outbound is done consistently, not whether the pages are pretty.

The cash caveat that always applies

Contribution is not cash in the bank. Growth consumes stock ahead of demand, and this plan explicitly requires restocking three products in week one. Expect R20,000 to R35,000 a month of that contribution to be reinvested in inventory while scaling. Separately, advertising hits the card daily while Shopify pays out two to three days later, so the binding constraint on scaling spend is float, not margin. Never step a budget up from a return figure alone.

What this does to the sale price

ReviewTap is under a live sale process at a R995,000 ask, on a floor of R750,000. The current valuation is built as hardware SDE at a 2.0x multiple plus recurring revenue at 2.5x ARR.

Read this before quoting that number to anyone

The R2.19m figure is what the current model implies if its multiples hold, and there are three reasons they might not.

1. SDE percentage falls with scale. The existing SDE of R318,000 on R577,240 of revenue is 55%, which is only achievable because the owner does everything. Doubling revenue means fulfilment help, admin and probably a salesperson. The model above already discounts incremental SDE to 40% for this reason, and a buyer may push it lower.

2. Multiples compress on owner dependency. If the new revenue is generated by one person doing nine hours a week of outbound, a buyer will correctly ask what happens when that person leaves. Recurring revenue from group contracts is more defensible than founder-driven outbound, which is another reason lever B matters more than its rand figure suggests.

3. Concentration risk. If two franchise groups make up most of the new MRR, that is a discount, not a premium.

The defensible claim is the narrow one: R14,200 of additional monthly recurring revenue is R170,400 of ARR, which at the existing 2.5x SaaS multiple is roughly R426,000 of additional enterprise value. That figure does not depend on SDE assumptions and it is the number to take into a negotiation.

The rule of thumb, corrected

R1,000 of held MRR is worth about R30,000, not R60,000

The R60,000 figure in circulation comes from dividing the whole R995,000 ask by R15,548 of MRR, which credits the recurring line with the hardware business's value as well. The rigorous number is the SaaS multiple alone: R1,000 per month is R12,000 of ARR, at 2.5x that is R30,000. Use the honest number. A buyer's advisor will do this arithmetic in about ninety seconds and finding an inflated multiplier costs credibility across every other figure in the pack.

The honest summary

What is well supported

  • Bulk demand exists and is measurable: 55 orders, R299,458, 31% of lifetime revenue
  • Franchise demand is proved inside four networks already
  • The margin structure genuinely supports the discount ladder
  • Google returns are real even when halved for the tracking correction
  • Fulfilment at volume has already been run end to end

What is assumption, not evidence

  • Two group accounts per month. Nobody has run this motion yet.
  • Six active partners. The channel does not exist today.
  • 4% monthly reactivation. Reasonable, but untested here.
  • Organic revenue by month 5. SEO timelines are notoriously optimistic.
  • Multiples holding at double the revenue

The conservative case is what happens if most of the assumptions disappoint and only the offer work ships: R58,000 of incremental monthly revenue and R7,000 of MRR, which is still roughly R210,000 of additional enterprise value on the SaaS line alone, against about R305,000 of twelve-month cost. That case is worth executing on its own terms, which is the real test of whether a plan is sound.
