Paid channels and budget reallocation

ReviewTap spends the overwhelming majority of its advertising budget on the channel with the worst return and almost nothing on the channel with the best. This document reallocates it, rebuilds search around bulk and smart cards, and sets the rules that stop the same mistake recurring.

The allocation problem in one line

98% of the budget sits in the 1.2x channel and 2% in the 7x channel

Google Search delivered R534.90 of total spend across thirty days against a budget of roughly R108 a day that should have produced about R3,240. It under-delivered by 84% and nobody noticed, because the channel is too small to appear in a revenue conversation. Meanwhile Meta ran at R750 a day.

The honest caveat on the 7x

A conversion double-count in the Google account was only corrected on 9 July, so part of that window's reported value is inflated. Halve it and non-brand is still above 3.5x, which is still roughly three times Meta's in-account return. The direction of the decision does not change. The size of the first budget step does, which is why the reallocation below is staged rather than immediate.

The bulk campaign will look inefficient on cost per click and it should be funded anyway. These queries are low volume and high value. One conversion is worth R2,500 to R20,000 against a R424 average. Judge it on enquiry value, never on cost per acquisition.

Structural rules for the search account

  • One ad group per intent cluster. Never mix formats, because the ad copy has to match the format the person asked for.
  • Every ad group points at the page built for that cluster in doc 07, not at the shop.
  • Sitelinks to the volume ladder and the multi-location enquiry on every campaign.
  • Negative keywords: free, bot, fake, buy reviews, paid reviews. The integrity position in doc 06 has to be enforced in the account, not just on the website.
  • Location targeting South Africa, with home and recent presence explicitly set.

Creative angles that are missing

The proven winners are time-saving framing (Time Counter, 2.16 to 2.6x), real testimonials (Hoffs, 2.25x warm) and social-proof statics (Static Orange, 3.21x warm but only 0.17% CTR cold, so warm-only). What does not exist is any creative addressed to someone buying more than one. Five briefs:

  1. The fleet shot. Twenty branded stands laid out together. "One for every branch." Speaks directly to ICP 1 and 2 and looks like nothing else in the feed.
  2. The team card. A sales team each holding their own smart card. "Everyone on your team, one tap." ICP 6.
  3. The technician handover. A plumber handing a card to a customer at a front door. ICP 5, and it is the most relatable review moment there is.
  4. The group dashboard. Rating by location on one screen. Speaks to the head-office buyer, who is not currently addressed by any asset.
  5. The refusal. "We will not sell you fake reviews." Contrarian, on-brand, and it separates ReviewTap from the commodity tier in one line.

Copy fixes required before any new creative ships

Three live creatives carry the unverified 4.8 star claim and the unsupported "1,000+ SA businesses" line. The "Then vs Now" static contradicts itself. Fix these first (doc 06), because scaling spend behind an unverifiable claim scales the risk with it.

Abandoned checkout recovery

Roughly fifty abandoned checkouts a month, about R40,000 of value, no systematic recovery. Standard recovery of 10 to 15% returns R4,000 to R6,000 monthly.

  • Verify Shopify's built-in abandoned checkout email is actually on and firing.
  • Standing decision respected: recovery link and a human nudge only. No discount, no free-shipping re-pitch on single units.
  • Carts above R2,500 do not go into an email flow. They get a personal email from Jacques, because a R5,115 cart is a sales conversation.
  • Dedupe against completed orders before every send. Roughly one in four apparent abandonments actually completed. One buyer abandoned three times then completed thirteen minutes later.

Two hard rules that override this table

1. Profitable is not the same as affordable. Advertising hits the card daily and immediately. Shopify payouts lag two to three days, and bank-deposit orders inside that revenue may never land at all. On a thin balance the binding constraint is cash float, not margin. Never scale spend from a ROAS figure alone. Check the balance and the payout timing first. A declined card has previously cost roughly a week of store revenue, which is far worse than running light.

2. Do not tinker. Every budget edit re-triggers learning on the proven Meta creative. Batch changes to a fixed schedule and leave them alone in between.

Operational cautions carried forward

  • A budget-only edit via the API sometimes force-pauses the entity and sometimes does not. It has behaved both ways on different dates. Always follow any edit with an activate call and read back the effective status.
  • New ad sets must be created with location types explicitly set to home and recent, or every ad set blocks publishing with the removed-location-option error.
  • When updating targeting via the API, pass the complete targeting object. Omitted fields are wiped, not preserved.
  • Some ad sets on this account reject a second ad with a dynamic-creative error even when the flag is false. One creative per ad set always works.
